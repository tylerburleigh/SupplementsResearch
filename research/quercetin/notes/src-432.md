# Reader Note: src-432

## Source Identification
- **Title:** Senogenic-senolytic treatment strategies enhance tumor control and can improve survival in murine cancer models: a systematic review
- **Authors:** Eleane C.B. Hamburger, Paolo Brigato, Derek H. Rosenzweig, Lisbet Haglund
- **Journal:** BMC Cancer (Article in Press), 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s12885-026-15650-x
- **Type:** Systematic review (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO-registered CRD420251161998)

## Summary

PRISMA-compliant systematic review synthesizing 36 in vivo murine cancer studies evaluating senolytic/senomorphic agents combined with senescence-inducing anticancer therapies (senogenics) across 11 cancer types. All preclinical (no human clinical data). Quercetin appears in four distinct contexts: (1) Dasatinib + Quercetin (D+Q) with Carboplatin or Olaparib in ovarian cancer (Wang 2024); (2) D+Q with Doxorubicin in hepatocellular carcinoma (Kovacovicova 2018); (3) Quercetin alone at 1.25 mg/kg in melanoma (Magkouta 2025); (4) Quercetin-loaded IQ nanoparticles (Quercetin + IR1061 NIR-II) in triple-negative breast cancer (Yu 2025). The review also references D+Q human clinical trials in non-cancer contexts (idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis NCT02874989, diabetic kidney disease NCT02848131) and ongoing cancer trials (HNSCC NCT05724329, TNBC NCT06355037).

## Key Findings

1. **D+Q effective in ovarian cancer but failed in HCC:** D+Q (5 mg/kg Dasatinib + 50 mg/kg Quercetin) combined with Carboplatin or Olaparib significantly reduced peritoneal and adipose tissue metastases in ovarian cancer (luminescence signal dropped from ~7.5x10^6 to ~2.0-2.5x10^6), decreased SA-beta-gal, and was well tolerated with stable body weight (~21-23 g). In sharp contrast, D+Q (5 mg/kg D + 50 mg/kg Q) with Doxorubicin in hepatocellular carcinoma (Kovacovicova 2018) produced no improvement over Doxorubicin alone -- D+Q alone actually increased tumor burden, and the combination did not reduce SA-beta-gal. This underscores that D+Q senolytic efficacy is highly context- and tumor-type-dependent.

2. **Quercetin as a standalone senolytic showed modest effect in melanoma:** At 1.25 mg/kg (very low dose), Quercetin alone contributed to reduced tumor volume when combined with Palbociclib in a B16 melanoma model, though the strongest effect was seen with the novel senolytic mGL392. Quercetin's contribution was supplementary rather than primary in this context.

3. **Nanoparticle-delivered quercetin (IQ NPs) showed strong synergy in TNBC:** Quercetin + IR1061 NIR-II nanoparticles combined with Palbociclib and NIR-II laser exposure produced the strongest anti-tumor response across all breast cancer models (tumor volume reduced to ~23.7 mm^3 from ~444.9 mm^3 for IQ+Laser alone). This represents a novel delivery strategy that combines photothermal therapy with senolytic activity, significantly outperforming free quercetin.

4. **Clinical translation of D+Q is actively progressing:** D+Q has reached human trials for IPF and diabetic kidney disease, where intermittent dosing reduced senescence biomarkers (p16INK4A, circulating SASP cytokines). In oncology, D+Q + immune checkpoint blockade for HNSCC just completed Phase II (NCT05724329), and a Phase II for reversing chemoresistance in TNBC is recruiting (NCT06355037).

5. **Cross-cancer safety profile is favorable across all senolytic combinations:** No treatment-related mortality or systemic toxicity attributable to any combination therapy was reported across the 36 studies. Body weight remained stable. Several studies demonstrated protective effects (reduced chemotherapy-induced diarrhea, preserved ovarian function, normalized liver/kidney markers).

## Coverage Signal

Specialized and high-quality for Q3 (senolytic evidence) and Q4 (combination protocols), but exclusively preclinical. This is the most rigorous systematic review encountered for senolytic quercetin in cancer models, with PRISMA methodology, SYRCLE risk-of-bias assessment, and PROSPERO registration. Quercetin is one of many senolytics reviewed (alongside Navitoclax, Venetoclax, Fisetin, Procyanidin C1, etc.), so quercetin-specific data must be extracted from the broader synthesis. The review provides strong mechanistic evidence (senescence markers, SASP, proliferation, apoptosis) but no human efficacy data. The clinical trial references are valuable for establishing the translational pipeline.

## Quality Concerns

1. **All preclinical, no human data:** The 36 included studies are all murine in vivo models. Xenograft models predominantly use immunodeficient mice, limiting assessment of immune-mediated senescent cell clearance. Translation to human supplementation contexts is uncertain.

2. **Heterogeneity limits meta-analysis:** The authors explicitly note that variability in animal strain, treatment duration, dosing, and outcome reporting precluded quantitative meta-analysis. All synthesis is qualitative and descriptive.

3. **Risk of bias concerns:** SYRCLE assessment revealed frequent incomplete reporting. Random sequence generation and allocation concealment were seldom described. Blinding of caregivers and outcome assessors was almost uniformly unclear. Several studies lacked vehicle-treated controls.

4. **Quercetin is a minor component:** Of the 36 studies, only 4 involve quercetin. The review's primary conclusions about senolytic efficacy are driven by BCL-2 family inhibitors (Navitoclax, Venetoclax). Quercetin-specific conclusions cannot be extrapolated from the overall positive trends.

5. **Article in press:** The manuscript has been accepted but has not undergone final copyediting. The authors note "there may be errors present which affect the content."

6. **Dosing translation issue:** Murine quercetin doses (1.25-50 mg/kg) do not directly translate to human supplementation doses. The 50 mg/kg murine dose used in ovarian/HCC studies would be roughly equivalent to ~4 mg/kg human (~280 mg for a 70 kg adult) by allometric scaling, but this is imprecise and the authors do not address this.

7. **Negative result underemphasized:** The D+Q failure in HCC (Kovacovicova 2018) -- where D+Q alone increased tumor burden -- is a significant cautionary finding that is somewhat buried in the overall positive framing.

## Extractable Data Points for Synthesis

- D+Q + Carboplatin in ovarian cancer: tumor luminescence reduced from ~7.5x10^6 to ~2.5x10^6 (Wang 2024, ref 15)
- D+Q + Olaparib in ovarian cancer: tumor luminescence reduced from ~7.5x10^6 to ~2.0x10^6 (Wang 2024, ref 15)
- D+Q ovarian cancer: SA-beta-gal decreased, body weight stable 21-23 g, no added toxicity (Wang 2024)
- D+Q dose in ovarian cancer: Dasatinib 5 mg/kg + Quercetin 50 mg/kg, i.p. (Wang 2024)
- D+Q + Doxorubicin in HCC: no improvement over Dox alone; D+Q alone increased tumor burden; SA-beta-gal unchanged (Kovacovicova 2018, ref 57)
- D+Q dose in HCC: Dasatinib 5 mg/kg + Quercetin 50 mg/kg, oral (Kovacovicova 2018)
- Quercetin alone in melanoma: 1.25 mg/kg, oral; contributed to tumor reduction with Palbociclib (Magkouta 2025)
- IQ NPs (Quercetin + IR1061) + Palbociclib + NIR-II laser in TNBC: tumor volume reduced to ~23.7 mm^3 from ~444.9 mm^3 (Yu 2025, ref 6)
- D+Q human trial references: Hickson 2019 (diabetic kidney disease, eBioMedicine), Nambiar 2023 (IPF pilot, EBioMedicine)
- Ongoing cancer trials: NCT05724329 (D+Q + pembrolizumab in HNSCC, Phase II completed), NCT06355037 (D+Q for TNBC chemoresistance, Phase II recruiting)
- Across 36 studies: tumor volume reduced in 97% of combination comparisons; survival extended in all 9 studies reporting survival; no treatment-related mortality
- IL-6 most consistently suppressed SASP cytokine across models
