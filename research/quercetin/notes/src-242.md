## Summary

This is a BenchChem product support document for spiraeoside (quercetin-4'-O-glucoside, CAS 20229-56-5, Cat. No. B190383). It provides a comparative overview of bioavailability across quercetin glycoside forms: aglycone, rutin (quercetin-3-O-rutinoside), isoquercitrin (quercetin-3-O-glucoside), and enzymatically modified isoquercitrin (EMIQ). The guide summarizes known pharmacokinetic differences: glucosides (isoquercitrin) are better absorbed in the small intestine via enzymatic hydrolysis, while rutinosides (rutin) require colonic microbial degradation, resulting in delayed and lower absorption. EMIQ is presented as the superior form with significantly enhanced bioavailability due to increased water solubility and efficient intestinal hydrolysis. The document references 15 primary literature sources but truncates key data tables and diagrams behind a paywall/gate, limiting independent verification of quantitative pharmacokinetic claims.

## Key Findings

1. **Glucosides outperform rutinosides for absorption.** Isoquercitrin (quercetin-3-O-glucoside) is more readily absorbed in the small intestine because intestinal enzymes can hydrolyze the glucose moiety, releasing quercetin aglycone for absorption. Rutin requires colonic microbial cleavage of its rhamnose-glucose disaccharide, leading to delayed and lower overall bioavailability.

2. **EMIQ is the most bioavailable form.** Enzymatically modified isoquercitrin -- a mixture of quercetin monoglucoside and alpha-oligoglucosides -- exhibits significantly enhanced absorption compared to other glycosides and the aglycone. This is attributed to increased water solubility and efficient hydrolysis by intestinal enzymes.

3. **Quercetin aglycone has poor bioavailability.** The document confirms the widely reported finding that quercetin in its unmodified aglycone form has notoriously poor oral bioavailability, motivating the development of glycoside and modified forms.

4. **Pharmacokinetic study design principles are summarized.** The guide outlines standard crossover study methodology, including low-flavonoid diet washout, equimolar dose standardization, blood sampling over 48 hours, beta-glucuronidase/sulfatase treatment of plasma for total quercetin measurement, and HPLC-UV/MS quantification. No novel PK data is presented; the document synthesizes published findings.

5. **Key quantitative data is gated.** The document references a pharmacokinetic comparison table and metabolic pathway diagrams but truncates them behind an "Unlock Full Protocol" gate. The specific Cmax, Tmax, AUC, and relative bioavailability numbers promised in the text are not available in the accessible version.

## Quality Concerns

1. **Commercial source, not peer-reviewed.** This is a product monograph from BenchChem, a chemical supplier. It is not a peer-reviewed publication. The primary purpose is product support and marketing, not scientific communication.

2. **Key data is gated.** The pharmacokinetic comparison table, metabolic pathway diagrams, and experimental workflow figures are all truncated with "Unlock Full Protocol" prompts. This makes independent verification of the quantitative claims impossible from the accessible document alone.

3. **No novel data.** The document synthesizes and restates findings from 15 referenced primary sources without presenting original experimental results. It functions as a secondary review with commercial framing.

4. **PDF conversion artifacts.** The document header warns that conversion fell back to raw text extraction, meaning layout and headings may be unreliable. Navigation elements ("Check Availability & Pricing," "Get Quote") are mixed with scientific content.

5. **Referenced sources are legitimate but repackaged.** The 15 references include PubMed-indexed papers and reputable sources. However, the document's synthesis may be selective to favor BenchChem products. The framing around spiraeoside specifically (the product being sold) introduces commercial bias.
