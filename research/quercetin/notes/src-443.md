# Reader Note: src-443

## Source Identification
- **Title:** Identification of HSP90 inhibitors as a novel class of senolytics
- **Authors:** Heike Fuhrmann-Stroissnigg, Yuan Yuan Ling, Jing Zhao, et al.
- **Journal:** Nature Communications 8, 422 (2017)
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41467-017-00314-z
- **Type:** Original research (in vitro screen + in vivo mouse model)

## Summary

Landmark paper establishing a high-throughput screening platform for senotherapeutics using Ercc1-deficient murine embryonic fibroblasts that senesce rapidly at atmospheric oxygen. The primary finding is the identification of HSP90 inhibitors (geldanamycin, 17-AAG, 17-DMAG, ganetespib, and others) as a novel class of senolytic agents. Quercetin appears as a reference senolytic compound: the dasatinib + quercetin (D+Q) combination was used to validate the screening assay alongside navitoclax, and the paper discusses quercetin's mechanism as involving inhibition of AKT activity and effects on Bcl-2 family members. In vivo, 17-DMAG extended healthspan in Ercc1-/- progeroid mice, reducing p16INK4a expression and delaying multiple age-related symptoms.

## Key Findings

1. **HSP90 inhibitors are a novel senolytic class:** Seven HSP90 inhibitors from four structural classes (ansamycin, resorcinol, purine, pyrimidine) showed dose-dependent senolytic activity at nanomolar concentrations in both mouse and human cells, with EC50 values for senescent cell killing approximately 10-fold lower than for non-senescent cell toxicity. This represents a new target beyond the previously known Bcl-2/Bcl-XL, p53/p21, and PI3K/AKT pro-survival pathways.

2. **D+Q and navitoclax validated the screening platform:** The dasatinib + quercetin combination and navitoclax were used as positive senolytic controls and confirmed as reducing both senescent cell counts and total cell counts in the assay, distinguishing senolytic from senomorphic (e.g., rapamycin) activity. Quercetin's senolytic mechanism is described as targeting survival pathways including Bcl-2 family members and AKT inhibition.

3. **HSP90 inhibitors work across cell types and species:** 17-DMAG showed senolytic activity in mouse embryonic fibroblasts, mouse mesenchymal stem cells, human IMR90 fibroblasts (etoposide-induced senescence), human WI38 cells (replicative senescence), and HUVECs (radiation-induced senescence). Not all cell types responded -- ganetespib was not senolytic in pre-adipocytes -- indicating cell-type specificity even within the HSP90 inhibitor class.

4. **In vivo healthspan extension with senolytic treatment:** Intermittent 17-DMAG dosing (10 mg/kg, 3x/week, 1 week on / 2 weeks off) in Ercc1-/- progeroid mice significantly delayed onset of kyphosis, dystonia, tremor, loss of grip strength, ataxia, gait disorders, and other age-related symptoms. p16INK4a expression was reduced >50% in kidney tissue after acute treatment. These results parallel prior findings with D+Q in the same mouse model.

5. **Mechanism involves PI3K/AKT pathway destabilization:** Senescent cells showed upregulated p-AKT (Ser473), an HSP90 client protein. HSP90 inhibition by 17-DMAG reduced p-AKT within 6 hours without affecting total AKT, destabilizing the pro-survival signaling that protects senescent cells from apoptosis. This mechanism parallels quercetin's reported AKT inhibition.

## Coverage Signal

Specialized and high-quality for Q3 (senolytic mechanism) and Q4 (combination rationale). This is a seminal paper in the senolytics field from the Scripps/Mayo Clinic collaboration (Niedernhofer, Robbins, Kirkland). Quercetin is not the primary subject -- HSP90 inhibitors are -- but the paper provides important mechanistic context for quercetin's senolytic action through the PI3K/AKT pathway. The screening platform validation using D+Q as a positive control independently confirms quercetin's senolytic activity. The in vivo healthspan extension data (using 17-DMAG) parallels and strengthens the broader argument that senolytics, including D+Q, can improve healthspan.

## Quality Concerns

1. **Quercetin is a secondary subject:** The paper's primary focus is HSP90 inhibitors. Quercetin/D+Q is used as a validation control, not systematically studied. No dose-response, pharmacokinetic, or standalone quercetin data are provided.

2. **Progeroid mouse model limitation:** In vivo healthspan data comes from Ercc1-/- progeroid mice, not naturally aged animals. These mice have a maximum lifespan of 7 months and represent accelerated aging due to DNA repair deficiency, which may not translate to normal aging physiology.

3. **Small sample sizes in vivo:** Only 6 mice per treatment group (4 females, 2 males in treatment; 3 males, 3 females in vehicle). No power analysis was reported beyond reference to prior studies.

4. **Single in vivo senolytic tested:** Only 17-DMAG was tested in vivo. D+Q in vivo results in this model are cited from a prior publication (Zhu et al. 2015, ref 29) rather than reproduced here.

5. **No human clinical data:** Entirely preclinical. No bridge to human dosing, bioavailability, or clinical outcomes.

6. **Competing interests:** Y.Z., T.T., and J.L.K. hold patents on senolytic drugs licensed to Unity Biotechnology, creating a financial interest in promoting senolytic research broadly.

## Extractable Data Points for Synthesis

- D+Q confirmed as senolytic positive control in Ercc1-/- MEF screening assay at 1 uM concentration
- Quercetin mechanism described: inhibits AKT activity, affects Bcl-2 family members (PI3K/AKT pro-survival pathway)
- Senolytic vs. senomorphic distinction validated: D+Q and navitoclax reduced both senescent and total cell counts (senolytic); rapamycin and NDGA reduced senescent cells but not total cells (senomorphic)
- Rapamycin and NDGA (lifespan-extending compounds) act as senomorphics, not senolytics
- Curcumin and resveratrol had no effect on senescent cells (consistent with their failure to extend mouse lifespan in NIA ITP)
- HSP90 inhibitor 17-DMAG in vivo: 10 mg/kg oral gavage, 3x/week, 1 week on/2 weeks off, starting at 6 weeks of age
- 17-DMAG reduced p16INK4a >50% in kidney after 1 week acute treatment (3 doses)
- 17-DMAG significantly delayed composite age-related symptom score in Ercc1-/- mice (p < 0.001 at later time points)
- Senolytic activity demonstrated across 5 cell types, 2 species, 3 senescence triggers (oxidative, genotoxic, replicative)
- Fisetin noted as a "quercetin-related flavonoid" with senolytic activity in certain cell types
