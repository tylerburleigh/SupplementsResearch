# Reader Note: src-942

## Source Identification
- **Title:** Dasatinib and Quercetin Combination Increased Kidney Damage in Acute Folic Acid-Induced Experimental Nephropathy
- **Authors:** Battaglia-Vieni A, Marchant V, Tejedor-Santamaria L, Garcia-Caballero C, Flores-Salguero E, Ruiz-Torres MP, Rayego-Mateos S, Sanz AB, Ortiz A, Ruiz-Ortega M
- **Journal:** Pharmaceuticals (Basel), 18(6), 822 (2025)
- **DOI:** 10.3390/ph18060822
- **PMID:** 40573218 / PMCID: PMC12195755
- **Type:** Original preclinical research (murine model)

## Summary

This study tested the senolytic combination dasatinib plus quercetin (D&Q) in a murine model of acute kidney injury (AKI) induced by folic acid nephrotoxicity (FAN-AKI). Mice received a single oral dose of D&Q (dasatinib 5 mg/kg + quercetin 50 mg/kg) 24 hours before folic acid injection, and kidneys were evaluated at 48 hours. Contrary to the hypothesis that D&Q would be protective, the treatment did not improve renal function (serum creatinine, BUN) and actually worsened several damage markers. The combination increased gene expression of kidney injury biomarkers (Lcn2, Havcr1), the senescence marker Cdkn1a (p21), and the proinflammatory SASP component Ccl2. D&Q did not reduce the number of p21-positive senescent cells, did not restore the antiaging factor Klotho, and showed no proapoptotic or antinecroptotic effect. The authors conclude that D&Q was not protective in acute-phase AKI and may exacerbate damage, serving as a cautionary note for clinical translation of D&Q senolytic therapy.

## Key Findings

1. **D&Q did not protect against acute kidney injury and worsened some markers:** Serum creatinine and BUN at 48 hours were not improved by D&Q pretreatment. Kidney injury biomarker gene expression (Lcn2/NGAL and Havcr1/KIM-1) was significantly higher in D&Q-treated FAN-AKI mice versus untreated FAN-AKI, indicating increased severity of injury.

2. **No senolytic effect was observed in acute-phase AKI:** D&Q did not reduce the number of p21-positive tubular senescent cells. Cdkn1a (p21) gene expression was significantly increased by D&Q treatment rather than decreased. The combination failed to attenuate any measured senescence-associated markers.

3. **Proinflammatory SASP components were not reduced; Ccl2 was increased:** D&Q did not diminish SASP proinflammatory gene expression (IL1beta, Ccl5). Ccl2 expression was significantly increased by D&Q, indicating a proinflammatory rather than anti-inflammatory effect.

4. **Klotho downregulation was not restored:** The antiaging factor Klotho, which is suppressed in AKI, remained downregulated despite D&Q treatment. This contrasts with reports of D&Q increasing urinary alpha-Klotho in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis clinical trials.

5. **No proapoptotic or antinecroptotic effects detected:** D&Q did not modify Bax, Bcl2l1/BCLxL, Ripk3, or Mlkl expression, indicating no effect on apoptosis or necroptosis pathways in this model.

6. **Gender differences in injury severity but similar D&Q response:** Female mice showed more severe FAN-AKI (higher BUN, Havcr1, Lcn2, Cdkn1a) than males, but D&Q worsened damage similarly in both sexes.

7. **Authors cite context-dependent D&Q effects from literature:** The discussion notes that D&Q has shown benefits in CKD models, adipose tissue, and old mice, but also cites Raffaele et al. (2021) showing D&Q worsened obesity- and age-dependent liver disease progression, and Manavi et al. (2025) showing senescent cell reduction did not improve recovery in experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis.

## Coverage Signal

High relevance for Q3 (senolytic evidence) and Q5 (arguments against supplementation). This is a significant counter-narrative source: rather than supporting D&Q senolytic benefits, it demonstrates potential harm in acute kidney injury. The study provides direct experimental evidence that the D&Q combination can exacerbate tissue damage, increase proinflammatory markers, and fail to deliver any senolytic effect in certain contexts. The discussion section offers a nuanced comparison with the broader D&Q literature, noting benefits in CKD, adipose tissue, and aging models but contrasting these with negative outcomes in AKI, liver disease, and autoimmune encephalomyelitis. This context-dependence is critical for the research brief. The study also raises the important theoretical point that early senescence activation may be protective in acute injury (via p21-mediated cell cycle arrest preventing uncontrolled cell death), making senolytic elimination of these cells counterproductive.

## Quality Concerns

1. **Single dose and single timepoint:** The study tested only one dose of D&Q (dasatinib 5 mg/kg + quercetin 50 mg/kg) at a single timepoint (48 hours after injury, with pretreatment 24 hours before). Whether different doses, timing (post-injury treatment), or repeated dosing would yield different results is unknown. The authors acknowledge this limitation.

2. **Pretreatment design has limited clinical relevance:** D&Q was administered 24 hours before AKI induction, modeling a prophylactic rather than therapeutic scenario. In clinical practice, AKI is typically unpredictable, so post-injury treatment would be more relevant.

3. **Only young mice studied:** All mice were 3 months old. Since aging increases AKI severity and senescent cell burden, the results may not generalize to aged animals or humans. The authors note this limitation.

4. **FAN-AKI model specificity:** Folic acid nephrotoxicity is one specific AKI model. The authors note that other AKI causes (ischemia-reperfusion, sepsis-induced) should be tested. Different AKI etiologies may respond differently to senolytic therapy.

5. **No quercetin-only control:** The study tested only the D&Q combination, not quercetin alone or dasatinib alone. It is impossible to determine whether the negative effects are attributable to quercetin, dasatinib, or their interaction. The introduction notes that quercetin alone showed renal protection in a diabetic contrast-induced AKI model via Sirt1 activation, suggesting the negative outcome here may be driven by dasatinib or the combination rather than quercetin alone.

6. **Small sample sizes:** Groups ranged from 4 to 9 animals. Some comparisons used non-parametric tests with Dunn's test without correction, which may be underpowered for detecting moderate effects.
