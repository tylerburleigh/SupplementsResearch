# Reader Note: src-401

## Source Identification
- **Title:** Quercetin as a Possible Complementary Agent for Early-Stage COVID-19: Concluding Results of a Randomized Clinical Trial
- **Authors:** Francesco Di Pierro, Amjad Khan, Somia Iqtadar, Sami Ullah Mumtaz, Muhammad Nabeel Akbar Chaudhry, Alexander Bertuccioli, Giuseppe Derosa, Pamela Maffioli, Stefano Togni, Antonella Riva, Pietro Allegrini, Martino Recchia, Nicola Zerbinati
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Pharmacology, 2023, Vol. 13, 1096853
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fphar.2022.1096853
- **Type:** Randomized, open-label, controlled clinical trial (n=100)

## Summary

This is a single-center, open-label randomized controlled trial conducted at King Edward Medical University, Lahore, Pakistan, investigating the adjuvant effect of oral quercetin (Quercetin Phytosome, 500 mg tablets containing 200 mg pure quercetin) in 100 mild-to-moderately symptomatic COVID-19 outpatients. Patients were randomized 1:1 to standard of care (SC) plus quercetin (TDS week 1, BDS week 2) or SC alone. After one week, the quercetin group showed significantly faster viral clearance (68% vs. 24% SARS-CoV-2 negative, p=0.0004), faster symptom resolution (52% vs. 24% symptom-free, p=0.0051), and significant reduction in LDH levels (from 406.56 to 257.74 U/L, p=0.0001). No adverse effects were reported. The study extends preliminary results previously reported for the first 42 patients.

## Key Findings

1. **Significantly faster viral clearance with quercetin:** By day 7, 68% of quercetin-treated patients tested negative for SARS-CoV-2 by RT-PCR vs. 24% in the control group (p=0.0004). By week 2, both groups converged near 100% negative, suggesting quercetin accelerates rather than alters ultimate clearance.

2. **Accelerated symptom resolution:** 52% of quercetin patients had all COVID-19 symptoms resolved by day 7 vs. 24% of controls (p=0.0051). A discrete-time Markov chain analysis categorized patients as "healed," "improved," "unchanged," or "worsened," with quercetin group showing significantly better transitions.

3. **Significant reduction in LDH inflammatory biomarker:** LDH levels in the quercetin group dropped from 406.56 +/- 183.92 to 257.74 +/- 110.73 U/L (p=0.0001). CRP, ferritin, and D-dimer also trended lower but did not reach statistical significance.

4. **Excellent safety and tolerability:** All 50 quercetin patients completed treatment with no reported side effects. No treatment-emergent adverse events.

5. **Brief mention of senolytic combination:** The discussion notes that quercetin combined with dasatinib has been shown to selectively eliminate SARS-CoV-2 virus-induced senescent cells, mitigate COVID-19 lung disease, and reduce inflammation in animal models (citing Lee et al., 2021, Nature). This is a discussion reference only -- not tested in this trial.

## Coverage Signal

This source is primarily relevant as a clinical trial demonstrating short-term efficacy and safety of quercetin phytosome in an acute infectious disease context (Q1). It provides modest supporting evidence for Q2 (formulation choice) and Q7 (safety). For the core brief questions about healthspan, longevity, senolytic activity, and long-term supplementation, this source has limited direct relevance. The key value is the safety data and the demonstration that a phytosome formulation at ~600 mg/day pure quercetin was well-tolerated over 2 weeks in a diverse patient population (mean age ~47, mixed comorbidities). The discussion's reference to the dasatinib+quercetin senolytic combination (Lee et al., 2021, Nature) may be useful as a pointer to that primary source.

## Quality Concerns

1. **Open-label design:** No blinding or placebo control. Patients and treating physicians knew group assignments, creating substantial potential for expectation bias in subjective symptom reporting and clinical assessments.

2. **Small sample size (n=100, 50 per arm):** While the effect sizes on viral clearance and symptom resolution are large and statistically significant, the study is underpowered for safety signals and subgroup analyses. Only 8 patients were lost to follow-up, but the total sample is modest.

3. **Significant baseline age imbalance:** The control group was significantly older than the quercetin group (54.1 vs. 41.1 years, p<0.001). Although ANCOVA found no confounding effect, this imbalance is a notable design weakness -- older age is a known risk factor for worse COVID-19 outcomes, and it may have inflated the treatment effect.

4. **Industry conflicts of interest:** Multiple authors have financial relationships with Indena SpA (developer of Quercetin Phytosome) and Pharmextracta SpA (manufacturer of Quevir tablets). FDP belongs to the Pharmextracta scientific board; ST, PA, and AR belong to the Indena scientific board; AB is a Pharmextracta advisor. The study product was donated by these companies.

5. **Single-center study in Pakistan:** External generalizability may be limited. The standard of care, patient population, and healthcare infrastructure may differ substantially from other settings.

6. **No long-term follow-up:** Outcomes were assessed at day 7 primarily, with some patients followed to day 14-21 for viral clearance. No data on long COVID, persistent symptoms, or delayed adverse effects.

7. **Inflammatory biomarkers partially negative:** Only LDH reached statistical significance among the four inflammatory biomarkers measured (CRP, ferritin, D-dimer were non-significant). The focus on LDH in the conclusions may represent selective reporting.

- Dose: 500 mg Quercetin Phytosome (200 mg pure quercetin) TDS week 1, BDS week 2
- Total daily pure quercetin: ~600 mg (week 1), ~400 mg (week 2)
- Formulation: Quercetin Phytosome with sunflower lecithin (Indena SpA)
- Viral clearance at day 7: 68% quercetin vs. 24% control (p=0.0004)
- Symptom resolution at day 7: 52% quercetin vs. 24% control (p=0.0051)
- LDH reduction: 406.56 -> 257.74 U/L in quercetin group (p=0.0001)
- CRP, ferritin, D-dimer: non-significant improvement trends
- Adverse events: 0 in quercetin group (n=50)
- One death in control group (female, 69, hypertension, T2DM, CKD)
- Study registered: NCT04861298
- Population: unvaccinated COVID-19 outpatients, mean age 47.6 years
- Quercetin mechanisms discussed: 3CL protease (Mpro) inhibition, ACE2 inhibition, NLRP3 inflammasome modulation, anti-inflammatory cytokine suppression (IL-1beta, IL-6, INF-gamma, TNF-alpha)
- Senolytic reference: dasatinib+quercetin eliminates virus-induced senescent cells in animal models (Lee et al., 2021, Nature)
- FDA GRAS status for quercetin noted
- Prior quercetin COVID-19 trials cited: Di Pierro et al. 2021a (hospitalized, reduced mortality/hospitalization), Shohan et al. 2022 (1000 mg + antivirals), Onal et al. 2021 (1000 mg + vitamin C + bromelain), Zupanets et al. 2021 (IV then oral), Rondanelli et al. 2022 (prophylaxis, 500 mg/day)
