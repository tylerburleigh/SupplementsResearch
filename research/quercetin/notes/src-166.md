## Source Identification
- **Source ID:** src-166
- **Citation:** Chaib S, Tchkonia T, Kirkland JL. "Cellular senescence and senolytics: the path to the clinic." *Nature Medicine* 28, 1556-1568 (2022). DOI: 10.1038/s41591-022-01923-y
- **Type:** Narrative review (peer-reviewed, high-impact journal)
- **Affiliation:** Mayo Clinic, Rochester, MN (Kirkland lab -- originators of D+Q senolytic concept)

## Summary

This is a comprehensive review of cellular senescence as a therapeutic target and the translation of senolytic drugs from preclinical models to clinical trials. Authored by the Mayo Clinic group that discovered the dasatinib + quercetin (D+Q) combination, it provides the most authoritative overview of the senolytic pipeline as of mid-2022. The paper covers senescence biology (SASP, SCAPs), the rationale for first-generation senolytics (dasatinib, quercetin, fisetin), extensive preclinical evidence across dozens of disease models, an exhaustive table of clinical trials (Table 1), and discussion of biomarkers, regulatory hurdles, and future directions.

## Key Findings

1. **SCAP-based drug discovery rationale:** Senescent cells upregulate senescent cell antiapoptotic pathways (SCAPs) including SRC kinases, PI3K-AKT, HSP90, and BCL-2 family members. Quercetin targets PI3K-AKT and other SCAP nodes; dasatinib targets SRC kinases. Different senescent cell types are sensitive to different agents (e.g., senescent human fat cell progenitors are sensitive to D but not Q; senescent endothelial cells are sensitive to Q but not D), providing the mechanistic basis for combination therapy.

2. **Quercetin alone has senolytic activity in specific cell types:** Senescent human umbilical vein endothelial cells are sensitive to Q (and fisetin) but not D. In mice, Q alone reduced renal senescent cell burden, alleviated fibrosis, and improved kidney function in high-fat-diet models (ref 102, Kim et al. 2019). A dedicated clinical trial of quercetin monotherapy is underway for coronary artery bypass graft surgery (Q-CABG, NCT04907253).

3. **D+Q clinical evidence (early phase):** Three published early-phase trials are discussed: (a) IPF pilot (n=14, open-label, D+Q 3 d/wk for 3 wk) showed improved physical function; (b) diabetic kidney disease pilot (n=9, open-label, 3-day course) showed decreased adipose tissue senescent cell burden, inflammation, and circulating SASP factors for at least 11 days; (c) increased urinary alpha-Klotho levels after oral D+Q in IPF patients. Multiple additional D+Q trials are listed as ongoing (Alzheimer's, frailty, skeletal health, childhood cancer survivors, hematopoietic stem cell transplant).

4. **Pharmacokinetic advantage of intermittent dosing:** Quercetin has an 11-hour half-life in humans (dasatinib 4 h, fisetin 3-4 h), supporting intermittent "hit-and-run" administration. Intermittent dosing appears as effective as continuous treatment in preclinical models, potentially reducing side effects compared to continuous therapy.

5. **Cautionary stance on self-supplementation:** The authors explicitly state: "unless and until carefully monitored, rigorous clinical trials demonstrate safety and effectiveness of senolytics or SASP inhibitors, they should not be endorsed for the prevention or treatment of diseases over-the-counter or in clinical practice."

## Coverage Signal
**Authoritative but not quercetin-specific.** This is the flagship review of the senolytic field from the Mayo Clinic group that pioneered D+Q. It provides essential context for Q3 (senolytic evidence chain) and Q4 (combination strategies) but treats quercetin as one component of the broader senolytic landscape rather than as a standalone supplement. Quercetin-specific clinical data is limited to the Q-CABG trial listing and a single preclinical kidney study.

## Quality Assessment

- **Strengths:** Peer-reviewed Nature Medicine review from the originating lab; comprehensive Table 1 of clinical trials; Table 2 of preclinical disease models; explicit discussion of mechanisms (SCAPs, SASP heterogeneity); balanced cautionary language; 223 references.
- **Limitations:** (1) Published August 2022 -- clinical trial landscape has expanded since then, and several listed trials may now have published results. (2) PDF conversion fell back to raw text extraction, so some figure content (Figs. 1-3) is partially garbled, though tables are intact. (3) As authors are the originators of D+Q, there is inherent intellectual and financial interest in the field, though disclosure is expected per Nature Medicine policies. (4) No systematic methodology described; this is a narrative review.
- **Overall quality:** High. This is a foundational reference for the senolytic evidence chain as it relates to quercetin.
