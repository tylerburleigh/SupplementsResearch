## Source Identification
- **Title:** Protective effect of quercetin on kidney diseases: From chemistry to herbal medicines
- **Authors:** Yi-Qin Chen, Hao-Yin Chen, Qin-Qi Tang, Yi-Fan Li, Xu-Sheng Liu, Fu-Hua Lu, Yue-Yu Gu
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Pharmacology, 2022 Sep 2;13:968226
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fphar.2022.968226
- **PMID:** 36120321 | **PMCID:** PMC9478191
- **Type:** Narrative review (open-access, Frontiers)

## Summary

Focused narrative review examining quercetin's renoprotective effects across kidney disease models. Covers pharmacokinetics/bioavailability of quercetin (with a detailed metabolic pathway diagram), quercetin content in traditional Chinese herbal medicines, and therapeutic effects organized by disease category: nephrotoxicity (drug-induced, chemical, heavy metal), acute kidney injury (ischemia-reperfusion, sepsis, COVID-19), chronic kidney disease/fibrosis, diabetic nephropathy, senolytic therapy (D+Q), and other renal disorders (hypertension, renal cell carcinoma, polycystic kidney disease, kidney stones). Predominantly preclinical evidence (in vitro and animal models).

## Key Findings

1. **Pharmacokinetics / Bioavailability (Q2-relevant):** Quercetin aglycone has ~2% oral bioavailability. Poor water solubility (0.17-7 ug/ml), gastric fluids (5.5 ug/ml), intestinal fluids (28.9 ug/ml). Rapid elimination with 1-2 hour half-life. Transport via SGLT1; glycosides hydrolyzed by LPH and beta-glucosidases to release aglycone. Metabolized via glucuronidation (UGT), methylation (COMT), and sulfation (SULT) in enterocytes. Main metabolites: 3-O-glucuronide and quercetin 3'-O-sulfate. ~80% of metabolites bound to plasma albumin; only ~20% free to enter tissues. Renal excretion accounts for 20-60% of total intake (monoglucuronide sulfates, methylated monoglucuronides, diglucuronides). Pluronic F127 micelle encapsulation improved water solubility, bioavailability, and kidney protective effect.

2. **Nephrotoxicity Protection (Q1, Q6-relevant):** Extensive table of 17+ toxins with animal model doses (5-200 mg/kg). Quercetin protected against cisplatin (50-100 mg/kg), methotrexate (15-50 mg/kg), cyclophosphamide (50 mg/kg), doxorubicin (10-50 mg/kg), cadmium (10-50 mg/kg), gentamicin (50 mg/kg), acetaminophen (50 mg/kg), and others. Key mechanisms: anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidative, anti-apoptotic, Nrf2/HO-1 pathway activation. Notably, 100 mg/kg/d showed no significant improvement against doxorubicin-induced renal injury (dose ceiling effect). Quercetin did not interfere with cisplatin antitumor activity and may enhance it. One human study: 500 mg quercetin reduced contrast-media-induced renal injury.

3. **Acute Kidney Injury (Q1-relevant):** Quercetin prevented glomerular loss from renal ischemia, inhibited ferroptosis in proximal TECs, protected against LPS-induced AKI via TLR4/NF-kB and Mincle/Syk/NF-kB pathways, and modulated macrophage polarization. COVID-19-associated AKI: quercetin may target SARS-CoV-2 3Clpro to inhibit viral invasion and cytokine storm.

4. **Chronic Kidney Disease / Fibrosis (Q1-relevant):** Quercetin alleviated inflammation via miR-124/NF-kB in LPS-stimulated TECs. Reduced macrophage accumulation and inhibited fibrosis. Suppressed TGF-beta signaling via Sonic Hedgehog, PTEN/TIMP3, and PI3K/Akt pathways. Downregulated EMT-related proteins to halt glomerulosclerosis.

5. **Diabetic Nephropathy (Q1, Q6-relevant):** Dose range studied: 10-100 mg/kg/d. Reduced blood glucose and triglycerides at 10 mg/kg/d. Improved lipid metabolism and albuminuria at 50-100 mg/kg/d via SCAP-SREBP2-LDLr pathway. Antioxidant effects: scavenged ROS, downregulated NLRP3 inflammasome (dihydroquercetin at 100 mg/kg/d). Promoted autophagy via AMPK/mTOR and Hippo pathways.

6. **Senolytic Therapy / D+Q (Q3-relevant):** Most relevant section for the brief. Quercetin + dasatinib (D+Q) identified as senolytic agents for kidney disease. Mechanisms: D+Q induces apoptosis of senescent TECs, restoring renal function and ameliorating fibrosis. Improved renal artery stenosis by reducing p21-positive stenotic TECs. Attenuated mesenchymal transition. In high-fat-diet mice, quercetin reduced senescence markers p16, p19, p53 and improved renal function. **Key human evidence:** Open-label Phase 1 pilot study (NCT02848131) in diabetic kidney disease patients showed D+Q eliminated senescent cells and significantly reduced senescent cell burden in adipose and skin tissues within 11 days (Hickson et al., 2019). Mechanisms: decreased p16/p21-expressing cells, downregulated SASP (IL-6, IL-1alpha, MMP-9).

7. **Combination Strategies (Q4-relevant):** D+Q is the primary combination discussed in depth. Quercetin + resveratrol for acetaminophen nephrotoxicity. Quercetin + carvedilol for renal IRI. Quercetin + anti-sense oligo gene therapy for renal cell carcinoma. Encapsulation with Pluronic F127 for improved delivery. No systematic comparison of combination vs monotherapy efficacy.

## Coverage Signal

Organ-specific (kidney) review with strong mechanistic depth in nephrology. The bioavailability/pharmacokinetics section is detailed and extractable. The senolytic/D+Q section is directly relevant to Q3 and references the key human pilot trial (Hickson et al., 2019). However, the source is narrowly focused on kidney pathology; it does not address general supplementation for health/longevity in healthy populations. Most therapeutic evidence is from animal models of disease, not prevention. The traditional Chinese medicine section catalogs quercetin content across herbs (0.29-315.8 mg/g) but is of limited relevance to the brief.

## Quality Concerns

1. **Narrow organ focus:** The review addresses kidney disease exclusively. Findings may not generalize to quercetin supplementation for healthy populations or longevity.
2. **Predominantly preclinical:** The vast majority of cited evidence is from rat/mouse models and cell lines. Human clinical data is extremely sparse.
3. **No systematic methodology:** Narrative review without PRISMA protocol, no inclusion/exclusion criteria, no risk-of-bias assessment.
4. **Dose translation gap:** Animal doses (5-200 mg/kg) are reported without human equivalent dose conversion, making clinical translation difficult.
5. **Frontiers in Pharmacology:** Open-access Frontiers journal. Adequately peer-reviewed but the specialty section (Renal Pharmacology) means narrower reviewer pool.
6. **Chinese medicine framing:** Traditional Chinese medicine perspective is prominent, which adds cultural context but may introduce confirmation bias toward quercetin's efficacy without critical evaluation.
7. **No safety data:** Despite reviewing high doses (up to 200 mg/kg), there is no discussion of toxicity thresholds, adverse effects, or drug interactions.
8. **Senolytic section is brief:** While present, the senolytic section cites only 4-5 studies. It does not discuss the broader D+Q literature outside nephrology, dosing regimens used in human trials, or limitations of the Hickson pilot study (open-label, small sample, short duration).

## Extractable Data Points for Synthesis

- Oral bioavailability of quercetin aglycone: ~2%
- Water solubility: 0.17-7 ug/ml; gastric fluid solubility: 5.5 ug/ml; intestinal fluid solubility: 28.9 ug/ml
- Elimination half-life: 1-2 hours
- Plasma protein binding: ~80% albumin-bound; ~20% free
- Urinary excretion: 20-60% of total quercetin intake
- Nephrotoxicity protection doses (animal): 5-200 mg/kg across 17+ toxin models
- Diabetic nephropathy doses (animal): 10-100 mg/kg/d
- Dose ceiling noted: 100 mg/kg/d no improvement vs doxorubicin
- Contrast nephropathy (human): 500 mg reduced renal injury
- D+Q Phase 1 trial: NCT02848131, diabetic kidney disease patients, reduced senescent cell burden in adipose/skin within 11 days (Hickson et al., 2019)
- Quercetin content in Chinese herbs: 0.29-315.8 mg/g (Houttuyniae cordata highest at 315.8 mg/g)
- Quercetin enhances cisplatin antitumor activity (does not interfere)
- Key pathways: Nrf2/HO-1, TGF-beta/Smad, TLR4/NF-kB, Mincle/Syk/NF-kB, AMPK/mTOR, Sirt1, PI3K/Akt, Sonic Hedgehog, PTEN/TIMP3
