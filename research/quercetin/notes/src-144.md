## Source
- **Title:** Quercetin as a Therapeutic Product: Evaluation of Its Pharmacological Action and Clinical Applications -- A Review
- **Authors:** Mirza MA, Mahmood S, Hilles AR, Ali A, Khan MZ, Zaidi SAA, Iqbal Z, Ge Y
- **Journal:** Pharmaceuticals (Basel). 2023 Nov 20;16(11):1631
- **DOI:** 10.3390/ph16111631
- **PMID:** 38004496 | **PMCID:** PMC10674654
- **Type:** Narrative review (pharmacological actions, clinical trials, patents, nanoformulation)

## Summary

Broad-scoped narrative review covering quercetin's chemical structure and dietary sources, pharmacological mechanisms (focused on prostate cancer -- proliferation, apoptosis, androgen receptor inhibition, angiogenesis), a patent landscape, a large table of clinical trials across multiple therapeutic areas, marketed products, and nanoformulation strategies to improve bioavailability (SLNs, NLCs, liposomes, niosomes, transferosomes, nanoemulsions, microemulsions).

## Key Findings

1. **Clinical trials are numerous but heterogeneous and often conflicting.** The review tabulates trials across CVD risk, inflammation, pulmonary/COVID-19, PCOS, diabetes, obesity, blood pressure, and NAFLD. Results are mixed: some RCTs show significant lipid/blood pressure improvements while others with similar dosing show null effects. No single therapeutic area has a consistent, replicated signal of efficacy.

2. **Dose-response signal emerges from meta-analyses.** Doses >=500 mg/day for >=8 weeks appear necessary to reduce fasting plasma glucose and insulin in metabolic syndrome (9 RCTs). Anti-inflammatory effects on CRP are significant only above 500 mg/day in patients with elevated baseline CRP. Blood pressure reductions (SBP/DBP) were significant in a meta-analysis of 17 RCTs (n=896) at 30-1000 mg/day for 2-12 weeks, with greater effect at >=8 weeks.

3. **Bioavailability is the central translational bottleneck.** Oral quercetin has poor water solubility and extensive first-pass metabolism. A prodrug study (QC12) estimated oral bioavailability at only 20-25%. The review extensively discusses nanoformulation approaches -- SLNs achieved 5.71x bioavailability increase over suspension in rats; NLCs improved water solubility >=1000-fold. These are preclinical formulations, not commercially available.

4. **Dasatinib + quercetin senolytic combination appears.** Briefly mentioned: 1000 mg quercetin + 100 mg dasatinib reduced adipose tissue senescent cell burden in diabetic kidney disease patients within 11 days, decreasing SASP markers (MMPs-9/12, IL-1alpha, IL-6). No detail on study design, n, or follow-up.

5. **Safety profile is favorable.** GRAS status from USFDA. Doses up to 2000 mg/day for 1 week were safe in COPD patients. No adverse effects on kidney or liver function reported across trials. Average dietary intake is 16-25 mg/day; supplements typically 500-1000 mg/day.

## Coverage Signal
Broad narrative review with strong breadth but limited depth on any single therapeutic area. Clinical trials are catalogued rather than critically appraised. Nanoformulation section is comprehensive but entirely preclinical. Good reference collection (120+ citations) for a scoping overview.

## Quality Concerns
- **Narrative review, not systematic.** No PRISMA flowchart, no formal risk-of-bias assessment, no GRADE evaluation of evidence certainty.
- **Clinical trial descriptions are uneven.** Some entries lack sample sizes, exact dosages, or study durations. Hard to assess internal validity.
- **Senolytic claim is underdeveloped.** The dasatinib + quercetin mention lacks study details (n, design, follow-up), reducing its evidentiary value.
- **Potential overlap with primary studies already captured in other sources.** Many cited RCTs may appear as standalone source files in the research set.
- **Focused heavily on prostate cancer mechanistic data** (in vitro/cell line work) that has limited direct relevance to the brief's healthspan/longevity focus.
- **MDPI journal.** Pharmaceuticals is a legitimate but lower-tier open-access journal. Peer review standards are adequate but not equivalent to high-impact specialty journals.
