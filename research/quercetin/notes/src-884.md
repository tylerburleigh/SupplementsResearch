## Source Identity
- **Title:** Clinical effectiveness of quercetin supplementation in the management of weight loss: a pooled analysis of randomized controlled trials
- **Authors:** Huang H, Liao D, Dong Y, Pu R
- **Journal:** Diabetes, Metabolic Syndrome and Obesity: Targets and Therapy
- **Year:** 2019
- **PMID:** 31114281 | **PMCID:** PMC6497115
- **DOI:** 10.2147/DMSO.S199830
- **Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs

## Summary
This meta-analysis pooled 9 RCTs (11 treatment arms, N=525 participants) examining quercetin supplementation for weight loss. Doses ranged from 100 to 1,000 mg/day over 2 to 12 weeks. The primary finding was that quercetin had no statistically significant effect on body weight (WMD: -0.35 kg, 95% CI: -2.03 to 1.33, P=0.68), BMI (WMD: -0.04 kg/m2, 95% CI: -0.54 to 0.45, P=0.87), waist circumference (WMD: -0.37 cm, P=0.61), or waist-to-hip ratio (WMD: -0.01, P=0.48). No dose-response or duration-response relationship was found via meta-regression. GRADE evidence quality was high for weight, BMI, and WC; moderate for WHR (only 2 studies). No significant publication bias was detected.

## Key Findings
1. **Null result for weight loss:** Quercetin supplementation did not produce clinically meaningful reductions in body weight, BMI, waist circumference, or WHR compared to placebo.
2. **No dose-response relationship:** Meta-regression showed no association between quercetin dose (100-1,000 mg/day) and changes in body weight (P=0.915) or BMI (P=0.981).
3. **No duration-response relationship:** No association between supplementation duration (2-12 weeks) and changes in body weight (P=0.711) or BMI (P=0.644).
4. **Low heterogeneity:** I-squared was 0% for weight, BMI, and WC, indicating consistent null findings across studies.
5. **Good tolerability:** No drug-related adverse reactions were reported across eligible trials.
6. **Subgroup analyses all null:** No significant effects stratified by study design, dose, intervention type, gender, baseline BMI, or duration.

## Relevance to Research Questions
- **Q1 (clinical trials):** Directly relevant. Aggregates 9 RCTs with diverse populations (overweight/obese, hypertensive, PCOS, healthy smokers). Doses 100-1,000 mg/day, durations 2-12 weeks. All trials were randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled.
- **Q2 (bioavailability):** Minor indirect relevance. Notes that 5 of 9 trials used quercetin-rich onion peel extract rather than pure quercetin, which may affect bioavailability, but does not directly assess pharmacokinetics.
- **Q3 (senolytic):** Not addressed. The paper focuses exclusively on anthropometric weight-loss outcomes.
- **Q4 (combinations):** Not addressed. All included trials used quercetin as a stand-alone intervention.
- **Q5 (against supplementation):** Highly relevant. Provides strong null-evidence argument that quercetin is not effective for weight management, a frequently claimed benefit. Authors conclude quercetin supplementation "is not recommended as a potential therapeutic strategy in weight loss."
- **Q6 (dose-response):** Directly relevant. Meta-regression found no dose-response (100-1,000 mg/day) or duration-response (2-12 weeks) for weight or BMI outcomes.
- **Q7 (risk-benefit):** Partially relevant. Reports no adverse reactions across trials, supporting a favorable safety profile, but the null efficacy findings weaken the risk-benefit case for this indication.

## Coverage Signal
- **Study designs:** 6 parallel, 3 crossover RCTs; all double-blind, placebo-controlled
- **Populations:** Overweight/obese subjects, healthy smokers, hypertensive subjects, PCOS patients, healthy normal-weight women, men with APOE variants
- **Geography:** Korea (6 trials), USA (1), Iran (2), Germany (1)
- **Dose range:** 100-1,000 mg/day
- **Duration range:** 2-12 weeks
- **Sample sizes per trial:** 12-92 participants (total N=525)
- **Outcomes measured:** Body weight, BMI, waist circumference, waist-to-hip ratio

## Quality Concerns
1. **Small sample sizes:** Individual trials ranged from 12 to 92 participants; total pooled N=525 is modest.
2. **Short intervention durations:** Maximum 12 weeks; long-term effects unknown. Authors acknowledge this as a key limitation.
3. **Weight not primary endpoint in most studies:** Most included RCTs did not have weight loss as the primary outcome, raising risk of selective reporting of secondary null results.
4. **Limited WHR data:** Only 2 studies reported waist-to-hip ratio, making that estimate unreliable.
5. **Unadjusted confounders:** Most studies did not adjust for dietary intake, physical activity, or other weight-influencing factors.
6. **Predominantly Korean studies:** 6 of 9 trials from Korea, with 5 using onion peel extract, limiting generalizability.
7. **Risk of bias:** Only 3 of 9 studies had adequate random sequence generation; allocation concealment was unclear in 6 of 9 studies.
8. **Publication year range:** Literature search conducted through August 2018 only; newer RCTs are not captured.
