# src-994 — Reader Note

## Citation

Brüll V, Burak C, Stoffel-Wagner B, Wolffram S, Nickenig G, Müller C, Langguth P, Alteheld B, Fimmers R, Naaf S, Zimmermann BF, Stehle P, Egert S. Effects of a quercetin-rich onion skin extract on 24 h ambulatory blood pressure and endothelial function in overweight-to-obese patients with (pre-)hypertension: a randomised double-blinded placebo-controlled cross-over trial. *British Journal of Nutrition* (2015), 114, 1263-1277. doi:10.1017/S0007114515002950

## Study Design

- **Type:** Randomised, double-blinded, placebo-controlled cross-over trial
- **N:** 68 completers (34 M / 34 F; 2 dropped from 70 enrolled)
- **Population:** Overweight-to-obese (BMI 25-35), age 25-65, with pre-hypertension or stage I hypertension and metabolic syndrome traits (central obesity, dyslipidaemia, or elevated hs-CRP)
- **Intervention:** 162 mg/d quercetin from onion skin extract (3 x 54 mg capsules with meals) vs placebo (mannitol), 6-week treatment periods with 6-week washout
- **Primary outcome:** 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure (ABP); also office BP, endothelial function (EndoPAT/RHI), vasoactive biomarkers, lipids, glucose metabolism
- **Registration:** DRKS00000555

## Key Findings

1. **Total group (n=68):** No significant effect of quercetin on 24 h ABP parameters, office BP, or any secondary endpoints.
2. **Hypertensive subgroup:** Quercetin significantly reduced 24 h systolic ABP by -3.6 mmHg (P=0.022 intra-group; treatment difference vs placebo -3.9 mmHg, P=0.049). Significant reductions in daytime SBP (-4.6 mmHg, P=0.014) and nighttime SBP (-6.6 mmHg, P=0.007) from baseline, though inter-group comparisons for these did not reach significance.
3. **Pre-hypertensive subgroup:** No significant BP effects, suggesting a threshold of elevated BP may be required for quercetin's antihypertensive action.
4. **Mechanistic markers:** No changes in endothelin-1, sE-selectin, sVCAM-1, ADMA, ACE activity, RHI, augmentation index, oxLDL, urinary isoprostanes, hs-CRP, lipids, glucose, insulin, or HbA1c. Mechanism of BP reduction remains unexplained.
5. **Compliance:** 98% by capsule count; confirmed by plasma quercetin increase of ~1150% (P<0.0001). No side effects reported.
6. **Plasma quercetin:** Large inter-individual variation (baseline range 0.2-330.5 nmol/L; post-supplementation 99.1-1313.1 nmol/L). BP changes did not correlate with plasma quercetin or metabolite concentrations.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (clinical trials):** High relevance. Well-designed RCT with gold-standard 24 h ABP monitoring. Demonstrates modest but statistically significant antihypertensive effect in hypertensive (not pre-hypertensive) individuals at a moderate supra-nutritional dose (162 mg/d). Adds to the body of evidence from Edwards et al. (730 mg/d), Egert et al. (150 mg/d), and Zahedi et al. (500 mg/d).
- **Q2 (bioavailability):** Moderate relevance. Onion skin extract quercetin aglycone showed good absorption (plasma quercetin increased ~12-fold). Reports that onion skin extract has higher bioavailability than quercetin dihydrate (unpublished data cited). Highlights large inter-individual variability in plasma levels. No dose-response or pharmacokinetic profiling.
- **Q3 (senolytic):** Not addressed. No discussion of senescent cell clearance, dasatinib combinations, or aging-related endpoints.
- **Q4 (combinations):** Not addressed. Quercetin was administered alone.
- **Q5 (against supplementation):** Some relevance. The null result in the total study population (pre-hypertensives included) and inability to identify a mechanism temper enthusiasm. High inter-individual variability in response (~50% of participants showed BP reduction) suggests unpredictable benefit. The authors note that the conclusion applies strictly to onion skin extract, not pure quercetin.
- **Q6 (dose-response):** Limited relevance. Single dose tested (162 mg/d). No dose-response analysis. Notes that similar SBP reductions have been seen across a wide dose range (150-730 mg/d), suggesting a possible ceiling effect.
- **Q7 (risk-benefit):** Moderate relevance. No adverse effects reported. The 3.6 mmHg SBP reduction in hypertensives is comparable to lifestyle modifications (sodium reduction, weight loss) and would be clinically meaningful at population level. However, effect confined to hypertensive subgroup; no metabolic benefits observed.

## Methodological Notes

- Strengths: Cross-over design (each participant serves as own control), gold-standard 24 h ABP, high compliance, broad biomarker panel, no carry-over effects detected.
- Limitations: Onion skin extract rather than pure quercetin (other phytochemicals may contribute); exploratory subgroup analysis for hypertensives (not pre-specified as primary); multiple testing without correction; 6-week duration may be insufficient for some endpoints.
- Sample size calculation was based on office BP (not ABP), powered for 90% at P<0.01 to detect 3 mmHg SBP difference.

## Quality Assessment

- **Risk of bias:** Low. Double-blinded, placebo-controlled, cross-over, allocation concealed, compliance objectively verified.
- **Generalisability:** Limited to overweight-to-obese individuals with pre-hypertension/stage I hypertension in a German population. Excluded smokers, diabetics, and those with history of cardiovascular events.
- **Funding:** German Research Foundation (DFG), no industry involvement. Authors declared no conflicts of interest.
