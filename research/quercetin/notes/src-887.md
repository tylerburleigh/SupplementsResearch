## Source Identification

- **Title:** The effect of quercetin supplementation on clinical outcomes in COVID-19 patients: A systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Authors:** Ziaei S, Alimohammadi-Kamalabadi M, Hasani M, Malekahmadi M, Persad E, Heshmati J
- **Journal:** Food Science & Nutrition, 11(12):7504-7514
- **Year:** 2023 (published Sep 26, 2023; accepted Sep 13, 2023)
- **DOI:** 10.1002/fsn3.3715
- **PMID:** 38107099; PMCID: PMC10724618
- **Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs
- **Registration:** PROSPERO CRD42023407390

## Scope and Content

This is a systematic review and meta-analysis examining the effect of quercetin supplementation on inflammatory biomarkers (CRP, LDH, ferritin, D-dimer), hospital admission, ICU admission, and mortality in COVID-19 patients. Five RCTs totaling 544 patients were included. The search covered PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, EMBASE, and Cochrane Library through August 25, 2023.

## Key Findings

### 1. Primary Meta-Analysis Results
- **LDH:** Significant reduction with quercetin (SMD: -0.42, 95% CI: -0.82 to -0.02, I-squared=48.86%)
- **Hospital admission:** 70% risk reduction (RR: 0.30, 95% CI: 0.14-0.62, I-squared=0%)
- **ICU admission:** 73% risk reduction (RR: 0.27, 95% CI: 0.09-0.78, I-squared=20.66%)
- **Mortality:** 82% risk reduction (RR: 0.18, 95% CI: 0.03-0.98, I-squared=0%)
- **CRP:** No significant change (SMD: -0.04, 95% CI: -0.36 to 0.27, I-squared=71.14%)
- **D-dimer:** No significant change (SMD: 0.02, 95% CI: -0.27 to 0.30, I-squared=63.85%)
- **Ferritin:** No significant change (SMD: -0.17, 95% CI: -0.53 to 0.18, I-squared=74.37%)

### 2. Included Studies
Five double-blind RCTs conducted in Pakistan (3), Iran (1), and Ukraine (1), published 2021-2023:
- Di Pierro, Iqtadar et al. 2021: 42 participants, 600 mg/day, 2 weeks (Pakistan)
- Di Pierro, Derosa et al. 2021: 152 participants, 1000 mg/day, 4 weeks (Pakistan)
- Di Pierro et al. 2023: 100 participants, 1500 mg/day, 2 weeks (Pakistan)
- Shohan et al. 2022: 60 participants, 1000 mg/day, 1 week (Iran)
- Zupanets et al. 2021: 200 participants, 480 mg/day, 2 weeks (Ukraine)

### 3. Dose Range
Quercetin doses ranged from 480 to 1500 mg/day, with supplementation durations of 1-4 weeks. Subgroup analysis by dose did not show significant changes in pooled effect sizes.

### 4. Mechanisms Discussed
- Inhibition of hACE-2 gene expression, reducing viral cell entry
- Suppression of 3CLpro and RdRp enzymes, inhibiting viral replication
- Anti-inflammatory via NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition, NF-kB pathway modulation
- Antioxidant: induces SOD, catalase, GPx expression
- Cholesterol metabolism modulation via ABCA1 regulation
- Synergistic effects noted with fenofibrate and dexamethasone (in vitro/computational)

## Coverage Signal

**Narrow but clinically strong** -- focused exclusively on quercetin supplementation in COVID-19 patients. Provides the best available meta-analytic evidence for quercetin's clinical efficacy in acute viral illness, with statistically significant reductions in hard endpoints (hospitalization, ICU, mortality). However, the evidence base is small (5 RCTs, 544 patients), GRADE certainty is low, and generalizability to healthy supplementation or longevity contexts is limited. Three of five trials are from the same research group (Di Pierro et al.).

## Quality Concerns

1. **Small evidence base:** Only 5 RCTs with 544 total patients. Individual study sizes ranged from 42-200, limiting statistical power and generalizability.
2. **Low GRADE certainty:** Authors explicitly state low certainty of evidence for LDH, hospitalization, ICU admission, and mortality; very low certainty for CRP, D-dimer, and ferritin.
3. **Single research group dominance:** Three of five included studies are by Di Pierro et al. (all in Pakistan), raising concerns about replication independence and geographic bias.
4. **Disease-specific context:** All findings are in COVID-19 patients; extrapolation to general health, longevity, or healthy populations is uncertain.
5. **Formulation heterogeneity:** Different quercetin formulations across trials were noted as a source of high heterogeneity, particularly for continuous outcomes (CRP I-squared=71%, ferritin I-squared=74%).
6. **No safety analysis:** The review does not systematically evaluate adverse events or safety endpoints, only efficacy outcomes.
7. **Abbreviated abstract claim:** The abstract states "decreased mortality by 82%" but the text also notes "decreased mortality by 18%" -- the 82% refers to relative risk reduction (RR 0.18), while the 18% figure appears to be a different framing. This inconsistency could be confusing.
