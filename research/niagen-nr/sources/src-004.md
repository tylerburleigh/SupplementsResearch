What is Nicotinamide Riboside?

Nicotinamide riboside is a newly appreciated form of Vitamin B3 with unique properties; it is a formal precursor of NAD+.

Forms of Nicotinamide Riboside NR

There are two forms of NR available: one is Nicotinamide Riboside powder, and the other is Nicotinamide Riboside chloride.

Chemically speaking, they are two completely different compounds since they have different CAS numbers, NR with 1341-23-7 and NR chloride with 23111-00-4. NR is not stable at room temperature, while NR chloride is stable. Nicotinamide riboside food sources

The amount of NR in foods is minute compared with that in nutritional supplements. However, what are the primary food sources that contain nicotinamide riboside (NR)?

Cow milk

Cow milk usually has ∼12 μmol NAD(+) precursor vitamins/L, of which 60% was present as nicotinamide and 40% was current as NR. (Conventional milk contained more NR than organic milk), a study published by the University of Iowa, in 2016

Yeast

An old study has stated, “One inhibitory substance was isolated from yeast and was found to be nicotinamide riboside, it may have from NAD (P) during the preparation of yeast extracts, or from dietary yeast supplements during digestion in vivo.” While there is no quantitative data on yeast

Beer

The beer has a fair amount of carbs and drinking those in a liquid form like that will have a glycemic impact; various research papers have mentioned beer as being a food source of nicotinamide riboside.

Also, there may be trace amounts of NR like whey protein, mushrooms, etc.

For many people, dairy is off limits for other reasons. The food sources of nicotinamide riboside might be useful, but are less efficient than an NR supplement.

Why is Nicotinamide Riboside (NR) designated as an authentic NAD+ precursor vitamin invertebrates?

There are five lines of reasoning to support it:

Haemophilus influenza, a flu-causing bacterium, which has no de novo pathway and cannot utilize Na or Nam, is strictly dependent on NR, NMN, or NAD+ for growth in the host bloodstream.

Milk is a source of NR.

NR protects murine DRG neurons in an ex vivo axonopathy assay via transcriptional induction of the nicotinamide riboside kinase (NRK) 2 gene.

Exogenously added NR and derivatives increase NAD+ accumulation in a dose-dependent fashion in human cell lines.

Candida glabrata, an opportunistic fungus that depends on NAD+ precursor vitamins for growth, utilizes NR during disseminated infection.
