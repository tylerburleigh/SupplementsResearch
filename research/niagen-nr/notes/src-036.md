# Source src-036: CoNR Trial - NR and CoQ10 in CKD (Secondary Outcomes)

**Citation:** Ahmadi et al. (2024). medRxiv preprint. doi:10.1101/2024.08.23.24312501
**Status:** Preprint (not peer-reviewed), CC-BY 4.0
**Quality:** ok (68,096 chars, 686 sentences, substantive full text)
**Prior publication:** Primary outcomes published in JCI Insight 2023 (ref 14 in paper)

---

## Core Findings

Six weeks of NR supplementation (1000 mg/day Niagen) improved monocyte mitochondrial bioenergetics (increased BHI and spare respiratory capacity) and reduced oxidative stress markers (5-series F2-isoprostanes) in moderate-to-severe CKD patients, but did not improve the trial's primary outcomes of physical endurance. CoQ10 (1200 mg/day) reduced inflammatory markers (CRP, IL-13) and oxidative stress but had no effect on cellular bioenergetics. Both supplements induced distinct transcriptomic changes related to metabolism and immune function.

---

## 1. Study Design (Crossover RCT Details)

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial (CoNR Trial, NCT03579693)
- **Population:** 25 adults with moderate-to-severe CKD (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73m2)
- **Demographics:** Mean age 61 +/- 11.6 years; 60% male; 80% White; BMI 27.7 +/- 5.2 kg/m2; 16% diabetes; mean eGFR 36.9 +/- 9.2 mL/min/1.73m2
- **Interventions:** Three treatment arms in random order, each 6 weeks:
  - NR (Niagen): 1000 mg/day
  - CoQ10 (Tishcon Corp): 1200 mg/day
  - Placebo (identical-looking tablets)
- **Washout:** 1 week between treatment periods
- **Duration:** November 2018 to April 2021
- **Blinding:** Participants, study physicians, assessors, and study staff blinded
- **6 treatment sequences** with 3 randomly ordered treatment periods each

## 2. Primary and Secondary Outcomes

**Primary outcomes** (previously reported, JCI Insight 2023):
- VO2 peak (maximal aerobic capacity) -- NOT improved by NR or CoQ10
- Total work efficiency (graded cycle ergometry) -- NOT improved by NR or CoQ10

**Secondary outcomes** (this paper):
- Plasma inflammatory biomarkers: IL-6, CRP, IL-2, IL-4, IL-8, IL-10, IL-12, IL-13, TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma
- Oxidative stress biomarkers: F2-isoprostanes (5-series and 15-series)
- Lymphocyte and monocyte bioenergetics (subset, n=14): spare respiratory capacity, BHI, basal/maximal respiration, proton leak, ATP-linked respiration

**Exploratory outcomes:**
- Whole blood transcriptomics via 3'-Tag-Seq gene expression profiling

## 3. Mitochondrial Bioenergetics Findings

- **NR improved monocyte bioenergetics** (n=14 subset):
  - Spare respiratory capacity: +3.52 pmol/min/10,000 cells (95% CI 0.04 to 7.0, P=0.04)
  - Bioenergetic Health Index (BHI): +0.29 (95% CI 0.06 to 0.53, P=0.02); **survived BH correction** (q<0.05)
  - Maximal respiration: borderline +4.32 pmol/min/10,000 cells (P=0.05)
  - Basal respiration: trend +0.79 (P=0.08)
- **NR did NOT improve T-cell bioenergetics**
- **CoQ10 had no meaningful effect** on monocyte or T-cell bioenergetics
- PBMC bioenergetics are noted to correlate with skeletal muscle and cardiac bioenergetics

## 4. Metabolic Health Outcomes

**Inflammatory biomarkers:**
- NR increased IL-2 (+0.32 pg/mL, P<0.01) -- a proinflammatory cytokine that can also promote Treg expansion
- NR modestly decreased IL-13 (P=0.05)
- CoQ10 decreased IL-13 (-0.12 pg/mL, P=0.02) and CRP (-0.27 mg/dL, P=0.04; marginal after BH correction q=0.08)
- No significant changes in IL-6, IL-10, IL-12, IL-4, IL-8, TNF-alpha, or IFN-gamma for either supplement

**Oxidative stress:**
- Both NR and CoQ10 reduced 5-series F2-isoprostanes (-0.16 and -0.11 pg/mL, respectively; q<0.05 after BH correction)
- NR reduced total F2-isoprostanes (P=0.05)
- Neither supplement affected 15-series F2-isoprostanes

**Transcriptomics:**
- NR altered 503/13,101 genes (291 up, 212 down); enriched in carbohydrate metabolism, lipid metabolism, immune signaling, and mitochondrial function GO terms (including "regulation of mitochondrial membrane potential" and "proton-transporting ATPase activity")
- CoQ10 altered 389/13,101 genes (187 up, 203 down); enriched in immune/stress response, NF-kB signaling, MAPK cascade, and glycerophospholipid metabolism

## 5. Safety Data

- The paper states "Authors have no conflict of interest to disclose"
- ChromaDex provided Niagen as manufacturer/supplier
- No specific adverse event data reported in this manuscript (safety addressed in prior JCI Insight 2023 publication, ref 14)
- No retraction noted

## 6. Key Findings and Limitations

**Key findings:**
1. NR (but not CoQ10) improved monocyte mitochondrial bioenergetics (BHI and spare respiratory capacity)
2. Both NR and CoQ10 reduced oxidative stress (5-series F2-isoprostanes)
3. CoQ10 (but not NR) reduced systemic inflammation markers (CRP, IL-13)
4. NR increased IL-2, contrasting with other studies showing NR reduces inflammatory markers
5. Transcriptomic analysis reveals distinct mechanistic pathways for each supplement

**Limitations:**
1. Small sample size (n=25 total; n=14 for bioenergetics) -- powered for primary outcome (VO2 peak), not secondary outcomes
2. Short 6-week supplementation period; long-term effects unknown
3. Bioenergetics subset (n=14) may not be representative of full cohort
4. Exploratory transcriptomics without multiple comparison correction for DGE analysis
5. Preprint (not yet peer-reviewed)
6. Predominantly White (80%), limiting generalizability
7. NR-induced IL-2 increase is unexpected and its significance unclear
8. Primary outcomes (exercise capacity) were negative; all reported findings are secondary/exploratory

## Claims to Verify

- CRP reduction with CoQ10: abstract reports -0.11 mg/dL but text reports -0.27 mg/dL (95% CI -0.49 to -0.05). The Supplemental Table 2 shows effect size -0.45. The abstract number may be a log-scale value while the text reports the raw scale.
- BHI improvement with NR: reported as estimated difference 0.29 (95% CI 0.06 to 0.53, P=0.02). Only BHI survived BH correction. The spare respiratory capacity (P=0.04) did not survive correction.
- Sample size consistency: 25 participants for most outcomes, 14 for bioenergetics. Supplemental Table 1 confirms the n=14 subset had similar demographics to the full cohort.
