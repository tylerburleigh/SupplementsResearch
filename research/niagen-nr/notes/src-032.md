# src-032: NICE Randomized Clinical Trial — Nicotinamide Riboside for Peripheral Artery Disease

**Citation:** McDermott MM et al. Nature Communications (2024) 15:5046. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-49092-5
**Venue:** Nature Communications | **Year:** 2024 | **Citations:** 23 | **Quality:** ok

---

## Core Findings

In this Phase II double-blind RCT of 90 people with peripheral artery disease (PAD), 6 months of oral nicotinamide riboside (NR) 1000 mg/day significantly improved 6-minute walk distance by 17.6 meters vs placebo (one-sided P=0.08, meeting the pre-specified threshold of P<0.10). This is the first RCT to demonstrate beneficial effects of NR on walking performance in any human population. Resveratrol (125 mg/day) added no benefit beyond NR alone, likely due to poorer adherence driven by GI side effects.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Parallel-group, double-blind, randomized clinical trial (Phase II)
- **Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03743636
- **Setting:** Northwestern University Feinberg School of Medicine, Chicago, IL
- **Enrollment:** 12/13/2018 to 9/14/2022; final follow-up 4/3/2023
- **Sample size:** 90 randomized (target 30 per group), 89 (98.9%) completed 6-month follow-up
- **Arms:**
  1. NR 1000 mg/day (2x250 mg pills twice daily) — N=28
  2. NR 1000 mg/day + resveratrol 125 mg/day — N=33
  3. Placebo — N=29
- **Duration:** 6 months intervention with assessments at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months
- **Run-in:** 14-day placebo run-in to screen for non-adherence; 70% pill consumption required
- **Randomization:** Computer-generated, permuted blocks of 4 and 6, equal allocation
- **Manufacturers:** NR by ChromaDex; resveratrol by ReserveAge

## Population

- Mean age 71.2 years (SD 9.1)
- 47.8% Black, 46.7% female
- PAD defined by ABI <=0.90 or documented vascular evidence
- Mean ABI: 0.67-0.72 across groups
- Excluded: critical limb ischemia, amputation, end-stage kidney disease, significant liver impairment, wheelchair-dependent, recent revascularization, current NR/niacin/resveratrol users

## Primary Outcome

**6-month change in 6-minute walk distance (6MWD):**

| Comparison | Between-group diff (meters) | 90% CI | One-sided P |
|---|---|---|---|
| NR vs placebo | +17.6 | +1.8, +inf | 0.08 |
| NR+Resveratrol vs placebo | +3.7 | -11.2, +inf | 0.38 |

- NR alone: within-group change +7.0 m (SE 8.7)
- NR+Resveratrol: within-group change -6.9 m (SE 7.8)
- Placebo: within-group change -10.6 m (SE 8.5)
- The MCID for 6MWD in PAD is ~8 meters (small) and ~20 meters (large)
- The 17.6-meter difference is close to the large MCID threshold

## Secondary Outcomes

**3-month 6MWD:**
- NR alone: +22.4 m vs placebo (P=0.029) — significant
- NR+Resveratrol: +20.6 m vs placebo (P=0.034) — significant
- Both groups showed earlier benefit at 3 months

**Max treadmill walking time (6-month):**
- NR alone: +2.1 min vs placebo (P=0.08) — significant at pre-specified threshold
- NR+Resveratrol: +1.7 min vs placebo (P=0.12) — not significant
- Only N=26 had baseline + follow-up data (COVID-19 impact)
- Large MCID for treadmill time: 2.5-4.0 min

**Other secondary outcomes (6-month):**
- WIQ distance score: No significant improvement for either NR group vs placebo
- Physical activity (ActiGraph counts/day and counts/min): No significant improvement
- Gastrocnemius muscle NAD+ abundance (combined NR groups vs placebo): No significant difference (P=0.84), only N=17 with paired biopsies
- Satellite cell abundance (combined NR vs placebo): +11.14 cells/100 fibers (P=0.06) — significant at pre-specified threshold
- Percent Type I myofibers: No significant difference

## Post-Hoc Analyses (>=75% Adherence)

Among participants taking >=75% of study pills:
- NR alone: +31.0 m vs placebo (P=0.014)
- NR+Resveratrol: +26.9 m vs placebo (P=0.028)
- Authors note these effects are comparable to supervised exercise for PAD
- Adherence rates: NR alone 75%, NR+Resveratrol 52%, Placebo 76%

## Safety Data

**Adverse events:**
- Diarrhea: NR+Resv 54.6%, NR alone 39.3%, Placebo 27.6%
- Nausea/emesis: NR+Resv 36.4%, NR alone 14.3%, Placebo 24.1%
- One serious AE in placebo group (chest pain during 6-min walk → CABG)
- No deaths, no withdrawals due to study drug AEs
- No SAEs attributed to NR

## Effect Sizes and Clinical Significance

- Primary ITT effect: +17.6 m (close to large MCID of 20 m for PAD)
- Per-protocol effect: +31.0 m (well above large MCID)
- Treadmill effect: +2.1 min (approaching MCID of 2.5-4.0 min)
- Satellite cell increase suggests potential muscle regenerative mechanism
- The effect is clinically meaningful by PAD-specific MCID standards, and comparable to supervised exercise programs
- Notable that placebo group declined by 10.6 m over 6 months (natural disease progression)

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size (N=90)** — Phase II trial; results need confirmation in larger study
2. **Very small biopsy subsample (N=17)** — limits statistical power for mechanistic conclusions
3. **COVID-19 disrupted treadmill testing (N=26 paired)** — key secondary outcome underpowered
4. **One-sided P<0.10 threshold** — more liberal than conventional two-sided P<0.05; increases false positive risk
5. **No adjustment for multiple comparisons** — 2 primary comparisons and multiple secondary/exploratory analyses
6. **Run-in selection bias** — 14-day placebo run-in excluded non-adherent participants; may inflate adherence and effect sizes in real-world settings
7. **Short duration (6 months)** — unknown whether benefits persist
8. **Single-center study** — all participants from Chicago area
9. **Post-hoc adherence analyses** — per-protocol results are hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory
10. **NR+Resveratrol arm had only 52% adherence** — confounds comparison between NR alone and NR+Resveratrol

## Context from Prior NR Trials

- 40 sedentary obese men: NR 1000 mg BID showed no effect on NAD+ metabolites, insulin sensitivity, or mitochondrial activity
- 30 heart failure patients (EF<40%): NR 2000 mg/day increased blood NAD+ but had no effect on 6-min walk
- NICE trial is the first to show walking improvement with NR, potentially because PAD has unique pathophysiology (ischemia, elevated ROS) that NR specifically addresses

## Relevance to Research Question

This is a directly relevant clinical trial of NR (Niagen's active ingredient) in a specific disease population. It provides the strongest clinical evidence to date that NR supplementation can improve functional walking performance in humans. The effect sizes are clinically meaningful by PAD standards. However, the liberal statistical threshold and small sample size mean confirmation is needed before drawing firm conclusions. The study does not address NR effects in healthy populations or general aging.
