# src-050: NAD+ Enhancers as Therapeutic Agents in the Cardiorenal Axis

**Citation:** Marin-Blazquez et al., *Cell Communication and Signaling* (2024) 22:537. DOI: 10.1186/s12964-024-01903-4
**Type:** Comprehensive review article
**Quality:** ok (full text, ~185K chars, 1557 sentences)

---

## Core Findings

This review provides the most updated survey (as of 2024) of NAD+ metabolism modulation as a therapeutic strategy for cardiorenal syndromes (CRS). It covers NAD+ precursors (NA, NAM, NR, NMN, next-generation NRH/NMNH/trigonelline), PARP inhibitors, sirtuin activators, CD38 inhibitors, NAMPT activators, and combination strategies. While preclinical evidence for NAD+ enhancement in cardiorenal disease is extensive and compelling, clinical translation remains limited: most human trials are short-duration (median 8 weeks for NR), and classical precursors produce only ~2-fold NAD+ increases at best. The authors highlight next-generation precursors (NRH, NMNH) and combination therapies as promising future directions.

## 2. NR-Specific Evidence for Cardiovascular and Kidney Outcomes

### Cardiovascular (preclinical)
- **DOX-induced cardiomyopathy** (C57BL/6 mice): Single dose 100/300/500 mg/kg NR i.p. reduced cardiac injury, necrosis, and myocardial dysfunction via SIRT1 activation and reduced ROS formation. [Ref 203]
- **Genetic cardiomyopathy** (Lmna H222P/H222P mice): 400 mg/kg NR in chow for 9 weeks rescued cardiac NAD+ levels, improved survival and cardiac function. [Ref 204]
- **HFpEF** (HFD + L-NAME C57BL/6J mice): 400 mg/kg/day NR in chow for 8 weeks rescued NAD+ levels, improved cardiac function, reversed HFpEF phenotype. [Ref 206]
- **TAC-induced cardiac hypertrophy** (C57BL/6J mice): 400 mg/kg/day NR by OG for 8 weeks activated SIRT3, reduced TNF-alpha/IL-1beta, inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome, reduced oxidative stress. [Ref 208]
- **Dilated cardiomyopathy** (SRFHKO mice): 450 mg/kg/day NR in chow for 45 days attenuated heart failure by stabilizing myocardial NAD+ levels. [Ref 130]

### Kidney (preclinical)
- **AKI by bilateral IRI** (Wistar rats): 500 mg/kg/day NR by OG for 2 weeks rescued NAD+ levels, enhanced SIRT1 activity and autophagy, but **failed to ameliorate renal tubular damage and profibrotic gene expression** (TGF-beta1, inhibin subunit beta-A, Periostin). [Ref 143] -- MIXED RESULTS
- **Diabetic nephropathy** (db/db C57BL/6 mice): 500 mg/kg/day NR in chow for 20 weeks reduced albuminuria, Kim-1 expression, inflammation genes (IL-6, MCP-1, TNF-alpha), and augmented mitochondrial biogenesis. [Ref 209]

## 3. Comparison with Other NAD+ Enhancers

### Nicotinic Acid (NA)
- Strongest clinical evidence base (50+ years of use as antidyslipidemic drug).
- Preclinical: Benefits in atherosclerosis, vascular inflammation, CKD models.
- Clinical: Improves lipid profiles (increases HDL, decreases LDL/triglycerides). The Coronary Drug Project showed 6.2% absolute mortality reduction at 15-year follow-up. HDL-Atherosclerosis Treatment Study showed NA + simvastatin reduced coronary stenosis and cardiovascular events.
- **Limitation:** Beneficial cardiorenal effects may be partly due to GPR109A receptor activation (antilipolytic effect), not purely NAD+ boosting.

### Nicotinamide (NAM)
- Does not cause skin flushing (unlike NA).
- Preclinical: Anti-inflammatory effects in atherosclerosis, cardiotoxicity, cardiac arrest, hypertension, AKI, renal fibrosis models.
- Clinical: Safety confirmed in polycystic kidney disease (but no efficacy); reduced phosphorus and increased HDL in dialysis patients.

### Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN)
- Extensive preclinical cardiovascular evidence: heart failure, DOX-cardiotoxicity, cardiac fibrosis, IRI, Friedreich's ataxia.
- Limited preclinical renal evidence: IRI-AKI, cisplatin-AKI, adriamycin-AKI.
- Only 2 ongoing clinical trials (hypertension, diabetic kidney disease).

### Next-generation precursors (NRH, NMNH, Trigonelline)
- **NMNH:** Increased NAD+ in mouse tissues (heart, kidney) to higher extent than NMN. Protected kidney cells against hypoxia-reoxygenation injury.
- **NRH:** Increased NAD+ in renal cells, protected against cisplatin-AKI (reduced BUN, fibrosis markers).
- **Trigonelline:** Newly described NAD+ enhancer; increased NAD+ in kidney cells in vitro but in vivo cardiorenal effects unknown.

### PARP Inhibitors
- Preclinical: INO-1001 and 3-AB show cardioprotection in IRI and heart failure models; renoprotection in IRI-AKI and transplant rejection models.
- Clinical: Only one trial (INO-1001 in STEMI) showed non-significant CRP and IL-6 reduction.

### Sirtuin Activators
- Resveratrol: Most studied. Cardioprotective in IRI, cardiomyopathy, hypertension models. Renoprotective in CKD, sepsis-AKI.
- Clinical: Resveratrol + grape extract reduced inflammation markers and improved lipid profiles in high-risk CVD patients.

### CD38 Inhibitors / NAMPT Activators
- Limited preclinical evidence; 78c and other CD38 inhibitors show cardioprotection in isolated heart models.

## 5. Safety Considerations

### NR Safety
- Generally safe with mild side effects (nausea, leg cramps) in healthy adults.
- Does NOT trigger SARM1 activation (advantage over NMN).
- **Concern:** Reduced stability in blood; degradation leads to NAM formation which could cause sirtuin end-product inhibition.
- **Long-term concern:** NR unavoidably produces NMN during salvage pathway metabolism, which could theoretically trigger SARM1 activation long-term.
- Classical precursors produce only ~2-fold NAD+ increases at best.

### NA Safety
- Skin flushing at doses >30 mg (via GPR109A activation).
- Separate from NAD+ boosting mechanism.

### NAM Safety
- May cause sirtuin end-product inhibition in vitro.
- Can deplete methyl donors (irreversibly methylated to MeNAM by NNMT).

### NMN Safety
- Known to allosterically activate SARM1, potentially triggering axonal degeneration.

## 6. Mechanism of Action in Cardiorenal Context

### NAD+ Depletion in Cardiorenal Disease
- Heart: NAD+ depleted in myocardial infarction, DCM, TAC, heart failure. Mechanisms include impaired salvage pathway (decreased NAMPT expression), defective de novo synthesis (KYNU/HAAO mutations), and hyperactivation of consumers (CD38, PARPs).
- Kidney: NAD+ depleted in AKI and CKD. Mechanisms include accelerated PARP consumption, defective de novo synthesis (reduced QPRT), and increased NNMT-mediated NAD+ excretion.

### NAD+ Repletion Mechanisms
- **NR path:** NR is phosphorylated to NMN by NRKs, then adenylated to NAD+ by NMNATs (salvage pathway).
- **Downstream effects:** Increased NAD+ activates sirtuins (SIRT1, SIRT3), improving mitochondrial function, reducing oxidative stress, inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome, and modulating inflammation (reduced TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IL-6).
- **NR-specific cardiovascular mechanisms:** SIRT3/MnSOD axis activation (antioxidant), NLRP3 inhibition (anti-inflammatory), Smad3 deacetylation (anti-fibrotic, via NMN), stabilization of myocardial NAD+ levels.

## Key Limitations

1. **Narrative review** (not systematic), so subject to selection bias in included studies.
2. **Preclinical-to-clinical gap:** Extensive animal data but limited human evidence; clinical trials have not met expectations from animal studies.
3. **Short trial durations:** Median NR treatment duration in human trials is 8 weeks.
4. **Limited NAD+ enhancement:** Classical precursors produce only ~2-fold increases in NAD+ at best.
5. **Unclear mechanism separation:** For NA, cardiorenal benefits may be partly GPR109A-mediated rather than NAD+-mediated.
