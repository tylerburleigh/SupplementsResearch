## Core Findings

ChromaDex submitted GRAS Notice No. 635 to FDA for Niagen (nicotinamide riboside chloride), a form of vitamin B3, for use in vitamin waters, protein shakes, nutrition bars, and gum/chews. The GRAS determination was based on scientific procedures (21 CFR 170.30(b)), supported by genotoxicity testing, a 90-day subchronic rat toxicology study, a human pharmacokinetic study, and corroborative safety data on nicotinamide. An Expert Panel (Drs. Roger Clemens, Thomas Sox, John Thomas) independently concluded Niagen is GRAS under the proposed conditions of use. The document does not contain an FDA response letter -- it is the submission dossier only.

---

## 1. GRAS Determination Basis

- **Basis:** Scientific procedures under 21 CFR 170.30(b)
- **Expert Panel:** Three independent experts (Roger Clemens, DrPH; Thomas Sox, PhD/JD; John Thomas, PhD) reviewed all data and concluded Niagen is GRAS
- **Key rationale:** NR is a form of vitamin B3 that is metabolized similarly to nicotinamide; its safety profile is comparable
- **GRAS conclusion:** "There is no evidence...that demonstrates, or suggests reasonable grounds to suspect, a hazard to the public when this product is used at levels that might reasonably be expected from the proposed applications"
- **Manufactured in cGMP-compliant facility** per 21 CFR 110

## 2. Safety Data Cited

### Genotoxicity (published as Conze et al. 2015)
- **Ames assay:** Negative (bacterial reverse mutagenicity)
- **In vitro chromosomal aberration assay:** Negative (human peripheral blood lymphocytes)
- **In vivo micronucleus assay:** Negative
- **Conclusion:** Niagen is not genotoxic

### 90-Day Subchronic Toxicity (Sprague-Dawley rats)
- **Design:** OECD Guideline 408, GLP-compliant; n=10/sex/group
- **Doses:** Vehicle (water), 300, 1000, 3000 mg/kg/day Niagen, plus 1260 mg/kg/day nicotinamide (equimolar to 3000 mg/kg/day Niagen)
- **NOAEL:** 300 mg/kg/day (no treatment-related adverse effects)
- **LOAEL:** 1000 mg/kg/day (mild, potentially adaptive: increased liver/kidney weights, increased neutrophils, leukocytes, ALT, triglycerides in females only; no histopathological correlates)
- **Severe effects at 3000 mg/kg/day:** Treatment-related adverse effects in liver (centrilobular hepatocellular hypertrophy, single cell necrosis), kidneys (chronic progressive nephropathy), testes (degeneration/atrophy of seminiferous tubules), epididymides, and ovaries (hypertrophy of corpora lutea). All effects mirrored in the equimolar nicotinamide control group.
- **Key finding:** Toxicity profile of Niagen is essentially identical to nicotinamide at equimolar doses

### Clinical Pharmacokinetic Study (Study 14NBHC)
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, single-dose, three-arm crossover; 12 healthy adults (6M/6F)
- **Doses:** 100, 300, 1000 mg Niagen, with 7-day washout between doses
- **Duration:** 24-hour post-dose monitoring per dose
- **Results:** No clinically adverse effects on hematology, clinical chemistry, urinalysis, or liver/kidney function at any dose
- **Adverse events:** 18 total AEs reported by 10 participants; all mild to moderate; 3 subjects withdrawn (2 for low hemoglobin likely due to repeated blood draws, 1 for low WBC)
- **PK findings:** Very low detection of intact NR in blood/urine (blood collection method later found inappropriate); dose-dependent increases in nicotinamide metabolites (MeNam, Py_4, Py_2) confirming NR is metabolized similarly to nicotinamide
- **Mean age:** 45.8 years; Mean BMI: 25.85 +/- 2.49

### Corroborative Nicotinamide Safety Data
- No significant adverse effects in clinical trials using nicotinamide up to 3000 mg/day for up to 3 years (diabetes prevention trials)
- EU SCF UL for nicotinamide: 900 mg/day; UK Expert Group UL: 500 mg/day
- Only one reported case of hepatotoxicity at very high nicotinamide dose (>3 g/day followed by 9 g/day for several days; confounded by concurrent nicotinic acid use)
- NR does not bind GPR109a, the receptor mediating nicotinic acid-induced flushing

## 4. Manufacturing and Purity Specifications

### Manufacturing Process
- Two-step chemical synthesis:
  - **Step 1:** D-Ribofuranose tetraacetate + HCl (gas) in acetonitrile, then nicotinamide added to form nicotinamide-D-riboside triacetate chloride (48-50% yield)
  - **Step 2:** Deacetylation with ammonium hydroxide in methanol, precipitation with methyl t-butyl ether, washing, vacuum drying, milling, sieving
- **Processing aids:** Acetone, methanol, acetonitrile, methyl t-butyl ether, tributyl amine, acetyl chloride, sodium carbonate, ammonium hydroxide -- all GRAS or compliant with 21 CFR

### Stability
- Powder: Stable 11 months at ambient (25C/60% RH) and 9 months at accelerated (40C/75% RH) conditions
- In solution: Stable up to 83 days at 4C; significant degradation at 25C and 40C (breaks down to nicotinamide)
- Beverages containing Niagen recommended for refrigerated storage, up to 83 days

- **NR in milk:** Naturally present at 1.9-3.1 uM in cow's milk (second most abundant NAD precursor after nicotinamide)

- **Patent/licensing context:** Not discussed in this GRAS notice; ChromaDex licensed NR patents from academic institutions

## Limitations

1. **No FDA response included** -- the document is the sponsor's submission only; the FDA's evaluation/conclusion is absent
2. **Clinical study was small** (n=12) and single-dose only -- no long-term human safety data
3. **PK study limitation:** Blood collection method was later found inappropriate for NR detection; metabolite data used as surrogate
4. **90-day study only** -- no chronic (2-year) or reproductive/developmental toxicology data
5. **No carcinogenicity studies** on NR itself (only nicotinamide carcinogenicity data cited from Toth 1983)
6. **Stability data was preliminary** (ongoing at time of submission)
7. **Infant/toddler exposure noted** (ages 0-2) despite intake estimates exceeding the UL on a mg/kg basis for these groups

## Claims to Verify

- **Mean EDI for ages 0-1 at 3.93 mg/kg/day** exceeds the UL of 3 mg/kg/day -- this population subgroup would exceed the derived UL, which is notable
- **Mean EDI for ages 1-2 at 4.64 mg/kg/day** also exceeds the UL -- same concern
- **Batch 13201** (used in clinical study) had acetone at 4818 ppm, exceeding the commercial specification of <=3000 ppm, and higher lead than commercial spec (0.054 ppm vs 0.5 ppm limit) -- though noted as "worst case" for testing purposes
