# src-031: Effect of NR on airway inflammation in COPD

**Citation:** Norheim et al. (2024). *Nature Aging*, 4, 1772-1781. DOI: 10.1038/s43587-024-00758-1
**Venue:** Nature Aging (peer-reviewed)
**Citation count:** 25
**Quality:** ok (68,918 chars, 945 sentences, full paper text available)

---

## Core Findings

This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=40 COPD patients + 20 lung-healthy controls) found that 6 weeks of NR supplementation (2 g/day) significantly reduced airway inflammation as measured by sputum IL-8, with an estimated treatment difference of -52.6% vs placebo (P=0.030). NR also robustly increased whole-blood NAD+ levels (>2-fold in COPD). No effects on lung function or symptom severity were observed, consistent with the short treatment duration and mechanistic study design.

---

## Study Design and Methodology

- **Design:** Single-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial
- **Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04990869; CONSORT and SPIRIT guidelines followed
- **Location:** Bispebjerg Hospital, Copenhagen, Denmark (Aug 2021 - Aug 2022)
- **Population:** 40 patients with stable non-eosinophilic COPD (mean age 71.9, 62.5% female, ex-smokers, FEV1/FVC < 0.7, FEV1 64.5% predicted) + 18 lung-healthy controls (mean age 70.9)
- **Intervention:** NR 1 g twice daily (2 g/day total; four 250 mg capsules with meals) vs placebo for 6 weeks, with 12-week follow-up
- **Randomization:** 1:1, permuted block (size 4), stratified by CAT score (0-15 vs 16-40) for COPD patients
- **Visits:** Pre-treatment (week 0), post-treatment (week 6), follow-up (week 18)
- **Blinding:** Capsules identical in size, color, smell, appearance; study team and participants blinded
- **Primary outcome:** Change in sputum IL-8 from baseline to week 6
- **Secondary outcomes:** Whole-blood NAD+ levels, plasma IL-6
- **Exploratory outcomes:** Epigenetic age (PBMCs), RNA-seq (nasal epithelial cells), cellular senescence prediction (sputum), lung function, symptom scores (CAT, SGRQ-C, EQ-5D-5L)
- **Statistical analysis:** Constrained linear mixed model on log-transformed data, adjusted for sex and baseline CAT score; intention-to-treat; no multiplicity corrections
- **Power calculation:** 10 per group needed for 40% IL-8 reduction (alpha=0.05, beta=0.20); enrolled 20/group accounting for 20% dropout and 60% sputum success rate

---

## Primary Outcome: IL-8 Reduction

- **Estimated treatment difference at week 6:** -52.6% (95% CI: -75.7% to -7.6%; P=0.030)
- NR group change from baseline: -46.2% (95% CI: -69.5% to -5.2%)
- Placebo group change from baseline: +13.4% (95% CI: -25.9% to +73.6%)
- **Persistence at 12-week follow-up:** -63.7% (95% CI: -85.7% to -7.8%; P=0.034)
- Baseline geometric mean IL-8: 1.02 ng/ml; NR group decreased to 0.55 ng/ml; placebo increased to 1.16 ng/ml
- **Caveat:** Baseline IL-8 was higher in NR group by randomization chance, which may confound results; however, negative correlation between baseline IL-8 and change in NR group (r=-0.77, P=0.04) suggests NR has stronger effect in individuals with higher baseline inflammation
- Sputum samples: n=30 at baseline, n=26 at post-treatment, n=24 at follow-up (paired n=22)

---

## Secondary Outcomes

### NAD+ in Whole Blood
- COPD patients: +71.1 uM increase (95% CI: 57.2-85.0 uM); >2-fold increase from baseline (~31.9 uM)
- Healthy controls: +49.4 uM increase (95% CI: 30.9-68.0 uM)
- No significant difference in NAD+ response between COPD and controls (P=0.11)
- NAD+ returned to baseline at 12-week follow-up
- Baseline NAD+ was lower in COPD vs controls (31.9 vs 34.8 uM; P=0.008)
- Baseline NAD+ was positively associated with lung function (higher NAD+ = better FEV1)

### IL-6 in Plasma
- No detectable effect of NR treatment on plasma IL-6 in either COPD or controls
- IL-6 was higher in COPD vs controls at baseline

### Post Hoc: Sputum Neutrophils
- Estimated treatment difference in sputum neutrophil differential count: -58% (95% CI: -78% to -17%; P=0.009)
- Sputum IL-6, neutrophil elastase, and macrophage counts unchanged

---

## Exploratory Outcomes

### Epigenetic Aging (PBMCs)
- Rate of aging (RoA) reduced after NR by Horvath clock (P=0.024), but not different from placebo (P=0.16)
- At 12-week follow-up, pattern of reduced RoA for Index (P=0.027), Metabolic (P<0.0001), Brain (P=0.0002), Liver (P=0.010) -- none differed from placebo
- COPD patients showed accelerated epigenetic aging vs controls across multiple clocks (Cohen's d 0.55-0.85)

### RNA Sequencing (Nasal Epithelial Cells)
- NR upregulated gene sets related to genomic integrity maintenance (DNA repair) in both COPD and controls
- NR downregulated gene sets related to immunity and antigen processing in both groups
- Immunity gene suppression persisted 12 weeks after treatment in COPD but normalized in controls
- NR upregulated cellular respiration gene sets in COPD at follow-up
- Sexual dimorphism observed in gene expression

### Cellular Senescence (Sputum, DNN Prediction)
- No effect at 6 weeks
- At 12-week follow-up, pattern of decreased predicted IR-like senescence (P=0.064)
- In vitro: NR decreased predicted senescence in dose-dependent manner after IR/UV damage in primary fibroblasts; maintained NAD+ levels and increased survival after oxidative stress in BEAS-2B cells

### Physiological Outcomes
- No effect on lung function (FEV1, FVC) or symptom severity (CAT, SGRQ-C, EQ-5D-5L)
- Not expected given short trial duration and stable patient population

---

## Safety and Adverse Events

- 29 AEs reported by 26 participants (out of 60 total); no difference between NR and placebo
- GI issues: 10% in NR group vs 14% in placebo group
- **No serious adverse events** observed
- NR at 2 g/day deemed safe and well tolerated in older individuals with and without COPD
- Dropouts: 1 COPD placebo, 2 COPD NR before post-treatment; 2 additional NR before follow-up; 2 healthy controls in placebo excluded (asthma diagnosis)

## Clinical Significance and Limitations

### Strengths
- Rigorous randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design
- Inclusion of lung-healthy control group for comparison
- Published in Nature Aging, a high-impact journal
- Multiple mechanistic readouts (IL-8, NAD+, RNA-seq, epigenetic clocks, senescence prediction)
- 12-week follow-up showing persistent anti-inflammatory effect

### Limitations
1. **Small sample size:** n=40 COPD patients (20 per group); the study was powered for 10 per group with sputum, but only ~13/17 provided paired sputum samples at post-treatment
2. **Sputum collection challenges:** Not all patients could produce sputum (30 baseline, 26 post, 24 follow-up), reducing power for primary outcome
3. **Baseline IL-8 imbalance:** NR group had higher baseline IL-8 by randomization chance, potentially confounding the treatment effect estimate
4. **No multiplicity corrections:** Multiple secondary and exploratory analyses without adjustment increase false positive risk
5. **Short treatment period:** 6 weeks insufficient to assess clinical outcomes (lung function, symptom scores); authors estimate ~130 patients needed for CAT score changes
6. **No clinical endpoint effects:** No change in lung function or symptom severity
7. **COVID-19 impact:** Pandemic caused loss of some assessments (additional inflammatory markers, NAD+ metabolomics)
8. **Senescence measurement:** Only DNN-based nuclear morphology, not comprehensive senescence markers (p16, p21, SA-beta-gal)

### Clinical Significance
- First randomized trial showing NR reduces airway inflammation (IL-8) in COPD
- The IL-8 reduction is biologically meaningful (IL-8 drives neutrophil recruitment, a key inflammatory pathway in COPD)
- However, clinical significance remains unproven -- no improvement in lung function or symptoms
- Authors suggest NR could reduce exacerbation risk, but this needs testing in larger, longer trials

---

## Claims to Verify

1. **Power calculation claims:** The paper states power was calculated for 10/group to detect 40% IL-8 reduction with SD=5.74 ng/ml, but actual paired sputum samples were only ~13 (NR) and ~16 (placebo) at post-treatment, not the intended 10/group with sputum. The effective power at the achieved sample size may be lower than 80%.
2. **Baseline NAD+ difference:** COPD baseline 31.9 uM vs healthy 34.8 uM -- stated as significant (P=0.008 per figure) but the clinical meaningfulness of a ~3 uM difference is unclear given the >2-fold treatment response.
3. **"More than twofold" NAD+ increase:** From ~31.9 uM baseline to ~103 uM after NR -- this is indeed >2-fold (approximately 3.2x), but the paper says "more than twofold" which is accurate if conservative.
