# Source src-040: Oral NR Raises NAD+ and Lowers Neurodegenerative Biomarkers in Neuronal EVs

**Citation:** Vreones et al. (2023). Aging Cell, 22, e13754. DOI: 10.1111/acel.13754
**Citations:** 60 | **Quality:** ok | **Type:** Short Communication (peer-reviewed)

## Core Findings

Oral NR supplementation (1000 mg/day, 6 weeks) significantly increased NAD+ levels in plasma extracellular vesicles enriched for neuronal origin (NEVs) in healthy older adults, providing the first human evidence that orally administered NR can augment neuronal NAD+. In a responder subgroup (9/10 subjects with quantifiable NAD+ increases), NR also decreased NEV levels of A-beta-42, phospho-JNK, and phospho-ERK1/2 -- biomarkers linked to Alzheimer's disease pathology and neuroinflammatory/insulin resistance pathways.

## 1. Study Design and Methodology

- **Design:** Secondary analysis of a previously published double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover clinical trial (NCT02921659; Martens et al., 2018)
- **Intervention:** NR 500 mg twice daily (1000 mg/day total) for 6 weeks
- **Participants:** 22 healthy older adults (11M/11F; mean age 65 +/- 7 years)
- **Novelty:** First study to examine NEV biomarkers in response to oral NR supplementation; first to measure NAD+ and NADH in NEVs
- **Crossover design:** Each participant served as their own control, with placebo and NR arms

## 2. NAD+ Elevation Data

- NAD+ in NEVs was significantly greater after NR vs. placebo (paired t-test, **p = 0.0092**, n = 10)
- NAD+ could only be quantified in 10 of 22 subjects due to assay sensitivity limitations (LC-MS could detect but not quantify NAD+; a more sensitive luciferase-based plate assay was needed)
- 9 of 10 subjects with quantified NAD+ showed increases following NR
- NADH levels in NEVs were above quantitation but showed **no significant change** (p = 0.215, n = 22)
- This is consistent with a prior report (Brakedal et al., 2022, NADPARK study) showing brain-specific NAD+ increases after NR

## 3. Neurodegenerative Biomarker Changes

**Full cohort (n = 22):**
- No significant changes in A-beta-42, p-Tau-181, total Tau, pSer-IRS-1, pAkt, pGSK3-beta, pp70S6K
- pERK1/2 showed marginal decrease (p = 0.066)
- pJNK and pp38 were unchanged

**Responder subgroup (n = 9; 5M/4F with documented NAD+ increases):**
- **A-beta-42 significantly decreased** (p = 0.015)
- **pJNK significantly decreased** (p = 0.036)
- **pERK1/2 significantly decreased** (p = 0.038)
- p-Tau-181 trended toward decrease (p = 0.095)
- Total Tau trended toward decrease (p = 0.089)

**Correlation analyses:**
- Changes in NAD+ were positively correlated with changes in pAkt, tGSK3-beta, pGSK3-beta, tp70S6K, pp70S6K, pERK1/2, and pJNK
- Changes in NADH were negatively correlated with the same biomarkers (except tGSK3-beta)
- Notable: delta pSer473-Akt vs. delta NAD+ had r = 0.759, R2 = 0.575, p = 0.011
- A-beta-42 changes did NOT correlate with NAD+ changes, suggesting pleiotropic NR effects on amyloid independent of NAD+ mechanism

## 4. Clinical Significance

- First demonstration that oral NR can augment neuronal NAD+ levels in living humans
- Decreases in A-beta-42, pJNK, and pERK1/2 suggest engagement of AD-relevant and neuroinflammatory pathways
- JNK is highly phosphorylated and co-localizes with A-beta in AD brain; NAD+ augmentation attenuates both A-beta accumulation and JNK activation in transgenic AD mice
- NR may decrease JNK and ERK1/2 phosphorylation rather than boost them, contrary to initial hypothesis about enhanced insulin signaling
- Proposed mechanism for A-beta-42 reduction: NR increases PGC1-alpha expression, which downregulates BACE1 (rate-limiting enzyme in amyloidogenic APP processing); also enhances mitophagy and microglial clearance of extracellular amyloid

## 5. Plasma Extracellular Vesicle Methodology

- **Isolation:** Immunoaffinity capture targeting L1CAM (neuronal marker) on plasma EV sub-population
- **Validation:** NEVs expressed EV markers ALIX and CD9; depleted of Golgi marker GM130 and lipoprotein APOA1 (per MISEV2018 guidelines)
- **Enrichment:** 1.6-fold enrichment for L1CAM, 3.7-fold for beta-III-tubulin vs. total circulating EVs
- **Size:** Particles predominantly 150-250 nm by nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA)
- **Normalization:** NEV concentration used to normalize protein biomarker values
- **NAD+ measurement:** LC-MS initially; luciferase-based plate assay (Promega) for quantitative NAD+ due to LC-MS sensitivity limitations

## 6. Key Limitations

1. **Small sample size:** Only 22 participants total; NAD+ quantifiable in only 10; responder subgroup n = 9
2. **Assay sensitivity:** NAD+ could be detected but not reliably quantified by LC-MS in most NEV isolates, requiring a secondary assay and reducing the analyzable sample
3. **Subgroup analysis:** Key biomarker findings (A-beta-42, pJNK, pERK1/2 decreases) were only significant in the NAD+ responder subgroup, not the full cohort
4. **Responder definition:** Potential for circular analysis -- responders defined by NAD+ increase, then used to test biomarker changes
5. **Crossover design limitations:** No washout period details reported in this paper (described in parent trial)
6. **Surrogate marker:** NEV NAD+ levels are a surrogate for neuronal NAD+; not a direct brain measurement
7. **Healthy population:** Results may not generalize to patients with neurodegenerative disease
8. **No clinical endpoints:** Biomarker changes only; no cognitive or functional outcomes measured
9. **NR capsules provided by ChromaDex, Inc.** (potential COI, though authors declare no competing interests)

## Claims to Verify

- **NAD+ quantified in only 10/22 subjects:** Confirmed in text (line 164). This is a significant limitation -- the primary NAD+ finding rests on fewer than half the enrolled participants.
- **9/10 subjects showed NAD+ increases:** Confirmed (line 170-171). Only 1 of 10 quantified subjects did not show an increase.
- **Responder subgroup n = 9:** This excludes the 1 non-responder from the 10 with quantified NAD+ (line 173-174). All downstream biomarker analyses rest on this small subgroup.

## Relevance to Niagen/NR Research

This is a highly relevant study for Niagen/NR research, providing the first human evidence that oral NR supplementation can:
1. Increase NAD+ in neuron-derived extracellular vesicles (a brain surrogate)
2. Modulate neurodegenerative disease biomarkers (A-beta-42, pJNK, pERK1/2)
3. Engage insulin signaling pathways in neurons

The crossover design strengthens internal validity. The small sample and responder-only analysis weaken the evidence but do not negate the provocative findings. The NEV methodology represents an innovative non-invasive approach to assessing brain-level NAD+ metabolism and drug target engagement.
