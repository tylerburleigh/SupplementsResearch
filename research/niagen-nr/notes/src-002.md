## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive 2020 review covering NR's mechanism of action, preclinical and clinical evidence for metabolic, cardiovascular, and neurodegenerative applications, safety, bioavailability, and comparison with other NAD+ precursors. The review synthesizes data from numerous animal studies and six completed clinical trials, concluding that NR is a leading NAD+ precursor candidate due to safety profile and efficacy in raising NAD+ levels, though translation to human therapeutic outcomes remains limited.

---

## 1. Key Findings About NR Research Status

- NR is described as one of the most studied NAD+ precursors, with rapidly accumulating preclinical evidence
- As of 2020, six clinical trials had been completed establishing safety and oral availability
- Multiple additional clinical trials were underway investigating cardiovascular, neural/cognitive, metabolic, muscular, kidney, aging, and chemotherapy applications
- NR chloride (Niagen, ChromaDex) received GRAS status
- Key gap: many promising animal results (metabolic, cardiovascular, neurodegenerative) had not yet been replicated in humans

---

## 2. Mechanism of Action and Pathways

**NR to NAD+ conversion:**
- Primary route: NR -> NMN via NRK1/NRK2 kinases -> NAD+ via NMNATs (2-step pathway)
- Alternative route: NR -> NAM via purine nucleoside phosphorylase (NP) -> NMN via NAMPT -> NAD+ via NMNATs
- NRK pathway bypasses the rate-limiting NAMPT enzyme, avoiding PRPP requirement and NAD+ feedback inhibition
- NRK1 is ubiquitously expressed; NRK2 mainly expressed in cardiac and skeletal muscles, also liver and brown adipose tissue

**NAD+ consuming enzymes relevant to NR action:**
- Sirtuins (SIRT1-7): NAD+-dependent deacetylases regulating metabolism, DNA repair, stress response
- PARPs: DNA repair enzymes that increase with age, competing with sirtuins for NAD+
- CD38: Primary NADase, increases with age, degrades NR and NMN intermediates
- SARM1: Promotes axonal degeneration using NAD+

**Tissue-specific NR effects:**
- NR increased NAD+ in liver, skeletal muscles, and brown adipose tissue
- NAD+ levels did NOT significantly increase in brain or white adipose tissue (differential NRK expression)
- NMRK2 pathway activation is a common adaptive mechanism in the failing heart

## 3. Clinical Trial Evidence Reviewed

**Completed trials at time of publication (6 total):**
1. **Trammell et al. (2016):** 1000 mg twice daily increased whole-blood NAD+ up to 2.7-fold (single-dose study in healthy volunteers)
2. **Martens et al. (2018):** NR supplementation reduced systolic blood pressure and aortic stiffness; confirmed long-term (6 weeks) oral availability and safety
3. **Dollerup et al. (2018):** 12-week, 2000 mg/day NR did NOT improve insulin sensitivity or metabolic parameters in insulin-resistant obese men (negative result)
4. **Trammell et al. (2018):** Pharmacokinetic profiling; peak NAD+ increase after 9 days; variable oral bioavailability
5. **Conze et al.:** Randomized 8-week placebo-controlled trial with doses up to 1 g in overweight/healthy adults - confirmed safety and oral availability
6. Additional trial referenced

**Key negative finding:** 2000 mg/day for 12 weeks failed to improve insulin sensitivity in obese, insulin-resistant men, despite positive mouse data at 400 mg/kg/day.

**Preclinical positive findings (not yet replicated in humans):**
- Mouse models: improved glucose tolerance, reduced weight gain, neuroprotection against diabetic neuropathy
- AD mouse models: improved oxidative stress, DNA repair, reduced pTau, amyloid-beta, neuroinflammation; improved cognition in 6 weeks
- Heart failure models: preserved cardiac function, limited adverse remodeling in DCM
- Muscular dystrophy (mdx mice): improved muscular function, decreased inflammation and fibrosis
- Lifespan extension in mice (small but significant)

---

## 4. Safety and Side Effects Data

**Human safety data:**
- No severe side effects reported across all clinical trials
- Specifically: no flushing, pruritus, hyperglycemia, hyperuricemia, or increased liver/muscle enzyme activity
- NR does not activate GPR109A (the receptor mediating nicotinic acid flushing)
- Safe for short-term (8 days) and long-term (6 weeks to 12 weeks) use at doses up to 2000 mg/day

**Animal toxicology:**
- 90-day rat toxicology study: LOAEL = 1000 mg/kg/day, NOAEL = 300 mg/kg/day for NR chloride
- GRAS status granted for NR chloride

**Comparison with other precursors:**
- NA: causes flushing at doses >50 mg/day
- NAM: can inhibit sirtuins at higher doses
- NMN: limited clinical safety data as of 2020 (one study with 10 healthy men, 100-500 mg single dose, appeared safe)

---

## 5. Comparison With Other NAD+ Precursors

| Property | NR | NA | NAM | NMN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oral bioavailability (liver NAD+) | Highest of tested | Lowest | Moderate | Limited data |
| Flushing | No | Yes (>50 mg/day) | No | No |
| Sirtuin inhibition | No | No | Yes (high doses) | No |
| Stem cell regeneration | Yes (unique) | No | No | No |
| ADPR accumulation (sirtuin activity) | ~3x more than NAM | Lower | Baseline | Not specified |
| NAAD increase | 45-fold | Least NAAD produced | Moderate | Not specified |
| Clinical safety data | Multiple trials | Well-established | Well-established | Very limited (2020) |

- NR is the only precursor that could prolong survival and induce hematopoietic stem cell regeneration in irradiated mice
- NR uniquely increases NAD+ in multiple subcellular compartments (mitochondrial and nuclear)
- NRH (reduced form of NR) emerged as a more stable alternative with faster/potent NAD+ increase in mice, using NRK-independent pathway
- NAD+ and NMN must be converted to NR extracellularly (via CD73) before cellular entry; NMN-specific gut transporter (Slc12a8) recently identified

## 6. Dosing and Pharmacokinetics

**Human dosing data:**
- 1000 mg twice daily (2000 mg total): increased whole-blood NAD+ up to 2.7-fold
- Peak NAD+ increase reached after 9 days of chronic supplementation
- Once-daily or twice-daily dosing may be sufficient for clinical outcomes
- Relatively short elimination half-life; repeated dosing prevents wide fluctuations

**Bioavailability issues:**
- Highly variable among individuals
- NR is unstable in blood (partially degrades to NAM)
- NR hydrophilicity limits passive intestinal permeability
- Gut degradation to NAM via purine nucleoside phosphorylase may be a variable step
- Multiple NR-to-NAD+ conversion pathways identified in humans and mice
- 45-fold increase in NAAD (most sensitive NAD+ supplementation biomarker; undetectable at baseline)

**Animal dosing references:**
- 400 mg/kg/day improved insulin sensitivity and protected against weight gain in high-fat-fed mice
- Dosing for heart failure models not specified in detail in this review

---

## Limitations

1. This is a review article, not a primary study -- evidence is aggregated from heterogeneous sources
2. Author conflict of interest: affiliated with Biosynth Carbosynth, which sells NR derivatives; one author holds NR-related patents
3. As of 2020, human clinical data was limited to 6 trials with relatively small sample sizes
4. Many positive findings are from mouse models and may not translate to humans (as demonstrated by the negative insulin sensitivity trial)
5. NR bioavailability variability is not well understood
6. Long-term safety beyond 12 weeks not established at time of publication
