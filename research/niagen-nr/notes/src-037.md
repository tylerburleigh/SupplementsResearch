# src-037: NR-SAFE Trial - High Dose NR in Parkinson's Disease

**Citation:** Berven et al. (2023). "NR-SAFE: a randomized, double-blind safety trial of high dose nicotinamide riboside in Parkinson's disease." *Nature Communications*, 14, 7793. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-43514-6

**Quality:** ok | **Citations:** 56 | **Peer-reviewed:** Yes (Nature Communications)

## Core Findings

NR at 3000 mg/day (the highest dose tested in humans at the time) was safe and well-tolerated over 4 weeks in 20 Parkinson's disease patients, with no moderate or severe adverse events. NR produced a pronounced augmentation of the blood NAD metabolome (~3.7-fold increase in NAD+) and a statistically significant improvement in MDS-UPDRS scores, though the clinical improvement was confounded by levodopa dosing interval differences. Methyl donor pool integrity was preserved despite a mild transient increase in serum homocysteine.

## 1. Study Design

- **Design:** Phase I, single-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **Location:** Department of Neurology, Haukeland University Hospital, Bergen, Norway
- **Dates:** April 29, 2022 to July 1, 2022
- **Participants:** 20 individuals with idiopathic PD (MDS criteria), Hoehn & Yahr <4, ages 35-100
- **Randomization:** 1:1, NR (n=10) vs placebo (n=10), 2 blocks of size 4
- **Dose:** NR 1500 mg twice daily = 3000 mg/day total (Tru Niagen by ChromaDex, 250 mg capsules)
- **Duration:** 4 weeks (28 days)
- **Compliance:** NR 95.6 +/- 2.45%; Placebo 94.4 +/- 3.27% (p=0.52)
- **All 20 participants completed the trial** (0 dropouts)
- **Clinicaltrials.gov:** NCT05344404

## 2. Safety Outcomes (Primary Endpoint)

- **Primary outcome met:** No moderate or severe adverse events in either group
- 42 total AEs: 25 in NR group, 17 in placebo group (not significantly different)
- All AEs were Grade 1 (mild) by CTCAE v5.0
- 9/10 NR participants and 8/10 placebo participants had at least one AE
- No significant changes in blood pressure, pulse, weight, or ECG
- Two NR participants developed asymptomatic bradycardia (58 bpm and 46 bpm)
- No abnormal changes in clinical laboratory values except mild homocysteine increase
- No painful flushing (which occurs with nicotinic acid at >50-100 mg)

## 3. NAD+ Metabolome Changes

- **NAD+ increased ~3.7-fold** (range 1.8-5.8-fold) in whole blood - considerably higher than 1.37-2.8-fold reported at 1000-2000 mg/day
- Significant increases in: NAD+, NADH, total NAD, NAD+/NADH ratio, NADP+, total NADP, NMN, NAAD, nicotinamide (Nam), Me-Nam, Me-2-PY, Nam N-oxide, ADPR
- NADP+/NADPH ratio showed trend toward increase (p=0.056)
- **No changes** in: glutathione (GSH, GSSG), ATP, GTP, ADP, AMP, adenosine, GDP
- NR itself did not significantly increase in blood (p=0.135), but between-group delta was significant (p=0.029)
- Urinary metabolites (NAR, Nam, Me-Nam, Me-2-PY, Nam N-oxide) all increased in NR group

## 4. Clinical Efficacy Signals (MDS-UPDRS)

- **Total MDS-UPDRS (I-IV):** NR group improved from 51.0 +/- 21.28 to 40.3 +/- 17.1 (mean change -10.7 +/- 9.94; p=0.007); Placebo unchanged (41.8 to 41.8; p=1)
- **Between-group comparison:** p=0.024 (significant)
- **Driven primarily by Part III (motor):** V1: 29.7 to V7: 22.7 (p=0.0214)
- Part I (non-motor daily living): trend improvement (p=0.079)
- Parts II and IV: no significant change
- **Subparts did not survive multiple testing correction** (Benjamini-Hochberg)
- **Major confound:** NR group had shorter interval since last levodopa dose at V7 vs V1 (-49 +/- 72 min), while placebo had longer interval (+28 +/- 33 min)
- Subset analysis of 8 participants per group with more comparable levodopa intervals still showed significant NR improvement (p=0.028), but confound cannot be fully excluded

## 5. Adverse Events Profile

Most common AEs in NR group:
- Extrapyramidal disorder: 3 events / 3 participants (includes increased dyskinesia, increased rigidity post-cessation, reduced need for levodopa)
- Headache: 3/3
- Tremor: 2/2
- Muscle cramp: 2/1 (one participant had multiple events; history of dystonia)
- Fatigue: 2/2
- Nausea: 2/2
- Dyspepsia: 2/2 (all resolved)
- One case of maculo-papular rash (deemed unlikely related to NR)

Notable: One NR participant reduced levodopa due to increased dyskinesia; another reduced levodopa due to reduced subjective need.

## 6. Homocysteine and Methyl Donor Pool

- Serum homocysteine increased in NR group: delta = +1.66 +/- 0.63 umol/L (p=5.4x10^-4)
- The increase occurred rapidly (by day 3: +2.29 umol/L), then stabilized
- Most participants remained within normal HCy range; one shifted from normal to slightly elevated
- **No corresponding increase** in whole blood HCy (by LC-MS), SAM, SAH, SAM/SAH ratio, or methionine
- Betaine decreased slightly in NR group
- Conclusion: methyl donor pool integrity remained intact at 3000 mg/day for 4 weeks

## Key Limitations

1. **Short duration (4 weeks):** No long-term safety data for 3000 mg/day
2. **Small sample (n=20):** Limits statistical power and generalizability
3. **Low female representation:** 5/20 total (2 NR, 3 placebo) vs general PD population
4. **Clinical efficacy confounded:** Levodopa interval differences between groups may partially explain MDS-UPDRS improvement
5. **Not designed for efficacy assessment:** Was a Phase I safety trial; efficacy signals are preliminary
6. **PD-specific population:** Findings may not generalize to other conditions or healthy individuals

## Relevance to Research on Niagen/NR

This is a landmark dose-finding study that established the upper boundary for NR dosing in clinical trials (3000 mg/day). It demonstrates dose-dependent NAD+ augmentation (3.7-fold vs 1.37-2.8-fold at lower doses), supports the safety of Tru Niagen at high doses in a disease population, and provides preliminary evidence of symptomatic improvement in PD. The study directly enables Phase II efficacy trials at this dose range.

## Claims to Verify

- The "~5-fold increase in blood NAD+" stated in the abstract appears to refer to the upper end of the range (5.8-fold). The mean was 3.7-fold. The abstract could be slightly misleading.
- Drug compliance rates (95.6% and 94.4%) are based on self-reporting and pill counts, which tend to overestimate actual compliance.
- The p=0.024 between-group MDS-UPDRS comparison is uncorrected; subpart analyses did not survive correction.
