# Source src-051: NAD+-Increasing Strategies to Improve Cardiometabolic Health?

**Authors:** Blanco-Vaca F, Rotllan N, Canyelles M, Mauricio D, Escola-Gil JC, Julve J
**Year:** 2022 | **Venue:** Frontiers in Endocrinology | **Type:** Mini Review
**DOI:** 10.3389/fendo.2021.815565 | **Citations:** 8

---

## Core Findings

This mini-review synthesizes clinical evidence on NAD+-increasing strategies (NR, NMN, NAM) for cardiometabolic health. The overall conclusion is that accumulated clinical data are "moderately promising," but trial results are heterogeneous, with few hard clinical end points achieved. Blood pressure reduction is the most robust finding; other benefits (exercise capacity, insulin sensitivity, anti-inflammatory effects) were trial-specific and generally not reproduced.

## Review Scope and Methodology

- **Design:** Narrative mini-review of clinical trials testing vitamin B3 forms for cardiometabolic outcomes
- **Search strategy:** PubMed search for clinical reports published in the last 5 years using "nicotinamide" and "randomized clinical trials" as keywords
- **Focus:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in obesity or pathologies linked to cardiovascular and skeletal muscle function
- **Limitation:** Not a systematic review or meta-analysis; no formal quality assessment or PRISMA protocol reported

## NR Clinical Trial Evidence for Cardiometabolic Outcomes

The review identifies multiple NR trials:

| Trial | Population | Dose | Duration | Key Findings |
|-------|-----------|------|----------|-------------|
| Martens et al. (28) | 24 lean, aged 55-79 | 1000 mg/day | 6 wk crossover | Decreased BP, aortic PWV; increased carotid compliance. No metabolic changes |
| Dollerup et al. (32-34) | 40 obese men, 40-70 yr | 2000 mg/day | 12 wk | No changes in HbA1c, insulin sensitivity, glucose disposal, body composition, or muscle NAD+ |
| Conze et al. (29) | 133 overweight, 40-60 yr | 100/300/1000 mg/day | 8 wk | Dose-dependent NAD+ increase (22-142%); no BP, weight, or energy expenditure changes |
| Elhassan et al. (30) | 12 old men, 70-80 yr | 1000 mg/day | 21 days crossover | Decreased inflammatory cytokines; no metabolic, BP, or muscle function changes |
| Remie et al. (37) | 13 overweight/obese, 59 yr | 1000 mg/day | 6 wk crossover | Increased fat-free mass (esp. women); increased skeletal muscle acetylcarnitine; no insulin sensitivity change |

**Blood pressure:** Only the Martens et al. trial (lean, healthy older adults) found significant BP reduction with NR. Other NR trials did not reproduce this finding.

**Exercise capacity:** No NR trial showed exercise capacity improvements. This was only seen with NMN.

**Insulin sensitivity:** No NR trial demonstrated improved insulin sensitivity. The Dollerup et al. trial specifically designed for this endpoint in obese men was negative.

## Comparison with NMN and Nicotinamide

**NMN:**
- 48 recreational runners (6 wk, 300-1200 mg/day): medium and high doses increased VO2 and exercise capacity
- 25 overweight/obese prediabetic women (10 wk, 250 mg/day): improved insulin-stimulated glucose disposal via hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp; increased skeletal muscle insulin signaling (AKT/mTOR); no body weight/composition changes
- NMN appears to show more consistent metabolic benefits than NR in the limited trials available

**NAM:**
- ENDIT study (n=276, type 1 diabetes, up to 3 g/day, 5 years): failed to prevent progression to overt type 1 diabetes
- Minor inconsistent effects on glucose kinetics and insulin sensitivity; some reports of minor insulin resistance
- Phase 1 study showed dose-related NAD+ increase and less acute kidney injury
- Associated with reduced cardiovascular mortality in 20-year Bruneck Study follow-up (epidemiological)

## Heterogeneity of Outcomes

Key factors contributing to heterogeneity:
1. **Gender effect:** Trials with more women (Martens, Yoshino/NMN, Remie) showed more positive results; male-only trial (Dollerup) was entirely negative. Possible gender-specific response pattern.
2. **Baseline cardiometabolic status:** Lean healthy subjects (Martens) showed BP benefits but no metabolic changes; obese/prediabetic subjects showed some metabolic signals but inconsistent.
3. **Dose and duration:** Trials ranged from 21 days to 12 weeks, doses from 250-2000 mg/day. Short duration limits ability to detect changes in HbA1c.
4. **Tissue bioavailability:** The Dollerup trial suggested limited NR tissue bioavailability despite elevated urine metabolites; muscle NAD+ did not increase.

## Authors' Overall Conclusions

- Accumulated clinical data are "moderately promising"
- Favorable outcomes are heterogeneous across trials
- Only blood pressure reduction qualifies as a hard clinical end point; other benefits (exercise capacity, anti-inflammatory, insulin sensitivity) were not consistently reproduced
- No significant reductions in body weight, BMI, fasting glucose, or HbA1c observed
- Future trials should: (1) identify subjects with recognized NAD+ deficiency using novel biomarkers, (2) assess gender-specific effects, (3) include longer treatment durations, (4) consider combining with exercise

## Limitations of This Review

- Not a systematic review; no formal risk-of-bias assessment
- PubMed-only search may have missed relevant trials
- Narrative synthesis without quantitative meta-analysis
- Published January 2022; does not include more recent NR/NMN trials
- Some referenced trials had very small sample sizes (n=5 to n=40)
- Quality field in metadata: "ok"; content confirmed as full paper text

## Claims to Verify

- "NR increased whole blood NAD+ from 22% to 142%" (Conze et al., ref 29) -- stated but no p-values reported in this review
- "Fat-free mass increased in 6 out of 7 women vs 1 out of 6 men" (Remie et al., ref 37) -- small sample; gender interaction not formally tested
- "NA up to 1000 mg/day restored muscle NAD+ levels up to eightfold" in 5 mitochondrial myopathy subjects (ref 14) -- very small sample, non-randomized
