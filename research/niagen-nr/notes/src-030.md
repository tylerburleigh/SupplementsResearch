# Source src-030: Critical Review of NR Human Trials

**Paper:** Critical assessment/review of nicotinamide riboside supplementation in humans (PMID 37478182)
**Source URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37478182
**Content quality:** Abstract-only (PubMed landing page). Full text behind paywall. The abstract is substantive and provides the core conclusions of the review.

## Core Findings

This is a critical review of all 25 published human NR supplementation trials available at the time of writing. The authors conclude that oral NR supplementation has shown **few clinically relevant effects** in humans, despite promising preclinical data. The review specifically calls out a tendency in the literature to **exaggerate the importance and robustness** of reported effects.

## Key Evidence and Claims

### 1. Overall Assessment of NR Efficacy
- NR has shown metabolic benefits in multiple preclinical (animal) studies
- Translation to human clinical benefits has been largely unsuccessful
- The first human NR trial was conducted in 2016
- As of publication, 25 human trials had been published

### 2. Exaggeration in the Literature
- The authors identify an "unfortunate tendency" to exaggerate the importance and robustness of reported NR effects
- This review was motivated by the need to identify "poorly founded claims" in the NR literature
- This is significant because it directly challenges the marketing and popular claims made about Niagen/NR supplements

### 3. Areas of Remaining Potential
- NR may play a role in **reduction of inflammatory states** (some evidence)
- NR has shown **some potential in treatment of diverse severe diseases** (qualified language)
- These are described cautiously -- not as established benefits but as areas warranting further investigation

### 4. Specific Trials Referenced (from references list)
The following key trials are cited in the reference list, suggesting they were among those critically assessed:
- **Dollerup et al. (2018):** Randomized placebo-controlled trial of NR in obese men -- safety, insulin sensitivity, lipid-mobilizing effects (Am J Clin Nutr)
- **Dollerup et al. (2019):** Effects of NR on endocrine pancreatic function and incretin hormones in nondiabetic men with obesity (J Clin Endocrinol Metab)
- **Zhang et al. (2020):** Acute effect of metabolic cofactor supplementation and NAFLD (Mol Syst Biol)
- **Altay et al. (2021):** Combined metabolic activators in COVID-19 recovery (Adv Sci)
- **Yulug et al. (2023):** Combined metabolic activators in Alzheimer's disease (Transl Neurodegener)

Note: The first author of this review (Dollerup) also authored two of the key trials being reviewed (2018, 2019), which may indicate particular depth of expertise but also potential authorship bias in framing.

## Methodology
- Systematic review of 25 published human NR supplementation articles
- Critical assessment approach: designed to identify poorly founded claims
- Covers both healthy volunteer and disease-state trials

## Relevance to Research Question

This source is **highly relevant** as it directly addresses the gap between NR marketing claims and actual human evidence. Key contributions:
- Provides a counternarrative to exaggerated NR claims
- Systematically reviews the human trial literature (25 studies)
- Identifies inflammation reduction as a possible genuine benefit area
- Warns about claim inflation in the NR literature
