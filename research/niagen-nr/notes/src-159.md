# src-159: Differential Impact of Three NAD+ Boosters on Circulatory NAD and Microbial Metabolism

**Citation:** Christen S, Redeuil K, Goulet L, et al. Nature Metabolism, Volume 8, January 2026, 62-73. DOI: 10.1038/s42255-025-01421-8
**Trial Registration:** NCT05517122
**Study Type:** Randomized, open-label, placebo-controlled, four-arm parallel group study (Letter)

---

## Core Findings

This is the first head-to-head clinical comparison of NR, NMN, and nicotinamide in humans. After 14 days, NR (1 g/d) and NMN (1 g/d) comparably increased baseline whole-blood NAD+ by approximately 2-fold, while Nam (0.5 g/d) did not chronically raise NAD+. However, only Nam produced acute transient NAD+ increases. The study provides strong evidence that NR and NMN work via gut microbial conversion to nicotinic acid (NA) and the Preiss-Handler pathway, while Nam acts via rapid absorption and the salvage pathway.

## Key Findings: Comparing NR, NMN, and Nicotinamide

### Chronic effects (14 days)
- **NR:** Whole-blood NAD+ increased by 49.4 uM (95% CI: 39.5-59.3; P < 0.001) vs placebo
- **NMN:** Whole-blood NAD+ increased by 43.1 uM (95% CI: 32.7-53.4; P < 0.001) vs placebo
- **Nam:** No significant increase in NAD+ (P = 0.461)
- NR and NMN produced comparable NAD+ elevations (~2-fold)
- NADP+, NADPH, and NADH levels were not significantly changed in any arm
- All three precursors increased NAD+ degradation metabolites MeNam and MeXPY in whole blood, plasma, and urine

### Acute effects (4 hours post-dose)
- **Nam only:** Profound acute impact on whole-blood NAD+ metabolome
  - Nam Cmax at ~1 hour, iAUC of 105.2 uM.h (95% CI: 87.4-122.9; P < 0.001)
  - Mild transient elevation of NAD+, NR, and NMN at 1 hour
  - Build-up of degradation metabolites MeNam and MeXPY
- **NR and NMN:** No acute change in circulatory NAD+ metabolome over 4 hours
  - Whole-blood NR and NMN concentrations remained <2 uM
  - No acute increase in NA or NAR detected (LLOQ 0.02 uM)

### Differential metabolic effects
- NR and NMN increased riboflavin 5-monophosphate (FMN), 2-methylmalonate, and phenylacetyl glutamine -- all potentially derived from gut microbiota
- Nam caused a strong acute increase in homocysteine (>8-fold median iAUC increase; P < 0.005), related to methyl pool depletion from Nam methylation; NR and NMN did not affect homocysteine
- NR and NMN (but not Nam) led to more sustained homocysteine and cysteine concentrations chronically

## Bioavailability and Pharmacokinetics

- **Nam:** Rapidly absorbed (Cmax ~1 hour), quickly metabolized and excreted; circulates at meaningful concentrations
- **NR and NMN:** Not directly absorbed in systemic circulation in appreciable amounts (whole blood < 2 uM after dosing); retained longer in GI lumen
- NR degraded to Nam via purine nucleoside phosphorylase (PNP), producing ribose-1-phosphate
- NMN degraded via CD38, producing ribose-5-phosphate and sedoheptulose-7-phosphate
- These distinct degradation pathways explain different rates of breakdown

## Microbial Metabolism Findings

### Ex vivo fermentation (SIFR technology)
- **NR metabolism by gut microbiota:**
  - Rapidly cleaved to Nam (peak 2.1 mM at 8 hours)
  - Nam further deamidated to NA (sustained 200-400 uM over last 24 hours)
  - Produced 6-hydroxynicotinic acid (bacterial NA degradation intermediate)
  - Transient NMN peak (6.8 uM at 2h), NAR peak (4.9 uM at 4h), NAMN peak (2.4 uM at 4h)
- **Nam metabolism by gut microbiota:** Converted only to NA; not converted to NR or NMN
- NR metabolism was notably consistent across 6 individual healthy donors
- NMN showed similar build-up of Nam and NA as NR, plus rapid increase in its deamidated form NAMN

### Impact on microbial growth and metabolism
- NR significantly increased acetate, propionate, and total SCFA concentrations
- NR increased total bacterial cell density over 48 hours
- NR specifically and significantly increased Enterocloster aldensis abundance (no other species significantly affected)
- Effects consistent across healthy adults (n=6), healthy older donors (n=10), and Crohn's disease patients (n=10)
- NR and NMN both increased growth, decreased pH, increased gas production, and increased SCFAs

### Proposed mechanism
- NA and SCFAs locally produced by the microbiome activate GPCRs (GPR109a, GPR41, GPR43) on intestinal epithelial and immune cells
- Combined increase in NA and SCFA may enhance epithelial integrity, mitigate mucosal inflammation, and maintain intestinal homeostasis

## Proposed Model (Figure 4)

- **NR and NMN:** Low absorption; retained in gut where microbiota hydrolyze and deamidate to NA; NA enters Preiss-Handler pathway for sustained NAD+ elevation; also boost gut health via SCFA production
- **Nam:** Fast absorption in upper gut; acute transient NAD+ increase via salvage pathway; no chronic elevation; depletes methyl pool (raising homocysteine)

## Clinical Significance and Implications

1. **NR and NMN offer dual benefits:** Sustained systemic NAD+ elevation AND enhanced gut health via SCFA production and microbial growth stimulation
2. **Nam is not equivalent:** Despite being an NAD+ precursor, Nam does not chronically elevate NAD+ and raises homocysteine (cardiovascular risk factor)
3. **Gut microbiome is essential:** The NAD+-boosting effect of NR and NMN depends on microbial conversion to NA, confirming in humans what was previously shown only in rodents
4. **Population consistency:** Microbial effects were consistent across healthy adults, older adults, and Crohn's disease patients, suggesting broad applicability

## Methodology

### Study design
- Randomized, open-label, placebo-controlled, four-arm parallel group study
- Conducted at Nestle Research, Lausanne, Switzerland (July-November 2022)
- 14-day intervention with once-daily dosing
- Primary endpoint: change in whole-blood baseline NAD+ after 14 days
- Secondary endpoints: acute (4-hour) and chronic (day 1 to day 14) NAD+ metabolome changes
- ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05517122

### Sample size and population
- 67 enrolled, 65 in modified ITT (2 excluded for receiving wrong product)
- NR: n=16, NMN: n=15, Nam: n=17, Placebo: n=17
- Mean age: 34.7 +/- 7.4 years; 32 males, 33 females
- BMI: 21.4 +/- 1.8 kg/m2 (females), 23.7 +/- 1.8 kg/m2 (males)
- Healthy adults aged 18-50, BMI 18.5-27.0

### Dosing
- NR: 1000 mg/d (3.4 mmol/d) -- TRU NIAGEN Pro
- NMN: 1000 mg/d (3 mmol/d) -- UltraHealth
- Nam: 500 mg/d (4.1 mmol/d) -- Pure Encapsulations
- Placebo: 500 mg/d microcrystalline cellulose -- Pure Encapsulations
- All administered once daily for 14 consecutive days

### Analytical methods
- Targeted NAD+ metabolome: LC-MS/MS (whole blood, plasma, urine)
- Untargeted metabolomics: LC-HRMS (Orbitrap)
- Plasma amino acid analysis: LC-MS/MS
- Ex vivo fermentation: SIFR technology (Cryptobiotix)
- Whole-blood cell culture: 50 uM precursors, 7-hour time course
- Statistics: ANCOVA, mixed models for repeated measurements, linear mixed-effects models

## Safety Data

- All three precursors were well tolerated
- Only 3 adverse events with probable relation to the product out of 65 participants:
  - 1 in placebo group: abdominal pain
  - 1 in NR group: hypotension
  - 1 in NMN group: headache
- No serious adverse events reported

## Limitations

1. Ex vivo whole-blood experiments could not capture the mild acute impact of Nam on NAD+ metabolome
2. No increased NA detected in venous whole blood despite proposed NA-mediated mechanism (presumably due to rapid uptake and metabolism)
3. No stool samples collected to confirm deamidation reaction rates
4. Study performed in healthy population with already-normal NAD+ levels; disease populations not tested
5. Shallow shotgun sequencing provided limited depth for functional gene analysis
6. Cannot exclude that simultaneously released Nam may serve other organs via the salvage pathway
7. Open-label design (not blinded)
8. Different molar doses between groups (Nam at 4.1 mmol vs NR at 3.4 mmol vs NMN at 3 mmol)

## Claims to Verify

- The ~2-fold increase claim for NAD+ is stated in the text; the absolute numbers (49.4 uM and 43.1 uM increases) should be cross-referenced with baseline NAD+ levels (~25 uM typical) to confirm the fold-change
- The Nam dose (0.5 g/d) is lower than NR and NMN (1 g/d), though molar amounts are closer (4.1 vs 3.4 vs 3.0 mmol/d) -- this dose difference should be considered when interpreting Nam's lack of chronic effect
