# Source src-027: Nicotinamide riboside is uniquely and orally bioavailable in mice and humans

**Citation:** Trammell et al., Nature Communications 7:12948 (2016). DOI: 10.1038/ncomms12948

**Authors:** Trammell, Schmidt, Weidemann, Redpath, Jaksch, Dellinger, Li, Abel, Migaud, Brenner
**Affiliations:** University of Iowa, Queens University Belfast, ChromaDex Inc.

## Core Findings

This is the first clinical pharmacokinetic study of NR in humans, establishing that oral NR dose-dependently elevates blood NAD+ metabolome and identifying NAAD as a novel, highly sensitive biomarker of effective NAD+ repletion. NR demonstrated superior hepatic NAD+ boosting and NAD+-consuming activity (sirtuin-relevant) compared to equimolar nicotinamide (Nam) and nicotinic acid (NA) in mice.

## Study Design

### Pilot Study (n=1)
- Single healthy 52-year-old male (65 kg) self-administered 1,000 mg NR chloride daily for 7 consecutive days
- Blood collected at 11 time points across the study; PBMC, plasma, and urine analyzed via targeted LC-MS quantitative NAD+ metabolomics
- IRB ruled it not subject to human subjects research (informed self-administration)

### Clinical Trial (n=12)
- Randomized, double-blind, three-arm crossover pharmacokinetic study (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT02191462)
- 12 healthy subjects (6 male, 6 female), ages 30-55, BMI 18.5-29.9 kg/m^2
- Single oral doses of 100, 300, and 1,000 mg NR chloride with 7-day washout between doses
- Blood collected at pre-dose, 1, 2, 4, 8, and 24h; urine at pre-dose, 0-6h, 6-12h, 12-24h
- Safety monitoring: vitals, CBC, comprehensive metabolic panel at time zero and 24h
- Approved by Health Canada Natural Health Products Directorate and IRB Services (Ontario)

### Mouse Pharmacokinetic Study (n=128 total)
- 12-week-old male C57Bl/6J mice, chow diet
- Oral gavage of 185 mg/kg NR chloride or equimolar NA or Nam (n=3-4 per group per time point)
- 7 time points (0.25, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12h) before tissue harvest at ~2pm to control circadian effects
- Saline controls at every time point; liver harvested by freeze-clamping
- Additional IP experiment: 500 mg/kg NR chloride for 6 days in 6-8-week-old mice (heart + liver)
- Isotope tracing: double-labelled [13C, D1]-NR administered to 15 mice at 185 mg/kg

## Pharmacokinetics Data

### Human PBMC (Clinical Trial, n=12)
- **Time course:** NAD+ elevation not statistically significant at 1 or 2h; significant at 8h for NAAD and 24h for NAD+ across all doses
- **NAD+ peak:** NAD+ rose to ~2 and 20 uM higher at 300 and 1,000 mg versus 100 mg
- **Dose-response:** 100 mg produced ~4 +/- 2 uM NAD+ increase; 300 and 1,000 mg produced ~6.5 +/- 3.5 uM
- **NAAD peak (8h):** 0.56 +/- 0.26 uM (100 mg), 0.74 +/- 0.27 uM (300 mg), 1.24 +/- 0.51 uM (1,000 mg)
- **Nam:** No statistically significant increase at any dose -- NR does not simply function as a Nam precursor

### Pilot Human (n=1)
- PBMC NAD+ rose 2.7-fold from ~18.5 uM baseline, peaking at 7.7-8.1h
- NAAD rose from <20 nM to 0.91 uM (>=45-fold increase)
- Me2PY rose ~10-fold in plasma/urine; Me2PY peak at 8.4-fold in PBMCs
- NAD+ and NAAD remained elevated 24h after the last (7th) dose

### Mouse Liver
- NR: >4-fold NAD+ elevation, peak at 6h
- Nam: ~50% greater NAD+ AUC than NA, peak at 8h
- NA: doubled hepatic NAD+ (~1 to ~2 mM), fastest kinetics (peak at 2h)
- NR produced the greatest elevation of NMN, NADP+, Nam, NAMN, NAAD, and ADPR

## NAD+ Elevation Magnitude and Duration

| Dose | NAD+ AUC increase | NAAD AUC increase | Time to peak |
|------|-------------------|--------------------|--------------|
| 100 mg (human) | Not reported separately | Significant at 8h | ~8h (NAAD) |
| 300 mg (human) | ~6.5 uM increase | Significant | ~8h (NAAD) |
| 1,000 mg (human) | ~6.5 uM increase (~90% AUC) | ~2,900% AUC | ~8h (NAAD) |
| 185 mg/kg (mouse, NR) | >4-fold hepatic NAD+ | ~10% of NAD+ peak | 6h (NAD+) |

- In the clinical trial, NAD+ was significantly elevated at 24h across all doses (P=0.0267 at 1000 mg)
- NAAD was significantly elevated at 4h, 8h, and 24h at the 1,000 mg dose (all P<0.001)

## NAAD as Biomarker Discovery

- **Key finding:** Nicotinic acid adenine dinucleotide (NAAD) was not expected to be on the pathway from NR to NAD+; it was thought to derive only from NA and tryptophan via the Preiss-Handler pathway
- NAAD rose from undetectable (<20 nM) in human PBMCs before supplementation
- In mice, NAAD was elevated ~100-fold in both heart and liver after 6 days of IP NR
- NAAD was the most sensitive biomarker: ~2,900% AUC increase vs ~90% for NAD+ at 1,000 mg
- Unlike MeNam/Me2PY (waste products that can form without NAD+ synthesis), NAAD is a functional NAD+ precursor, making it a more meaningful biomarker
- Isotope tracing with double-labelled NR confirmed NAAD is a direct product of NR utilization (45% single+double labelled at 2h; 8% double-labelled)
- Proposed mechanism: a deamidating activity at high NAD+ concentrations converts NAD+ to NAAD; alternatively, NMN deamidation to NAMN produces NAAD

## Comparison with Nicotinic Acid and Nicotinamide

- **NR > Nam > NA** for hepatic NAD+ AUC in mice at equimolar oral doses
- **NR >> Nam for ADPR:** NR drove ~3x more ADPR accumulation than Nam, indicating greater sirtuin/NAD+-consuming activity
- Nam showed a paradox: 50% more NAD+ AUC than NA but 450% deficit in ADPR versus NA -- consistent with Nam's known sirtuin inhibition at high doses
- NA produced fastest kinetics (NAD+ peak at 2h) but least total NAD+ and lowest NAAD
- NR is uniquely orally bioavailable through the NRK pathway, not dependent on conversion to Nam first (no hepatic Nam peak at 15 min from NR, unlike Nam gavage)
- Each vitamin produces a temporally distinct hepatic metabolomic fingerprint

## Key Mechanism Insights

1. **NR is directly utilized, not merely a Nam prodrug:** No Nam peak at early time points after NR gavage; distinct pharmacokinetics
2. **NAAD forms from NR via NAD+ deamidation:** Isotope tracing proves NR carbon/nitrogen atoms appear in NAAD; this was unexpected as NAAD was only thought to derive from NA
3. **NAD+ pool is highly dynamic:** 54% of hepatic NAD+ turned over within 2h of NR gavage before steady-state NAD+ even rose
4. **NRK pathway limits NR/NMN utilization:** Confirmed that extracellular NMN and NR both depend on NRK activity
5. **NAAD as reserve metabolite:** Unlike waste products, NAAD contributes to NAD+ over time via NAD+ synthetase
6. **Potential NAADP connection:** Authors speculate the deamidating activity producing NAAD could be related to the long-sought enzyme forming intracellular NAADP

## Safety

- No serious adverse events in 36 person-days of observation
- No dose-dependent adverse events
- Two subjects self-reported flushing at 300 mg but not 100 mg or 1,000 mg (possibly not drug-related given the non-monotonic dose)
- Two subjects self-reported feeling hot at 1,000 mg
- No sex differences in pharmacokinetic response

## Limitations

1. **Pilot study n=1:** The dramatic 2.7-fold NAD+ increase in the pilot subject was not replicated at that magnitude in the n=12 trial; individual variation was substantial
2. **Single-dose design:** The clinical trial used single doses, not repeated daily dosing; steady-state effects of chronic NR supplementation were not assessed
3. **Blood measures only (human):** Tissue-level NAD+ changes in humans could not be measured; PBMC data may not reflect liver, muscle, or brain pharmacokinetics
4. **Small sample size (n=12):** The clinical trial was powered to detect 2-fold NAD+ differences; smaller effects may not be captured
5. **Conflicts of interest:** Multiple authors (Jaksch, Dellinger, Brenner, Migaud) have financial relationships with ChromaDex (NR supplier and study sponsor); Brenner is CSO of ProHealthspan which sells NR supplements
6. **No placebo control in clinical trial:** The crossover design with baseline subtraction partially addresses this, but there was no explicit placebo arm
7. **Mouse doses higher than human equivalent:** 185 mg/kg in mice corresponds to the 15 mg/kg human dose, but direct translation of tissue-level findings to humans requires caution
8. **NAAD mechanism unresolved:** The biochemical basis for the proposed NAD+ deamidation to NAAD remains unknown

## Claims to Verify

- **"2.7-fold" NAD+ increase (pilot):** This comes from a single individual and was not replicated at this magnitude in the n=12 trial. The n=12 data show a ~90% AUC increase (~1.9x), substantially less than the pilot's 2.7x.
- **"128 mice" total:** The paper describes multiple mouse experiments. The gavage study used 3-4 mice per group x 7 time points x 4 conditions (NR, NA, Nam, saline) plus saline timecourse = ~91-112 mice, plus the IP experiment and isotope tracing. The 128 figure appears reasonable but is not explicitly tabulated.
- **"NAAD rise ~2,900%":** This is the AUC increase at 1,000 mg. The calculation involves baseline subtraction from near-zero, which can amplify percentage changes. The absolute increase (~1 uM from undetectable) is modest but clearly real.

## Relevance to Research Question

This is a foundational source for understanding NR pharmacokinetics and bioavailability in humans. It provides the first controlled clinical evidence that oral NR reaches systemic circulation, elevates blood NAD+ in a dose-dependent manner, and is safe at single doses up to 1,000 mg. The NAAD biomarker discovery is particularly relevant for clinical trial design and compliance monitoring. The mouse data provide mechanistic context showing NR's superiority over conventional niacin forms.
