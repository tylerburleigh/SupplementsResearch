# src-033: Martens et al. (2018) - Chronic NR supplementation in healthy older adults

**Citation:** Martens CR, Denman BA, Mazzo MR, Armstrong ML, Reisdorph N, McQueen MB, Chonchol M, Seals DR. Chronic nicotinamide riboside supplementation is well-tolerated and elevates NAD+ in healthy middle-aged and older adults. *Nature Communications* 9:1286 (2018). DOI: 10.1038/s41467-018-03421-7

**Quality:** ok (73K chars, 979 sentences, peer-reviewed, 516 citations)

---

## Core Findings

This is the first randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover clinical trial of chronic NR supplementation (1000 mg/day for 6 weeks) in healthy middle-aged and older adults (55-79 years). The study demonstrated that NR effectively elevates blood cellular NAD+ by approximately 60% and is well tolerated. Exploratory analyses suggested potential benefits for reducing systolic blood pressure and aortic stiffness, particularly in individuals with elevated baseline blood pressure, but these findings did not survive multiple-comparison correction and require confirmation in larger trials.

## Study Design and Methodology

- **Design:** 2 x 6-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial
- **Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02921659; conducted March 2015 - September 2016
- **Participants:** 60 consented, 30 randomized (15 per group), 24 completed (12 per group)
  - Age: 55-79 years (mean 65 +/- 7)
  - Sex: 11M/13F among completers
  - BMI: 24 +/- 4 kg/m^2 (lean, healthy)
  - All had age-related vascular endothelial dysfunction (FMD < 6%)
  - Free of clinical diseases, normal blood chemistries
- **Intervention:** Nicotinamide riboside chloride (NIAGEN) 500 mg twice daily (1000 mg/day total) vs placebo capsules, each for 6 weeks with random order assignment
- **Randomization:** Group A = placebo then NR; Group B = NR then placebo
- **Primary outcome:** NAD+ and related metabolites in PBMCs (assessed by LC-MS)
- **Secondary/exploratory outcomes:** Blood pressure, aortic stiffness (carotid-femoral PWV), carotid artery compliance, flow-mediated dilation, metabolic function, exercise capacity, motor function
- **Statistical approach:** One-tailed paired t-tests; primary outcomes at alpha = 0.05; secondary outcomes with Bonferroni-adjusted alpha = 0.006 for 9 hypothesis families
- **Sample size justification:** Powered to detect 50% increase in NAD+ (effect size = 0.7, power = 0.8, alpha = 0.05), requiring 19 completers; 60 consented to account for 20% dropout and 40% screen failure

## NAD+ Elevation Data

- **NAD+:** Increased ~60% vs placebo (median: 7.7 to 12.2 pmol/mg protein; p = 0.048, unadjusted; n = 21 with metabolite data)
- **NAAD:** Increased nearly 5-fold vs placebo (p = 0.018), confirming NAAD as a sensitive biomarker of NR utilization
- **NADP+:** Mean increase but not statistically significant (p = 0.267)
- **NaM (nicotinamide):** Elevated mean but not significant (p = 0.065); would indicate increased NAD+-consuming enzyme activity
- **NMN:** ~1.5-fold increase, not significant (p = 0.179)
- **ATP:** Mean increase (1592 to 2205 pmol/mg protein; p = 0.032), but did not survive Bonferroni correction (adjusted threshold p < 0.006)
- **NR:** Undetectable in PBMCs despite optimized recovery methods
- **Dose-response relation:** Magnitude of NAD+ increase was negatively correlated with baseline NAD+ (R = -0.49, R^2 = 0.25), suggesting greater response in those with lower baseline levels

## Clinical Outcomes

### Blood Pressure
- Overall: SBP decreased 3.9 mmHg (p = 0.048 unadjusted), DBP decreased 2.0 mmHg (p = 0.030 unadjusted) -- neither significant after Bonferroni correction (alpha = 0.006)
- **Subgroup analysis (post-hoc, exploratory):** In participants with elevated/stage I hypertension (SBP 120-139 mmHg, n = 13), SBP was ~9 mmHg lower after NR vs placebo; no change in normal SBP group (n = 11)
- Authors note a 10 mmHg SBP reduction is associated with 25% decrease in CV events per SPRINT trial

### Arterial Stiffness
- Carotid-femoral PWV: trend toward reduction (mean change = -41.5 cm/s; p = 0.033 unadjusted), not significant after correction
- Subgroup: larger reductions in individuals with higher baseline BP
- Carotid artery compliance: no effect (p = 0.386)
- Brachial artery FMD: no effect (p = 0.429)

### Metabolic Function
- No effect on body mass, BMI, body fat, fasting glucose, insulin sensitivity (HOMA), energy expenditure, RER, or physical activity

### Exercise Capacity and Motor Function
- No effect on VO2 max, treadmill time to exhaustion, submaximal exercise performance, or motor function tests

## Safety and Adverse Events

- **Adherence:** >95% pill consumption in all subjects
- **Serious adverse events:** None
- **Treatment-emergent AEs:** 14 events reported by 7/30 subjects, all mild
  - During NR: nausea (1), skin rash (1), flushing (1), leg cramps (1), increased bruising (1)
  - During placebo: headache (4), skin rash (1), flushing (2), fainting (1), drowsiness (1)
- **Dropouts due to side effects:** 2 subjects, both during placebo phase (headache, skin rash); 0 dropouts during NR phase
- **Clinical labs:** No meaningful differences between conditions for hematology, renal/liver function, or lipid profiles; all values remained within normal reference ranges
- **Flushing:** 3 subjects reported flushing; 2 were on placebo, suggesting NR was not associated with flushing

## Key Limitations

1. **Small sample size:** Only 24 completers (21 with metabolite data); insufficient to establish broader safety profile
2. **Exploratory cardiovascular outcomes:** BP and PWV findings were not statistically significant after Bonferroni correction; subgroup analyses were post-hoc with no statistical inference
3. **Short duration:** Only 6 weeks per treatment phase
4. **Healthy population:** Lean, healthy middle-aged/older adults without metabolic dysfunction -- may have limited ability to detect improvements in outcomes near ceiling
5. **No formal washout period:** Though crossover analysis showed no carryover effect
6. **Surrogate NAD+ measurement:** PBMC NAD+ rather than tissue-specific NAD+ (brain, muscle, vasculature)
7. **One-tailed tests:** All hypotheses tested directionally, which limits interpretation of null/non-significant findings
8. **Dose above label recommendation:** 1000 mg/day exceeds NIAGEN label dose; authors note this should be considered investigational

## Claims to Verify

- **NAD+ increase of ~60%:** The median NAD+ values (7.7 to 12.2 pmol/mg protein) represent approximately a 59% increase, which is consistent with the stated ~60% claim. Verified.
- **NAAD fivefold increase:** Median went from 0.0 to 0.0 (both at detection limit), so the "nearly fivefold" claim appears to be based on mean data not directly shown in the table. The range data (placebo: 0 to -2.3; NR: 0 to -8.7) and the p-value (0.018) support an increase, but the fivefold claim may be from graphical/mean data. Moderate confidence.
- **SBP decrease of ~9 mmHg in hypertensive subgroup:** Stated in text (line 408-409) as post-hoc analysis without formal statistical testing; plausible but not verified with reported statistics.

## Relevance

This is a landmark first-in-human chronic NR supplementation trial. It directly addresses the core research questions about NR efficacy for elevating NAD+, safety/tolerability, and potential cardiovascular benefits. The NAD+ elevation data is the most robust finding. The blood pressure and arterial stiffness signals, while not statistically definitive, generated the hypotheses that have driven subsequent larger trials. The null findings for metabolic and exercise outcomes in healthy adults are also informative for scoping NR's effects.
