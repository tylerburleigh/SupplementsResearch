# Source src-026: Conze, Brenner & Kruger (2019) - Safety and Metabolism of NIAGEN NR

**Citation:** Conze D, Brenner C, Kruger CL. Scientific Reports 9:9772 (2019). DOI: 10.1038/s41598-019-46120-z
**Venue:** Scientific Reports (Nature) | **Citations:** 179 | **Quality:** ok | **Type:** RCT

## Core Findings

This 8-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 140 overweight adults (40-60 years, BMI 25-30) demonstrated that daily oral NR (100, 300, 1000 mg) dose-dependently increased whole blood NAD+ levels with an excellent safety profile. NR produced no flushing, no significant adverse events vs. placebo, no elevation of LDL cholesterol, and no disturbance of one-carbon metabolism (homocysteine). These data support a tolerable upper intake level for NR based on human data.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel-group trial
- **Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT0271593
- **Population:** 140 healthy overweight adults (BMI 25-30.1), age 40-60, predominantly Western European White (~80%)
- **Groups:** 35 per group -- placebo, 100 mg NR/day, 300 mg NR/day, 1000 mg NR/day
- **Duration:** 2-week run-in + 8-week supplementation (56 days total intervention)
- **Dosing:** Once daily after breakfast (4 capsules, using 100 mg and 250 mg NR chloride capsules with placebo fillers)
- **Compliance:** 98% mean compliance across all groups (capsule count)
- **Attrition:** 7/140 (5%) dropped out -- 1 placebo (nausea), 1 non-compliance, 4 withdrew consent, 1 lost to follow-up
- **Dietary controls:** Participants restricted high-tryptophan and vitamin B3 foods throughout
- **Study site:** KGK Science Inc., London, Ontario, Canada; GCP-compliant
- **Statistical approach:** ANCOVA with baseline covariate, Tukey-Kramer post hoc, modified ITT, LOCF imputation, significance at p < 0.05

## NAD+ Dose-Response Data

Whole blood NAD+ increases from baseline:

| Dose | Day 14 increase | Day 56 increase |
|------|----------------|-----------------|
| 100 mg | 22% +/- 9% | 10% +/- 4% |
| 300 mg | 51% +/- 7% | 48% +/- 8% |
| 1000 mg | 142% +/- 14% | 139% +/- 19% |

- 300 and 1000 mg groups showed significant (p < 0.05) NAD+ increases vs. baseline and placebo within 7 days
- 100 mg group reached significance vs. baseline at day 14 but was similar to placebo at most time points
- Placebo group showed small but significant NAD+ decreases over 56 days
- Day 56 effect sizes: dose-dependent increase to 1.74 (level) and 1.98 (rate of change)
- Plasma NAM significantly increased in 1000 mg group vs. placebo (effect size 1.21)
- Plasma MeNAM, urinary MeNAM, and urinary Me2PY all significantly and dose-dependently increased in 300 and 1000 mg groups vs. placebo

## Safety Outcomes (Primary Focus)

**Adverse events (95 total AEs in 61 participants):**
- No serious adverse events
- No reports of flushing at any dose
- AE distribution: Placebo 20 AEs (17 participants, 48.6%), 100 mg 26 AEs (16 participants, 45.7%), 300 mg 27 AEs (14 participants, 40.0%), 1000 mg 22 AEs (14 participants, 40.0%)
- No dose-dependent pattern in AE incidence or severity
- Most AEs were mild and deemed unlikely or not related to study product
- Possibly-related AEs: 100 mg (leg pain, high blood pressure), 300 mg (nausea, muscle pain), 1000 mg (sore back, muscle soreness, nausea) -- all mild
- All AEs resolved by end-of-study

**Vital signs:** No between-group differences in systolic/diastolic blood pressure, heart rate, or weight.

**Hematology:** Some statistically significant within-group changes (decreases in WBC counts, neutrophils, lymphocytes across groups; increases in MCV, MCH, RDW in 1000 mg group only) but all within normal clinical reference ranges, not dose-dependent, and deemed not clinically meaningful.

**Clinical chemistry:** No statistically significant between-group differences in any parameter including sodium, potassium, chloride, creatinine, eGFR, bilirubin, BUN, AST, ALT, GGT.

## Metabolism Findings

- NR is metabolized to NAD+ through NRK1/NRK2 pathway, distinct from NA and NAM pathways
- NAD+ consuming enzymes (sirtuins, PARPs, cyclic ADPribose synthases) hydrolyze NAD+ to NAM
- NAM is methylated to MeNAM via NNMT using SAM, producing SAH then homocysteine
- Plasma and urinary MeNAM and Me2PY serve as biomarkers of increased NAD+ metabolism
- All metabolites showed dose-dependent increases consistent with increased NAD+ turnover

## One-Carbon Metabolism and LDL Cholesterol

**Homocysteine (HCY):**
- Post hoc analysis showed NR had no effect on plasma HCY levels at any dose vs. baseline or placebo
- This is notable because single 300 mg oral doses of NA and NAM have been reported to increase plasma HCY
- On a molar basis, 300 mg NAM (MW 122 Da) = 716 mg NR Cl (MW 291 Da); 1000 mg NR showed no HCY elevation
- This supports that NR does not dysregulate 1-carbon metabolism at daily doses up to 1000 mg for 8 weeks

**LDL Cholesterol:**
- No statistically significant differences in LDL-C, HDL-C, total cholesterol, or triglycerides between NR and placebo groups
- LDL-C changes from baseline: Placebo +0.04, 100 mg -0.06, 300 mg +0.02, 1000 mg -0.11 mmol/L
- Authors contrast with Dellinger et al. (2017) who found LDL-C elevation with NR + pterostilbene combination, attributing the effect to pterostilbene alone

**Exploratory endpoints:** No significant differences in resting energy expenditure, branched-chain amino acids, or hsCRP.

## Limitations

1. Conducted in predominantly white, middle-aged overweight adults -- limited generalizability
2. Dietary restriction of tryptophan and vitamin B3 may not reflect real-world supplementation conditions
3. Homocysteine analysis was post hoc (not pre-specified primary/secondary endpoint)
4. Industry-funded: ChromaDex funded the study; two authors are ChromaDex employees; Brenner is inventor of licensed IP and serves as ChromaDex's chief scientific adviser
5. 8-week duration may not capture longer-term effects

## Relevance

This is a key safety and dose-finding study for NR. It provides the most comprehensive dose-response data for oral NR (3 doses, 8-week duration) and directly addresses two safety concerns previously raised: LDL-C elevation and homocysteine dysregulation. The finding that NR alone does not elevate LDL-C clarifies that the LDL-C increase seen in the Dellinger et al. NR+pterostilbene trial was attributable to pterostilbene. The absence of HCY elevation differentiates NR from NAM and NA at comparable or higher molar doses. The study supports the establishment of a tolerable upper intake level for NR equal to or greater than NAM's UL of 900 mg/day.
