## Core Findings

1. **Vitamin K is entirely absent from this guideline.** The Endocrine Society's comprehensive 2019 guideline for osteoporosis pharmacological management in postmenopausal women makes no mention of vitamin K in any form (K1, K2, menaquinone, MK-4, MK-7) -- not as a recommended therapy, not as an adjunct, and not in any discussion of evidence. This is a significant negative finding for the research question about whether clinical guidelines recommend vitamin K supplementation.

2. **Calcium and vitamin D are the only recommended nutritional supplements.** Recommendations 8.1 and 8.2 address calcium and vitamin D specifically. Rec 8.1 suggests calcium and vitamin D as adjuncts to osteoporosis therapies (Grade 2/conditional). Rec 8.1 recommends daily calcium and vitamin D supplementation to prevent hip fractures in women who cannot tolerate other therapies (Grade 1/strong).

3. **The evidence base is extensive.** The systematic review supporting the guideline included 107 RCTs with 193,987 postmenopausal women (mean age 66, median follow-up 28 months). Despite this breadth of evidence review, vitamin K was not identified as having sufficient evidence for any recommendation.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **107 RCTs, 193,987 participants** formed the evidence base for the guideline's meta-analyses
- **Calcium + vitamin D**: 19% hip fracture reduction (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.71-0.93); no significant effect on vertebral fractures
- **Calcium alone**: 37% reduction in nonvertebral fractures (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.45-0.90); no significant effect on vertebral or hip fractures
- **Vitamin D alone**: 56% reduction in nonvertebral fractures (HR 0.44, 95% CI 0.22-0.88); no significant effect on vertebral or hip fractures
- **Recommended drug classes**: bisphosphonates, denosumab, teriparatide/abaloparatide, SERMs, HT, tibolone, calcitonin
- **52 national guidelines across 36 countries** were surveyed; none appear to include vitamin K recommendations
- Vitamin D threshold recommended: at least 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L) per European guidelines, or 30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L) per Endocrine Society

## Methodology

- Endocrine Society guideline using GRADE framework
- Two commissioned systematic reviews: (1) efficacy meta-analysis of RCTs, (2) qualitative review of patient values/preferences
- Both direct meta-analysis and network meta-analysis approaches used
- Recommendations classified by quality of evidence and strength of recommendation
- 5-member Writing Committee of content experts plus methodologist

## Limitations

- Guideline is specific to **postmenopausal women with osteoporosis** -- may not generalize to other populations (men, premenopausal women, general population)
- The absence of vitamin K could reflect insufficient evidence rather than evidence of no effect -- the guideline does not explain why vitamin K was excluded
- Published in 2019; may not reflect more recent vitamin K trial data
- The scope is pharmacological management; nutritional supplements beyond calcium/vitamin D may have been outside scope

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q4 (Arguments against supplementation / do clinical guidelines recommend vitamin K?):** STRONG evidence that a major Endocrine Society clinical practice guideline does NOT recommend vitamin K supplementation. This is one of the most authoritative guidelines on bone health and fracture prevention, and its complete omission of vitamin K is a powerful argument against routine supplementation. The guideline identifies 52 national guidelines in 36 countries, none of which appear to include vitamin K.

**Q1 (Hard endpoints in guidelines):** The guideline focuses exclusively on fracture endpoints (vertebral, hip, nonvertebral) as hard outcomes. Its calcium/vitamin D recommendations are backed by fracture reduction data from meta-analysis. The absence of vitamin K from this fracture-focused guideline indicates the evidence for vitamin K reducing hard fracture endpoints was not considered sufficient for recommendation by this body.
