# src-073: Atteia (2023) — Vitamin K2 + Selenium in Dyslipidemic Rabbits

**Citation:** Atteia, H.H. (2023). Co-supplementation of Vitamin K2 and Selenium Synergistically Improves Metabolic Status and Reduces Cardiovascular Risk Markers in Dyslipidemic Rabbits. *Biological Trace Element Research*, 201, 4758-4768. DOI: 10.1007/s12011-023-03569-4

**Quality note:** Only abstract available (paywall). No full text. Evidence drawn from abstract only.

## Core Findings

This study investigated the combined effect of vitamin K2 (menatetrenone) and selenium co-supplementation on metabolic profile and cardiovascular health markers in dyslipidemic rabbits. The combination treatment significantly improved multiple cardiovascular risk markers (lipid profile, inflammatory markers, aortic calcification, oxidative stress) and metabolic parameters (glucose, insulin sensitivity, body weight) compared to untreated dyslipidemic controls.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Animal experiment (rabbits)
- **Subjects:** 50 adult male rabbits, equally allocated into 5 groups (n=10/group)
  - Group 1: Control
  - Group 2: Dyslipidemic (0.5% cholesterol diet for 12 weeks)
  - Group 3: Dyslipidemic + Vitamin K2 (10 mg/kg bw daily, 8 weeks)
  - Group 4: Dyslipidemic + Selenium (1 mg/kg bw daily, 8 weeks)
  - Group 5: Dyslipidemic + Vitamin K2 + Selenium (both doses, 8 weeks)
- **Dyslipidemia induction:** 0.5% cholesterol in diet for 12 weeks
- **Treatment period:** 8 weeks of daily supplementation
- **Species:** Rabbit (not human) -- limits direct clinical translation

## Key Evidence

### CVD Markers (Q1)
- Co-supplementation lowered: atherogenic index (LDL/HDL), serum CRP, heart FABP, asymmetric dimethylarginine (ADMA)
- Reduced aortic: ox-LDL, lipid peroxidation, calcium content
- Increased: serum nitric oxide, aortic total antioxidants
- Lowered serum lipids: cholesterol, triglycerides, LDL; raised HDL
- Decreased blood pressure elevation

### Metabolic Effects
- Reduced body weight gain
- Lowered fasting glucose and insulin
- Enhanced insulin sensitivity

### Safety (Q4)
- No adverse effects mentioned in abstract
- The abstract describes the co-supplementation as beneficial; no safety concerns noted
- The combination appeared well-tolerated over 8 weeks in this rabbit model

## Limitations

1. **Animal study** -- rabbit model of diet-induced dyslipidemia; cannot directly extrapolate dosing or effects to humans
2. **Abstract only** -- full text behind paywall; no access to detailed methods, statistical values, individual group comparisons, or safety data tables
3. **No quantitative results reported** in abstract (no p-values, effect sizes, or confidence intervals)
4. **Doses are pharmacological** (10 mg/kg vitamin K2; 1 mg/kg selenium) -- much higher than typical human supplementation
5. **Single author study** with 3 citations as of metadata fetch
6. **Synergy claim in title** but abstract does not explicitly present statistical interaction testing between K2 and selenium groups
7. Cannot assess whether K2 alone vs. selenium alone vs. combination showed differential effects without full text

## Relevance

- **Q1 (CVD markers):** Moderate relevance. Shows K2 + selenium reduces multiple CVD risk markers in a rabbit model, including aortic calcification, lipid peroxidation, inflammatory markers, and atherogenic index. However, this is an animal study with no isolated K2-only vs. combination comparison reported in the abstract.
- **Q4 (Safety):** Limited relevance. No adverse effects noted, but absence of full safety data prevents thorough assessment. The pharmacological doses used are far above human supplement doses.

## Claims to Verify

- The abstract states "synergistically" in the title but does not present interaction statistics in the abstract text. The synergy claim requires full-text verification.
- Specific quantitative values for all outcomes (lipid levels, CRP, blood pressure, glucose, etc.) are unavailable from the abstract.
- Whether the K2-only group (Group 3) showed significant improvements versus dyslipidemic control cannot be determined from the abstract.
