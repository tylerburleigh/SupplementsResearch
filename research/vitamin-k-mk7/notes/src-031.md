# src-031: Ma et al. (2022) - VK2 for Postmenopausal Osteoporosis Meta-Analysis

## Core Findings

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (6,425 postmenopausal women) found that VK2 significantly improved lumbar spine BMD (MD = 1.02, P = 0.006) but not hip, femoral neck, or forearm BMD. Fracture reduction was NOT significant in the primary analysis (6 RCTs, RR = 0.56, P = 0.10); significance only emerged after post-hoc removal of the largest trial (Inoue et al., n = 4,378), which the authors themselves caution may introduce selection bias. VK2 consistently reduced surrogate markers (uc-OC and uc-OC/cOC ratio, P < 0.0001) with no increase in adverse events.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **Lumbar spine BMD**: MD = 1.02, 95% CI 0.30-1.75, P = 0.006 (10 studies)
- **Hip BMD**: No significant difference (P = 0.79, 4 studies)
- **Femoral neck BMD**: No significant difference (P = 0.24, 5 studies)
- **Forearm BMD**: No significant difference overall (P = 0.21); VK2 alone showed benefit (P = 0.03)
- **Fracture incidence (primary analysis)**: RR = 0.56, 95% CI 0.28-1.11, P = 0.10 (6 studies) -- NOT significant
- **Fracture incidence (after removing Inoue)**: RR = 0.38, 95% CI 0.20-0.76, P = 0.006, I2 = 0% -- but removal is questionable
- **uc-OC reduction**: MD = -39.52, P < 0.0001 (6 studies)
- **uc-OC/cOC ratio**: MD = -49.41, P < 0.00001 (5 studies)
- **cOC**: No significant change (P = 0.24)
- **Adverse events**: RR = 1.03, 95% CI 0.87-1.21, P = 0.76 (5 studies) -- safe
- **Combination therapy**: VK2 + vitamin D/calcium/bisphosphonate showed better BMD-LS than VK2 alone (P = 0.03 vs P = 0.16)

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis per PRISMA guidelines
- **Databases**: PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library (through March 17, 2022)
- **Inclusion**: RCTs in postmenopausal women with VK2 (alone or combined) vs placebo or active control; outcomes including BMD, fractures, osteocalcin markers
- **Analysis**: RevMan 5.3 and STATA 16.0; random-effects model for high heterogeneity (I2 > 50%); fixed-effects for low heterogeneity
- **Subgroups**: VK2 combination therapy vs VK2 alone
- **Quality assessment**: Cochrane Risk of Bias tool
- **Publication bias**: Egger's test showed no significant bias for BMD-LS (P = 0.134)

## Included Study Characteristics

- 16 RCTs total, 6,425 subjects
- Majority conducted in Japan (10 of 16 studies)
- Three multicenter studies (Jiang et al., Emaus et al., Inoue et al.)
- VK2 forms: MK-4 (menatetrenone, typically 45 mg/day) in ~10 studies; MK-7 in ~3 studies (doses 180-375 mcg/day or 360 mg/day)
- Follow-up: 6-48 months
- Largest trial: Inoue et al. (n = 4,378, 48-month follow-up, MK-4 45 mg/day + calcium vs calcium)

## Limitations

1. **Fracture conclusion is fragile**: Only 6 of 16 studies reported fractures. Significance required removing the largest, highest-quality trial (Inoue et al.). The authors explicitly caution about this.
2. **Geographic bias**: Most studies were Japanese; generalizability to other populations is uncertain.
3. **Mixed VK2 forms**: Combined MK-4 and MK-7 studies; cannot separately analyze MK-7 effects on fractures with only ~3 MK-7 trials.
4. **Small sample sizes**: Many individual RCTs had <200 participants.
5. **Variable follow-up**: 6-48 months; no dose-duration subgroup analysis.
6. **Quality concerns**: Several studies had high selection and performance bias.
7. **Data extraction from graphs**: Some studies required extraction via Web Plot Digitizer, introducing measurement error.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: MK-7 effect on hard clinical endpoints vs surrogate markers
- **Directly relevant.** Demonstrates the surrogate-to-hard-endpoint gap: VK2 robustly improves surrogate markers (uc-OC, uc-OC/cOC ratio) but the effect on fractures is null in the primary analysis and only reaches significance through post-hoc exclusion of the largest trial. BMD improvements were limited to lumbar spine only.

### Q2: MK-7 vs MK-4 vs K1 clinical outcomes
- **Relevant as context.** This meta-analysis pooled MK-4 and MK-7 together, making it impossible to disentangle their individual effects. Most studies used MK-4 (menatetrenone 45 mg/day); only ~3 used MK-7. This highlights the paucity of MK-7-specific fracture data.

### Q3: Synergistic vs additive effects of K2 + D3
- **Moderately relevant.** Subgroup analysis showed VK2 combination therapy (with D3, calcium, or bisphosphonates) was superior to VK2 alone for lumbar spine BMD (P = 0.03 vs P = 0.16). However, the combination analysis lumped several co-interventions together; specific K2+D3 synergy cannot be isolated.

### Q4: Arguments against widespread MK-7 supplementation
- **Directly relevant.** The primary fracture analysis was null (RR = 0.56, P = 0.10). The positive finding required questionable post-hoc removal of the largest trial. Geographic bias (mostly Japan) limits generalizability. No significant BMD effects at hip or femoral neck -- the most clinically important fracture sites.

### Q5: Dose-response for MK-7 (45-360 mcg/day)
- **Partially relevant.** Included MK-7 doses of 180 mcg/day (Knapen), 375 mcg/day (Ronn), and 360 mg/day (Emaus -- likely a typo in the paper, probably 360 mcg/day). No dose-response analysis was performed.

### Q6: Meta-analysis disagreements and methodological drivers
- **Directly relevant.** This meta-analysis illustrates how sensitivity analysis decisions drive conclusions. The fracture finding flips from null (P = 0.10) to significant (P = 0.006) based on a single study exclusion, demonstrating the fragility of meta-analytic conclusions in this domain.

## Claims to Verify

1. **Emaus et al. dose listed as "360 mg/day MK-7"** (line 359) -- this is almost certainly 360 mcg/day, not mg. The unit appears to be an error in either the original paper or this meta-analysis.
2. **Total N = 6,425**: Inoue et al. alone contributes n = 4,378, meaning 68% of total participants come from a single trial. If that trial is removed, total N drops to ~2,047.
3. **Fracture RR = 0.96, P = 0.65 in abstract** vs **RR = 0.56, P = 0.10 in results section**: The abstract reports different numbers than the results. The abstract RR of 0.96 appears to be from a different analysis configuration. This inconsistency needs attention.
