# src-052: Chen et al. (2019) - Vitamin K and CVD/Mortality Meta-Analysis

**Citation:** Chen HG, Sheng LT, Zhang YB, et al. Association of vitamin K with cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *European Journal of Nutrition* 58:2191-2205, 2019. DOI: 10.1007/s00394-019-01998-3

**Quality assessment:** Abstract-only (paywall). The full text is behind a Springer subscription wall. Content includes the full abstract, references, and author information but no methods/results/discussion body text.

**Citation count:** 73

## Core Findings

This meta-analysis of 21 prospective studies (222,592 participants) found that higher dietary phylloquinone and menaquinone intake were both associated with lower coronary heart disease risk. Dietary menaquinone was significantly associated with lower total CHD (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.53-0.93, 2 studies). Elevated plasma dp-ucMGP (a vitamin K deficiency marker) was associated with substantially higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.84) and CVD mortality (HR 1.96). However, no significant association was found between dietary vitamin K and all-cause mortality, CVD mortality, or stroke.

## Key Evidence

### MK / Menaquinone-specific findings
- **Menaquinone and total CHD:** Pooled HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.53-0.93), I-squared=32.1%, based on only 2 studies
- **Phylloquinone and total CHD:** Pooled HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.84-0.99), I-squared=0%, based on 4 studies

### Vitamin K deficiency marker (dp-ucMGP)
- **All-cause mortality:** HR 1.84 (95% CI 1.48-2.28), I-squared=16.8%, 5 studies
- **CVD mortality:** HR 1.96 (95% CI 1.47-2.61), I-squared=0%, 2 studies

### Null findings
- No significant association between dietary vitamin K and all-cause mortality, CVD mortality, or stroke
- No significant association between circulating total osteocalcin and all-cause mortality or total CVD

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies
- **Databases:** PubMed and EMBASE, searched through January 2019
- **Inclusion:** Prospective studies reporting vitamin K (dietary intake or circulating concentration) and CVD events or mortality
- **Analysis:** Random-effects meta-analysis; multivariable-adjusted HRs comparing top vs bottom tertiles
- **Quality assessment:** Newcastle-Ottawa Scale
- **Outcomes assessed:** Total CVD, CVD mortality, total CHD, fatal CHD, nonfatal MI, stroke, all-cause mortality
- **Funding:** National Key Research and Development Program of China; Hubei Province Science Fund

## Limitations

1. **Only 2 studies for menaquinone-CHD association** -- this is a very small evidence base for the MK-specific finding
2. Abstract only available; full methods, subgroup analyses, and dose-response results not accessible
3. Observational design precludes causal inference (authors explicitly state this)
4. Heterogeneity in vitamin K assessment (dietary intake vs circulating biomarkers) across studies
5. Potential confounding by dietary patterns (e.g., menaquinone comes from fermented foods, cheese)

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (MK-7 effect on CVD events and mortality):** Provides moderate evidence that dietary menaquinone is associated with lower CHD risk (HR 0.70) based on only 2 studies. The dp-ucMGP findings indirectly support a role for vitamin K status in mortality. Does not isolate MK-7 specifically from broader menaquinone intake.
- **Q6 (Meta-analysis methodology and disagreements):** Relevant as a 2019 meta-analysis with broad scope (all vitamin K forms, multiple outcomes). The small number of studies for MK-specific outcomes (only 2 for menaquinone-CHD) highlights the limited evidence base. The concurrent Zhang et al. (2019) meta-analysis (cited in references) may offer methodological comparisons.

## Claims to Verify

- The menaquinone-CHD pooled HR of 0.70 is based on only 2 studies -- this should be caveated heavily in any synthesis
- Total participant count of 222,592 covers all 21 studies but individual outcome analyses have far fewer participants
- The abstract does not specify which MK subtypes (MK-4, MK-7, etc.) were included or whether MK-7 was analyzed separately
