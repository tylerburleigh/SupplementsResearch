# src-035: Three-year low-dose menaquinone-7 supplementation helps decrease bone loss in healthy postmenopausal women

**Citation:** Knapen MHJ, Drummen NE, Smit E, Vermeer C, Theuwissen E. (2013). Osteoporosis International, 24, 2499-2507. DOI: 10.1007/s00198-013-2325-6. Cited 146 times.

**Quality note:** Content is paywalled -- only abstract, references, and metadata are available. Full text not accessible. Evidence strength is therefore limited to what can be extracted from the abstract.

## Core Findings

A 3-year RCT of 180 mcg/day MK-7 in 244 healthy postmenopausal women found that MK-7 supplementation significantly decreased the age-related decline in bone mineral content (BMC) and bone mineral density (BMD) at the lumbar spine and femoral neck, but not at the total hip. Bone strength indices of the femoral neck were also favorably affected. MK-7 significantly decreased vertebral height loss in the lower thoracic region.

## Key Evidence

- **Dose:** 180 mcg/day MK-7 (a single dose arm, no dose-response range tested)
- **Duration:** 3 years
- **Population:** 244 healthy postmenopausal women (placebo-controlled, randomized)
- **Primary outcomes:** BMD (DXA) at lumbar spine, total hip, femoral neck; bone strength indices; vertebral fracture assessment by DXA
- **Biomarkers:** ucOC/cOC ratio as vitamin K status marker; measured at baseline and years 1, 2, 3
- **Significant results:** Lumbar spine BMD/BMC, femoral neck BMD/BMC, femoral neck bone strength, lower thoracic vertebral height preservation
- **Non-significant:** Total hip BMD
- **Fractures:** Vertebral fracture assessment was performed but abstract does not report actual fracture incidence data -- only vertebral height changes as a surrogate

## Methodology

- Randomized, placebo-controlled trial
- DXA for BMD measurement at three sites
- Femoral neck bone strength indices calculated from DXA data
- Vertebral fracture assessment by DXA (semiquantitative)
- ucOC and cOC measured; ucOC/cOC ratio as vitamin K status marker
- Measurements at baseline, 1, 2, and 3 years

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (Hard clinical endpoints vs surrogates):** This study is directly relevant. The primary outcomes are surrogate markers (BMD, bone strength indices, vertebral height) rather than hard clinical endpoints (fractures, CV events, mortality). Vertebral fracture assessment was conducted but the abstract does not report fracture incidence -- only vertebral height loss as a surrogate. This illustrates the common pattern of MK-7 studies relying on intermediate outcomes.

**Q2 (MK-7 vs MK-4 vs K1):** Indirectly relevant. The introduction references the authors' prior work showing high-dose K1 and MK-4 improved bone health, and justifies MK-7 use based on its longer half-life and greater potency compared to K1 and short-chain MK-4. No direct head-to-head comparison in this study.

**Q5 (Dose-response):** Partially relevant. Tests a single dose (180 mcg/day), which sits within the 45-360 mcg/day range of interest. Shows efficacy at this dose for BMD outcomes but does not establish a dose-response curve.

## Limitations

- Paywalled -- only abstract available; cannot verify quantitative claims in detail
- Single dose arm (180 mcg/day) -- no dose-response data
- Surrogate endpoints (BMD, bone strength) rather than clinical fractures
- Funded by Nattopharma ASA (vitamin K supplement manufacturer)
- Healthy postmenopausal women only -- may not generalize to other populations
- Abstract states "vertebral fracture assessment" was performed but does not report fracture counts

## Claims to Verify

- **n=244**: Stated in abstract methods. Cannot verify randomization ratio or dropout rate from available content.
- **Significant BMD effects**: The abstract claims significance at lumbar spine and femoral neck but not total hip. Cannot verify p-values or effect sizes from available content.
- **No conflicts of interest declared** despite Nattopharma ASA funding. This is noteworthy given the funder is a vitamin K supplement company.
