## Core Findings

This review article examines the interplay between calcium supplementation and vitamin K2 in cardiovascular health. It argues that while supplemental calcium benefits bone mineral density, elevated calcium supplementation may increase cardiovascular risk by accelerating calcium deposition in blood vessel walls. Vitamin K2 is presented as a protective factor because it activates matrix GLA protein (MGP), which inhibits vascular calcium deposits. The authors suggest that increased vitamin K2 intake could mitigate calcium-associated cardiovascular risks.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

1. **Calcium supplements and CVD risk:** Elevated calcium supplement consumption is linked to increased risk of heart disease via accelerated calcium deposits in blood vessel walls and soft tissues.
2. **Vitamin K2 mechanism:** Activates MGP (matrix GLA protein), which inhibits calcium deposits on arterial walls, thereby lowering vascular damage risk.
3. **Vitamin K2 dietary inadequacy:** Nearly absent in junk food and low even in healthy Western diets, suggesting widespread suboptimal intake.
4. **Deficiency consequence:** Inadequate vitamin K leads to insufficient MGP activation, impairing calcium removal from vessels and increasing calcification risk.
5. **Referenced meta-analyses:** Cites Bolland et al. (BMJ 2010, 2011) on calcium supplements and cardiovascular events, and Li et al. (Heart 2012) on calcium intake and MI/stroke risk.
