# src-030: Vitamin K2 -- A Neglected Player in Cardiovascular Health (Narrative Review)

## Bibliography
- **Authors:** Hariri E, Kassis N, Iskandar J-P, Schurgers LJ, Saad A, Abdelfattah O, Bansal A, Isogai T, Harb SC, Kapadia S
- **Year:** 2021
- **Venue:** Open Heart (BMJ), peer-reviewed, open access
- **DOI:** 10.1136/openhrt-2021-001715
- **Citations:** 54
- **Study Type:** Narrative review (not systematic)

## Core Findings

This comprehensive narrative review synthesizes the evidence linking vitamin K2 to cardiovascular health through activation of matrix Gla protein (MGP). The review argues that vitamin K2 supplementation (especially MK-7 at 180-360 mcg/day) has potential cardioprotective effects via inhibition of vascular calcification, improvement in arterial stiffness, and possible reduction in cardiovascular mortality. However, the authors acknowledge that most evidence comes from observational studies and small RCTs, and definitive causal inference for hard clinical endpoints is not yet established.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Vitamin K status and mortality (observational)
- **Rotterdam Study** (n=4,807): Upper tertials of vitamin K2 intake inversely associated with severe aortic calcification (OR=0.48), CV mortality (RR=0.43), and all-cause mortality (RR=0.74) over 7 years.
- **Meta-analysis** (Chen et al 2019, 5 studies): Elevated dp-ucMGP pooled HR for all-cause mortality = 1.84 (95% CI: 1.48-2.28); CV mortality HR = 1.96 (95% CI: 1.47-2.61).
- **Ischemic heart disease cohort** (n=894): High dp-ucMGP associated with 2.4x all-cause and 3.5x CV mortality at 5 years.
- **Aortic stenosis** (n=147): dp-ucMGP correlated with all-cause mortality (HR ~7) at 23 months.
- **Kidney transplant** (n=518, median 9.8 yr follow-up): dp-ucMGP associated with all-cause mortality (HR=3.10, 95% CI: 1.87-5.12).
- **Two Mendelian randomization studies** found causal association between genetically predicted dp-ucMGP and CV risk/mortality.

### Null / negative findings acknowledged
- **EPIC-NL Study** failed to show association between high dp-ucMGP and CVD or stroke at 11.5 years.
- **Health ABC Study** (n=1,061, >12 years): No association of vitamin K markers with incident CVD overall (except low phylloquinone associated with higher CVD risk in hypertension subset: HR=2.94).
- **Zwakenberg et al RCT** (360 mcg/day MK-7, 6 months, n=68 diabetics): No change in femoral arterial calcification (PET p=0.06, CT p=0.18).
- **De Vriese et al (VALKYRIE) RCT** (2000 mcg K2 3x/week, 18 months, n=132 hemodialysis): No differences in PWV, calcium scores, or clinical events.
- **Oikonomaki et al RCT** (200 mcg/day, 12 months, n=102 hemodialysis): No change in aortic calcification.
- **Bartstra et al post-hoc** (360 mcg/day MK-7, 6 months): No change in total arterial calcification.

### Interventional trial results (positive)
- **Knapen et al** (n=244 postmenopausal women, 180 mcg/day MK-7, 3 years): Decreased carotid-femoral PWV and Stiffness Index Beta (p<0.02).
- **Mansour et al** (n=60 renal transplant, 360 mcg/day MK-7, 8 weeks): Mean carotid-femoral PWV reduced 14.2% (p<0.001).
- **Braam et al** (n=181, vitamin D3 + K1, 3 years): Improved distensibility +8.8%, compliance +8.6%, pulse pressure -6.3% (all p<0.05).
- **Brandenburg et al** (n=99 AS patients, 2 mg/day K1, 1 year): Slowed aortic valve calcification (10% vs 22%, p=0.04).
- **Kurnatowska et al** (n=42 CKD, MK-7 90 mcg + D3, 9 months): Reduced carotid IMT (0.06 vs 0.14 mm, p=0.005) but nonsignificant CAC change.

### Dose-response
- Recommended MK-7 dose for cardiovascular benefit: 180-360 mcg/day.
- K1 studies use much higher doses (1-2 mg/day) due to predominant hepatic activity of K1.
- MK-7 shows dose-dependent reduction in dp-ucMGP.
- No documented toxicity for K1, MK-4, or MK-7. WHO has set no upper tolerance level.

### K2 vs K1 differences
- K1 concentrates in liver (coagulation); K2 distributes to extrahepatic tissues (bones, arteries).
- Long-chain menaquinones (MK-7) transported more efficiently to extrahepatic tissues.
- Both may be comparable in overall health effects, but K2 has higher vascular-specific activity.

## Methodology Notes
- Narrative review, not systematic: no PRISMA flow, no formal quality assessment of included studies.
- Two-part organization: (1) role of MGP in CV health, (2) CV benefits of K2 intake.
- Comprehensive tables of all published studies (Table 1: K effects on CVD markers; Table 2: dietary K intake and CV mortality).
- Acknowledges limitations: non-randomised design predominance, heterogeneity, variable dosages, small samples, short follow-up, inability to infer causality.
- Competing interest noted: Schurgers received institutional grant from NattoPharma (K2 supplement company).

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: MK-7 effect on hard clinical endpoints vs surrogate markers
**Highly relevant.** This review thoroughly catalogs the gap between surrogate markers (dp-ucMGP, PWV, calcification scores) and hard endpoints (mortality, CV events). Observational data show strong associations between vitamin K status and mortality, but interventional trials primarily use surrogate endpoints. The review acknowledges several null RCTs on calcification outcomes. The review is itself advocacy-oriented and tends to emphasize positive findings while noting null results in passing.

### Q2: MK-7 vs MK-4 vs K1 clinical outcomes, pharmacokinetics
**Relevant.** Discusses tissue distribution differences (K1 hepatic, K2 extrahepatic), MK-7 superior transport to extrahepatic tissues, dose differences (MK-7 at 180-360 mcg vs K1 at 1-2 mg). However, does not directly compare MK-7 vs MK-4 head-to-head. Notes MK-4 used at 45 mg/day (pharmacological dose) in Japanese studies. The review covers both K1 and K2 trials in its tables.

### Q3: Synergistic vs additive effects of vitamin K2 + D3
**Moderately relevant.** Reports several combination studies: Braam et al (D3+K1), Kurnatowska et al (MK-7 90 mcg + D3 10 mcg), Asemi et al (D+K+calcium). The combination of K2+D3 showed improved carotid IMT vs D3 alone (Kurnatowska), but does not systematically analyze additive vs synergistic mechanisms.

### Q4: Arguments against widespread MK-7 supplementation
**Relevant.** The review itself acknowledges limitations: most evidence is observational, RCTs are small and short, null trials exist. Notable null results: De Vriese VALKYRIE (large dose, no effect), Zwakenberg (no calcification benefit), Oikonomaki (no aortic calcification change). EPIC-NL and Health ABC cohorts failed to show associations with hard endpoints. However, the review frames these as limitations to be overcome rather than arguments against supplementation.

### Q5: Dose-response for MK-7 (45-360 mcg/day)
**Relevant.** Specifies 180-360 mcg/day as the effective dose range. Reports dose-dependent dp-ucMGP reduction. Studies reviewed span 90 mcg (Kurnatowska) to 360 mcg (Knapen, Mansour, Zwakenberg) to 2000 mcg (De Vriese). Lower doses (90 mcg) still showed some benefit in combination with D3.

### Q6: Meta-analysis disagreements and methodological drivers
**Moderately relevant.** Cites Chen et al 2019 meta-analysis showing significant associations, but also notes conflicting individual studies (EPIC-NL, Health ABC). Points to heterogeneity in confounder adjustment, population characteristics, and vitamin K assessment methods as sources of disagreement. Does not conduct its own meta-analysis.

## Limitations
1. Narrative review without systematic methodology -- selection bias in included studies likely.
2. Advocacy-oriented framing: the review tends toward recommending K2 supplementation while acknowledging evidence gaps, which may introduce confirmation bias.
3. Competing interest: Schurgers received institutional grant from NattoPharma.
4. Does not critically appraise the mechanistic gap between dp-ucMGP reduction and clinical outcomes.
5. The dose-response evidence is largely indirect (different studies using different doses) rather than formal dose-finding studies.
6. Null trials are noted but not systematically analyzed for why they failed (e.g., insufficient baseline deficiency, too-short follow-up, wrong population).

## Claims to Verify
- The "HR ~7" for dp-ucMGP and mortality in aortic stenosis patients (line ~507) is presented imprecisely -- the tilde suggests approximate value; the original study (Ueland et al 2010) should be checked for the exact figure.
- The recommended dose range of 180-360 mcg/day is cited to references 89 and 90 (Theuwissen et al 2012, Knapen et al 2015), but these are single-dose studies, not dose-ranging trials.
- The claim that 31% of the PREVEND subcohort (n=4,275) had functional vitamin K insufficiency (dp-ucMGP >500 pmol/L) comes from Riphagen et al 2017 and appears consistent.
