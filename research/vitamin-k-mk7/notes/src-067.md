# Source src-067: Zhou et al. (2022) — Meta-analysis of Vitamin K2 for Postmenopausal Osteoporosis

## Metadata
- **Title:** Efficacy and safety of vitamin K2 for postmenopausal women with osteoporosis at a long-term follow-up: meta-analysis and systematic review
- **Authors:** Zhou M, Han S, Zhang W, Wu D
- **Year:** 2022
- **Venue:** Journal of Bone and Mineral Metabolism, 40, 763-772
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s00774-022-01342-6
- **Citations:** 21
- **Quality note:** Paywall — only abstract available. No full-text methods or results accessible.

## Core Findings
A meta-analysis of 9 RCTs (n=6853) found vitamin K2 supplementation significantly increased lumbar BMD (WMD 2.17, 95% CI [1.59-2.76]) and forearm BMD (WMD 1.57, 95% CI [1.15-1.99]) in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis at long-term follow-up. Vitamin K2 also significantly reduced undercarboxylated osteocalcin and increased osteocalcin. Adverse reactions were slightly higher with K2 (RR 1.33, 95% CI [1.11-1.59]) but no serious adverse events were attributed to K2 supplementation.

## Key Evidence
- **9 RCTs, 6853 participants** (postmenopausal women with osteoporosis)
- **Lumbar BMD:** WMD 2.17, 95% CI [1.59-2.76] (significant)
- **Forearm BMD:** WMD 1.57, 95% CI [1.15-1.99] (significant)
- **uc-OC reduction:** WMD -0.96, 95% CI [-0.70 to 0.21] (significant)
- **OC increase:** WMD 26.52, 95% CI [17.06-35.98] (significant)
- **Adverse reactions:** RR 1.33, 95% CI [1.11-1.59], but no serious AEs related to K2
- Searched PubMed, Cochrane Library, EMBASE through Nov 16, 2021

## Methodology
- Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs
- Databases: PubMed, Cochrane Library, EMBASE
- Search through November 16, 2021
- Population: postmenopausal women with osteoporosis
- Intervention: vitamin K2 (form not specified in abstract — could include MK-4 and/or MK-7)
- Outcome measures: BMD percentage change (lumbar, forearm), uc-OC, OC, fracture incidence, adverse events

## Limitations
- **Full text behind paywall** — only abstract available; cannot verify specific K2 forms (MK-4 vs MK-7), dosing, study durations, or fracture outcomes
- Abstract mentions BMD and biomarkers but does not report fracture outcome data explicitly
- uc-OC confidence interval ([-0.70 to 0.21]) appears unusual for a WMD of -0.96 — potential reporting artifact
- Included studies are from Japan predominantly; generalizability uncertain
- K2 form(s) not specified in abstract — may mix MK-4 (menatetrenone) and MK-7 studies

## Relevance to Research Questions
- **Q1 (MK-7 effect on fractures and BMD):** Relevant for BMD outcomes in postmenopausal women. Meta-analysis shows significant BMD improvement at lumbar spine and forearm. However, abstract does not clearly report fracture outcomes despite the title mentioning fractures. Cannot determine what proportion of included studies used MK-7 vs MK-4 (menatetrenone).
- **Q2 (K2 form comparison):** Limited relevance. The abstract does not distinguish between MK-4 and MK-7; form-specific conclusions cannot be drawn without full text. The reference list includes both MK-4 studies (menatetrenone trials by Shiraki, Iwamoto, Inoue) and MK-7 studies (Knapen et al. 2013), suggesting the meta-analysis likely pooled both forms.

## Claims to Verify
- The uc-OC WMD confidence interval (WMD -0.96, 95% CI [-0.70 to 0.21]) appears inconsistent — if WMD is -0.96, the CI should typically bracket that value. This may be a transcription error in the abstract or a non-standard statistic.
- 6853 participants across 9 RCTs averages ~761 per study, which is large for nutrition RCTs. The largest studies likely contributed most participants (e.g., Inoue 2009 OF study, Tanaka 2017 JOIT-03).
- Fracture outcomes are mentioned in the study objective but not explicitly reported in the abstract results — unclear whether fracture data were available but non-significant, or not analyzed.
