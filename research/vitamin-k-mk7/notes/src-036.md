# src-036: Vitamin K + Bisphosphonate Additive Effect on Fracture Risk (Moore et al. 2023)

## Core Findings

This 18-month, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT of 105 post-menopausal women with osteoporosis on established bisphosphonate therapy found that neither vitamin K1 (1 mg/day) nor MK-4 (45 mg/day) significantly improved bone mineral density (BMD) at the lumbar spine, femoral neck, or total hip compared to placebo. Vitamin K1 showed modest but significant improvements in select hip geometry parameters (endocortical diameter, outer diameter, cross-sectional area) in per-protocol exploratory analyses. The study was not powered for fracture endpoints, though 12 fractures occurred across 11 patients (6 placebo, 4 K1, 2 MK-4).

## Study Design and Population

- **Design**: 18-month, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial with 3 arms
- **Population**: 105 ambulatory post-menopausal women (age 55-85, mean 68.7 years) with osteoporosis, sub-optimal vitamin K status (serum K1 <= 0.4 ug/L at pre-screen), already on oral bisphosphonate (alendronate or risedronate) for at least 12 months
- **Arms**: Vitamin K1 (1 mg/day, n=35), MK-4 (45 mg/day in 3 divided doses, n=35), Placebo (n=35)
- **Primary outcome**: Change in BMD at LS, FN, and TH at 18 months
- **Secondary outcomes**: Bone turnover markers (CTX, P1NP), hip structural analysis (HSA) parameters
- **Analysis**: ITT (n=91) and per-protocol (PP, n=84); ANCOVA with Bonferroni correction
- **Registration**: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01232647

## Key Evidence Points

### BMD (Primary Outcome) - Negative
- No significant differences in BMD at any site between either treatment arm and placebo in ITT or PP analyses
- TH BMD % change: K1 arm +0.71% vs placebo -0.23% (p=0.18) -- a non-significant positive trend
- BMI interacted significantly with FN BMD changes (ITT: p=0.012) and LS BMD (PP: p=0.007)

### Bone Turnover Markers - Negative
- No significant group differences in CTX or P1NP at any time point
- All arms showed gradual increases in CTX and P1NP over time (p<0.001), possibly due to declining bisphosphonate adherence

### Hip Geometry (Exploratory) - Modest Positive for K1
- Per-protocol analysis with covariate adjustment showed:
  - Intertrochanter endocortical diameter: placebo +1.5% vs K1 -1.02% (p=0.04)
  - Femoral shaft outer diameter: placebo +1.78% vs K1 +0.46% (p=0.04)
  - Femoral shaft CSA: placebo +1.47% vs K1 -1.02% (p=0.03)
- Authors suggest this indicates reduced endocortical widening/cortical thinning, potentially protective against atypical femoral fractures in long-term bisphosphonate users
- No significant effects of MK-4 on any HSA parameters

### Fractures (Not Powered)
- 12 fractures in 11 patients: placebo 6, K1 4, MK-4 2
- Study explicitly states it was not powered for fracture endpoints

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: MK-7 effect on hard clinical endpoints (fractures) vs surrogate markers
- **Directly relevant but uses K1 and MK-4, not MK-7**. Provides evidence that vitamin K supplementation as an add-on to bisphosphonates does not improve BMD (a surrogate marker) over 18 months. The modest hip geometry improvements are also surrogate measures. The fracture data is underpowered and non-significant. This supports the broader finding that vitamin K effects on surrogate bone markers do not translate clearly to hard endpoints.

### Q6: Meta-analysis disagreements
- The discussion explicitly addresses conflicting evidence: the Mott et al. 2019 meta-analysis found reduced clinical fractures (OR 0.72) but no BMD improvement; the large Japanese JOINT-03 trial found no benefit of MK-4 + risedronate over risedronate alone; smaller trials suggested synergy. This trial adds to the body of evidence showing null effects on BMD and bone turnover, with only exploratory geometry findings.
- Notes MK-4's poor bioavailability and short half-life compared to MK-7 as a potential explanation for null MK-4 results.

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size**: 35 per arm, limiting statistical power
2. **Short duration**: 18 months may be insufficient to detect BMD changes in patients on established bisphosphonates (already had ~3 years of treatment)
3. **Baseline vitamin K contamination**: 36 of 105 participants had serum K1 > 0.4 ug/L at study entry despite meeting criteria at pre-screen
4. **No MK-4 serum measurements**: Could not confirm bioavailability of the MK-4 preparation used
5. **HSA findings were exploratory**: Significant only in PP analysis with covariate adjustment, not in ITT
6. **Not powered for fractures**: Cannot draw conclusions about clinical fracture endpoints

## Claims to Verify

- Sample size of 105 randomized (35 per arm): Confirmed in CONSORT description and multiple places
