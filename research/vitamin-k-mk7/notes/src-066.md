# src-066: Should We Recommend Vitamin K2 Supplement to Prevent Coronary Artery Calcification for Patients Receiving Statins and/or Warfarin?

**Citation:** Zhang AJ, Ballantyne CM, Birnbaum Y. *Cardiovascular Drugs and Therapy*, 2025. DOI: 10.1007/s10557-024-07661-2. Cited by: 1.

**Type:** Editorial / opinion piece (not full-text available; abstract only from paywall landing page).

## Core Findings

This editorial argues that the observed increase in coronary artery calcification with long-term warfarin and statin use should not automatically prompt vitamin K2 supplementation. Both drugs increase coronary calcification but simultaneously reduce cardiovascular events, suggesting calcification may reflect plaque stabilization rather than worsening disease. Clinical evidence for K2 supplementation attenuating calcification in atherosclerosis patients is lacking, and the impact of such attenuation on clinical outcomes is unknown.

## Key Evidence

1. **Calcification paradox:** Long-term warfarin and statins both increase coronary calcification in animal models and clinical settings, yet both reduce cardiovascular events (warfarin reduces re-infarction; statins have extensive trial support). The authors propose this increase may mark plaque stabilization.

2. **Mechanism:** Warfarin inhibits VKORC1, blocking regeneration of reduced vitamin K1 and K2. K1 is hepatic (clotting factors); K2 circulates systemically with broader effects.

3. **Evidence gap for K2 supplementation:** Animal models show K2 can attenuate atherosclerosis and coronary calcification progression, but clinical studies in patients are lacking.

4. **Over-the-counter K2 use is increasing** despite absence of clinical outcome data, and several manuscripts recommend K2 for patients on long-term warfarin.

5. **Central question remains unanswered:** It is unclear whether attenuating coronary calcification in these patients has beneficial or detrimental effects on cardiovascular outcomes.

This editorial directly questions the rationale for K2 supplementation in warfarin/statin patients, arguing that the calcification-increase signal may be a marker of plaque stabilization and that clinical outcome data for K2 supplementation is absent.
