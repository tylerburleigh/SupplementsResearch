# src-061: Vitamin K-dependent Proteins in Bone and Cardiovascular Health (Review)

**Citation:** Wen L, Chen J, Duan L, Li S. *Molecular Medicine Reports* 18: 3-15, 2018. DOI: 10.3892/mmr.2018.8940
**Citations:** 36 | **Type:** Narrative Review | **Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

This review surveys 17 vitamin K-dependent proteins (VKDPs) and their roles in bone and cardiovascular health, focusing on osteocalcin (OC), matrix Gla protein (MGP), Gla-rich protein (GRP), periostin, and growth arrest-specific protein 6 (Gas6). The central thesis is that carboxylated VKDPs protect against vascular calcification and support bone mineralization, and vitamin K2 (particularly MK-7) is more effective than K1 for extrahepatic protein carboxylation. The authors propose a novel hypothesis that the bone-cardiovascular link may originate from shared embryonic development from mesoderm.

## Key Evidence

### MK-7 vs other K forms (Q2)
- MK-7 has 7-8x higher extrahepatic availability than vitamin K1 (refs 41, 42)
- MK-7 has a longer half-life (3 days) compared to MK-4 (ref 43, 44)
- MK-7 is the most hydrophobic menaquinone due to its longer isoprenoid chain, permitting transport by plasma lipoproteins and increasing extrahepatic availability
- Japan recommends MK-7 intake of 250-300 ug/day for osteoporosis prevention
- Daily MK-7 dose >100 ug/day promotes gamma-carboxylation of OC (ref 41)
- Long-term MK-7 intake maintains OC gamma-carboxylation and promotes bone metabolism

### Hard clinical endpoints vs surrogates (Q1)
- The evidence presented is entirely based on surrogate endpoints: dp-ucMGP levels, coronary artery calcium scores, OC carboxylation status, arterial calcification markers
- No RCT evidence for hard clinical endpoints (fracture reduction, MI, cardiovascular mortality) is presented in this review
- dp-ucMGP is proposed as a surrogate marker for vascular vitamin K status and vascular calcification
- High dp-ucMGP associated with peripheral artery calcification, aortic calcification in CKD, and CVD risk in type 2 diabetes
- >96% of individuals >70 years exhibit aortic and coronary artery calcification

### Arguments against supplementation / Safety (Q4)
- The review states "no serious side effects have been reported regarding vitamin K" (refs 123, 124) -- this is a weak safety claim, citing a systematic review and a toxicology study
- Reviews the paradox that calcium supplements may increase CVD risk while benefiting bone, implicitly arguing that vitamin K2 could redirect calcium to bone
- The review does not address potential harms of K2 supplementation, interactions with anticoagulants (warfarin), or contraindications in any detail
- VKDP antagonism: the authors speculate that OC and periostin may antagonize MGP, GRP, and Gas6 under certain calcification conditions -- implying supplementation effects are not straightforward

## Methodology

- Narrative review; no systematic search strategy described
- Covers in vitro, animal, and epidemiological evidence
- Primarily mechanistic focus on VKDP biology
- Proposes novel embryological hypothesis linking bone and cardiovascular systems

## Limitations

- Narrative review without systematic methodology -- susceptible to selective citation
- No original data; synthesizes primarily mechanistic and observational evidence
- The embryological hypothesis (shared mesoderm origin explaining bone-vascular calcification link) is speculative and acknowledged as such by authors
- Safety claim is based on absence of reported serious adverse effects, not rigorous safety data
- Does not address MK-7 vs K1/K2 in any direct comparative clinical trial context
- Many mechanistic claims based on in vitro or animal data

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Hard endpoints vs surrogates):** Moderate relevance. Illustrates that most vitamin K research relies on surrogate markers (dp-ucMGP, OC carboxylation, calcium scores). No hard endpoint data presented.
- **Q2 (MK-7 vs other K forms):** Strong relevance. Provides pharmacokinetic comparisons (half-life, bioavailability) and dosing information for MK-7.
- **Q4 (Arguments against supplementation):** Moderate relevance. Safety data is weak (absence-of-harm argument). The VKDP antagonism discussion suggests complexity that could be used to argue against blanket supplementation.

## Claims to Verify

- "MK-7 content has significantly higher levels compared with vitamin K1 (7-8 times higher)" -- verify from primary sources (refs 41, 42) whether this refers to serum levels, tissue levels, or specific measures
- "Japan's recommended intake of vitamin K2 in the form of MK-7 is 250-300 ug/day" -- verify this represents the official Japanese guideline (ref 39)
- ">96% [of those >70] exhibit aortic and coronary artery calcification" -- verify from ref 22 (Lahtinen et al., 2015) whether this is the correct interpretation
