Abstract 27 
Imaging mass spectrometry (IMS) has become an indispensable analytical tool for visualizing 28 
molecular distributions in biological tissues. However, the detection of many impor tant 29 
biomolecules, such as nicotinamide mononucleotide  (NMN) in tissue by IMS is still 30 
challenging due to their lower abundance. Although t he sensitivity of widely used IMS 31 
instruments, desorption electrospray ionization (DESI) utilizing  a time-of-flight (TOF) mass 32 
analyzer, is not sufficient to detect NMN in tissues, the recently developed DESI coupled with 33 
a triple-quadrupole (TQ) provides about ten times higher sensitivity than the DESI-Q-TOF. In 34 
this study, we first screened six insect species for NMN content using ultra-performance liquid-35 
chromatography–tandem mass  spectrometry (UPLC -MS/MS). Interestingly, Shelfordella 36 
lateralis showed significantly higher NMN content (15.5 mg/100  g of dry wei ght) than five 37 
other insect species used in this study. After screening, we tried to observe the distribution of 38 
NMN in S. lateralis using both DESI -Q-TOF and DESI -TQ, and only the DESI-TQ 39 
successfully detected the NMN in S. lateralis. DESI-TQ revealed prominent distributions of 40 
NMN in the abdomen of S. lateralis in selective reaction monitoring mode. To the best of our 41 
knowledge, this is the first report of NMN imaging in animal tissue applying IMS.  Our study 42 
will contribute to the extended application of IMS for NMN imaging in mammalian tissues in 43 
the future. Furthermore, NMN imaging may help researchers to study nicotinamide adenine 44 
dinucleotide (NAD+) metabolism in living organisms, which  has significant implications for 45 
medical research and diagnosis. 46 
Key words: IMS, NMN, DESI-TQ, DESI-Q-TOF, UPLC-MS/MS, Shelfordella lateralis  47

Introduction 48 
Imaging mass spectrometry (IMS) has been increasingly used to visualize biomolecules 49 
due to its high sensitivity and label-free nature. This technology has made it possible to reveal 50 
the identity and distribution of th e specific molecule and  the localization of the molecule in 51 
organs1). Nowadays, imaging with desorption electrospray ionization (DESI), in combination 52 
with quadrupole-time-of-flight (Q-TOF) mass spectrometry, has been gaining much popularity 53 
for visualizing molecular distribution on a sample surface under ambient conditions 2). DESI-54 
Q-TOF has shown the capability of visualizing spatial distributions of molecules with diverse 55 
chemical nature s, such as small molecules 3), free fatty acid s4–6), phospholipids 7), lipid 56 
biomarker8), active compounds9), and other metabolites10). One of the significant advantages of 57 
DESI-IMS is that it requires a minimal sample preparation than traditional mass spectrometry, 58 
such as matrix-assisted laser desorption ionization mas s spectrometry (MALDI -MS)11). 59 
Recently, DESI combined with triple quadrupoles (TQ) mass spectrometry has emerged and 60 
offers advantages over DESI-Q-TOF. The TQ mass analyzer consists of three quadrupole mass 61 
filters, while DESI-Q-TOF consists of a single quadrupole mass filter. These three quadrupole 62 
mass filters make DESI -TQ more specific for precursor-to-product ion transitions of the 63 
targeted compound analysis12). This allows DESI-TQ to exhibit higher specificity and excellent 64 
sensitivity in selective reaction monitoring (SRM) mode than other high-resolution mass 65 
analyzers13).  66 
Despite the significant improvements in the sample preparation and instrumentations of IMS, 67 
detecting and imaging of many biologically important molecules in tissue is still challenging. 68 
For example, nicotinamide mononucleotide  (NMN), a well-known vital precursor of 69 
nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD +), has been found difficult to be detected by IMS.  70 
The analytical detection of NMN is challenging due to its physicochemical characteristics14). 71 
NMN is a nucleotide containing a pyridine base. It is an acid - and water-soluble nucleotide 72 

4 
 
found in all living cells as a coenzyme. Increasing NAD+ levels improves insulin sensitivity, 73 
reverses mitochondrial dysfunction, and enhances lifespa n15). When taken orally, NMN is 74 
quickly absorbed and converted into NAD +16). NAD+ availability is essential for slowing the 75 
aging process 17). Oral nicotinamide supplementation within the daily acceptable upper 76 
threshold is an efficient strategy to raise NAD+ levels and influence blood lipid composition18). 77 
Thus, NMN has important applications in medicine and healthcare 19), but the chemical 78 
synthesis of NMN is complex20). Therefore, it is worth exploring a rich source of natural NMN 79 
and its imaging in medical research.  80 
Unlike other natural products, insects have received comparatively little attention in imaging 81 
studies as sources of modern medicines 21). Insects can provide proteins for human use,  and 82 
people in many parts of the world consume insects as a potential source of calories and 83 
protein22). In traditional folk medicine and Chinese medicine, the whole body extracts of 84 
several bees, wasps, flies, butterflies, moths, co ckroaches, beetles, and other insects act as 85 
antiviral, antibacterial, and anti-cancer agents21). While insects are consumed as food in various 86 
cultures around the world and are known to be rich in protein and nutrients, there is not 87 
adequate knowledge or research about their NMN content. 88 
Recently, our group has explored high NMN content in Cinnamomum verum J. Presl bark, a 89 
medicinal plant, using liquid chromatography mass spectrometry (LC -MS)23). Applying the 90 
same method, we first screened NMN content in six insect species in this study, namely , the 91 
Turkestan cockroach Shelfordella lateralis, the field cricket  Gryllus bimaculatus, the damp -92 
wood termite Hodotermopsis sjostedti, the black soldier fly  Hermetia illucens, the Japanese 93 
subterranean termite  Reticulitermes speratus , and the giant mealworm  Zophobas atratus . 94 
Finally, we employed both DESI-Q-TOF and DESI-TQ to observe the distribution of NMN in 95 
S. lateralis bodies, which showed the highest NMN content among the other species under this 96 
study.  97

Chemicals and reagents 99 
We purchased the standard β -nicotinamide mononucleotide (β -NMN) from Tokyo  100 
Chemical Industry Co. (Tokyo, Japan). LC/MS -grade formic acid (99%), acetonitrile (ACN), 101 
methanol, ethanol, and ultra-pure water were obtained from Fujifilm Wako Pure Chemical 102 
Corporation, Ltd. (Osaka, Japan). Ammonium format solution was purchased  from Kanto 103 
Chemical Co., INC. (Tokyo, Japan).  Sodium formate was obtained from Sigma -Aldrich Co. 104 
LLC (St. Louis, MO, USA) and, leucine enkephalin from Waters Corporation (Milford, MA, 105

Sample preparation for the quantification of NMN 107 
Two types of termite species (H. sjostedti, and R. speratus) were used in this study. H. 108 
sjostedti were collected initially from logs from brown -rotted Luchu pine wood ( Pinus 109 
luchuensis) in the forest of Amami Oshima Island in Japan. And the colonies of R. speratus 110 
were collected from logs from brown -rotted cedar wood (Cryptomeria japonica) in the forest 111 
in Kyoto, Japan. The termite colonies were maintained at the Laboratory of Insect Ecology, 112 
Kyoto University, at about 25 ºC under dark. The Individuals of H. sjostedti were randomly 113 
extracted from each of the six c olonies (colony code: KM022, KM024, KM025, KM103, 114 
KM109, TMK091). And the individuals of R. speratus were randomly extracted from each of 115 
the three colonies (colony code: MT616D, MT617E, and MT619G). We bought the cockroach 116 
S. lateralis from Rakuten Group, Inc., Tokyo, Japan, and the freeze-dried G. bimaculatus from 117 
Leaf Corporation, Gunma, Japan. The two other insect species, dried H. illucens, and dried Z. 118 
atratus, were purchased from GEX Corporation, Osaka, Japan, and Sanko -Tokai Co., Ltd., 119 
Osaka, Japan, respectively. 120

All the samples were kept in a −80 ºC freezer until freeze -dried treatment. The frozen insect 121

Standard sample preparation 127 
For standard sample preparation, an analytical balance (AUY220, SHIMADZU, Kyoto, 128 
Japan) was used to weight the NMN standards . Then, 25% ethanol was used to dissolve the 129 
NMN standard. The prepared NMN standard was then serially diluted to control solutions at 130 
concentrations of 500, 200, 100, 50, and 20 ng/mL for calibration curve preparation. 131 
 Insect extract preparation for UPLC-MS/MS 132 
For each sample, approximately 50 mg of powder was weighed three times. Then 25% 133 
ethanol was added (40 µL/ mg of the sample) and extracted for one minute on ice with a 134 
mechanical homogenizer (HK -1, AS ONE, Osaka, Japan) at 9000 rpm . The homogenized 135 
insect extracts were then centrifuged for 10 minutes at 3,000 rpm in 4 ºC. Then the supernatant 136 
was filtered through a 0.42 µm filter, and the cleared sample was transferred to LC-MS vials.  137 
UPLC-MS/MS analysis 138 
ACQUITY (UPLC H -Class PLUS, Waters ) coupled with a triple qua drupole mass 139 
spectrometer (Xevo TQ-XS, Waters) was used to analyze the insect extracts. An Intrada Amino 140 
Acid column (3 µm, 3.0 x100 mm, Imtakt)  was carried out for chromatographic separation , 141 
which was kept at 40 ºC throughout the measurement. The electrospray ionization (ESI) source 142 
was used on the mass spectrometer, and the analysis was performed in positive ion mode. The 143 
capillary voltage was set to 3.5 kV, the cone voltage to 22 V and the source temperature was 144

150 ºC. The desolvation temperature was fixed at 500 ºC and the desolvation gas flow rate was 145 
600 L/h. The cone and nebulizer gas flows were set to 150 L/h and 7.0 Bar, respectively. Mobile 146 
phase A consisted of 0.3% formic acid in ACN, while mobile phase B was acetonitrile/100 147 
mM ammonium formate (20:80, v/v). The flow rate used for elution was 0.6 mL/min. The 148 
following setup was used to perform the gradient elu tion procedure: 0 to 0.5 min, 20% B; 0.5 149 
to 2.5 min, 20% to 100% B; 2.5 to 4.5 min, 100% B; 4.5 to 4.6 min, 100% to 20% B; 4.6 to 7.0 150 
min, 20% B. The whole duration of the run time was 7 minutes. An aliquot of 5 µL of the 151 
prepared sample was injected into the system while the autosampler was kept at a constant 152 
temperature of 10 ºC. The SRM transition from m/z 335.1 to m/z 123.0 using a collision energy 153 
of 10.0 eV was used for NMN quantification.  154 
A MassLynx software (Waters, version 4.1) was used to acquire and analyze the UPLC -155 
MS/MS data, and, TargetLynx software (Waters, version 4.1) was used for the quantitative 156 
measurement. The statistical analysis was carried out using GraphPad Prism (version 8.42). 157 
Sample preparation for DESI-IMS analysis 158 
After collecting the samples, the whole body of the cockroach was embedded overnight 159 
by a super cryo -embedding medium in the middle of a cryo mold and frozen at -20 ºC. The 160 
frozen cockroach samples were then mounted on a sample holder usi ng the optimal cutting 161 
temperature (OCT) compound (Sakura Finetek Japan, Tokyo, Japan). The tissues were 162 
sectioned sagittally at 20 µm thickness at -20 ºC by a cryostat system (CM1950; Leica 163 
Biosystems, Wetzlar, Germany). Then the sections were mounted on glass slides (Matsunami, 164 
Osaka, Japan) and stored at -80 ºC. Before DESI-IMS analysis, the slices were air-dried.  165 
DESI-Q-TOF analysis 166 
DESI-IMS analysis was performed in positive ion mode using DESI source attached to 167 
a Q -TOF mass analyzer (Xevo G2 -XS Q-TOF, Waters ). Before the measurements, sodium 168

formate solution (500 µM) in 2 -propanol: water (90:10, v/v) was used to calibrate the mass 169 
spectra externally, and leucine enkephalin solution (500 µM) was used to set up the detector.  170 
A solvent pump (ACQUITY UPLC Binary Solvent Manager, Waters) was used to deliver the 171 
spray solvent (98:2 methanol/water, v/v) at a flow rate of 2 μL/min. A 2D sampling stage was 172 
equipped with a DESI source to scan the designated area with a scan rate of 200 µm/sec, and 173 
the pixel size was 100 µm × 100 µm.  The DESI source conditions were optimized using the 174 
following parameters: a nebulizing nitrogen gas pressure of 0.4 MPa, a capillary voltage of 4.0 175 
kV, a spray impact angle of 70 º, a sampling cone of 50 , and an inlet temperature of 120 ºC . 176 
The setup for mass resolution, mass window, and collision energy were at 20000, 0.02 Da, and 177 
4.00 V. The mass spectra were collected  using a mass range of m/z 100 to 1000 of the 1000 178 
peaks with the highest intensities. The data were then further processed at the range of m/z 300 179 
to 400 to detect the ion images of lower intensities.  180 
DESI-Q-TOF data were acquired and processed using the MassLynx software (Waters, version 181 
4.1). And, HDI Imaging software (Waters, version 1.4) was used to analyze the ion images.  182 
DESI-TQ analysis 183 
A Xevo TQ-XS equipped with a DESI source (Waters) has been employed for imaging 184 
in SRM mode. A syringe pump delivered the DESI-IMS solvent (MeOH/H2O, 98/2, v/v) at a 185 
flow rate of 2 μL /min. For NMN imaging, the DESI source conditions were optimized using 186 
the following parameters: N2 nebulizing gas pressure of 0.8 Mpa; capillary voltage of 0.85 kV 187 
(Positive); MS source temperature of 150 °C; sampling cone voltage of 20 V. The distances 188 
between the emitter tip and the surface, the mass inlet and the surface, and the mass inlet were 189 
roughly 2, 6, and 0.5 mm, respectively. The identified areas on the glass slide were scanned at 190 
a scan rate of 10 scan/sec. The pixel size was se t as 50 µm × 50 µm for tissue and 100 µm × 191 
100 µm for standard. A collision energy of 20 eV was used for NMN transition (m/z 335.1 to 192

m/z 123.0). The SRM dwell time was set as 0.0016 sec/pixel. At least two serial sections of the 193 
cockroach sample were analyzed under the same experimental conditions. Following the 194 
measurement, tissue sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for histological 195 
analysis.  196 
The ion image of DESI-TQ data was acquired and processed using the  updated version of  197

Discussion  241 
For the first time, in this study, we demonstrated the NMN imaging in biological tissue 242 
employing DESI-TQ in SRM mode that showed higher sensitivity and excellent selectivity 243 
than DESI-Q-TOF. Detection of NMN in tissue is challenging by IMS  since this molecule is 244 
very labile and readily undergoes in -source fragmentation under standard electrospray 245 
ionization settings24). DESI-Q-TOF is typically used for untargeted analysis where the target 246 
ion is mixed with myriads of other ions  and could not be detected.  In our experience, DESI-247 
TQ provides about ten to a thousand times higher sensitivity than the DESI -Q-TOF. In this 248 
study, DESI-TQ successfully detected NMN in S. lateralis (Fig. 3), which could be attributed 249 
to its improved sensitivity. It is to be noted that the sensitivity  of DESI-TQ is influenced by 250 
various factors, including dwell time. Dwell time refers to the duration the mass spectrometer 251 
spends analyzing a particular ion in a particular spot on the sample surface. Increasing dwell 252 
time leads to higher sensitivity as the instrument has more time to accumulate ions from the 253 
sample. However, longer dwell times can compromise spat ial resolution. This is because the 254 
instrument spends more time at each spot, resulting in a larger area being sampled, which may 255 
reduce the ability to discriminate features at a finer spatial scale. Therefore, finding the optimal 256 
balance between dwell time, sensitivity, and spatial resolution is recommended. 257 
The analysis of insects, such as S. lateralis , by IMS presents inherent challenges. Ideally, 258 
freezing the sample immediately after sacrificing the animal or insect is crucial to prevent post-259 
mortem degradation of certain analytes. However, the delicate anatomical structure of S. 260 
lateralis poses difficulties in cryo-sectioning fresh-frozen samples without using an embedding 261 
medium. To address this issue, we employed a super cryo -embedding medium for the entire 262 
body of S. lateralis. This approach facilitates cryo-sectioning without compromising the mass 263 
spectral quality, thanks to the medium's monomeric property. It is important to note that 264 
freezing samples using cryo -embedding medium requires a longer time compared to direct 265 

15 
 
freezing, this procedure may impact the imaging of certain biomolecules suscep tible to rapid 266 
post-mortem degradation. Fortunately, there is no reported literature on the post -mortem 267 
degradation of NMN, supporting the compatibility of NMN imaging in frozen and embedded 268 
samples.  269 
One of the challenges in NMN imaging is the potential d elocalization during sample 270 
preparation. In our experience, NMN delocalization was observed when tissue section was 271 
stored in the freezer before analysis. This is because NMN is highly soluble in water . In our 272 
study, we dried the sample immediately after c ryo-sectioning to minimize the NMN 273 
delocalization. Previously MALDI-IMS was used for peptide imaging in formalin-fixed and 274 
paraffin-embedded (FFPE) S. lateralis25). However, FFPE samples are not suitable for NMN 275 
imaging since they require deparaffinization and extensive washing procedure.  276 
Our group recently developed a rapid SRM method (7 min run per injection) to screen and 277 
quantify NMN in plant extract using UPLC -MS/MS23). SRM has been a gol d standard for 278 
quantification studies. We employed the same method in our current study  and found  a 279 
significantly higher content of NMN in S. lateralis  compared to other species of insects. 280 
Cockroaches are  one of the most successful and diverse insects in traditional and Chinese 281 
medicine26). Previously Miles et al. investigated NMN content in various natural raw foods , 282 
including edamame, broccoli, cucumber seed, cucumber peel, cabbage, avocado, tomato, 283 
mushroom, beef, and shrimp. Among these , edamame showed the h ighest content of NMN 284 
(0.47-1.88) mg/100 g27). Considering the moisture content (60 -65%)28), the NMN content in 285 
edamame should be approximately 1.18 -4.7 mg/100 g dry weight. In contrast, cockroaches 286 
have an NMN content of 15.5  mg/100 g dry weight (Fig. 2), about 3 to 13 times higher than 287 
edamame. This is the first study that revealed the surprisingly higher content of NMN in insects. 288 
Cockroach extracts have been used for centuries to treat aches, pains, inflammation, and even 289 
chronic heart failure29). The gut microbial metabolites of cockroach is a valuable source of new 290 

16 
 
bioactive compounds with therapeutic promise for human health 26). Our study also indicate d 291 
the promising use of this insect, particularly in pharmaceuticals. A recent study found that milk 292 
from cockroach species had a higher potential for nutrition than milk from typical mammals30). 293 
As a result, it is thought of as a potential new superfood that might be made accessible for 294 
ingestion shortly. It is to be noted that  cockroaches carry food -borne pathogens that may 295 
transmit diseases to humans31). Therefore, more research is required to focus on the sanitation 296 
and safety of cockroaches.  297 
In our study, t he second higher amount of NMN was present in G. bimaculatus. This insect 298 
species is used as food and feed32). A previous study showed that the extract of G. bimaculatus 299 
can treat aging in the WKY mice model by dramatically lowering creatinine phosphokinase 300 
levels in the serum 33). We also found a significant amount of NMN in G. bimaculatus , 301 
suggesting it might have a potential role in anti -aging. We have analyzed two species of 302 
termites, R. speratus, and H. sjostedti. In our investigation, the third higher amount of NMN 303 
was present in H. sjostedti. Termites are eusocial insects  used as model organism s in aging 304 
research34). Recently, DESI-IMS analysis of 13C-labeled termites revealed that the royal diet  305 
contained phosphatidylinositol and acetyl-L-carnitine, which are known to be anti-aging agents 306 
35) and this may explain termite royals’ remarkable longevity in eusocial insects 36). This fact 307 
strongly suggests that termites have anti-aging agents in their bodies, and therefore, NMN may 308 
also be involved in the longevity of H. sjostedti.         309 
Our study has some limitations. We could not assign the anatomical region of S. lateralis 310 
unambiguously where NMN exhibited high distribution. The current data did not explore the 311 
potential post-mortem degradation of NMN within the S. lateralis.  312 
In conclusion, the current study demonstrated the NMN imaging in tissue by DESI -TQ and 313 
revealed S. lateralis as a rich source of NMN , specifically in the abdomen . We believe that, 314 

17 
 
our method will help to develop other animal imaging in the field of biomedical research.  In 315 
the future, NMN imaging could aid in understanding age-related changes in NAD+ metabolism 316 
and its potential role in human health. 317
