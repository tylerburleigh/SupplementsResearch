# src-089: NAD+ Metabolism and Arterial Stiffness After Long-Term NMN Supplementation

**Citation:** Katayoshi T, Uehata S, Nakashima N, Nakajo T, Kitajima N, Kageyama M, Tsuji-Naito K. (2023). *Scientific Reports*, 13:2786. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-29787-3

**Quality assessment:** Quality field = "ok". Full text available via PDF extraction with minor formatting artifacts. Peer-reviewed open-access journal. 39 citations. Funded by DHC Corporation (a supplement company).

## Core Findings

This 12-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 36 healthy middle-aged adults (40-59 years) found that 250 mg/day NMN supplementation significantly increased serum nicotinamide (NAM) levels (ANCOVA p=0.037; +57.6% in NMN vs. -26.8% in placebo), indicating enhanced NAD+ metabolism. NMN supplementation showed a non-significant trend toward reduced arterial stiffness (baPWV decreased 25.1 cm/s, p=0.097). In subgroup analyses of participants with above-average BMI or blood glucose, baPWV reduction was significant. No adverse events were observed.

## Methodology

- **Design:** 12-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial
- **Registration:** UMIN000045205 (registered 20/08/2021)
- **Participants:** 36 healthy men and women aged 40-59; randomized 1:1 to NMN or placebo (n=18 per group). 34 completed (1 dropout per group).
- **Intervention:** NMN 125 mg/capsule, 2 capsules/day (250 mg/day total) for 12 weeks. Placebo capsules contained cornstarch.
- **Primary outcomes:** Serum concentrations of NAD+, NMN, and NAM; SIRT1 mRNA expression
- **Secondary outcomes:** Blood count, blood pressure, liver function, lipids, hormones, AGEs, 8-OHdG, vascular stiffness (baPWV, ABI)
- **NAD+ metabolite measurement:** Isotope dilution LC-MS/MS on serum samples
- **Vascular function:** baPWV measured with Omron BP-203RPE III waveform analyzer
- **Statistical analysis:** ANCOVA with baseline as covariate for between-group comparisons; paired t-test for within-group; ITT for baseline characteristics; FAS for efficacy. Sample size based on prior publications, no formal power calculation reported.

## Key Evidence

### NAD+ Metabolism
- Serum NAM significantly increased in NMN group vs. placebo (ANCOVA p=0.037)
- Within-group: NAM increased in NMN group (p=0.006) and decreased in placebo (p=0.014)
- Baseline NAM differed significantly between groups (p=0.001), a confound addressed by ANCOVA
- Serum NMN and NAD+ were detectable but below lower limit of quantification -- could not be compared between groups
- No significant change in SIRT1 mRNA (p=0.211)

### Arterial Stiffness (baPWV)
- Average baPWV decreased 25.1 +/- 14.5 cm/s in NMN group (non-significant, p=0.097)
- Right baPWV p=0.070, Left baPWV p=0.147
- Baseline baPWV was notably lower in NMN group (1208 cm/s) vs. placebo (1338 cm/s) -- ceiling effect may limit detectable improvement
- **Subgroup analyses (post-hoc):**
  - Above-average BMI: baPWV significantly decreased in NMN vs. placebo
  - Above-average blood glucose: baPWV significantly decreased in NMN vs. placebo
  - Above-average systolic or diastolic BP: no significant difference
- In high blood glucose subgroup, NMN significantly reduced diastolic blood pressure
- Estimated vascular age change: placebo +0.5 years, NMN -2.0 years (non-significant)

### Safety
- No adverse events reported
- No significant changes in hematology, clinical chemistry, or hormones after 12 weeks
- 250 mg/day was well tolerated

### Other Biomarkers
- SIRT1 mRNA: no significant change (p=0.211)
- Skin AGEs: no significant change (p=0.283)
- Urinary 8-OHdG: no significant change (p=0.735)

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size (n=34 completing):** The study was not powered to detect differences in baPWV, likely explaining the non-significant trend.
2. **Baseline imbalances:** Serum NAM (p=0.001) and baPWV values (especially left, significantly different) differed at baseline between groups, complicating interpretation.
3. **Serum NAD+ not quantifiable:** Below lower limit of quantification; a key metabolite could not be assessed. Whole blood or PBMC sampling would have been more appropriate (acknowledged by authors).
4. **No strict healthy ranges for inclusion:** Participants were not screened against specific metabolic health criteria.
5. **Concurrent supplement use allowed:** 35% of placebo and 32% of NMN group used other supplements during the study. Omega-3 fatty acids were used exclusively by the placebo group (5 participants), which may have confounded vascular outcomes.
6. **Industry funding:** DHC Corporation (a supplement manufacturer) funded the study and employs the authors. Ethics committee was also DHC's own committee.
7. **No follow-up:** No post-supplementation follow-up was conducted.
8. **Subgroup analyses were post-hoc and exploratory,** not pre-specified, with no correction for multiple comparisons.

## Claims to Verify

- **NAM baseline difference (p=0.001):** The baseline NAM values were 14.9 +/- 3.8 ng/mL (placebo) vs. 10.4 +/- 3.5 ng/mL (NMN). This substantial baseline difference is concerning and may inflate the apparent treatment effect via ANCOVA adjustment.
- **baPWV baseline imbalance:** Average baPWV was 1208 cm/s (NMN) vs. 1338 cm/s (placebo). The NMN group started with substantially lower stiffness, making further reduction harder to detect and complicating between-group comparison.
- **Subgroup significance for BMI and blood glucose:** No specific p-values are reported for these subgroup analyses in the text; they are only shown in Figure 3. The claim of "significantly decreased" should be interpreted cautiously given the small subgroup sizes.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (NMN mechanisms):** Provides evidence that oral NMN increases serum NAM (a downstream NAD+ metabolite), supporting enhanced NAD+ turnover. However, could not directly measure NAD+ levels.
- **Q2 (NAD+ levels and health outcomes):** Shows increased NAM but no change in SIRT1, AGEs, or 8-OHdG. The link between NAD+ metabolism and functional outcomes is suggestive but not conclusive.
- **Q3 (optimal dosing/timing):** Uses 250 mg/day for 12 weeks -- one data point for dose-response analysis. No dose comparison.
- **Q4 (health outcomes):** Non-significant trend toward reduced arterial stiffness; significant in BMI/blood glucose subgroups (exploratory). No effect on other metabolic parameters.
- **Q5 (side effects/contraindications):** Supports safety at 250 mg/day for 12 weeks. No adverse events.
- **Q6 (comparison with NR):** Does not directly compare with NR but discusses parallels with NR studies on PWV (Martens et al., 2018).
