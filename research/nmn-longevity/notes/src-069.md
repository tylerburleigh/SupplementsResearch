# src-069: Igarashi et al. (2022) - NMN Supplementation in Healthy Older Men

**Citation:** Igarashi M, Nakagawa-Nagahama Y, Miura M, et al. "Chronic nicotinamide mononucleotide supplementation elevates blood nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide levels and alters muscle function in healthy older men." npj Aging. 2022;8:5. doi:10.1038/s41514-022-00084-z

**Quality:** ok (full paper text available, peer-reviewed, 85 citations)

## Core Findings

Daily oral supplementation of 250 mg NMN for 12 weeks significantly elevated whole blood NAD+ and related metabolite levels in healthy older men (age 65+). NMN supplementation produced nominally significant improvements in gait speed and left-hand grip strength, but had no effect on skeletal muscle mass, body composition, insulin sensitivity, or metabolic markers. The intervention was safe and well-tolerated.

## Study Design and Methodology

- **Design:** Placebo-controlled, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group trial
- **Registration:** UMIN-CTR (UMIN000036321)
- **Duration:** July 2019 - November 2019
- **Intervention:** 250 mg NMN once daily for 12 weeks (Mitsubishi Corporation Life Sciences Limited)
- **Population:** Healthy Japanese men aged 65+, BMI 22-28, non-smokers, no active diseases
- **Primary objective:** Evaluate NMN effects on blood NAD+ concentration and body composition
- **Secondary objective:** Evaluate aging-related parameters (muscle strength, bone density, vision, hearing)

### Key Assessments
- NAD+ metabolites: LC-MS/MS from whole blood
- Body composition: Bioimpedance analysis (InBody S10), abdominal CT for visceral fat and liver fat
- Muscle function: 10-m gait speed, grip strength (Smedley dynamometer), 30-s chair-stand test
- Metabolic: 75g OGTT, HOMA-IR, HbA1c, lipids
- Other: Audiometry, MMSE-J, MOCA-J, flow-mediated dilation
- Statistical: Mixed-model analysis, MMRM, ANCOVA, t-tests/Mann-Whitney U; no correction for multiple comparisons

## Sample Sizes

- **Screened:** 65 men
- **Excluded:** 8 (medical history/lab abnormalities), 5 (other trials or withdrawal)
- **Enrolled and randomized:** 42 (21 per group)
- **Protocol deviation:** At the 6-week visit, 22 participants (11 per group) received the wrong supplement due to a supplier error
- **Completed 12-week study with correct allocation:** 20 (10 per group)
- **6-week data usable for all 42** (mixed-model analysis leverages all data points)

This is a critical limitation -- the 12-week comparison relies on only n=10 per group.

## Key Results

### NAD+ and Metabolites (12-week, n=10 per group)
- NMN significantly increased whole blood levels of: NMN, NAD+, NR, NAMN, NAR (P < 0.05 to P < 0.001)
- NA and NAM levels were not significantly different
- Unexpected elevation in NAMN and NAR (de novo pathway intermediates), suggesting possible NMN deamidation or gut microbiota involvement

### Muscle Function
| Measure | Placebo 12wk | NMN 12wk | P (mixed/ MMRM) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gait speed (m/s) | 1.30 +/- 0.22 | 1.60 +/- 0.13 | 0.033 / 0.015 |
| Left grip (kg) | 34.1 +/- 4.6 | 37.4 +/- 5.8 | -- / 0.019 |
| Right grip (kg) | 37.3 +/- 5.9 | 41.9 +/- 5.6 | 0.259 / 0.194 |
| 30-s chair stand | 14.0 +/- 5.2 | 16.3 +/- 3.6 | 0.509 / 0.309 |

Note: The laterality effect (left grip significant, right grip not) is discussed as possibly reflecting the fact that ~90% of Japanese are right-handed, so the left hand is less trained and more sensitive to intervention effects.

### Body Composition
- No significant effect on SMI, segmental lean mass, fat mass, visceral fat area, or CT L/S ratio
- Post-hoc power analysis for SMI: power = 0.991 for detecting 0.45 kg/m2 change, confirming the study had adequate power to detect the expected effect size

### Metabolic Parameters
- No significant effects on HbA1c, FBG, HOMA-IR, HOMA-beta, glucose/insulin/C-peptide AUC (OGTT), triglycerides, LDL, HDL, adiponectin, IL-6
- Authors attribute null metabolic results to the healthy status of participants

### Other Measures
- Hearing: borderline improvement in right ear (P = 0.054)
- Blood pressure / flow-mediated dilation: no effect
- Cognitive function (MMSE-J, MOCA-J): no effect

## Safety
- Excellent adherence (>90% of supplements consumed)
- No serious adverse events
- All clinical lab values remained within normal range
- No abnormalities in liver or muscle enzymes

## Limitations

1. **Major protocol deviation:** 22 of 42 participants received wrong supplement at 6-week visit, reducing 12-week evaluable sample to n=10 per group. This disrupted randomization balance.
2. **Small final sample size:** Only 20 completers for the 12-week primary endpoint limits statistical power for secondary outcomes.
3. **No multiple comparison correction:** All analyses were exploratory; P values should be interpreted cautiously.
4. **Narrow population:** Healthy older Japanese men only. Results may not generalize to women, other ethnicities, younger adults, or individuals with health conditions.
5. **Funding source:** Study funded by Mitsubishi Corporation Life Sciences Limited, which also provided the NMN. Three authors are employees of this company.

## Claims to Verify

- **Gait speed change at 12 weeks:** Placebo 1.30 m/s vs NMN 1.60 m/s is a very large difference (~0.30 m/s). This exceeds the typical clinically meaningful difference for gait speed (0.10 m/s). Given n=10 per group and no multiple comparison correction, this could be inflated. The P=0.002 for the 12-week t-test is striking but should be viewed in context of the small sample.
- **Left grip laterality:** The authors' explanation (less-trained hand more responsive) is plausible but unproven. The right grip also showed a trend (41.9 vs 37.3 kg, P=0.090), which in a larger sample might reach significance.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (NMN effectiveness for human health/longevity):** Directly relevant. Provides clinical trial evidence that NMN boosts blood NAD+ and may improve muscle function (gait speed, grip strength) in older adults. However, muscle mass was unaffected and metabolic benefits were absent in this healthy population.
- **Q2 (Optimal dosing, safety, pharmacokinetics):** Directly relevant. Supports safety of 250 mg/day for 12 weeks in older men. Shows NAD+ elevation is achievable at this dose. LC-MS/MS metabolomic data provide pharmacokinetic insight (increased NMN, NAD+, NR, NAMN, NAR).
- **Q3 (Mechanisms):** Limited relevance. No mechanistic data (e.g., sirtuin activity, mitochondrial function) was collected. The metabolomic profile (NAMN/NAR elevation) hints at possible gut microbiota-mediated deamidation pathways.
- **Q4 (Comparison with other interventions):** Mildly relevant. Authors compare with NR clinical trials but no direct comparison was made.
- **Q5 (Limitations and gaps):** Highly relevant. Major protocol deviation, small sample, narrow population, and lack of multiple comparison correction are significant limitations that qualify the positive findings.
- **Q6 (Practical recommendations):** Mildly relevant. 250 mg/day is a moderate dose; safety is supported but efficacy evidence is preliminary.
