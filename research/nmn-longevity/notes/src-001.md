# Source src-001: NMN as Anti-Aging Health Product -- Promises and Safety Concerns

**Citation:** Nadeeshani H, Li J, Ying T, Zhang B, Lu J. "Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) as an anti-aging health product -- Promises and safety concerns." J Adv Res. 2021 Aug 11;37:267-278. doi: 10.1016/j.jare.2021.08.003. PMCID: PMC9039735.

**Type:** Narrative review article (peer-reviewed)
**Year:** 2021
**Quality assessment:** Substantive full-text review with comprehensive coverage of preclinical and limited clinical data.

---

## Core Findings

NMN, as a direct precursor of NAD+, can restore declining NAD+ levels associated with aging through the salvage and Preiss-Handler biosynthesis pathways. Extensive animal model evidence demonstrates beneficial effects across multiple age-related conditions (diabetes, vascular dysfunction, cognitive decline, skeletal aging, kidney injury, neurodegeneration). However, human clinical evidence for long-term safety and anti-aging efficacy remains scarce despite widespread commercial availability of NMN supplements.

---

## Mechanism of Action (Q1)

- NAD+ levels decline with aging due to increased activity of NAD+-consuming enzymes: CD38/CD157, PARP, sirtuins, TNKS, BST (line 303-318)
- This depletion causes mitochondrial dysfunction (downregulation of energy production), oxidative stress, DNA damage, cognitive impairment, and inflammation
- NMN is an intermediate in NAD+ biosynthesis via the **salvage pathway** (main route; NAMPT converts nicotinamide + PRPP to NMN, then NMNAT conjugates with ATP to form NAD+) and the **Preiss-Handler pathway** (line 337-368)
- NMN activates SIRT1, which blocks HIF-1alpha, restoring communication between mitochondria and nucleus (line 404-414)
- NMN is absorbed in the small intestine via the Slc12a8 transporter (line 972-973)
- NMN is found naturally in foods: vegetables/fruits at 0.25-1.88 mg/100g, raw beef/shrimp at 0.06-0.42 mg/100g (line 263-267)

## Key Evidence from In Vivo Studies (Q2, Q3)

### Diabetes and Metabolic Function
- Yoshino et al.: NMN 500 mg/kg/day IP for 11 days enhanced NAD+ from 500 to 1550 pmol/mg-tissue, improved insulin secretion, sensitivity, and lipid profile in age-induced diabetic mice (line 433-439)

### Vascular Function
- De Picciotto et al.: NMN 300 mg/kg/day in drinking water for 8 weeks restored NAD+ by 3-fold, vascular SIRT1 activity, and reversed vascular dysfunction in aged (26-28 month) mice (line 467-476, 786-789)
- Das et al.: NMN 400 mg/kg/day in drinking water for 2 months increased endurance and blood flow in aged mice by increasing capillary density (line 583-591)

### Long-Term Anti-Aging (most comprehensive study)
- Mills et al.: NMN 100-300 mg/kg/day in drinking water for 12 months suppressed aging-induced weight gain, improved eye function, lipid profile, insulin sensitivity, physical activity, and energy metabolism without toxicity in C57BL/6N mice (line 506-516, 795-797)

### Lifespan Extension
- Fu and Zhang (patent): NMN extended mouse lifespan by ~29% (line 879-883)

### Cognitive and Neuroprotective Effects
- Multiple studies showed NMN improved cognitive function, reduced amyloid plaque burden, enhanced mitochondrial bioenergetics in Alzheimer's models (line 488-504, 893-905)
- Johnson et al.: Age-related 40% decrease in NAD+ in hippocampus CA1 region; NMN restored levels and mitigated cognitive/behavioral decline (line 830-841)

### Kidney Protection
- Guan et al.: NMN 500 mg/kg/day for 4 days boosted NAD+ and SIRT1 in aged kidneys, protecting against ischemia-reperfusion and cisplatin-induced injury (line 542-551)

### Fertility
- Miao et al.: NMN restored NAD+ by ~50%, improved oocyte quality in aged mice (line 873-879)
- Bertoldo et al.: NMN restored fertility, increased ovarian NAD+ from ~200 to 300 pmol/mg (line 879)

### Skeletal Health
- Song et al.: NMN 300 mg/kg/day for 3 months protected skeletal aging, reducing adipogenesis and increasing osteogenesis via SIRT1 (line 652-661)

## Human Clinical Evidence (Q4)

### Completed Clinical Studies
1. **Tsubota/Keio University Phase I (UMIN000021309):** 10 healthy volunteers; assessed bioavailability and safety. Unsuccessful in detecting NMN in plasma samples. (line 1070-1073)
2. **Irie et al. (non-blinded):** 10 healthy men; single oral doses of 100, 250, 500 mg NMN. All doses well-tolerated. No changes in body temperature, oxygen saturation, blood pressure, heart rate, ophthalmic/neurological parameters, urine/blood labs, or sleep quality. Increased serum bilirubin, decreased blood glucose/chloride/creatinine -- all within normal range. NMN did not raise nicotinamide to harmful levels. (line 1076-1077)
3. **Yoshino et al. (2021):** 250 mg/day for 10 weeks in 25 prediabetic overweight/obese women. Potential metabolic benefits without adverse effects. (line 1046-1048)

### Ongoing Clinical Trials (as of 2021)
- Phase II study at Keio University (UMIN000030609): safety of long-term NMN administration and glucose metabolism in healthy adults
- Hiroshima University (UMIN000025739): effect of long-term NMN on hormones in healthy individuals
- University of Tokyo Hospital (UMIN000036321): oral NMN on body composition in elderly (line 1079-1082)

## Safety Profile and Concerns (Q5)

### Favorable Preclinical Safety
- Mills et al.: Long-term NMN administration without noticeable toxicity or deleterious side effects (line 1067-1071)
- Cros et al.: NMN-C (synthetic NMN) in Sprague-Dawley rats: NOAEL >= 1500 mg/kg/day; no adverse effects at single dose of 2666 mg/kg; safe over 90-day sub-chronic period at 375, 750, 1500 mg/kg/day (line 1051-1053)

### Safety Concerns / Caveats
- No NOAEL or LOAEL established for human NMN administration (unlike NR which has NOAEL of 300 mg/kg/day)
- NR (another NAD+ precursor) has GRAS status from FDA; NMN does not (as of 2021)
- **NMN may interfere with exercise benefits:** Yu et al. found NMN (400 mg/kg) obstructed exercise-induced improvements in glucose tolerance and hepatic triglycerides in diet-induced obese mice (line 1111-1113)
- Potential for NAD+ upregulation to worsen SASP (senescence-associated secretory phenotype) via AMPK/p53 suppression, though evidence is lacking (line 1101-1109)
- Di Stefano et al.: Suggested NMN accumulation after nerve injury may promote axon degeneration (line 1085-1088)
- Commercial products contain 50-500+ mg/capsule; safety of these doses not clinically established for long-term use (line 959)

### Comparison with Other NAD+ Precursors
- Nicotinamide: causes adverse effects on kidney, liver, pancreatic beta-cells; induces nausea, headaches; decreases insulin sensitivity; causes DNA damage; neurodegeneration of dopaminergic neurons
- NR: increases LDL cholesterol; enhances fatty liver; high dose causes white adipose tissue dysfunction
- NMN appears to have better safety profile than nicotinamide in animal studies (improved lipid profile, insulin sensitivity, reduced DNA damage, neuroprotective effects)

---

## Limitations

1. This is a narrative review (not systematic), so selection bias is possible
2. Almost all efficacy data comes from animal models (mice, rats, C. elegans) -- translational gap to humans
3. Only 3 small human clinical studies available as of 2021 (n=10, n=10, n=25)
4. No long-term human safety data available
5. Many animal studies used IP injection, which has different pharmacokinetics than oral supplementation used in commercial products
6. Doses in animal studies (typically 100-500 mg/kg) are much higher than typical human supplement doses (250-500 mg)
7. The review was published in 2021; significant additional clinical trial data has likely emerged since then
8. One author (Jun Lu) received funding from EZZ Life Science Holdings, a company that may sell NMN products (potential COI, though authors declare none)
