# src-070: Safety evaluation of NMN oral administration in healthy adults

**Citation:** Fukamizu Y, Uchida Y, Shigekawa A, Sato T, Kosaka H, Sakurai T. (2022). Scientific Reports, 12:14442. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-18272-y

**Quality:** ok (full text available, peer-reviewed, 47 citations)

## Core Findings

Oral administration of 1250 mg NMN once daily for 4 weeks was safe and well-tolerated in 31 healthy adult men and women aged 20-65 years. No severe adverse events were observed, and all hematological, biochemical, body composition, urinalysis, and vital sign measurements remained within clinical laboratory reference values throughout the study. NMN also showed no mutagenicity in the Ames test.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study
- **Registration:** UMIN000043084 (registered January 21, 2021)
- **Population:** 31 healthy adults (14 men, 17 women), aged 20-65 years
  - Placebo group: n=15 (7 male, 8 female); mean age 35.7 +/- 7.2 years; BMI 22.1 +/- 3.3
  - NMN group: n=16 (7 male, 9 female); mean age 35.1 +/- 7.0 years; BMI 22.9 +/- 2.7
- **Intervention:** 1250 mg beta-NMN (H+ crystalline form) dissolved in 200 mL water, consumed once daily for 4 weeks
- **Placebo:** Same formulation without NMN (maltitol, acesulfame potassium, dextrin, citric acid, flavor)
- **Assessments:** Anthropometry, hematology, clinical biochemistry, urinalysis, body composition, vital signs at weeks 0, 2, 4, and observation period (week 6)
- **Statistics:** Two-way ANOVA with Dunnett's post-hoc test for within-group comparisons; unpaired t-test or Welch's t-test for between-group comparisons
- **Compliance:** Mean consumption rate 98.3% (placebo) and 98.9% (NMN)
- **Preclinical:** Ames test (bacterial reverse mutation) conducted at concentrations 313-5000 ug/plate across 5 strains (Salmonella TA100, TA1535, TA98, TA1537; E. coli WP2uvrA)

## Key Evidence

1. **No severe adverse events:** 5 adverse events reported total (4 in NMN group: loose stool x2, high blood pressure, acne vulgaris; 1 in placebo group: common cold). All assessed as unrelated to test food by principal investigator. All events resolved or remitted.

2. **Hematological safety:** All values (WBC, RBC, hemoglobin, hematocrit, platelets, MCV, MCH, MCHC, HbA1c) remained within clinical reference values with no significant within- or between-group differences.

3. **Clinical biochemistry safety:** Only minor statistically significant differences observed:
   - Placebo group: sodium decrease at week 2 (p=0.0148 vs week 0)
   - Between-group differences at week 0 for LDH (p=0.0340), A/G ratio (p=0.0345), sodium (p=0.0283), and total protein at observation period (p=0.0310) -- all within reference values and attributable to baseline variation.

4. **Body composition and vital signs:** No significant changes within or between groups.

5. **Ames test negative:** No mutagenicity at any concentration up to 5000 ug/plate.

6. **Renal function:** No significant increase in blood creatinine in humans at 1250 mg/day, contrasting with beagle dog data at 1340 mg/day showing mild adverse renal effects (You et al., 2020).

7. **Favorable comparison to other NAD+ precursors:** NMN at 1250 mg/day caused no hepatotoxicity or flushing, while NAM has upper tolerable limit of 900 mg/day (hepatotoxicity risk) and NA has upper limit of 10 mg/day (flushing risk).

## Limitations

1. **No metabolomic analysis:** Blood and urine were not analyzed for NMN, NAD+, NAM, NR, 2Py, or 4Py levels. Bioavailability and metabolite disposition were not assessed.
2. **Limited safety assessments:** Only standard clinical laboratory tests performed; no MRI, ECG, EEG, or histological analyses.
3. **Small sample size:** 31 total participants (15 placebo, 16 NMN).
4. **Short duration:** Only 4 weeks of administration. Long-term safety not assessed.
5. **Industry funded:** Study funded by Mitsubishi Corporation Life Sciences Limited, which manufactures NMN. Five of six authors are company employees.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q5 (Safety and side effects):** PRIMARY RELEVANCE. This is a dedicated safety study for high-dose NMN (1250 mg/day) in humans. Provides the strongest safety evidence for NMN at doses >= 1000 mg/shot. Extends prior safety data from Irie et al. (2020, single dose up to 500 mg) and Yoshino et al. (2021, 250 mg/day for 10 weeks).
- **Q2 (Optimal dosing):** Moderate relevance. Establishes safety at 1250 mg/day, a higher dose than most prior clinical studies. Notes that typical dietary NMN intake is < 2 mg/day.
- **Q6 (Bioavailability and pharmacokinetics):** Low relevance. Study explicitly did not measure NMN or NAD+ metabolite levels in blood or urine (noted as a limitation).
