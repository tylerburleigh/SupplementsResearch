# Source src-109: NAD+ Intermediates: The Biology and Therapeutic Potential of NMN and NR

**Authors:** Yoshino J, Baur JA, Imai S
**Venue:** Cell Metabolism (2018), Vol 27
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.cmet.2017.11.002
**Citations:** 920
**Type:** Comprehensive review article
**Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

This is a landmark review synthesizing the state of knowledge on NMN and NR as of late 2017. It covers NAD+ biosynthesis pathways, in vivo pharmacokinetics, systemic NAD+ regulation (the "NAD World" concept), and therapeutic effects in rodent disease models. The review compiles extensive preclinical evidence for NMN and NR efficacy across metabolic, neurological, cardiovascular, and renal disease models, and identifies critical open questions for translation to humans.

Key conclusions: (1) Both NMN and NR effectively increase NAD+ in multiple tissues with broad therapeutic benefits in rodents; (2) Aged animals appear more responsive to NMN than young animals; (3) NMN and NR have distinct pharmacokinetic behaviors and may have tissue-specific uptake mechanisms; (4) NR at 400 mg/kg in diet starting from 2 years of age extends mouse lifespan; (5) Long-term oral NMN (up to 300 mg/kg for 12 months) is safe in mice.

## Methodology

This is a narrative review synthesizing published preclinical studies. The authors compiled data from:
- Rodent studies of NMN and NR across multiple disease models (Tables 1 and 2 provide comprehensive summaries)
- Pharmacokinetic studies using isotopically labeled NMN (C13-D-NMN) and NR (C13-D-NR)
- Knockout mouse models (Nrk1 KO, Nampt KO, Sirt KO) to dissect mechanisms
- Table 3 documents tissue-specific NAD+ declines in aging and disease

No primary data collection; the paper synthesizes and interprets the existing literature through mid-2017.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### NMN Effects in Rodent Models (Table 1)
- **Doses tested:** 31.25-2000 mg/kg (IP, oral, IV, SC routes)
- **Most common dose:** 500 mg/kg IP (single injection or short-term)
- **Long-term oral dosing:** 100-300 mg/kg in drinking water for up to 12 months
- **Key outcomes:** Improved glucose tolerance, insulin sensitivity, mitochondrial function, neuroprotection, cardiovascular protection, renal protection

### NR Effects in Rodent Models (Table 2)
- **Doses tested:** 100-5000 mg/kg
- **Most common dose:** 400 mg/kg in diet
- **Lifespan extension:** NR at 400 mg/kg starting from age 2 years showed small but significant lifespan extension in mice (Zhang et al., 2016)
- **Key outcomes:** Weight control, improved insulin sensitivity, neuroprotection, hearing protection, muscle function

### NAD+ Decline with Age (Table 3)
- Muscle: ~30-85% of normal depending on age (22-32 months)
- Liver: ~55-90% of normal
- Adipose: ~55-75% of normal
- Brain: ~63-90% of normal
- Human brain NAD+ shows ~90% remaining at age 70 vs age 20 based on regression (Zhu et al., 2015)
- Human liver NAD+: ~70-75% in subjects >60 vs <45 years (Zhou et al., 2016)

### Pharmacokinetics
- NMN appears in plasma and peripheral tissues within 5-15 min of IP injection
- Leads to 2-3 fold increase in liver NAD+ within 60 min
- Orally administered NMN is rapidly absorbed (confirmed with isotopic labeling)
- NMN uptake mechanism debated: direct transport vs. conversion to NR by ectonucleotidases (CD73)
- NR is unstable in plasma, quickly degrades to nicotinamide
- Significant first-pass metabolism for both compounds

### Dose-Response Observations
- Lower NMN doses (100 mg/kg) improve physical activity better than 300 mg/kg
- 62.5 mg/kg NMN more effective for ischemia than 125-500 mg/kg
- Suggests non-linear dose-response, possibly due to local accumulation at high doses

## Limitations

1. **Preclinical focus:** Almost all data are from rodent models. Human clinical trials are noted as ongoing but no results reported.
2. **No head-to-head comparisons:** No side-by-side NMN vs NR comparisons have been conducted.
3. **Mechanism uncertainty:** Downstream mediators of NMN/NR benefits remain unclear in most cases. Sirtuins, PARPs, and CD38 are implicated but their relative contributions are unresolved.
4. **Cancer risk:** NAD+ supplementation could potentially promote tumor growth; SIRT1 has both pro- and anti-carcinogenic effects depending on context.
5. **Methyl donor depletion:** High-dose supplementation could deplete methyl donors via nicotinamide elimination through NNMT; this has been little investigated.
6. **Nicotinamide accumulation:** Possible detrimental effects from nicotinamide buildup (a potent sirtuin inhibitor).
7. **Transport uncertainty:** NMN uptake mechanism remains unresolved -- whether direct transporters exist or conversion to NR is required.
8. **BBB penetration:** Unclear whether NMN crosses the blood-brain barrier, though brain NAD+ increases are observed within 15 min.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (NMN efficacy for human health/longevity):** Highly relevant. Comprehensive preclinical evidence for NMN benefits across metabolic, neurological, cardiovascular, and renal models. Safety data from 12-month mouse study. No human efficacy data as of this review.
- **Q2 (NAD+ decline with aging):** Directly addresses with Table 3 compiling tissue-specific NAD+ decline data across multiple studies in rodents and humans.
- **Q3 (Dose-response, optimal dosing):** Reports non-linear dose-response for some outcomes; lower doses sometimes more effective. Common preclinical dose range 100-500 mg/kg. No human dose-response data.
- **Q4 (Lifespan extension):** Reports NR (400 mg/kg) extends mouse lifespan when started at 2 years. No lifespan data for NMN as of this review.
- **Q5 (Pharmacokinetics/bioavailability):** Detailed discussion of NMN and NR pharmacokinetics, uptake mechanisms, first-pass metabolism, and isotopic tracer studies.
- **Q6 (Safety/side effects):** Discusses potential cancer promotion risk, methyl donor depletion, nicotinamide accumulation, and long-term safety considerations. NR toxicity study (Conze et al., 2016) reports no adverse effects below 1000 mg/kg/day in rats.

## Claims to Verify

- **Human brain NAD+ decline:** The review states "~90% at age 70 vs 20 based on regression" citing Zhu et al., 2015. This is a modest decline compared to some rodent data. Worth verifying the original study methodology (was it in vivo NMR?).
- **Human liver NAD+ decline:** "~70-75% in subjects >60 vs <45 years" from Zhou et al., 2016. This was not a direct measurement in the cited study but was estimated from graphs. Verify original source.
- **NR lifespan extension:** The review states a "small but significant" lifespan extension from Zhang et al., 2016, but the magnitude is not quantified. The original paper should be checked for the actual percentage increase.
- **NMN rapid brain uptake:** NAD+ increase in hippocampus and hypothalamus within 15 min is cited from Stein and Imai, 2014, and Yoon et al., 2015. These are from IP injection, not oral administration. The BBB penetration question remains open.
