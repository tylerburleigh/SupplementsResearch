# src-023: Yi et al. (2023) - NMN Supplementation in Healthy Middle-Aged Adults

**Citation:** Yi L, Maier AB, et al. "The efficacy and safety of beta-nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) supplementation in healthy middle-aged adults: a randomized, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, dose-dependent clinical trial." *GeroScience* (2023) 45:29-43.

**DOI:** 10.1007/s11357-022-00705-1 | **Citations:** 131 | **Quality:** ok | **Peer-reviewed:** Yes

---

## Core Findings

This is one of the most comprehensive dose-ranging NMN trials in healthy adults to date. All three NMN doses (300, 600, 900 mg/day) significantly increased blood NAD concentrations and improved physical performance vs. placebo over 60 days, with the 600 mg dose emerging as optimal (900 mg offered no additional benefit). NMN was safe and well-tolerated at all doses, with zero dropouts and zero NMN-related adverse events. Biological age (blood-based) was maintained in NMN groups while increasing in placebo. Insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) was not affected by NMN.

---

## Methodology

- **Design:** Randomized, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, dose-dependent
- **Sites:** Two clinic centers in Pune, India (Lotus Healthcare & Aesthetics Clinic; Sunad Ayurved)
- **Population:** 80 healthy middle-aged adults (40-65 years), 59% female, mean age 49.3 (SD 6.8), Indian ethnicity
- **Groups:** 20 per group (placebo, 300 mg, 600 mg, 900 mg NMN/day), randomized
- **Duration:** 60 days, oral dosing once daily before breakfast
- **Product:** AbinoNutra(TM) NMN (fine crystalline powder, 150 mg/capsule)
- **Sponsor:** Aba Chemicals Co. (Shanghai) and Abinopharm, Inc. (Connecticut) -- both NMN manufacturers
- **Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04823260, CTRI/2021/03/032421
- **Analysis:** Per Protocol (all 80 completed without protocol violation); MMRM for repeated measures; significance p < 0.05

### Outcome Measures
- **Primary:** Blood NAD concentration (total NAD+ + NADH in serum, colorimetric kit)
- **Secondary:** Safety (AEs, clinical labs, vitals), 6-minute walking test (manual treadmill), blood biological age (Aging.Ai 3.0 calculator from 19 lab parameters), HOMA-IR (fasting insulin/glucose), SF-36 health survey

---

## Key Evidence

### Blood NAD Concentration (Primary Outcome)
- All NMN groups showed significant increases vs. baseline and placebo at both day 30 and day 60 (all p <= 0.001)
- **300 mg:** baseline 11.8 nM -> day 60: 32.6 nM
- **600 mg:** baseline 7.95 nM -> day 60: 45.3 nM
- **900 mg:** baseline 10.5 nM -> day 60: 48.5 nM
- **Placebo:** baseline 8.11 nM -> day 60: 11.8 nM (not significant)
- 600 mg significantly higher than 300 mg at both time points (p < 0.05, p < 0.01)
- No significant difference between 600 mg and 900 mg

### Safety and Tolerability
- 100% retention: all 80 participants completed the trial
- Zero dropouts, zero serious AEs, zero NMN-related AEs
- AEs: placebo 6 events in 5 participants; 300 mg: 3 events in 2 participants; 600 mg: 0; 900 mg: 0
- All AEs were mild/moderate (rashes, tingling, weakness, mouth ulcers, fever, hyperacidity)
- No clinically meaningful abnormalities in vital signs or physical findings
- Minor lab parameter differences (MCHC, LDL at 900 mg; uric acid at 600 mg) but no abnormality observed

### Six-Minute Walking Test
- Significant improvement vs. placebo at day 30 and day 60 for all NMN groups (all p < 0.01)
- **600 mg:** baseline 290 m -> day 60: 435 m (largest absolute improvement)
- **900 mg:** baseline 323 m -> day 60: 480 m
- **300 mg:** baseline 307 m -> day 60: 380 m (trend vs. baseline, p=0.079)
- **Placebo:** baseline 325 m -> day 60: 330 m (no significant change)
- 600 mg significantly better than 300 mg; 900 mg not significantly better than 600 mg

### Blood Biological Age
- Placebo biological age increased significantly: 39.8 -> 45.4 years (p = 0.029)
- NMN groups maintained biological age (no significant within-group change)
- Difference vs. placebo significant for all NMN groups (all p < 0.05)
- **Caveat:** Only 60 days; biological age change in placebo is unusually large (+5.6 years), raising questions about the metric's reliability over such short periods

### HOMA-IR (Insulin Resistance)
- No significant differences between any NMN group and placebo at day 60
- Interestingly, HOMA-IR increased significantly within the 600 mg (p=0.037) and 900 mg (p=0.006) groups vs. baseline, but this was not significant vs. placebo
- Consistent with other trials showing NMN does not affect insulin sensitivity in healthy adults (contrasts with Yoshino et al. 2021 findings in prediabetic women)

### SF-36 Health Survey
- Significant improvement vs. placebo at day 60 for all NMN groups (p < 0.01)
- At day 30: 600 mg and 900 mg significant (p < 0.05 and p < 0.001); 300 mg not significant
- Scores: placebo 122 -> 128; 300 mg 124 -> 137; 600 mg 118 -> 136; 900 mg 122 -> 140

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size:** 20 per group provides limited statistical power, especially for secondary outcomes
2. **Short duration:** 60 days is insufficient to assess long-term effects on aging, healthspan, or longevity
3. **Industry sponsorship:** Funded by NMN manufacturers (Aba Chemicals and Abinopharm); first author is an Abinopharm employee. This creates potential conflict of interest.
4. **Single ethnicity:** All participants were Indian; generalizability to other populations is uncertain
5. **Biological age metric:** Aging.Ai 3.0 is a blood biomarker-based calculator, not an epigenetic clock. The 5.6-year increase in placebo over 60 days is implausible as actual aging and likely reflects measurement variability or regression to the mean.
6. **NAD measurement method:** Colorimetric kit measuring total NAD+ + NADH in serum, not whole blood or tissue-specific NAD. Different measurement methods produce different results across trials.
7. **No mechanistic data:** No assessment of sirtuin activation, PARP activity, or other NAD-dependent pathways
8. **Open-label run issue:** Placebo baseline NAD was notably lower than some treatment group baselines (though not statistically significant), suggesting possible imbalance
9. **HOMA-IR concern:** The within-group increase in HOMA-IR for 600 mg and 900 mg groups (though not significant vs. placebo) is worth monitoring in larger studies
10. **Compliance was self-reported** via participant diaries and capsule counts

---

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (NMN effectiveness for human health/longevity):** Strong evidence. Dose-ranging trial showing NAD elevation, physical performance improvement, and subjective health benefits at 300-900 mg/day. Optimal dose appears to be 600 mg/day. 131 citations suggest significant impact on the field.
- **Q2 (Safety and side effects):** Strong evidence. Zero NMN-related AEs across all doses including 900 mg/day, 100% retention rate. Adds to growing safety evidence base.
- **Q3 (Optimal dosing protocols):** Key source. One of few dose-ranging trials (300/600/900 mg). Shows dose-response for NAD but plateau at 600 mg for clinical efficacy. 600 mg/day is suggested as optimal.
- **Q4 (Longevity pathways / biological age):** Moderate evidence. Shows NMN maintained blood biological age vs. placebo increase, but the metric (Aging.Ai 3.0) and short duration limit interpretability.
- **Q5 (Metabolic health / insulin sensitivity):** Relevant null finding. NMN did not improve HOMA-IR in healthy adults, consistent with other trials. Suggests metabolic benefits may be limited to metabolically compromised populations.
- **Q6 (Subpopulations / who benefits most):** Some relevance. Studied healthy middle-aged adults (40-65), both sexes (59% female). No significant sex-by-treatment interaction reported (underpowered for subgroup analysis).

---

## Claims to Verify

1. **Biological age increase in placebo (+5.6 years in 60 days):** This is biologically implausible as actual aging. The Aging.Ai 3.0 calculator uses 19 blood parameters that may fluctuate significantly over short periods. The large change likely reflects test-retest variability rather than genuine biological aging, which weakens the claim that NMN "maintained" biological age.
2. **Baseline NAD values vary widely between groups** (7.95 nM for 600 mg vs. 11.8 nM for 300 mg vs. 10.5 nM for 900 mg vs. 8.11 nM for placebo). With n=20 per group and SDs as high as 11.7 nM, the baseline imbalance could affect change-from-baseline analyses.
3. **NAD measurement method:** Colorimetric kit (MBS841786) measuring total NAD+ + NADH in serum. The dramatic increases (e.g., 7.95 -> 45.3 nM for 600 mg, a ~5.7x increase) seem large compared to other trials using LC-MS/MS methods. Different measurement methods may explain discrepant results across the NMN literature.
4. **HOMA-IR within-group increases** for 600 mg (1.70 -> 2.14, p=0.037) and 900 mg (2.01 -> 2.67, p=0.006) deserve attention: while not significant vs. placebo, this trend of worsening insulin resistance at higher NMN doses could be a safety signal.
