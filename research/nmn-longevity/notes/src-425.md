# src-425: The NAD+/sirtuin pathway modulates longevity through activation of mitochondrial UPR and FOXO signaling

**Authors:** Mouchiroud L, Houtkooper RH, Moullan N, Katsyuba E, Ryu D, Canto C, Mottis A, Jo Y-S, Viswanathan M, Schoonjans K, Guarente L, Auwerx J

**Venue:** Cell, 2013; 154(2):430-441. DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2013.06.016

**PMCID:** PMC3753670 | **PMID:** 23870130

**Quality:** ok -- full text available from PMC, substantive paper with methods, results, discussion.

---

## Core Findings

This landmark study establishes that NAD+ levels decline with age in both mice and C. elegans, and that restoring NAD+ levels -- either by inhibiting the NAD+-consuming enzyme PARP or by supplementing NAD+ precursors (NR, NAM) -- extends lifespan in C. elegans through a sir-2.1/SIRT1-dependent pathway. The mechanism involves two sequential mitochondrial stress responses: (1) early activation of the mitochondrial unfolded protein response (UPRmt) driven by mitonuclear protein imbalance, and (2) later nuclear translocation of the FOXO transcription factor DAF-16, which induces antioxidant defense (sod-3). Both UPRmt and FOXO/DAF-16 are necessary for the longevity benefit. The pathway is conserved in mammalian cells (AML12 hepatocytes, primary mouse hepatocytes), where SIRT1 is required for UPRmt induction by AZD2281 or NR.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### NAD+ decline with aging
- Aged mice (103 weeks) showed markedly increased PARylation and robustly decreased NAD+ levels in liver and muscle vs. young mice (24 weeks).
- In C. elegans, PARylation increased and NAD+ decreased with age (two-way ANOVA: p<0.008 for age, p=0.02 for genotype in pme-1 mutants).
- Lipofuscin (aging pigment) accumulation was reduced in pme-1 mutants.

### Lifespan extension data (C. elegans)
- pme-1(ok988) mutation: 29% mean lifespan extension
- pme-1 RNAi (rrf-3 background): 20% mean lifespan extension
- AZD2281 (100 nM): 22.9% lifespan extension
- ABT-888 (100 nM): 15% lifespan extension
- NR (500 uM): ~16% lifespan extension (p=0.01)
- NAM (200 uM): lifespan extension (optimal dose established)
- Combined optimal AZD2281 + NR: no additive benefit (~16%, same as individual), suggesting shared pathway
- Combined suboptimal AZD2281 (10 nM) + NR (100 uM): synergistic 11% extension (p=0.01)

### sir-2.1 dependence
- All lifespan extension by AZD2281 and NR was abrogated in sir-2.1(ok434) mutants.
- Outcrossed sir-2.1 transgenic worms (MV389 strain) showed 25% lifespan extension, fully dependent on sir-2.1, daf-16, and ubl-5 (UPRmt regulator).

### Mechanism: UPRmt and FOXO signaling
- **Day 1 (early phase):** AZD2281/NR induced mitonuclear protein imbalance (decreased ATP5A/MTCO1 ratio), ROS burst (MitoSOX), and UPRmt (hsp-6::GFP), but not sod-3.
- **Day 3 (late phase):** Continued UPRmt + strong induction of sod-3::GFP; DAF-16 nuclear translocation (2-3x increase vs. vehicle).
- ubl-5 RNAi blocked both UPRmt activation and lifespan extension by AZD2281/NR.
- daf-16 RNAi blocked lifespan extension and was required for sir-2.1 overexpression longevity.
- ubl-5 RNAi blocked sod-3 induction, suggesting UPRmt feeds into antioxidant defense.

### Mammalian conservation
- AML12 hepatocytes: NR (1 mM) and AZD2281 (1 uM) increased mtDNA/nDNA ratio, Mfn2 expression, Hsp60 promoter activity, CLPP protein, and SOD2 protein/activity.
- Mitonuclear protein imbalance (SDHA/MTCO1 ratio) observed in mammalian cells.
- SIRT1 overexpression in primary hepatocytes induced UPRmt (CLPP, HSP60) and SOD2.
- SIRT1 knockout (Sirt1 L2/L2 floxed + ad-Cre) completely blocked NR/AZD2281-induced UPRmt.

## Methodology

- **Primary model:** C. elegans lifespan assays with 60-100 animals per condition, scored every other day; Kaplan-Meier survival analysis with log-rank test.
- **Genetic tools:** pme-1(ok988) mutant, sir-2.1(ok434) mutant, MV389 sir-2.1 transgenic, rrf-3(pk1426) RNAi-sensitive strain, various GFP reporters (hsp-6::GFP for UPRmt, sod-3::GFP for ROS defense, daf-16::GFP for localization).
- **Pharmacological tools:** PARP inhibitors AZD2281 (olaparib) and ABT-888 (veliparib); NAD+ precursors NR and NAM.
- **Mammalian validation:** AML12 hepatocyte cell line; primary hepatocytes from Sirt1 floxed mice; adenoviral SIRT1 overexpression and Cre-mediated knockout.
- **Assays:** Oxygen consumption (Seahorse XF96), mtDNA/nDNA ratio, ATP levels, RT-qPCR, Western blot, confocal microscopy (mito::GFP, daf-16::GFP), MitoSOX ROS probe.

## Limitations

1. **C. elegans as primary model:** The lifespan data are from nematodes; direct lifespan extension was not demonstrated in mammals. Mammalian data are limited to cell-based assays (no in vivo longevity data in mice).
2. **NR doses not directly translatable:** NR at 500 uM in worm media and 1 mM in cell culture are pharmacological concentrations; clinical relevance for human NMN/NR supplementation requires bridging studies.
3. **NMN not directly tested:** The study uses NR and NAM as NAD+ precursors, not NMN specifically, though NMN is referenced as another established NAD+ precursor.
4. **sir-2.1 debate acknowledged:** The authors note the controversy around sir-2.1's role in basal lifespan (Burnett et al., 2011; Viswanathan and Guarente, 2011) and address it using the outcrossed MV389 strain.
5. **Paraquat confound:** NAD+ depletion via paraquat could cause lifespan shortening through DNA damage rather than NAD+ depletion alone; the authors address this with the genetic qns-1 RNAi approach as an alternative.
6. **No human data:** All experiments are in worms and mouse cells; no clinical data.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (What is NMN and how does it work?):** Directly relevant -- establishes the NAD+/sirtuin pathway as central to metabolic regulation and aging, and shows NAD+ precursors (NR, NAM, with NMN cited as analogous) can activate this pathway. Describes mechanism through UPRmt and FOXO/DAF-16.
- **Q2 (Does NMN extend lifespan?):** Provides strong C. elegans evidence that NAD+ boosting (via PARP inhibition or NAD+ precursors) extends lifespan 15-29%. NMN is cited as an established NAD+ precursor though not directly tested.
- **Q3 (What doses and protocols?):** Reports optimal NR at 500 uM and NAM at 200 uM in worms; AZD2281 at 100 nM. Suboptimal combined doses show synergy. No NMN dosing data.
- **Q4 (Safety and side effects?):** No direct safety data; combined optimal doses showed no additive toxicity.
- **Q5 (Mechanisms of action?):** Highly relevant -- detailed mechanistic dissection of NAD+/sir-2.1/UPRmt/FOXO pathway with temporal analysis (early UPRmt, late DAF-16/sod-3). Identifies mitonuclear protein imbalance as the upstream trigger.
- **Q6 (How does NMN compare?):** Establishes that NR, NAM, and PARP inhibitors converge on the same sir-2.1-dependent pathway, with shared ceiling effect -- relevant for comparing NAD+-boosting strategies.

## Claims to Verify

- The 25% lifespan extension for MV389 sir-2.1 transgenic worms (Figure 5A) is noted as slightly higher than the ~10% previously reported by Viswanathan and Guarente (2011); the authors attribute this to censoring worms with protruding vulva phenotype. This methodological difference should be noted when citing the effect size.
- The claim that NAD+ levels were "robustly decreased in older mice" references supplementary figures (S1B) that are not available in this text. The primary data for this key claim rests on referenced prior work (Braidy et al., 2011; Yoshino et al., 2011).
