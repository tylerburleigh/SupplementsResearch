# src-129: It takes two to tango: NAD+ and sirtuins in aging/longevity control

**Authors:** Imai, Shin-ichiro & Guarente, Leonard
**Year:** 2016
**Venue:** npj Aging and Mechanisms of Disease
**Citations:** 350
**DOI:** 10.1038/npjamd.2016.17
**Type:** Review article

---

## Core Findings

This review by two leading sirtuin researchers synthesizes the mechanistic framework connecting NAD+ metabolism and sirtuin activity to aging and longevity control across organisms. The central thesis is that NAD+ availability declines with age due to reduced NAMPT-mediated NAD+ biosynthesis and chronic PARP-mediated NAD+ depletion, and that this decline compromises sirtuin activity, disrupting both nuclear-mitochondrial communication (cellular level) and hypothalamus-adipose tissue communication (systemic level). Supplementation with NAD+ intermediates, particularly NMN and NR, is proposed as a therapeutic strategy to mitigate these age-associated declines.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Age-related NAD+ decline
- NAMPT and NAD+ levels decline with age in multiple organs: pancreas, adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, liver, and brain (lines 229-236)
- Oxidative stress and/or inflammatory cytokines are suggested mechanisms for decreased NAMPT expression
- PARP is chronically activated in aging worms and mice, contributing to NAD+ depletion (lines 320-327)

### Sirtuin-mediated lifespan extension in mammals
- Brain-specific SIRT1-overexpressing (BRASTO) transgenic mice show significant delay in aging and lifespan extension in both sexes (lines 106-110)
- Whole-body SIRT1-overexpressing mice fail to show lifespan extension -- brain/hypothalamic SIRT1 is specifically implicated
- Whole-body SIRT6 transgenic mice show lifespan extension, but only in males (lines 112-114)

### NAD+/Sirtuin kinetics
- Km values for NAD+ of SIRT1-3 are consistent with physiological NAD+ fluctuation directly regulating sirtuin activity (lines 79-82)
- Nicotinamide acts as a non-competitive sirtuin inhibitor (IC50 for SIRT1: 175 uM for acetylated substrate)
- Plasma nicotinamide ranges from 0.3 uM (human) to 5 uM (mouse)
- NADH is unlikely to be a physiologically relevant competitive inhibitor at reported physiological ranges

### Systemic regulation via eNAMPT
- Mammals have intracellular (iNAMPT) and extracellular (eNAMPT) forms
- eNAMPT is secreted from adipose tissue in a SIRT1-dependent manner (deacetylation at lysine 53)
- eNAMPT remotely controls hypothalamic NAD+ biosynthesis and SIRT1 activity
- ANKI mice (adipose-specific iNAMPT overexpression) show increased circulating eNAMPT, hypothalamic NAD+, and physical activity
- Serum eNAMPT shows diurnal rhythm in humans (peaking in early afternoon) (lines 207-211)

### NAD+ competition among consumers
- Sirtuins, PARPs, and CD38/157 ectoenzymes compete for NAD+ availability
- PARP1 inhibition increases NAD+ content and SIRT1 activity, enhancing oxidative metabolism
- CD38 knockout mice show increased NAD+ and SIRT1 activity

### Mitochondrial communication
- NAD+ deficiency causes pseudohypoxic state via decreased SIRT1, stabilizing HIF-1alpha, reducing TFAM expression and mitochondrial gene expression
- SIRT1 and SIRT3 involved in mitochondrial UPR (UPRmt) and mitophagy
- NR enhancement activates UPRmt genes in worms, mammalian cells, and skeletal muscle stem cells

### NMN and supplementation
- NMN is rapidly incorporated into major metabolic tissues and converted to NAD+
- NAMPT is induced by low glucose, fasting, caloric restriction, exercise, and stress
- Nampt transcription is mediated by circadian transcription factors CLOCK/BMAL, rendering NAD+ biosynthesis circadian
- Supplementation with NMN and NR proposed for treating type 2 diabetes, Alzheimer's, heart failure, and hearing loss (lines 276-283)

## Methodology

This is a narrative review article. It synthesizes findings from primary research studies across yeast, C. elegans, Drosophila, mice, and humans. No original experimental data are presented. The review draws heavily from the authors' own laboratories (Imai lab at Washington University; Guarente lab at MIT) and integrates findings from the broader literature. The scope covers biochemical mechanisms (kinetics, enzymology), genetic models (transgenic and knockout mice), and pathophysiological observations.

## Limitations

1. **Review, not primary data:** No new experimental results; dependent on the quality and interpretation of cited primary studies.
2. **Author bias potential:** Both authors are founders of companies in the NAD+/sirtuin space (SI co-founded Metro Midwest Biotech; LG founded Elysium Health). Competing interests are disclosed.
3. **Mostly animal data:** The mechanistic framework is built predominantly on mouse and lower-organism studies. Human clinical evidence for NMN/NR supplementation is not discussed in detail.
4. **Dated (2016):** The review predates several major human NMN clinical trials (2019-2024). The claims about NMN supplementation efficacy are based on mouse studies.
5. **Selective focus:** Emphasizes the NAD+/sirtuin axis, with less attention to other NAD+-consuming pathways and alternative NAD+ precursors.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Mechanisms of NAD+ decline):** Strong relevance. Provides detailed mechanistic framework for age-related NAD+ decline via NAMPT reduction and PARP chronic activation. Covers the pseudohypoxic state, nuclear-mitochondrial communication breakdown, and kinetic evidence for NAD+ regulation of sirtuins.

- **Q2 (NAD+ and sirtuin functions):** Central to this review. Comprehensive coverage of SIRT1, SIRT3, SIRT6 functions; the three-level regulation of NAD+/sirtuin connection; inter-tissue communication via eNAMPT; and competition with PARPs and CD38.

- **Q3 (NMN supplementation effectiveness):** Moderate relevance. Reviews the rationale for NMN/NR supplementation and cites mouse studies showing efficacy, but does not present human clinical trial data. The therapeutic claims are based on animal model evidence.

- **Q4 (Optimal dosing/regimen):** Not addressed. No dosing, bioavailability, or pharmacokinetic data discussed in detail.

- **Q5 (Safety and side effects):** Indirectly relevant. Notes that NMN is rapidly incorporated into tissues, but does not discuss safety profile or adverse effects.

- **Q6 (Comparison with NR, etc.):** Limited. Both NMN and NR are mentioned as NAD+ intermediates, but no head-to-head comparison or systematic evaluation of different precursors.

## Claims to Verify

- The BRASTO mouse lifespan extension claim (line 106-110) references Satoh et al. 2013 (Cell Metab. 18, 416-430). Worth verifying the magnitude and statistical significance in the primary paper.
- SIRT6 transgenic mouse lifespan extension "only in males" (line 113-114) references Kanfi et al. 2012 (Nature 483, 218-221). The sex-specificity of this finding should be verified.
- "NMN is rapidly incorporated to major metabolic tissues and converted to NAD+" (line 213-214) -- this claim from Yoshino et al. 2011 should be verified for the specific tissues and pharmacokinetic timeline.
