# src-020: Effectiveness of PLP in PNPO Deficiency (Systematic Review)

**Citation:** Stolwijk NN, van Dussen L, Reijnhout ND, et al. "Effectiveness of Pyridoxal-5'-Phosphate in PNPO Deficiency: A Systematic Review." *J Inherit Metab Dis.* 2025;48(5):e70074. doi:10.1002/jimd.70074

**Source type:** Peer-reviewed systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42024542199)
**Quality:** OK -- full text available, substantive content with methods, results, discussion
**Relevance to P5P/healthspan/Crohn's:** Directly relevant to PLP/P5P pharmacology, dosing, and safety. PNPO deficiency is an ultra-rare disease; findings on hepatotoxicity and therapeutic window are transferable to broader P5P supplementation considerations. No direct IBD/Crohn's content.

---

## Core Findings

1. PLP (P5P) effectively controls seizures in 77.6% of PNPO deficiency patients (38/49) and significantly improves survival vs. untreated siblings (p < 0.001). PLP is the only viable option for most PNPO patients because pyridoxine (PN) fails in 90.9% of PLP-responsive cases.
2. The therapeutic window for PLP is narrow: subtherapeutic doses cause seizure recurrence, while high-dose long-term use is associated with hepatotoxicity (elevated transaminases in 20.4%, cirrhosis in 4.1% of treated patients).
3. PLP is not authorized as a medicinal product (EMA/FDA), forcing reliance on food supplements with variable quality, purity, and dose accuracy -- a significant safety concern.

## Key Evidence

- **49 PNPO deficiency patients** treated with PLP across 30 studies (all case reports/series)
- **Median PLP dose:** 35.5 mg/kg/day (range 10-74 mg/kg/day) for responsive patients
- **Seizure outcomes:** Complete cessation in 27/49, improvement in 10/49, no response in 7/49, unclear in 4/49
- **Survival benefit:** 10/9 untreated siblings died (median age at death 32.6 days) vs. all PLP-treated siblings survived (p < 0.001)
- **Developmental outcomes (PLP monotherapy, n=24):** Normal 41.7%, minor/moderate delay 25.0%, severe delay 30.3%. Earlier PLP initiation correlated with better outcomes.
- **Adverse events (30.6% of patients):** Elevated transaminases (20.4%), vomiting (8.2%), feeding difficulties (6.1%), liver cirrhosis (4.1%), administration difficulties (6.1%), PLP intoxication due to labeling error (2.0%)
- **Hepatotoxicity mechanism hypotheses:** (1) PLP degradation products (alpha,beta-unsaturated carbonyl compounds) via intestinal bacteria; (2) non-enzymatic Schiff base formation modifying proteins; (3) PAP (4-pyridoxic acid 5'-phosphate) from photodegradation inhibiting PLP-dependent enzymes
- **Cirrhosis cases:** 2 patients on high-dose (50-100 mg/kg/day) long-term PLP; both confirmed by biopsy
- **Unpublished reports** of hepatocellular carcinoma in PNPO patients on long-term PLP

## Methodology

- Systematic review following PRISMA guidelines, registered PROSPERO CRD42024542199
- Search: PubMed, Embase, ClinicalTrials.gov (15 April 2024)
- 1049 records identified, 782 screened, 80 full-text reviewed, 30 included
- Risk of bias: Murad et al. tool for case reports/series: 2 low, 15 moderate, 13 high risk
- Narrative synthesis (meta-analysis not feasible due to heterogeneity)
- Survival analysis: Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank test for subgroup comparison

## Limitations

- All included studies are case reports or case series (lowest level of evidence for treatment efficacy)
- 43.3% of studies had high risk of bias
- Small sample size (49 patients total) -- inherent to ultra-rare disease
- Limited data on PLP dosing in non-responsive patients (dose reported for only 2/7)
- Mean PLP use duration of 3 years may miss long-term adverse effects (cirrhosis, HCC)
- No controlled trials; no comparison group beyond historical untreated siblings
- **Not generalizable to healthy populations or IBD/Crohn's patients** -- doses used are far higher than typical supplementation doses

## Claims to Verify

- **Median dose 35.5 mg/kg/day**: Reported in Table 1 and text (line ~369-371). Confirmed consistent across both locations.
- **77.6% seizure responsiveness (38/49)**: Directly verifiable from n=38/49. Confirmed.
- **p < 0.001 for survival comparison**: This compares 9 PLP-treated siblings vs. 10 deceased untreated siblings. Very small sample for survival analysis; the p-value should be interpreted with caution given n<10 per group.
- **90.9% PN failure rate**: Calculated as 30/33 PN-attempted patients. Confirmed.
- **20.4% hepatotoxicity**: 10/49 patients. Confirmed.
- **Cirrhosis at 50-100 mg/kg/day doses**: Only 2 patients; cirrhosis may be confounded by PNPO disease itself or duration of therapy. The paper notes mechanism remains unclear.
