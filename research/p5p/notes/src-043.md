## Core Findings

This study identifies a novel biochemical mechanism by which endogenous L-delta-1-pyrroline-5-carboxylic acid (P5C) -- a metabolite that accumulates in the inherited disorder hyperprolinemia type II -- chemically de-activates pyridoxal phosphate (PLP, the active B6 coenzyme). The mechanism involves a Claisen condensation (Knoevenagel-type reaction) at physiological conditions (pH 7.4, 310 K), producing three novel adducts. P5C is described as a "unique endogenous vitamin antagonist." Preliminary observations suggest P5C also reacts with other aromatic and aliphatic aldehydes and ketones, indicating a broader generic addition reaction.

## Key Evidence

1. **Mechanism of PLP de-activation:** The activated C-4 carbon of the pyrroline ring condenses with the aldehyde carbon of PLP, forming stable adducts that remove PLP from the active coenzyme pool.
2. **Three novel adducts identified** via high-resolution 1H NMR spectroscopy and mass spectrometry; structures confirmed by HPLC, NMR, and MS.
3. **Clinical context:** Vitamin B6 deficiency was observed in a child with hyperprolinemia type II presenting with seizures -- not explained by diet or medication, suggesting endogenous PLP consumption.
4. **Therapeutic implication:** Seizures may be preventable with long-term vitamin B6 supplementation.

## Methodology

- **In vitro** biochemical investigation using high-resolution 1H nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy and mass spectrometry
- Physiological conditions: pH 7.4, temperature 310 K (37 C)
- Adduct structures confirmed by HPLC, NMR, and mass spectrometry
- Originated from a clinical observation of B6 deficiency in a child with hyperprolinemia type II
