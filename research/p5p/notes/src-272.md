# Source src-272: Vitamin B6 prevents excessive inflammation via SPL-S1P axis

**Citation:** Du X, Yang Y, Zhan X, et al. Vitamin B6 prevents excessive inflammation by reducing accumulation of sphingosine-1-phosphate in a sphingosine-1-phosphate lyase-dependent manner. *J Cell Mol Med.* 2020;24(22):13129-13138. doi:10.1111/jcmm.15917

**PMID:** 32967056 | **PMCID:** PMC7701526 | **Open Access:** Yes

## Core Findings

This study identifies a novel anti-inflammatory mechanism of vitamin B6 (pyridoxal/PL): it enhances the enzyme activity of sphingosine-1-phosphate lyase (SPL), a PLP-dependent enzyme, thereby reducing accumulation of the pro-inflammatory lipid sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) in macrophages. The downstream effect is suppression of NF-kB and MAPK signaling pathways, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine production. Vitamin B6 supplementation at 20 mg/kg/day protected mice from lethal endotoxic shock and attenuated experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE, a model of multiple sclerosis).

## Methodology

- **In vivo models:** C57BL/6 mice (6-8 weeks); LPS-induced acute inflammation (5 mg/kg) and lethal endotoxic shock (10 mg/kg); MOG(35-55)-induced EAE model
- **In vitro model:** Bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) from C57BL/6 mice and Sgpl1-/- knockout mice
- **Vitamin B6 dose:** 20 mg/kg bodyweight/day oral (in vivo); 500 uM PL (in vitro)
- **SPL knockout:** Sgpl1+/- mice bred to generate Sgpl1-/- littermates; homozygous knockouts could not be used for in vivo experiments due to severe developmental defects
- **Assays:** ELISA for cytokines and S1P; Western blot for NF-kB/MAPK phosphorylation; qPCR for cytokine mRNA; SPL enzyme activity assay using tritium-radiolabeled substrate
- **Statistics:** Unpaired two-tailed t-test or one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni correction; log-rank test for survival; P<0.05 significant
- **Limitations:** No blinding during animal experiments; small group sizes (n=5-10 per group)

## Key Evidence

1. **Cytokine suppression (in vivo):** Vitamin B6 (20 mg/kg/day) significantly reduced serum IL-1-beta, TNF-alpha, IL-6, and NO in LPS-injected mice (n=5/group).
2. **Survival benefit:** Vitamin B6 delayed death and improved survival in lethal endotoxic shock (10 mg/kg LPS, n=10/group, P<0.001 by log-rank test).
3. **Signaling pathway suppression:** PL pre-treatment reduced phosphorylation of NF-kB p65, p38, ERK, and JNK in BMDMs (Western blot).
4. **SPL-S1P mechanism:** PL enhanced SPL enzyme activity (not expression), reducing S1P levels. S1P supplementation dose-dependently reversed PL's anti-inflammatory effects. SPL deficiency (Sgpl1-/- BMDMs) completely eliminated PL's anti-inflammatory effects.
5. **EAE model:** Vitamin B6 (20 mg/kg/day) significantly lowered EAE clinical scores, serum S1P, and pro-inflammatory cytokines vs. controls (P<0.001).

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Mechanism of action):** Highly relevant. Demonstrates PLP's role as SPL cofactor; SPL degrades S1P, reducing NF-kB/MAPK signaling. Novel mechanistic pathway identified.
- **Q2 (Inflammatory conditions):** Directly addresses anti-inflammatory effects in LPS-induced inflammation and autoimmune disease (EAE/MS model).
- **Q3 (Dose-response/efficacy):** Single dose tested (20 mg/kg/day in vivo, 500 uM in vitro). S1P dose-response reversal experiments demonstrate the SPL-S1P axis is the mechanism.
- **Q4 (Autoimmune/neurodegenerative):** EAE model shows vitamin B6 attenuates MS-like disease progression. References inverse relationship between B6 intake and Parkinson's/Alzheimer's risk.
- **Q7 (Crohn's/IBD):** Indirectly relevant. Paper mentions low plasma PLP linked to IBD (citing Selhub et al. 2013) and S1P's role in IBD pathogenesis (citing Suh & Saba 2015), but does not directly study Crohn's disease. S1P is noted as a therapeutic target in IBD.

## Limitations

- No blinding during animal experiments (acknowledged by authors)
- Small sample sizes (n=5-10 per group)
- Single dose of vitamin B6 tested; no dose-response curve for efficacy
- Sgpl1-/- mice could not be used for in vivo experiments due to developmental defects
- No direct testing in IBD/Crohn's disease models
- In vitro PL concentration (500 uM) is pharmacologically high

## Claims to Verify

- **20 mg/kg/day dose in mice:** Stated in Methods (line 379) and consistent across all experiments. Appears reliable.
