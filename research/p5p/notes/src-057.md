# Source src-057: PLP Concentrations in Children on Isoniazid for TB

## Metadata
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20146723
- **Type**: PubMed abstract (abstract only; full text behind paywall)
- **Population**: Children (including adolescents, preschool-age, infants) with TB in Western Cape, South Africa
- **Study design**: Prospective cohort, PLP measured at enrollment, 1 month, and 4 months
- **Sample size**: 19 HIV-infected + 33 HIV-uninfected = 52 total

## Core Findings

This study examined plasma pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (PLP) concentrations in children receiving isoniazid (INH) for tuberculosis treatment. All children received a supplement meeting pyridoxine requirements. Despite supplementation, PLP levels remained suboptimal, especially in HIV-infected children. After 4 months of INH treatment (4-20 mg/kg/day), HIV-infected children had significantly lower PLP (mean 6.75 ng/mL) compared to HIV-uninfected children (mean 14.76 ng/mL, p < 0.001). The study recommends additional pyridoxine supplementation for malnourished children on TB treatment, particularly those with HIV.

## Key Evidence

1. **Baseline PLP**: HIV-infected children 8.32 ng/mL vs HIV-uninfected 11.28 ng/mL (p=0.11)
2. **After 4 months**: HIV-infected 6.75 ng/mL vs HIV-uninfected 14.76 ng/mL (p<0.001)
3. **Suboptimal PLP (<6 ng/mL)**: At enrollment 50% HIV+ vs 15% HIV- (p=0.016); at 4 months 42% vs 6% (p=0.004)
4. **INH dosage**: 4-20 mg/kg daily
5. **Children received a supplement meeting pyridoxine requirements** yet PLP still declined in HIV-infected group

## Methodology

- Prospective cohort study
- Setting: Western Cape, South Africa (hospitalized TB management)
- PLP measured at 3 time points: enrollment, 1 month, 4 months
- Plasma PLP determined by standard assay
- All subjects received pyridoxine supplementation meeting requirements (exact dose not specified in abstract)

## Limitations

- **Abstract only**: Full text not available; methods details, exact supplementation dose, and adverse events are not accessible
- **Small sample size**: 52 total (19 HIV+, 33 HIV-)
- **Specific population**: South African children with TB on INH -- generalizability to adult IBD/Crohn's populations is limited
- **No control group** without supplementation
- **Exact pyridoxine supplement dose not reported** in abstract
- **Publication year unknown** (metadata fields empty)

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Pharmacokinetics)**: Moderately relevant. Shows PLP plasma levels under drug-induced depletion (isoniazid is a known B6 antagonist). Demonstrates that even with supplementation meeting requirements, PLP can remain suboptimal in certain populations (HIV, malnutrition). Suboptimal threshold defined as <6 ng/mL.
- **Q3 (B6 deficiency in Crohn's/IBD)**: Tangentially relevant. Demonstrates drug-induced PLP depletion as a mechanism, which parallels concerns in IBD patients on certain medications. However, the population (TB/HIV children) and drug (isoniazid) are quite different from Crohn's disease.
- **Q5 (Dosing/safety)**: Mildly relevant. Shows that standard pyridoxine supplementation may be insufficient when drug-induced depletion is present, suggesting dose escalation may be needed in specific populations.
- **Q2, Q4, Q6, Q7**: Not directly relevant.

## Claims to Verify

- The exact pyridoxine supplementation dose is described only as "meeting pyridoxine requirements" -- the actual dose and form (pyridoxine HCl vs P5P) are not specified in the abstract and would need verification from the full text.
- Sample size is small (n=52 total) and the p-values are quite strong for this sample size; worth confirming the statistical methods from the full text.
