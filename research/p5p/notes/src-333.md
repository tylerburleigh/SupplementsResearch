## Core Findings

Vitamin B6 deficiency is significantly more prevalent in Crohn's disease (CD) patients than in ulcerative colitis (UC) patients. Specific disease characteristics -- including small bowel involvement, ileocolonic disease (L3), extraintestinal manifestations, prior ileal resection, and immunosuppressant use -- are independently associated with abnormal B6 status. B6 deficiency is also associated with altered gut microbial composition and metabolic pathway profiles in CD patients.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **Sample size:** 360 CD patients + 55 UC patients from Shanghai Tenth People's Hospital (Tongji University)
- **Study design:** Retrospective analysis using serum vitamin B6 concentrations from computerized laboratory data; logistic regression for risk factor identification
- **Primary outcome:** Prevalence of vitamin B6 abnormality was significantly higher in CD than UC (exact percentages not available from abstract)
- **Independent risk factors for B6 abnormality in CD (logistic regression):**
  - Small bowel lesion
  - Ileocolonic lesion (L3 classification)
  - Extraintestinal manifestations
  - Ileal resection
  - Immunosuppressor usage
- **Microbiome substudy:** 20 CD patients (10 normal B6, 10 deficient); 16S rRNA sequencing showed significant differences in microbial structure; PICRUSt2 prediction revealed differential enzymes and metabolic pathways between groups
- **Published in:** European Journal of Clinical Nutrition (Nature Publishing Group), peer-reviewed
- **Citation count:** 7

## Methodology

- Single-center retrospective study at a tertiary IBD center in Shanghai, China
- Serum vitamin B6 levels extracted from laboratory records
- Logistic regression for identifying independent risk factors
- Microbiome analysis: 16S rRNA gene sequencing with SILVA database, QIIME/mothur pipelines, UniFrac for beta diversity, LEfSe for differential enrichment, PICRUSt2 for functional prediction (KEGG/MetaCyc pathways)
- Data deposited in NCBI SRA (BioProject: PRJNA867573)

## Limitations

- **Single-center, retrospective design** -- limits generalizability; no longitudinal follow-up
- **Abstract-only access** -- exact prevalence numbers, odds ratios, confidence intervals, and p-values not available for extraction
- **Small microbiome substudy** (n=20) -- underpowered for robust microbiome conclusions
- **Chinese population** -- findings may not generalize to other ethnicities
- **No intervention component** -- purely observational; cannot establish causation or assess supplementation effects
- **Serum B6 measured but not specified whether PLP (pyridoxal-5-phosphate) or total B6**

## Relevance to Research Questions
