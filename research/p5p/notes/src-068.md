## Core Findings

1. **Vitamin B6 supplementation improved social behaviors in BTBR mice** (an established autism spectrum disorder model), with the effect linked to modulation of vitamin B6/PLP metabolism and mitochondrial function.
2. **Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) from healthy humans alleviated social deficits** in BTBR mice and normalized their plasma metabolic profile, notably reducing elevated long-chain acylcarnitines.
3. **Specific Bacteroides species were linked to the PLP Biosynthesis I pathway**, suggesting that gut microbes contribute to PLP production and that FMT restores this pathway.

## Key Evidence

- **Study design**: BTBR mice (ASD model) received either human FMT, saline, or vitamin B6 supplementation. n=10 per group for FMT experiment; n=8 per group for B6 experiment.
- **Statistical analysis**: Two-way ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc test for interaction times; one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's post hoc test for sociability/social novelty indices.
- **Multi-omics integration**: Colon-content metagenomics, colon-content metabolomics, and plasma metabolomics were combined using WGCNA modules and correlation networks.
- **Key pathway identified**: Vitamin B6 metabolism (KEGG reaction R00174) linked to social behavior change via Bacteroides species and PLP biosynthesis.
- **Acylcarnitine reduction**: FMT normalized elevated long-chain acylcarnitines, connecting vitamin B6 metabolism to mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation.

## Methodology

- **Animal model**: BTBR T+ Itpr3tf/J mice (widely used ASD model with inherent social deficits)
- **Groups**: BTBR+FMT, BTBR+Saline, B6+Saline (control strain)
- **FMT protocol**: Human-derived fecal microbiota transplanted into BTBR mice
- **Behavioral tests**: Three-chamber social behavior test (sociability + social novelty)
- **Omics**: Metagenomics (colon content), metabolomics (colon content + plasma), WGCNA integration
