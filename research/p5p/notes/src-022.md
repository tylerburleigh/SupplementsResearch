## Core Findings

This RCT evaluated combined B-vitamin supplementation (methylfolate, P5P, methylcobalamin) in 54 patients aged 40-75 with elevated homocysteine and moderate LDL-C, stratified by MTHFR, MTR, and MTRR genetic polymorphisms. After 6 months, the treatment group showed significant reductions in homocysteine (-30.0%, p<0.01) and LDL-C (-7.5%, p<0.01). Homozygous minor allele carriers had dramatically greater reductions (homocysteine -48.3%, LDL-C -11.8%) compared to mixed allele carriers (homocysteine -18.6%, LDL-C -4.8%), demonstrating that genetic profiling can personalize B-vitamin supplementation for cardiovascular risk reduction.

## Key Evidence

1. **Homocysteine reduction**: 30.0% overall (95% CI: -39.7% to -20.3%, p<0.01)
2. **LDL-C reduction**: 7.5% overall (95% CI: -10.3% to -4.7%, p<0.01)
3. **Homozygous minor allele carriers**: Homocysteine -48.3% (95% CI: -62.3% to -34.3%), LDL-C -11.8% (95% CI: -15.8% to -7.8%)
4. **Mixed allele carriers**: Homocysteine -18.6% (95% CI: -25.6% to -11.6%), LDL-C -4.8% (95% CI: -6.8% to -2.8%)
5. **Between-group differences**: Homocysteine 29.7% (p<0.01), LDL-C 7.0% (p<0.01)

## Methodology

- **Design**: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **Sample**: 54 patients, aged 40-75, with elevated homocysteine and moderate LDL-C
- **Intervention**: Combination of methylfolate + P5P + methylcobalamin vs placebo, 6 months
- **Stratification**: Patients divided by MTHFR, MTR, MTRR genetic polymorphisms
- **Primary outcomes**: Homocysteine and LDL-C levels at 3 and 6 months

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size** (n=54) -- may be underpowered for subgroup analyses
2. **Abstract only** -- no full text available for verification of dosages, adverse events, or detailed methods
3. **Industry affiliation**: Four authors employed by Triangel Scientific (commercial research laboratory)
4. **Combination intervention**: Cannot isolate P5P's independent contribution from methylfolate and methylcobalamin
5. **No individual dose data**: Specific dosages of each B-vitamin not reported in abstract
6. **No safety/adverse event data**: Tolerability not described in available content
