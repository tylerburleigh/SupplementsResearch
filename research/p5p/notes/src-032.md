## Core Findings

This study examined the dose-response relationship of oral pyridoxine to plasma pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP) and pyridoxal (PL) levels in 9 female volunteers. The key finding is a **plateau effect**: plasma PLP increased significantly (p < 0.05) from 10 to 25 mg and from 25 to 50 mg, but 100 mg produced no further PLP increase over 50 mg. However, plasma PL (non-phosphorylated form) continued to rise (3.85x at 100 mg vs 50 mg within 3 hours) but was rapidly eliminated. Renal clearance of PL remained consistently low (<2% of supplement) regardless of dose.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: Pharmacokinetics of P5P supplementation
**Directly relevant.** Shows PLP saturation at 50 mg oral pyridoxine, rapid PL elimination at higher doses, and low renal excretion. Key pharmacokinetic constraint: hepatic phosphorylation of pyridoxine to PLP saturates at moderate doses.

### Q5: Dosing and safety
**Relevant.** Suggests 50 mg as a pharmacokinetic ceiling for PLP response; doses above this do not increase PLP further but generate excess PL that is cleared. This informs upper dosing limits.

### Q6: Tradeoffs
**Relevant.** The plateau effect is a key tradeoff: higher pyridoxine doses produce PL (which must be cleared) without additional PLP benefit. This may have implications for supplement form selection (P5P vs pyridoxine).
