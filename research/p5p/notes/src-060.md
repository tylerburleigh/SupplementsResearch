# src-060: Vitamin B6 Supplementation as Targeted Therapy for SPL Insufficiency Syndrome

**Source:** PubMed abstract page (PMID 32233035), published 2020, SSIEM / Wiley
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32233035
**Quality flag:** degraded (paywall detected; abstract and figure legends available, full text behind paywall)
**Peer-reviewed:** Likely (journal publication, NIH-funded)

## Core Findings

This paper demonstrates that vitamin B6 (pyridoxine and pyridoxal 5'-phosphate/PLP) supplementation serves as a potential targeted therapy for SPL insufficiency syndrome (SPLIS), a rare genetic disorder caused by mutations in SGPL1 encoding sphingosine phosphate lyase (SPL), a B6-dependent enzyme. In two of four patients treated, B6 supplementation improved disease biomarkers including lymphocyte counts and sphingolipid profiles. The study proposes that B6 acts via a chaperone mechanism, stabilizing mutant SPL enzyme variants, and that ~30% of SPLIS patients carrying the R222Q variant may benefit.

## Key Evidence

### Clinical responses
- **Patient 1:** Pyridoxine treatment increased absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) (P=.0025, N=14 pre vs N=17 post) and percent lymphocytes (P=.0013).
- **Patient 4:** PLP and pyridoxine treatment increased ALC (P=7.2e-5, N=4 pre vs N=13 post), percent lymphocytes (P=.0002), and T-cell subsets CD3/CD4/CD8 (all P<.017).
- **Patient 4 plasma S1P:** Increased after PLP treatment initiation, measured by targeted mass spectrometry.

### In vitro fibroblast studies
- SPL protein expression reduced in all 4 patient fibroblast lines vs control (P<.05 for patients 1-3, P<.01 for patient 4).
- SPL enzyme activity significantly reduced in all patient fibroblasts vs control (P<.0003 each).
- S1P and dihydroS1P elevated in patient fibroblasts (consistent with SPL deficiency).
- **Patient 4 fibroblasts** showed dose-dependent response to PN (0.3-50 uM): decreased S1P accumulation (P<.005 for 0.3, 1.0, 10 uM; P<.002 for 50 uM) and increased SPL activity (P=.035 for 50 uM PN).
- **Patients 1 and 4 fibroblasts** responded to PN treatment; patients 2 and 3 did not show significant response -- correlating with in vivo clinical responses.

### Molecular mechanism
- SPL R222Q variant (~30% prevalence in SPLIS patients) distorts the dimer interface via electrostatic repulsion.
- PLP bound at the dimer interface may stabilize the mutant protein, acting as a pharmacological chaperone.

## Methodology

- Retrospective clinical monitoring of disease biomarkers in B6-supplemented SPLIS patients.
- SPL activity and sphingolipid profiling in patient-derived fibroblasts (primary and transformed).
- Mass spectrometry-based sphingolipid quantitation.
- Molecular modeling (PyMol) of PLP-SPL interaction at dimer interface.
- Dose-response studies with PN at 0.3, 1.0, 10, and 50 uM in fibroblasts.

## Limitations

- Small sample: only 4 patients studied, 2 showing clinical response.
- Retrospective clinical data (no prospective controlled trial).
- Full text behind paywall -- analysis based on abstract and figure legends only.
- Genetic disease model (rare monogenic disorder), not directly generalizable to general population supplementation.

## Claims to Verify

- "30% of SPLIS patients" carry R222Q variant -- this statistic appears only in the abstract and should be verified against original prevalence data.
- Specific P-values for patient blood counts: these are based on small N (4-17 measurements per condition) and may not reflect robust statistical power.
- The chaperone mechanism is proposed based on molecular modeling, not direct experimental proof of protein stabilization.
