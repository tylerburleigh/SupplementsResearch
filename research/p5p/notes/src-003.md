## Core Findings

P-5-P (pyridoxal-5'-phosphate) is the biologically active coenzyme form of vitamin B6 that drives over 140 enzyme reactions. While P-5-P supplements are marketed as superior to pyridoxine due to bypassing hepatic conversion, **no clinical trial has validated this advantage**. The article acknowledges this evidence gap prominently. The article reviews conditions associated with low P-5-P levels (IBD, CVD, depression, dementia, cancer, diabetes) and discusses specific niches where P-5-P may have advantages (PLP-dependent epilepsy, sideroblastic anemia with pyridoxal-kinase deficiency).

## Methodology

This is a narrative review/blog article, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. No formal search strategy, inclusion criteria, or quality assessment of cited studies is described. The article references ~80 PubMed-linked citations. Claims are categorized by evidence strength ("Insufficient Evidence" sections are clearly marked).

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Pharmacokinetics (Q1)
- Two liver enzymes convert other B6 forms into P-5-P: **pyridoxal (PL) kinase** and **pyridoxine-5'-phosphate (PNP) oxidase** (lines 137-145)
- Genetic defects in these enzymes reduce P-5-P levels (line 147-148)
- In 17 patients with liver disease, IV P-5-P increased plasma levels better than equal-dose pyridoxine HCl (lines 415-418). **Caveat:** tiny sample, IV route, no oral data
- High pyridoxine doses (>1000 mg/day) may paradoxically block conversion enzymes, reducing P-5-P levels (lines 518-523)

### Healthspan Pathways (Q2)
- **Homocysteine:** B6, B12, and folate break down homocysteine; protects heart, brain, reproductive health (lines 40-54)
- **Inflammation:** Low P-5-P associated with IBD, rheumatoid arthritis (lines 165-173)
- **Cognition:** Low B6 reduces serotonin and GABA production; P-5-P drives enzymes for dopamine, norepinephrine, serotonin, GABA, acetylcholine (lines 56-65, 259-280). Whether supplemental P-5-P reaches the brain is unknown (line 280)
- **CVD:** Low P-5-P associated with heart disease (lines 175-181); B6 may help with atherosclerosis (line 346)
- **Sleep:** B6 increased melatonin release in a study of 120 children (lines 246-255)

### B6 Deficiency in Crohn's/IBD (Q3, Q4)
- Low P-5-P levels are present in IBD (lines 165-173)
- Experts suggest preventive supplementation in people with digestive disorders including IBD and celiac disease (lines 316-329)
- No specific P-5-P intervention studies in IBD/Crohn's are mentioned

### Safe Dosing and Neuropathy Risk (Q5)
- **Largest P-5-P trial:** 250 mg/day for 30 days was safe but had limited efficacy; 750 mg/day provided no further benefits (lines 582-588)
- **Pyridoxine neuropathy threshold:** >1000 mg/day can cause nerve damage, movement disorders, skin lesions (lines 504-511)
- **P-5-P safety unknown long-term** -- the conversion bypass is theoretical advantage but safety data lacking (line 524)
- P-5-P may cause digestive side effects: vomiting, diarrhea, stomach cramps; increased liver enzymes in newborns (lines 528-548)
- P-5-P suppressed prolactin and growth hormone in one 1982 pilot study of 6 healthy people and in mouse/cell studies (lines 294-311)
- **Safe upper limit for pyridoxine: 100 mg/day; no established limit for P-5-P** (lines 566-567)
- Pregnant women should avoid P-5-P (lines 567-570)

### Tradeoffs and Adversarial Evidence (Q6, Q7)
- **Critical evidence gap:** "No valid clinical evidence supports the potential P-5-P advantages" over pyridoxine (lines 390-396)
- P-5-P may suppress prolactin, growth hormone, and ACTH (lines 550-566) -- potential endocrine disruption
- P-5-P not FDA-approved for any medical use (line 576)
- Case reports of sideroblastic anemia and PLP-dependent epilepsy are the only direct evidence of P-5-P superiority (lines 462-497)

## Limitations

2. **No systematic methodology.** Narrative review with no formal search strategy or quality appraisal.
3. **Citation quality unknown.** While PubMed references are provided, the article does not distinguish between animal studies, cell studies, case reports, and clinical trials in its text (only in the "Insufficient Evidence" disclaimers).
4. **Potential commercial bias.** SelfHacked/SelfDecode sells supplements and genetic testing services.
