## Core Findings

1. Nineteen children with nephrotic syndrome and low plasma PLP levels were supplemented with oral pyridoxine hydrochloride at ~2 mg/kg/day, which achieved adequate plasma PLP levels in most cases.
2. A strong positive correlation (r = 0.83, p < 0.001) existed between plasma albumin and PLP levels both before and after supplementation, suggesting albumin binding or concurrent loss may govern PLP status.
3. Vitamin B6 repletion had no demonstrable effect on the clinical course of nephrotic syndrome, though supplementation was still recommended due to potential roles of B6 deficiency in immune dysfunction and thromboembolic complications.
