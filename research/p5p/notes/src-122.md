# Source src-122: Sakakeeny et al. (2012) - Plasma PLP Inversely Associated with Systemic Inflammatory Markers

**Citation:** Sakakeeny L, Roubenoff R, Obin M, Fontes JD, Benjamin EJ, Bujanover Y, Jacques PF, Selhub J. Plasma Pyridoxal-5-Phosphate Is Inversely Associated with Systemic Markers of Inflammation in a Population of U.S. Adults. J Nutr. 2012;142(7):1280-1285. doi:10.3945/jn.111.153056

**Note:** The directive referenced this as "Morris et al." but the actual first author is Sakakeeny. Morris et al. (2010) is cited as reference 17 within this paper (a related earlier study on B-6 intake and inflammation in NHANES).

## Core Findings

In 2229 participants from the Framingham Offspring Study (55% women, mean age 61 +/- 9 y), higher systemic inflammation -- measured by a composite score of 13 inflammatory biomarkers -- was strongly and inversely associated with plasma pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP) concentrations. Those in the highest tertile of inflammation had ~25% lower geometric mean PLP (61 nmol/L) compared to those in the lowest tertile (80 nmol/L; P-trend < 0.0001). This relationship persisted after adjustment for vitamin B-6 intake, homocysteine, and other B-vitamins, suggesting inflammation itself depletes PLP status regardless of dietary intake.

## Key Evidence

1. **Overall PLP-inflammation gradient:** Geometric mean PLP across IS tertiles: T1 = 80 (74, 86), T2 = 71 (67, 76), T3 = 61 (57, 65) nmol/L, P-trend < 0.0001. Approximately 25% reduction from lowest to highest inflammation.

2. **Functional sub-scores all significant:** Inverse associations observed for IS-acute phase reactants (P < 0.001), IS-cytokines (P < 0.001), IS-oxidative stress (P < 0.001), and IS-selectins (P = 0.08). ICAM-1 individually significant (P < 0.001).

3. **PLP inadequacy prevalence doubled:** The proportion of participants with PLP < 20 nmol/L nearly doubled from lowest to highest inflammation tertile categories.

4. **Individual biomarkers inversely associated:** CRP, fibrinogen, IL-6, LPL-A2 activity, TNFR-2, TNF-alpha, and ICAM-1 each showed inverse associations with PLP after multivariable adjustment. These remained significant after additional adjustment for CRP.

5. **B-6 intake does not explain the association:** Even among participants with the highest vitamin B-6 intake, higher inflammation was associated with significantly lower PLP. No significant interaction between inflammation and B-6 intake was observed.

6. **Specificity to B-6:** No inverse relationship was found between plasma folate or vitamin B-12 and overall inflammation, indicating the PLP-inflammation link does not extend to other B-vitamins.

7. **Robust to disease exclusion:** Excluding 401 participants with prevalent CVD or diabetes did not substantively alter findings.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Cross-sectional analysis of the Framingham Offspring Study, 7th examination cycle (1998-2001).
- **Population:** 2229 men and women from a community-based cohort; largely white/European ancestry.
- **Exposure:** Inflammation measured via 13 biomarkers (CRP, fibrinogen, IL-6, TNF-alpha, TNFR-2, osteoprotegerin, P-selectin, CD40L, ICAM-1, MCP-1, myeloperoxidase, LPL-A2 mass, LPL-A2 activity, isoprostanes). Combined into overall IS (summed Z-scores) and functional sub-scores.
- **Outcome:** Plasma PLP measured by tyrosine decarboxylase apoenzyme method.
- **Analysis:** Multivariable regression with log-transformed PLP as dependent variable, inflammation indices as tertile-category independent variables. Adjusted for age, sex, BMI, homocysteine, folate, B-12, creatinine, cholesterol, B-6 intake, protein intake, energy intake, multivitamin use, NSAID use, smoking.
- **Inadequacy threshold:** PLP < 20 nmol/L per IOM Dietary Reference Intakes.

## Proposed Mechanisms

The authors discuss two pathways by which inflammation may deplete PLP:
1. **Kynurenine pathway:** Inflammatory stimuli upregulate indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase, increasing tryptophan-to-kynurenine conversion. Downstream kynurenine degradation requires multiple PLP-dependent enzymes, potentially mobilizing PLP from plasma.
2. **Serine hydroxymethyltransferase (SHMT):** PLP is a required cofactor for SHMT, which is upregulated in stimulated lymphocytes. Inhibition of PLP-dependent enzymes reduces lymphocyte proliferation and cytokine production (IL-1b, IL-2, IL-2 receptor expression).

## Relevance to Crohn's Disease

The paper cites IBD-specific evidence: low PLP in IBD was seen only in patients with active disease, while those in remission had normal PLP levels without increased B-6 intake (Saibeni et al., 2003; Kuroki et al., 1993). This supports the hypothesis that inflammation depletes PLP rather than PLP deficiency causing inflammation. The finding that B-6 supplementation corrected PLP levels but did not improve inflammation markers in rheumatoid arthritis (Chiang et al., 2005) further supports a unidirectional inflammation-to-PLP-depletion model.

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference.
- Framingham cohort is predominantly white/European ancestry; limited generalizability.
- Participants had higher median B-6 intake (3.1 mg/d) than U.S. population (1.6 mg/d in NHANES supplement users); PLP depletion from inflammation may be more severe in populations with lower B-6 intake.
- Multiple statistical tests raise false-positive risk, though most associations remain significant after correction.
- Single time-point biomarker measurements.
