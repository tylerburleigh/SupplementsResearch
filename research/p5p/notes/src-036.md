# src-036: NiPPeR RCT - Maternal B-vitamin Status and Supplementation

**Citation:** Godfrey KM, Titcombe P, El-Heis S, et al. (2023) PLOS Medicine 20(12): e1004260
**Quality:** ok | **Venue:** PLOS Medicine | **Citations:** 20 | **Type:** Double-blind RCT

## Core Findings

This is a multicenter (UK, Singapore, New Zealand) double-blind RCT of 1,729 women planning pregnancy, randomized to standard vs. enhanced multinutrient supplements (including 2.6 mg/day vitamin B6) from preconception through delivery. The study provides the most detailed longitudinal data on plasma P5P (pyridoxal 5-phosphate) kinetics from preconception through pregnancy to 6 months postpartum. The key finding for P5P research: **54.2% of unsupplemented women developed low P5P (<20 nmol/L) by late pregnancy** despite only 0.8% having low P5P at baseline -- demonstrating dramatic B6 depletion during pregnancy that was largely prevented by a modest 2.6 mg/day B6 supplement (reducing prevalence to 10.2%).

## Key Evidence

### P5P Pharmacokinetics (Q1)
- Plasma P5P in controls: stable from baseline to 1 month, modest decline to early pregnancy (-0.25 SDS), then **steep decline to late pregnancy (-1.11 SDS)**
- Median P5P values (controls): baseline 55.8 nmol/L, early pregnancy 47.6 nmol/L, **late pregnancy 19.1 nmol/L**, postdelivery 56.3 nmol/L (return to baseline)
- Intervention group P5P: baseline 57.1, late pregnancy 41.9, postdelivery 56.1 nmol/L
- The late-pregnancy P5P crash is consistent with >10-fold estrogen increase inhibiting kynurenine pathway enzymes

### Supplementation Effects (Q5)
- **Dose:** 2.6 mg/day vitamin B6 (as part of multinutrient supplement, taken twice daily as powder)
- After 1 month: +1.07 SDS P5P (95% CI 0.99-1.14, p<0.0001)
- Late pregnancy: +0.84 SDS P5P (95% CI 0.70-0.97, p<0.0001) vs controls
- 6 months postdelivery: no difference (both returned to baseline) -- B6 has no persistent stores unlike B12

### Functional Markers (Q2)
- HK ratio (B6 insufficiency marker): rose 1.03 SDS in controls from early to late pregnancy
- Supplement reduced HK ratio by 0.51-0.63 SDS at each time point
- Cystathionine/cysteine ratio (transsulfuration marker): rose 1.36 SDS in late pregnancy in controls; reduced by 0.23-0.37 SDS in intervention group
- These functional markers confirm B6-dependent pathways are impaired in late pregnancy and amenable to correction with modest supplementation

### Dosing Context (Q5)
- US IOM RDA for B6 in pregnancy: 1.9 mg/day
- European Community recommendation: 2.5-4 mg/day to maintain pre-pregnancy levels
- Trial dose of 2.6 mg/day was within OTC supplement range and substantially prevented deficiency
- The dose was described as "amounts enhanced above those typical in over-the-counter products"

## Methodology

- **Design:** Double-blind, multicenter RCT (NCT02509988); prespecified secondary outcome
- **Population:** 1,729 women aged 18-38, UK/Singapore/New Zealand, planning conception
- **Intervention:** Enhanced supplement (B6 2.6 mg/day + B12 5.2 ug + riboflavin 1.8 mg + vitamin D 10 ug + zinc + myo-inositol + probiotics) vs. control (folic acid 400 ug + iron + calcium + iodine + beta-carotene)
- **Duration:** Preconception through delivery; 6-month postpartum follow-up
- **Measurement:** LC-MS/MS (Bevital, Bergen) for P5P and vitamers; CVs <10%
- **P5P threshold:** <20 nmol/L defined as low B6 status
- **Adherence:** 80.6% had 80-100% adherence; similar between groups
- **Singleton pregnancies reaching 28 weeks:** 580 women

## Limitations

- Not specific to P5P supplementation -- B6 was given as part of multinutrient formulation (form not specified in text, likely pyridoxine HCl)
- Study focused on healthy reproductive-age women in high-income countries; no data on Crohn's/IBD populations
- Secondary outcome of the trial (primary was gestational glycemia)
- Cannot isolate B6 effect from other components (riboflavin is a cofactor for B6 metabolism)
- B6 form not explicitly stated as P5P vs. pyridoxine

## Claims to Verify

- The exact form of B6 in the supplement is not explicitly stated in the extracted text. The trial protocol (reference 16) should be consulted. It is most likely pyridoxine HCl, not P5P.
- The assertion that "amounts enhanced above those typical in over-the-counter products" for B6 (line 349) seems to contradict the statement that doses were "available in over-the-counter supplements" (abstract/Conclusions). The 2.6 mg/day dose is indeed higher than many prenatal vitamins but is OTC-available.
