# src-058: The Relationship Between Vitamin B6, Diabetes and Cancer

**Authors:** Merigliano C, Mascolo E, Burla R, Saggio I, Verni F (2018)
**Venue:** Frontiers in Genetics, Volume 9, Article 388
**DOI:** 10.3389/fgene.2018.00388
**Citations:** 55
**Type:** Mini Review (peer-reviewed)
**Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

This review presents evidence primarily from Drosophila models demonstrating that PLP (pyridoxal 5'-phosphate) safeguards genome integrity and that PLP deficiency combined with hyperglycemia synergistically increases DNA damage. The authors propose PLP deficiency as a mechanistic link explaining the elevated cancer risk observed in diabetic patients. PLP counteracts DNA damage primarily through inhibition of Advanced Glycation End-product (AGE) formation; PLP deficiency permits AGE accumulation, which generates ROS and leads to DNA double-strand breaks and chromosome aberrations.

## Key Evidence

- **PLP cofactor scope:** PLP acts as coenzyme in approximately 160 enzymatic activities spanning amino acid, carbohydrate, and lipid metabolism. It also directly quenches ROS and counteracts AGE formation.
- **Genome integrity in Drosophila:** dPdxk1 mutants show chromosome aberrations (CABs) at ~6% vs. 0.5% in controls, fully rescued by PLP supplementation. Vitamin B6 antagonists (4-DP, penicillamine, cycloserine, isoniazid) produce CAB rates of 3-19%.
- **Glucose-CAB synergy:** 1% glucose treatment increases CABs from 6% to 20% in dPdxk1 mutants. This effect is conserved in PDXK-depleted HeLa cells.
- **AGE-mediated mechanism:** Alpha lipoic acid (AGE inhibitor) rescues both AGEs and CABs, confirming the AGE-mediated DNA damage pathway.
- **Diabetes + PLP depletion synergy:** In two Drosophila T2D models, PLP inhibitor 4-DP produced CAB rates of 60-80% vs. 25% in wild-type cells, demonstrating synergistic interaction.
- **Epidemiological support:** Inverse correlation between blood PLP levels and colorectal cancer risk; high PDXK expression positively correlated with NSCLC survival.
- **Clinical evidence for B6 in diabetes:** B6 administration reported beneficial for diabetic neuropathy, nephropathy, retinopathy, and gestational diabetes.

## Methodology

- Mini review synthesizing primarily Drosophila-based experimental work from the authors' own lab (Marzio et al., 2014; Merigliano et al., 2018) plus epidemiological studies and clinical reports.
- Drosophila used as model for both cancer biology and type 2 diabetes; T2D modeled via insulin pathway gene knockdown (InR, chico/IRS1, Akt1) and high-sugar diet.
- Human relevance supported by conservation in HeLa cells and epidemiological meta-analyses.

## Limitations

- Primary experimental evidence is from Drosophila models; direct human clinical data on PLP supplementation for cancer prevention in diabetics is limited.
- Review is heavily weighted toward the authors' own prior publications.
- No specific dosing recommendations or pharmacokinetic data for PLP supplementation.
- No discussion of Crohn's disease, IBD, or gastrointestinal conditions.
- Cause-effect direction between low PLP and diabetes remains unresolved (low PLP may cause or result from diabetes).

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Pharmacokinetics):** Minimal relevance. Notes the salvage pathway (PL, PM, PN converted by PDXK and PNPO to PLP) but provides no PK parameters.
- **Q2 (Healthspan pathways):** Strongly relevant. Provides mechanistic evidence for PLP's role in genome integrity, AGE inhibition, ROS scavenging, and the diabetes-cancer nexus. The AGE-mediated DNA damage pathway is a clear healthspan mechanism.
- **Q3 (B6 deficiency in Crohn's):** Not directly relevant. Mentions celiac disease as a cause of PLP deficiency but does not discuss Crohn's or IBD.
- **Q4 (IBD interventions):** Not relevant. No IBD-specific intervention data.
- **Q5 (Dosing/safety):** Limited relevance. Notes B6 has beneficial effects at adequate intake but no dosing or safety data discussed.
- **Q6 (Tradeoffs):** Some relevance. The dose-response relationship between PLP levels and genome protection suggests a clear benefit-to-deficiency gradient, but no upper-limit risks discussed.
- **Q7 (Adversarial):** Limited. Notes that the causal direction of PLP-diabetes relationship is unresolved and that human mechanistic evidence is limited to epidemiological correlations.

## Claims to Verify

- "about 160 distinct enzymatic activities" -- this is cited to Percudani and Peracchi (2003) and is a commonly cited figure but should be verified from the primary source.
- CAB percentages (6%, 0.5%, 20%, 60-80%, 25%) are all from the authors' own prior work and appear consistent across the review.
