## Core Findings

This landmark individual participant data meta-analysis of 30 observational studies (5073 IHD events, 1113 stroke events, ~16,786 participants) found that elevated homocysteine is **at most a modest independent predictor** of ischemic heart disease and stroke in healthy populations. After full adjustment for smoking, systolic blood pressure, and cholesterol, a 25% lower usual homocysteine (~3 umol/L, the typical reduction from folic acid supplementation) was associated with only an 11% lower IHD risk (OR 0.89, 95% CI 0.83-0.96) and 19% lower stroke risk (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.69-0.95). Substantial confounding by smoking, blood pressure, and cholesterol was demonstrated.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Primary results (prospective studies, fully adjusted)
- **IHD:** OR 0.89 (95% CI 0.83-0.96) per 25% lower usual homocysteine (~3 umol/L)
- **Stroke:** OR 0.81 (95% CI 0.69-0.95) per 25% lower usual homocysteine
- 12 prospective studies, 1968 IHD events, 463 stroke events
- Age- and sex-adjusted OR was 0.83 for IHD; adjustment for smoking, SBP, and cholesterol attenuated to 0.89

### Confounding analysis
- Homocysteine correlated with current smoking, total cholesterol, and systolic blood pressure
- Chi-squared statistic dropped from 24 (age/sex adjusted) to 9 (fully adjusted), indicating substantial confounding
- Authors suggest the homocysteine-disease relationship may be "largely due to confounding by the usual blood pressure"

### Retrospective vs. prospective comparison
- Retrospective studies showed much stronger associations (OR 0.67 for IHD with population controls)
- Prospective studies showed weaker associations (OR 0.83 for IHD)
- Significant heterogeneity between designs (chi-squared=21, P<0.001), consistent with reverse causality in retrospective studies

### MTHFR genetic evidence
- Accompanying MTHFR C677T meta-analysis: TT vs CC genotype associated with 16% higher IHD risk (95% CI 5%-28%)
- Concordance between genetic and observational estimates provides some support for causality

### Regression dilution correction
- Regression dilution ratio of 0.75 for IHD (5-year mean interval) and 0.77 for stroke (4-year mean interval)
- Based on repeat homocysteine measurements in 2318+500 individuals

## Methodology

- **Design:** Individual participant data (IPD) meta-analysis using conditional logistic regression
- **Search:** MEDLINE 1966-January 1999, supplemented by hand-searching and personal communication
- **Studies:** 30 studies total (12 prospective, 13 retrospective with population controls, 5 retrospective with other controls)
- **Participants:** Individuals aged 40+ without preexisting cardiovascular disease; homocysteine 3-40 umol/L
- **Analysis:** Conditional logistic regression stratified by study; log2 homocysteine transformation; correction for regression dilution bias; adjustment for smoking, SBP, total cholesterol
- **Statistical software:** SAS version 8.1

## Limitations

1. **Confounding cannot be fully excluded:** Measurement error in confounders means residual confounding likely remains
2. **Predominantly male:** 85% of participants were men (88% in prospective studies)
3. **Homocysteine measured once at baseline:** Regression dilution correction applied but based on limited repeat measurements
4. **Pre-fortification era:** Studies conducted before widespread folic acid fortification; folate status of participants generally lower
5. **No direct B6/P5P analysis:** This study focused on homocysteine as the exposure; it does not directly examine B6 or P5P supplementation effects
6. **Publication bias possible:** Authors note that small studies with less extreme findings may not have been reported
7. **Observational only:** Cannot establish causation; authors call for large randomized trials

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q4 (Cardiovascular disease prevention):** Directly relevant. Provides the most rigorous pre-RCT observational evidence that homocysteine is only a modest independent predictor of IHD and stroke. The 25% homocysteine reduction achievable via folic acid (with B6/B12) would yield at most ~11% IHD risk reduction and ~19% stroke risk reduction. This sets expectations for B6/P5P-mediated homocysteine lowering effects on CVD.

**Q5 (Mechanisms of action):** Highly relevant. Demonstrates that confounding by smoking, blood pressure, and cholesterol substantially explains the homocysteine-vascular disease association. The MTHFR genetic data provide some Mendelian randomization evidence for causality. The modest effect size after adjustment suggests any P5P effect on CVD via homocysteine lowering would be small.

**Q1-Q3 (General healthspan, deficiency, optimal dosing):** Tangentially relevant. Establishes that homocysteine is a biomarker influenced by B-vitamin status (including B6), but does not directly address P5P supplementation dosing or deficiency states.

**Q6-Q7 (Crohn's disease):** Not directly relevant. The study does not examine inflammatory bowel disease populations, though Crohn's patients may have elevated homocysteine due to malabsorption of B vitamins.
