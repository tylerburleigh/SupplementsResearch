# src-124: Dietary Vitamin B6 Intake Modulates Colonic Inflammation in the IL10-/- Model of IBD

**Authors:** Selhub J, Byun A, Liu Z, Mason JB, Bronson RT, Crott JW
**Venue:** Journal of Nutritional Biochemistry
**Year:** 2013
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jnutbio.2013.08.005
**PMID:** 24183308
**PMCID:** PMC4199223
**Quality note:** Metadata flagged as "degraded" due to paywall detection, but full text was accessible via PMC with complete methods, results, discussion, and references.

## Core Findings

This study demonstrates that both vitamin B6 deficiency (0.5 mg/kg diet) and moderate supplementation (24 mg/kg diet, 4x replete level) significantly attenuate histological and molecular markers of colonic inflammation by approximately 60% in IL10-/- mice infected with Helicobacter hepaticus, a well-established model of IBD. The biphasic (bell-shaped) dose-response relationship is novel and suggests two distinct mechanisms: (1) B6 supplementation promotes clearance of the chemotactic lipid sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) from the colon via S1P lyase, a PLP-dependent enzyme, reducing T-cell infiltration; (2) B6 deficiency may trap T cells in the thymus by impairing S1P lyase there, preventing their migration to the colon. This is the first report of B6 supplementation suppressing IBD pathology.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Study Design
- **Model:** IL10-/- mice (C3Bir.129P2 background) colonized with Helicobacter hepaticus
- **Diet groups:** 0.5 mg/kg (deficient), 6.0 mg/kg (replete/control), 24 mg/kg (supplemented) pyridoxine HCl
- **Duration:** 12 weeks starting from weaning
- **Sample size:** 10-11 mice per group; 9 baseline mice euthanized at weaning
- **Sex:** Both males and females included

### Primary Outcomes
- **Plasma PLP:** 85% lower in deficient group, 30% higher in supplemented group vs. replete (P<.0001)
- **Plasma-colon PLP correlation:** R=0.47, P=.009
- **Histological inflammation score (0-3 scale):** Bell-shaped relationship with dietary B6 (P=.05); strengthened when stratified by PLP tertile (P<.05); ~60% reduction in both lowest and highest PLP tertiles vs. mid-tertile
- **Inflammatory gene expression:** TNF-alpha, IL-6, IFN-gamma, iNOS, COX-2 all showed bell-shaped suppression pattern (Delta Ct correlated with inflammatory score, R=-0.41 to -0.55, P<.05)
- **S1P-PLP inverse correlation:** R=-0.52, P<.01, N=29
- **Kynurenine:tryptophan ratio:** Bell-shaped relationship with PLP (ANOVA P=.04)

### Safety Observation
- Male mice on deficient diet showed ~10% weight deficit (P=.045, repeated measures weeks 4-7); females unaffected
- No tumors observed (prior studies report 14-65% in this model)
- Supplemented group showed no adverse effects

## Methodology

### Strengths
- Pair-feeding design ensuring equal caloric intake across groups
- Blinded histological scoring by expert pathologist
- Multi-modal assessment: histology (H&E), gene expression (qRT-PCR), biochemistry (PLP, S1P, tryptophan metabolites)
- Both molecular and histological endpoints converge on same conclusion
- Inclusion of both sexes

### Limitations
- Animal model (IL10-/- mice), not human trial; generalizability to human IBD/Crohn's is uncertain
- Small sample size (10-11/group); histological inflammation difference only borderline significant (P=.05)
- Not powered for sex-stratified analysis
- Biphasic relationship means moderate supplementation is beneficial but the optimal dose range is narrow
- The mechanism for why B6 deficiency also reduces inflammation remains speculative (T-cell trapping hypothesis based on literature, not directly demonstrated)
- No direct measurement of S1P lyase activity -- only correlative evidence via S1P levels
- Form used was pyridoxine HCl, not P5P directly

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1 (B6 bioavailability/pharmacokinetics)
Plasma PLP increased 30% with 4x dietary supplementation over 12 weeks. Colonic PLP tracked plasma levels (R=0.47). Shows that dietary B6 (as pyridoxine HCl) reaches colonic tissue effectively.

### Q2 (Inflammation modulation)
Strong direct evidence that B6 modulates inflammation in the colon. Both deficiency and supplementation reduce inflammatory markers. The S1P lyase pathway is identified as a plausible mechanism. Inverse PLP-S1P correlation (R=-0.52) supports this.

### Q4 (IBD/Crohn's interventions) -- KEY PAPER
This is one of the most directly relevant studies for B6/P5P in IBD. Key findings:
1. First demonstration that B6 supplementation (not just deficiency) suppresses IBD pathology
2. Identifies S1P lyase as mechanistic link between B6 and colonic inflammation
3. IBD clinical context provided: 40-60% of patients don't benefit from available treatments; 60-80% of CD patients eventually need surgery
4. Authors explicitly propose B6 supplementation as therapeutic strategy for IBD management
5. Notes B6 is inexpensive, freely available, and non-toxic at moderate levels

### Q7 (Safety)
Supplementation at 4x replete showed no adverse effects. Deficiency caused growth retardation in males. Authors cite that B6 does not produce toxicity at moderate supplementation levels even for extended periods (ref 17: Huang et al., EJCN).

## Claims to Verify

1. **Sample size**: Stated as "10-11/gp" -- verify if exact numbers differ between males/females and if any attrition occurred (not explicitly stated)
2. **"Approximately 60% reduction"**: This is described for both histological score and gene expression; the histological ANOVA P=.05 is borderline, suggesting the effect size may be less robust than implied
3. **IBD prevalence figures**: "25-250 per 100,000" and "40-60% will not benefit from available IBD treatments" are cited from reviews (refs 14-16) but should be verified in primary sources
4. **"First report of suppression of IBD with supplemental B6"**: This claim appears valid based on the authors' literature review but should be cross-checked against subsequent publications

## Mechanistic Summary

The biphasic (bell-shaped) relationship between PLP and inflammation may be explained by two distinct S1P-mediated mechanisms operating in different tissues:

- **High B6 (supplemented):** Increases S1P lyase activity in colon --> degrades S1P locally --> reduces chemotactic signaling --> fewer T-cells infiltrate colon --> less inflammation
- **Low B6 (deficient):** Impairs S1P lyase in thymus --> S1P accumulates in thymus --> destroys concentration gradient (low thymus/high blood S1P) --> T-cells trapped in thymus --> cannot migrate to colon --> less inflammation

This dual mechanism explains why both extremes of B6 status reduce inflammation through different S1P-related pathways, while moderate B6 (replete) allows normal T-cell egress and colonic infiltration.
