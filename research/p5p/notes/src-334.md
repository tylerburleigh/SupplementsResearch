# src-334: Micronutrient Insufficiency in Crohn's Disease (Systematic Review)

**Source URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38004171
**PMID:** 38004171
**Type:** Systematic Review (PubMed abstract only; full text not available)
**Content Quality:** Abstract-level only (PubMed landing page). No full text available for detailed extraction.

## Core Findings

This is a systematic review synthesizing literature on micronutrient insufficiency in adults with Crohn's disease (CD) in clinical remission. It included 42 studies, of which 30 were rated medium/high quality, covering 21 micronutrients. The review found that CD populations consistently contain individuals with low levels of Vitamins A, B6, B12, C, beta-carotene, D, Magnesium, Selenium, and Zinc. However, the strongest evidence is limited to Vitamins D and B12; studies on other micronutrients including B6 are insufficient in number, standardization, and quality to inform clinical practice.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **42 studies** met inclusion criteria; **12 were rated low quality** and excluded from synthesis, leaving **30 medium/high quality studies** covering **21 micronutrients**.
- **Vitamin B6 is explicitly listed** among micronutrients consistently reported as low in CD remission populations.
- Vitamins D and B12 were the most frequently reported nutrients (8 and 11 studies respectively); there were few eligible studies for remaining micronutrients including B6.
- Comparator studies (CD vs. healthy controls) were inconsistent; even for Vitamin D (most reported), only one-quarter of studies found lower levels in CD vs. healthy controls.
- Mechanisms of insufficiency in CD: restrictive eating, malabsorption, abnormal losses, or inflammation.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review (PRISMA flow chart referenced).
- **Population:** Adults with Crohn's disease predominantly in clinical remission.
- **Inclusion criteria:** Studies where the CD population was predominantly in remission.
- **Quality assessment:** Studies rated for quality; 12 low-quality studies excluded from synthesis.
- **Outcomes:** (1) Prevalence of low circulating micronutrient concentrations; (2) Comparison against healthy controls.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q2 (Crohn's disease):** Directly relevant. Confirms Vitamin B6 insufficiency is consistently reported in CD patients in remission. However, the evidence base for B6 specifically is thin -- fewer studies than for D or B12, and insufficient to inform practice.
- **Q5 (Disease-specific populations):** Relevant. Identifies CD patients in remission as a population at risk for multiple micronutrient insufficiencies, with mechanisms (malabsorption, restrictive eating, inflammation) that could impair B6/P5-P status.
- **Q1, Q3, Q4, Q6, Q7:** Not directly addressed. No data on P5-P specifically, no supplementation trials, no effect sizes for B6, no dosage or safety data.

## Limitations

- **Abstract-only content:** Full text was not available; this summary is based solely on the PubMed abstract. No prevalence percentages, confidence intervals, or specific effect sizes could be extracted.
- **No B6-specific prevalence data:** The abstract reports B6 among nutrients with consistently low levels but does not quantify the prevalence.
- **Evidence gap acknowledged by authors:** Studies on micronutrients other than D and B12 are insufficient to inform practice.
- **Conflict of interest:** Authors received support from Nestle Health Sciences (industry-funded but investigator-led).

## Claims to Verify

- The exact number of studies reporting on Vitamin B6 specifically is not stated in the abstract; the full text would be needed to determine this.
- The claim that B6 is "consistently reported" as low needs verification -- this could mean as few as 2-3 studies.
- No quantitative prevalence data available from abstract.
