# src-375: Plasma Homocysteine as a Risk Factor for Dementia and Alzheimer's Disease

**Seshadri S, Beiser A, Selhub J, Jacques PF, Rosenberg IH, D'Agostino RB, Wilson PWF, Wolf PA.**
NEJM 2002; 346:476-83. ~3212 citations.

## Core Findings

Elevated plasma total homocysteine is a strong, independent, graded risk factor for the development of dementia and Alzheimer's disease in a prospective, community-based elderly cohort. The association persisted after adjustment for age, sex, APOE genotype, plasma B vitamin levels (folate, B12, B6/P5P), and vascular risk factors. Homocysteine levels measured 8 years before baseline also predicted incident dementia, establishing temporal precedence.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Prospective observational cohort (Framingham Study)
- **Sample:** 1,092 subjects without dementia (667 women, 425 men; mean age 76 +/- 6 years, range 68-97)
- **Follow-up:** Median 8 years (range 1-13), through December 31, 2000
- **Exposure:** Plasma total homocysteine measured at 20th biennial exam (baseline, 1986-1990) and 8 years earlier at 16th exam (1979-1982, n=935)
- **Outcomes:** Incident dementia (n=111) and Alzheimer's disease (n=83) diagnosed by neurologist/neuropsychologist committee blinded to homocysteine levels
- **Analysis:** Cox proportional-hazards regression; homocysteine log-transformed and analyzed both continuously (per SD) and by age-specific quartiles

## Key Evidence

### Primary risk estimates (multivariable-adjusted)
| Model | Dementia RR (95% CI) | Alzheimer's RR (95% CI) |
|-------|---------------------|------------------------|
| Per SD log-homocysteine, baseline | 1.4 (1.1-1.9) | 1.8 (1.3-2.5) |
| Per SD log-homocysteine, 8 yr prior | 1.4 (1.1-1.9) | 1.6 (1.2-2.1) |
| >14 umol/L (hyperhomocysteinemia) | 1.9 (1.3-2.8) | 1.9 (1.2-3.0) |
| Highest quartile vs. others | 1.9 (1.3-2.9) | 1.9 (1.2-3.1) |
| Highest quartile + vitamin adjustment | 2.5 (1.5-4.4) | 2.8 (1.4-5.4) |
| Sustained high (Q4 at both times) | 2.2 (1.2-4.1) | 2.2 (1.1-4.4) |

### Dose-response
- Each 5 umol/L increment in homocysteine increased AD risk by 40% (P<0.001)
- Quartile-specific RRs for AD (vs Q1): Q2=1.2 (0.6-2.2), Q3=1.3 (0.6-2.5), Q4=2.2 (1.2-4.1)

### P5P/Vitamin B6 relevance
- Plasma pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (P5P) was measured in 92% of subjects (mean 74.7 nmol/L in men, 79.9 nmol/L in women)
- Assay: tyrosine decarboxylase apoenzyme method (CV 16%)
- B6 levels did NOT independently predict dementia after age/sex/APOE adjustment
- Adjustment for P5P (along with folate and B12) did NOT diminish the homocysteine-dementia association; in fact, the HR strengthened slightly (1.4 -> 1.8 for AD)
- This suggests homocysteine's effect on dementia is not mediated through B6 status; rather, B6's role in lowering homocysteine may be the relevant pathway

### Population attributable risk
- Highest quartile homocysteine: 16% PAR for AD
- APOE e4 genotype (21% prevalence): 21% PAR for AD (RR 2.3, 95% CI 1.5-3.7)

## Methodology Notes

- Homocysteine measured by HPLC with fluorometric detection (CV 9%)
- Age-specific quartiles used because homocysteine rises with age
- Non-fasting blood samples used (may overestimate by up to 20% but bias is random)
- Dementia ascertainment: screening MMSE at each biennial exam, annual neuro/neuropsych assessment if impaired, committee diagnosis using DSM-IV and NINCDS-ADRDA criteria
- Diagnostic committee blinded to homocysteine levels
- Subjects who had stroke during follow-up were NOT excluded (stroke could be in causal chain)
- 30% of subjects had homocysteine >14 umol/L; none had severe hyperhomocysteinemia (>100 umol/L)

## Limitations

1. Predominantly white Framingham cohort -- limited racial/ethnic generalizability
2. Non-fasting blood samples may overestimate absolute homocysteine levels by up to 20%
3. Vitamin levels measured at baseline only, not at the 8-year-prior time point
4. Observational design -- cannot prove causation
5. No prospective vitamin supplementation trials existed at time of publication; authors explicitly state findings cannot guide treatment recommendations
6. Relatively small number of AD cases in the fully-adjusted model (n=44/680)

## Relevance to P5P Research

This paper is a landmark study establishing homocysteine as a modifiable risk factor for dementia/AD. Since P5P (vitamin B6) is a key coenzyme in homocysteine metabolism (transsulfuration pathway), this provides indirect support for the hypothesis that maintaining adequate B6 status (and thus lower homocysteine) could reduce dementia risk. However, the paper specifically shows that P5P levels did not independently predict dementia -- only homocysteine did. This suggests the mechanism of benefit from B6 supplementation would operate through homocysteine lowering rather than through other B6-dependent pathways.

Not directly relevant to Crohn's disease.
