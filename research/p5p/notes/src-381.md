# src-381: Smith et al. (2010) -- B Vitamins Slow Brain Atrophy in MCI (VITACOG Trial)

## Core Findings

Daily supplementation with high-dose B vitamins (0.8 mg folic acid, 0.5 mg cyanocobalamin, 20 mg pyridoxine HCl) for 24 months significantly slowed the rate of brain atrophy by approximately 30% in elderly individuals with mild cognitive impairment (MCI). The treatment effect was strongly modulated by baseline homocysteine levels: participants with tHcy >13 umol/L (top quartile) showed a 53% reduction in atrophy rate, while those with tHcy <=9.5 umol/L (bottom quartile) showed no benefit.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Single-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (VITACOG, ISRCTN94410159)
- **Population:** 271 individuals aged 70+ with MCI (of 646 screened), recruited in Oxford area 2004-2006
- **Intervention:** TrioBe Plus H containing folic acid (0.8 mg/d), cyanocobalamin (0.5 mg/d), pyridoxine HCl (20 mg/d) vs. placebo for 24 months
- **MRI sub-study:** 187 volunteered for cranial MRI; 168 completed both baseline and follow-up scans (85 active, 83 placebo)
- **Primary outcome:** Change in rate of whole-brain atrophy per year assessed by serial volumetric MRI using SIENA
- **Analysis:** Intention-to-treat for the MRI subgroup (n=168); also analyzed biologically compliant subset (n=136)
- **Note:** This was a B-vitamin combination; the specific form of B6 was pyridoxine HCl (not P-5-P)

## Key Evidence

1. **Primary outcome:** Mean brain atrophy rate was 0.76%/yr (95% CI 0.63-0.90) in active group vs. 1.08%/yr (0.94-1.22) in placebo; 29.6% reduction (P=0.001, age-adjusted). With full covariate adjustment: 27.1% reduction (P=0.003).

2. **Homocysteine interaction:** Significant baseline tHcy x treatment interaction (P=0.001). In placebo group, baseline tHcy strongly predicted atrophy rate (R^2=0.24); this association was abolished in the active treatment group (R^2=0.001).

3. **Dose-response by tHcy quartile:**
   - Bottom quartile (tHcy <=9.5 umol/L): no treatment effect
   - Above median: 43% reduction (P_interaction=0.019)
   - Top quartile (tHcy >13 umol/L): 53.3% reduction (P_treatment=0.001; P_interaction=0.023)

4. **Biochemical response:** Active group: folate increased ~270%, B12 doubled, tHcy decreased 22.5%. Placebo: tHcy increased 7.7%.

5. **Biologically compliant analysis (n=136):** Effect slightly larger at 31.1% reduction in atrophy rate (P=0.004, multi-adjusted).

6. **Cognition link:** Brain atrophy rate was a major determinant of final MMSE (partial r=-0.36) and TICS-M (partial r=-0.36) scores (both P<0.001). Study was not powered for cognitive endpoints.

7. **B6-specific finding:** Change in cystathionine (a B6 status marker) was NOT associated with atrophy rate, suggesting B6 may be less important than folate or B12 in this context.

## Safety

No significant safety issues. No significant differences in adverse events between groups. Fewer subjects in active group lost vibration sense.

## Limitations

- Combination of three B vitamins -- cannot determine individual contributions
- Not powered for cognitive outcomes (cognitive results reported separately)
- Subgroup analyses (stroke, diabetes, aspirin) limited by small sample sizes
- Single-center study in UK (no mandatory folic acid fortification)
- Participants already taking B-vitamin supplements showed no additional benefit (low baseline tHcy)
- B6 administered as pyridoxine HCl, not P-5-P

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Mechanisms/cognitive):** Provides strong RCT evidence that B-vitamin supplementation slows brain atrophy in MCI, with the effect mediated through homocysteine lowering. However, B6 (as pyridoxine) showed no independent association with atrophy in the change analysis.
- **Q5 (Neuro/cognitive outcomes):** Direct evidence that B-vitamin treatment reduces brain atrophy (a neurodegenerative marker) in MCI patients. Atrophy rate correlated with cognitive decline.
- **Q6 (Safety/dosing):** High-dose B vitamins (including 20 mg/d pyridoxine HCl) were safe over 24 months with no increase in adverse events.
- **Crohn's disease:** Not addressed. The study population was elderly with MCI, not IBD patients.
- **P-5-P specificity:** The study used pyridoxine HCl, not P-5-P, so findings are indirectly relevant at best for P-5-P-specific questions.
