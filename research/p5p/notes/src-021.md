## Core Findings

1. **PLP is a critical co-factor** in >150 enzymatic reactions. Plasma PLP is consistently low in inflammatory conditions, with parallel reduction in liver PLP, but only minor changes in erythrocyte/muscle PLP and functional B6 biomarkers. This suggests plasma PLP decline in inflammation is not simply systemic deficiency but may reflect redistribution to inflamed tissues.

2. **Plasma PLP predicts chronic disease risk**, including cardiovascular disease and some cancers, and is inversely associated with inflammatory markers across clinical and population-based studies.

3. **B6 supplementation improves immune functions** in deficient humans and animals. The proposed mechanism is mobilization of B6 to inflammation sites where it acts as a co-factor for immunomodulatory metabolite production.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- PLP co-factor in >150 enzymatic reactions
- Plasma PLP low in inflammatory conditions; liver PLP reduced in parallel
- Erythrocyte and muscle PLP show only minor changes during inflammation
- Functional B6 biomarkers relatively preserved despite plasma PLP drop
- Plasma PLP inversely associated with inflammatory markers in population studies
- Five key B6-dependent inflammatory pathways identified: B6 catabolism, kynurenine pathway, sphingosine 1-phosphate metabolism, transsulfuration pathway, serine/glycine metabolism
