## Core Findings

Pyridoxal phosphate (PLP/P5P) is the active coenzyme form of vitamin B6, participating in over 140 enzymatic reactions (~4% of all classified enzymatic activities). It is central to amino acid metabolism, neurotransmitter synthesis (serotonin, GABA, dopamine, histamine), heme biosynthesis, and one-carbon metabolism. The article provides detailed biochemical mechanisms and pharmacokinetic information relevant to supplementation considerations.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

1. **Enzymatic scope**: More than 140 PLP-dependent activities catalogued by IUBMB, representing ~4% of all classified activities (line 218).

2. **Absorption pathway**: The human intestine absorbs mainly non-phosphorylated B6 vitamers. Phosphorylated forms are hydrolyzed by intestinal phosphatases for passive diffusion, then rephosphorylated by pyridoxal kinase (PDXK) in the liver or intestine (lines 387-399).

3. **NAPE-PLD transport**: NAPE-PLD (N-acyl phosphatidylethanolamine-specific phospholipase D) serves as a membrane carrier for PLP and phosphorylated B6 forms, creating membrane pores in the presence of bile acids. This is particularly relevant in the brain, gut, liver, kidney, and reproductive system (lines 400-414).

4. **PLP homeostasis**: Intracellular-free PLP is maintained at approximately 1 uM. PLP-binding proteins include glycogen phosphorylase (muscle), hemoglobin (erythrocytes), albumin (plasma), and NAPE-PLD (brain, gut, liver, kidney). Excess B6 is dephosphorylated and oxidized to pyridoxic acid for urinary excretion (line 414).

5. **Pyridoxine paradox**: The inactive form pyridoxine competitively inhibits active P5P, meaning high-dose pyridoxine supplementation can mimic B6 deficiency symptoms. This provides mechanistic rationale for preferring P5P supplementation over pyridoxine (lines 713-718, citing Vrolijk et al. 2017, Toxicol In Vitro).

6. **Inflammation link**: Dimeric SHMT2 inhibits the BRISC deubiquitylase complex (immune signaling regulator). PLP induces SHMT2 tetramerization, preventing BRISC interaction -- potentially linking B6 status to inflammatory pathways (lines 441-445, citing Eyers & Murphy 2016).

7. **Neurotransmitter roles**: PLP is cofactor for aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (5-HTP to serotonin), glutamic acid decarboxylase (glutamate to GABA), and L-histidine decarboxylase (histidine to histamine). B6 deficiency may cause epileptic seizures in children due to impaired GABA synthesis (lines 310-352).

8. **Biosynthesis in humans**: PLP is synthesized from pyridoxal by pyridoxal kinase (PDXK, ATP-dependent), with pyridoxine 5'-phosphate oxidase converting alternative 5'-phosphates to PLP. PLP is primarily made and metabolized in the liver (lines 511-543).
