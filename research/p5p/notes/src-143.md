# src-143: Nygard et al. (1997) - Plasma Homocysteine Levels and Mortality in Patients with Coronary Artery Disease

**Citation:** Nygard O, Nordrehaug JE, Refsum H, Ueland PM, Farstad M, Vollset SE. NEJM 1997; 337:230-6.

**Quality:** ok (substantive full text, landmark paper)

## Core Findings

In 587 patients with angiographically confirmed coronary artery disease followed prospectively for a median of 4.6 years, plasma total homocysteine was the strongest modifiable predictor of overall and cardiovascular mortality, showing a strong graded dose-response relation. Patients with homocysteine >=15 umol/L had a 4.5-fold higher mortality than those with levels <9 umol/L after multivariate adjustment (P for trend = 0.02). Homocysteine was weakly related to the anatomic extent of coronary disease but strongly related to prior myocardial infarction, suggesting homocysteine drives acute thrombotic events rather than atherosclerosis progression per se.

## Key Evidence

### Mortality by homocysteine category (Table 1)
- **<9 umol/L** (n=130): 3.8% mortality at 4 years; reference group
- **9.0-14.9 umol/L** (n=372): 9.9% mortality; adjusted HR 1.92 (95% CI 0.73-5.09)
- **15.0-19.9 umol/L** (n=59): 25.4% mortality; adjusted HR 2.78 (95% CI 0.86-8.98)
- **>=20.0 umol/L** (n=26): 26.9% mortality; adjusted HR 4.51 (95% CI 1.22-16.6)
- **P for trend = 0.02** (fully adjusted model)

### Cardiovascular mortality (50 deaths, 78% of total)
Using CVD death as endpoint strengthened the association:
- Fully adjusted HRs: 2.3, 2.5, 7.8 for increasing tHcy categories (P trend = 0.01)

### Continuous dose-response
- Per 5 umol/L increase: HR = 1.6 (for range 10-15) and HR = 2.5 (for range 15-20)
- Relation was nearly linear from ~5 to >20 umol/L, steeper above 15

### Predictors of homocysteine levels
- Serum folate: r = -0.36 (strongest predictor)
- Creatinine: r = 0.30
- Uric acid: r = 0.17
- Vitamin B12: r = -0.15
- LVEF: r = -0.13

### Thrombosis vs. atherosclerosis distinction
- tHcy weakly related to number of stenosed vessels (P = 0.05)
- tHcy strongly related to prior MI (P < 0.001)
- Lipid factors strongly related to CAD extent but weakly to mortality
- Suggests tHcy primarily promotes acute thrombotic events

## Methodology

- **Design:** Prospective cohort study
- **Population:** 587 patients with angiographically confirmed CAD (>=50% stenosis in any main coronary artery) from Haukeland University Hospital, Bergen, Norway
- **Enrollment:** February 1991 - June 1992 (802 consecutive angiography patients; exclusions for non-ischemic indications)
- **Demographics:** 478 men, 109 women; median age 62 years
- **Treatment post-angiography:** CABG (318), PTCA (120), medical (149)
- **Follow-up:** Median 4.6 years (range 3.9-5.3); death data from National Population Register through April 30, 1996
- **Homocysteine measurement:** HPLC with fluorescence detection, fasting plasma
- **Statistical methods:** Cox regression with indicator variables for non-linear dose-response; Kaplan-Meier survival curves; generalized additive logistic regression for continuous dose-response
- **Multivariate adjustment:** Sex, age, LVEF, creatinine, total cholesterol, CAD extent, hypertension, diabetes, smoking (4 groups), platelet count, aspirin use

## Limitations

1. **Observational design** - authors explicitly state this "does not prove a causal relation between total homocysteine and mortality"
2. **Small numbers in extreme categories** - only 26 patients with tHcy >=20 umol/L; wide confidence intervals reflecting this
3. **Single homocysteine measurement** at baseline; no data on changes over time or response to B-vitamin therapy
4. **Homocysteine correlated with renal function and LVEF** - potential residual confounding despite statistical adjustment; authors note adjustment may underestimate true effect if these share causal pathways
5. **No direct measurement of B6/P5P** - measured folate and B12 but not pyridoxal phosphate; reference 34 (Robinson et al. 1995) noted low pyridoxal phosphate as independent risk factor
6. **Norwegian cohort** - generalizability may be limited
7. **No intervention component** - calls for homocysteine-lowering trials but does not test them

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (P5P mechanisms):** Indirectly relevant. Shows homocysteine is a strong predictor of CVD mortality, establishing the pathway that P5P (as cofactor for cystathionine beta-synthase) could modify. Does not directly measure P5P/B6.
- **Q2 (CVD/inflammation):** Highly relevant. Landmark prospective evidence that elevated homocysteine is a strong, graded, independent predictor of mortality in established CAD. 4.5x higher mortality in highest vs. lowest quartile.
- **Q5 (Optimal dosing/biomarkers):** Relevant. Establishes tHcy thresholds (<9, 9-15, 15-20, >20 umol/L) with clinical significance. Identifies folate as the strongest biochemical predictor of tHcy levels. Shows dose-response is continuous but steeper above 15 umol/L.
- **Q7 (Healthspan/longevity):** Relevant. Demonstrates that homocysteine predicts all-cause mortality, not just CVD mortality, in a diseased population. The strongest modifiable predictor identified.
- **Crohn's disease:** Not directly addressed, but the homocysteine-CVD mortality link is relevant since Crohn's patients frequently have elevated homocysteine due to B6/B12/folate malabsorption.

## Claims to Verify

1. **N=587 with confirmed CAD** - verified: 802 initial, minus 139 non-ischemic, minus 25 prior PTCA/MI with normal angiogram = 638; of these 587 had CAD confirmed, 51 did not. Numbers are consistent.
2. **64 deaths (10.9%)** - verified: 53 men (11.1%) + 11 women (10.1%) = 64. Consistent with 64/587 = 10.9%.
3. **Mortality ratios in Table 1** - the age/sex-adjusted ratios (1.00, 2.35, 5.75, 7.04) differ from the fully adjusted ratios (1.00, 1.92, 2.78, 4.51), showing meaningful attenuation but remaining statistically significant (P=0.02). Verified from table.
