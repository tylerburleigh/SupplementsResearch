# src-067: Vitamin B6 Metabolism in Liver and PLP Supplementation in Cirrhosis

**Funding:** NIH (5MO1-RR00039)

## Core Findings

This paper describes a kinetic model of human liver vitamin B6 metabolism, covering the interconversion of B6 vitamers (pyridoxine, pyridoxamine, pyridoxal) to PLP and the catabolite 4-pyridoxic acid. The model was validated against observed plasma B6 changes after loading doses, differential clearance rates of vitamers, and the low plasma PLP seen in cirrhosis. A key clinical finding is that cirrhotic patients retain PLP synthesis capacity, and oral pyridoxine supplementation can restore plasma PLP to normal levels. However, this PLP restoration did not correct deranged amino acid metabolism in cirrhosis, suggesting B6 abnormality is not the primary driver of amino acid disturbances in liver disease.

## Key Evidence

1. B6 vitamers metabolized primarily in liver to PLP and 4-pyridoxic acid (dead-end catabolite)
2. Kinetic model consistent with in vivo interconversion rates, post-load plasma B6 changes, viter-specific clearance differences, and low plasma PLP in cirrhosis
3. Cirrhotics retain PLP synthesis capacity; oral pyridoxine restores plasma PLP to normal range
4. PLP restoration in cirrhosis produced NO significant differences in methionine/cystathionine clearance or amino acid clearance after protein load
5. Abnormal B6 metabolism is not an important factor in deranged amino acid metabolism in cirrhosis

## Methodology

- Human liver enzyme activity characterization
- In vivo kinetic modeling of B6 vitamer interconversions
- Clinical intervention: oral pyridoxine supplementation in cirrhotic patients
- Outcome measures: plasma and urinary clearance of methionine, cystathionine, and amino acids after oral/protein loads

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Pharmacokinetics):** Directly relevant. Describes hepatic metabolism of B6 vitamers to PLP and 4-pyridoxic acid, and validates a kinetic model. Evidence is moderate due to abstract-only access.
- **Q2 (Healthspan pathways):** Marginally relevant. However, this is a specific disease context (cirrhosis), not general healthspan.
- **Q3 (B6 deficiency in Crohn's):** Not directly relevant. Studies liver cirrhosis, not IBD/Crohn's. However, the finding that liver dysfunction reduces plasma PLP may be tangentially relevant since Crohn's can involve hepatic complications.
- **Q4 (IBD interventions):** Not relevant. No IBD population studied.
- **Q5 (Dosing/safety):** Minimally relevant. Mentions oral pyridoxine supplementation restored PLP in cirrhotics but dosing details are in the full text (unavailable).
- **Q6 (Tradeoffs):** Relevant. Demonstrates that PLP restoration via pyridoxine does not automatically translate to functional metabolic improvement, at least for amino acid metabolism in cirrhosis. Important caveat for supplementation expectations.
- **Q7 (Adversarial):** The null result on amino acid metabolism could be cited as evidence questioning the functional benefit of PLP restoration, though in a liver disease (not IBD) context.
