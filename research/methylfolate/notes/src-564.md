# src-564: Associations of Plasma Folate and Vitamin B6 With Blood DNA Methylation Age

**Citation:** Nwanaji-Enwerem JC, Colicino E, Gao X, et al. J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med Sci. 2020;76(5):760-769. doi:10.1093/gerona/glaa257. PMC8355450.

**Status:** ok -- full-text PMC article, substantive methods/results/discussion.

## Core Findings

In 715 older male veterans (1153 observations, VA Normative Aging Study, 1999-2008), Bayesian Kernel Machine Regression identified plasma folate as important to both GrimAge (PIP=0.64) and PhenoAge (PIP=0.99), with higher folate levels paradoxically associated with *increased* epigenetic age on both clocks (GrimAge beta=+0.75 yr; PhenoAge beta=+1.62 yr). Vitamin B6 was identified as important to PhenoAge with a protective negative association (beta=-1.62 yr). The cumulative 4-metabolite mixture (B6, B12, folate, homocysteine) at lower percentiles was associated with decreased GrimAge, suggesting the full one-carbon mixture may reduce mortality-risk epigenetic age despite individual folate showing positive associations.

## Key Evidence

- **Folate-GrimAge:** PIP=0.64; beta=0.75 yr (95% CI: 0.41, 1.09) at 50th percentile of other metabolites
- **Folate-PhenoAge:** PIP=0.99; beta=1.62 yr (95% CI: 0.95, 2.29) at 50th percentile
- **B6-PhenoAge:** beta=-1.62 yr (95% CI: -2.28, -0.96) -- protective
- **Cumulative mixture-GrimAge:** At 30th percentile vs 50th, beta=-0.13 yr (95% CI: -0.26, -0.005)
- **DNAmAge:** No metabolites met importance threshold (PIPs 0.04-0.27) -- null for the chronological-age clock
- **Folate levels:** Mean 18.7 ng/mL (SD 15.9), above the clinical reference range upper bound (17.0 ng/mL)

## Methodology

- **Design:** Observational longitudinal cohort (VA Normative Aging Study)
- **Participants:** 715 older community-dwelling men (mean age ~73 yr), predominantly Caucasian, Northeastern US; 43% had 1 visit, 53% had 2 visits, 4% had 3 visits
- **Exposure measurement:** Plasma B6 (as PLP), B12, folate (radioassay, Bio-Rad), homocysteine (HPLC); CVs 4.0-5.0%
- **Outcome measurement:** Three epigenetic clocks (DNAmAge, PhenoAge, GrimAge) from Illumina HumanMethylation450 BeadChip, calculated via Horvath's online calculator
- **Statistical approach:** Bayesian Kernel Machine Regression (BKMR) with 20,000 MCMC iterations, PIP>0.60 threshold for variable selection; adjusted for chronological age, BMI, smoking, alcohol, education, GFR, WBC proportions, season; random intercept for repeated measures
- **Sensitivity analyses:** (1) first-visit-only models; (2) metabolites within clinical reference ranges only (353 participants, 453 observations); (3) PhenoAge models additionally adjusted for PhenoAge serum components (albumin, CRP, alkaline phosphatase, hemoglobin, RBC count)
- **Secondary models:** Additionally adjusted for diabetes, hypertension, ischemic heart disease

## Limitations

- Observational design -- no causal inference possible
- Older Caucasian males only -- limited generalizability to women, younger adults, other ethnicities
- No data on folate form (folic acid vs methylfolate), supplement use, or dietary sources
- Missing key one-carbon metabolites: methionine, betaine, SAM, SAH
- Plasma metabolites measured at single timepoints per visit
- Possible residual confounding
- Mean folate above reference range -- may reflect folic acid fortification era effects

## Paradoxical Folate Finding

The positive association of folate with epigenetic age is counterintuitive. Authors suggest it may reflect increased methyl group availability driving DNA methylation processes. They note similar paradoxical folate relationships in the literature with mtDNA copy number and telomere length. Context-dependent effects were also observed: the folate-GrimAge association lost significance when other metabolites were at their 90th percentile, while the folate-PhenoAge association lost significance when other metabolites were at their 10th percentile.

## Relevance to Q6 (Methylfolate and Epigenetic Aging Clocks)

This is the most directly relevant source for Q6. It examines the relationship between one-carbon metabolites (including folate) and all three major epigenetic aging clocks in a well-characterized human cohort using robust mixture modeling. Key implications:

1. **Folate does affect epigenetic clocks** in a statistically significant manner, but the direction is positive (age-increasing) for GrimAge and PhenoAge individually
2. **The full one-carbon metabolite mixture** shows a protective (age-decreasing) effect on GrimAge, suggesting synergistic interactions matter
3. **Folic acid vs methylfolate distinction is absent** -- this study measured total plasma folate and cannot differentiate forms
4. **B6 shows a protective effect** on PhenoAge, suggesting B-vitamin cofactors in one-carbon metabolism modulate epigenetic aging
5. **The B-PROOF trial** (cited in discussion) found folic acid + B12 supplementation reduced DNAmAge by 2.70 years in women with MTHFR 677CC genotype, offering RCT support for causal effects
