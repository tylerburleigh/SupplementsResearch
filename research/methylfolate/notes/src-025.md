# src-025: RCT of Methylfolate/P5P/Methylcobalamin on Homocysteine and LDL-C with MTHFR/MTR/MTRR Genotyping

**Source:** PubMed abstract (PMID 38892484), published in Nutrients (MDPI). Randomized Controlled Trial.
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38892484
**Content Quality:** Abstract only (no full text available); abstract contains detailed quantitative results.

## Core Findings

This RCT of 54 patients aged 40-75 with elevated homocysteine and moderate LDL-C found that 6-month supplementation with a combination of methylfolate, P5P, and methylcobalamin significantly reduced homocysteine by 30.0% and LDL-C by 7.5% vs placebo. The effect was substantially larger in homozygous minor allele carriers of MTHFR/MTR/MTRR polymorphisms (48.3% homocysteine reduction, 11.8% LDL-C reduction) compared to mixed allele carriers (18.6% and 4.8%, respectively), supporting genotype-guided supplementation.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Primary outcomes (treatment vs placebo, 6 months)
- **Homocysteine:** -30.0% (95% CI: -39.7% to -20.3%, p < 0.01)
- **LDL-C:** -7.5% (95% CI: -10.3% to -4.7%, p < 0.01)

### Genotype-stratified subgroup analysis
- **Homozygous minor allele carriers:**
  - Homocysteine: -48.3% (95% CI: -62.3% to -34.3%, p < 0.01)
  - LDL-C: -11.8% (95% CI: -15.8% to -7.8%, p < 0.01)
- **Mixed allele carriers:**
  - Homocysteine: -18.6% (95% CI: -25.6% to -11.6%, p < 0.01)
  - LDL-C: -4.8% (95% CI: -6.8% to -2.8%, p < 0.01)
- **Between-group differences (homozygous vs mixed):**
  - Homocysteine: 29.7% difference (95% CI: -50.7% to -8.7%, p < 0.01)
  - LDL-C: 7.0% difference (95% CI: -13.0% to -1.0%, p < 0.01)

## Methodology

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **Population:** 54 patients, aged 40-75, with elevated homocysteine and moderate LDL-C
- **Intervention:** Combination of methylfolate, pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (P5P), and methylcobalamin
- **Duration:** 6 months
- **Stratification:** MTHFR, MTR, and MTRR genetic polymorphisms (homozygous minor allele vs mixed allele carriers)
- **Outcomes:** Homocysteine and LDL-C levels at 3 and 6 months

## Limitations

1. **Combination therapy:** The intervention combined methylfolate with P5P and methylcobalamin, so individual contribution of methylfolate cannot be isolated.
2. **Small sample size:** Only 54 participants total; subgroup analyses (especially homozygous carriers) likely had very small cell sizes. The abstract does not report per-subgroup sample sizes.
3. **Abstract only:** Full text not available; cannot verify methods details, dosages, randomization procedure, or completeness of reporting.
4. **Conflict of interest:** Four authors employed by Triangel Scientific (the company whose product was tested), which is a significant COI for a supplement study.
5. **Surrogate endpoints:** Homocysteine and LDL-C are biomarkers, not hard cardiovascular outcomes.
6. **Dose not reported:** The abstract does not specify dosages of methylfolate, P5P, or methylcobalamin.

## Relevance to Research Questions

This source is directly relevant to questions about methylfolate's efficacy for homocysteine reduction, lipid modulation, and the role of MTHFR genotyping in personalizing supplementation. It provides strong quantitative evidence with confidence intervals and p-values, and uniquely demonstrates genotype-dependent response differences across MTHFR, MTR, and MTRR polymorphisms. However, the combination therapy design limits attribution of effects specifically to methylfolate.

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample size of 54:** Cannot verify subgroup allocation from abstract. If the homozygous minor allele group is small (e.g., n < 15), the large confidence intervals (-62.3% to -34.3%) suggest this.
- **Dose information:** Absent from abstract; critical for interpreting clinical applicability.
- **Venue:** Likely published in Nutrients (MDPI), an open-access journal; the COI and industry employment of authors warrant scrutiny of methodological rigor.
