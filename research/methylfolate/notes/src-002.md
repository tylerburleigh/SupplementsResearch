# src-002: Folinic Acid vs L-Methylfolate and Homocysteine in Healthy Greek Adults

## Source Metadata

- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38056998
- **Year:** 2023 (from copyright line)
- **Journal:** European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism (Elsevier)
- **Quality flag:** `degraded` in metadata (paywall detection), but the abstract itself is a complete structured abstract with Background, Methods, Results, and Conclusions sections
- **Content type:** Structured PubMed abstract (no full text)

## Core Findings

A 3-month randomized trial in 272 healthy Greek adults with elevated tHcy (>=10 umol/L) found that both folinic acid and l-methylfolate significantly reduced serum tHcy and increased serum folate and cobalamin. Folinic acid raised serum folate more than l-methylfolate, but tHcy reduction was not substantially different between groups overall. However, genotype mattered: MTHFR 677CT heterozygotes had a significantly greater tHcy reduction with folinic acid vs l-methylfolate, and 677TT homozygotes showed the largest tHcy reductions overall. The MTHFR A1298C polymorphism did not influence tHcy response.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, open-label (blinding not specified in abstract), parallel-group trial
- **Duration:** 3 months
- **Population:** 272 healthy Greek adults (143 men, 129 women; mean age 43.0 +/- 15.3 years)
- **Inclusion:** Serum tHcy >= 10 umol/L
- **Intervention:** Randomized to folinic acid or l-methylfolate, oral, 3 months
- **Co-intervention:** Subjects with Cbl <300 pg/mL received 1 mg hydroxycobalamine IM twice weekly for the first month
- **Measurements:** Serum folate, Cbl, tHcy by immunoassay at baseline and 3 months
- **Genotyping:** MTHFR C677T and A1298C by PCR and reverse hybridization

## Key Evidence

1. **Both supplements effective:** Both groups showed significant increases in serum folate and Cbl and significant decreases in tHcy after 3 months.
2. **Folinic acid raised folate more:** "Considerably higher increase" in serum folate with folinic acid vs l-methylfolate, but no significant difference in Cbl increase.
3. **tHcy reduction comparable overall:** No substantial difference in tHcy reduction between the two supplementation groups overall.
4. **MTHFR 677TT strongest responders:** Individuals with TT genotype had significantly higher tHcy reduction than CC or CT genotypes.
5. **MTHFR 677CT genotype-specific benefit:** CT heterozygotes had statistically significant higher tHcy reduction with folinic acid vs l-methylfolate.
6. **A1298C not influential:** MTHFR A1298C polymorphism did not affect tHcy reduction.
7. **Cobalamin co-intervention:** A subset of participants received hydroxycobalamine, which may confound attribution of tHcy reduction solely to folate supplementation.

## Limitations

- **Abstract only:** Full text was behind paywall; effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values, and exact dose information are not available from the abstract.
- **Cobalamin co-intervention:** Participants with low Cbl received hydroxycobalamine in the first month, potentially inflating tHcy reductions. The proportion receiving co-intervention is not stated in the abstract.
- **No dose information:** The abstract does not specify doses of folinic acid or l-methylfolate.
- **Blinding unclear:** The abstract does not state whether the trial was double-blind.
- **Short duration:** 3-month follow-up only.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Methylfolate supplementation and health outcomes:** Directly compares l-methylfolate to folinic acid on tHcy reduction (a cardiovascular risk marker).
- **MTHFR polymorphisms and treatment response:** Provides genotype-stratified results for C677T (CC, CT, TT) and A1298C, showing that genotype moderates supplement efficacy.
- **Folinic acid vs l-methylfolate:** One of few head-to-head comparisons; suggests comparable tHcy-lowering overall but genotype-dependent differences.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample size of 272 is stated as total enrollment; the abstract does not clarify how many were randomized to each group or how many completed the trial.
- "Significantly higher reduction" in TT genotypes and "statistically significant higher reduction" in CT with folinic acid -- no p-values, effect sizes, or confidence intervals provided in the abstract.
- Doses of folinic acid and l-methylfolate are not stated; these are critical for interpreting comparative efficacy.
