# src-469: Methyl Donors, Epigenetic Alterations, and Brain Health

**Citation:** Bekdash RA. *Int J Mol Sci.* 2023 Jan 25;24(3):2346. doi: 10.3390/ijms24032346. PMID: 36768667, PMC9917111.

**Type:** Review article (open access, MDPI)

**Quality:** ok -- full text available from PMC, substantive review with detailed discussion of mechanisms and evidence.

## Core Findings

This review synthesizes evidence that methyl-donor micronutrients (choline, betaine, folic acid, methionine, vitamins B6 and B12) are critical for one-carbon metabolism and SAM production, which powers DNA and histone methylation. Imbalances in these micronutrients during critical developmental windows cause epigenetic alterations in stress-related genes (GR/NR3C1, Bdnf, Avp, Crf) with long-term consequences for brain health, behavior, and stress resilience. Supplementation of methyl donors can reverse some adverse epigenetic programming and has neuroprotective effects in models of depression, anxiety, and Alzheimer's disease.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### One-Carbon Metabolism and SAM
- Methyl donors feed into the methionine and folate cycles, producing SAM (universal methyl donor) for DNMTs and HMTs.
- Elevation of SAH relative to SAM inhibits DNA methyltransferases, potentially altering DNA methylation patterns.
- MTHFR gene polymorphism alters enzymatic activity and is linked to altered cognitive functions.

### HPA Axis Programming and Epigenetics
- Early life stress causes developmental programming of the HPA axis via epigenetic changes in stress-related genes.
- Key genes affected: GR (NR3C1) promoter methylation altered by maternal care in rats and childhood abuse in humans; Bdnf methylation changes in prefrontal cortex (postnatal stress) and amygdala/hippocampus (prenatal stress); Avp hypomethylation in PVN increases stress responsiveness; Crf promoter hypomethylation in hypothalamus and central amygdala.
- MeCP2 phosphorylation modulates binding to Avp enhancer, linking early stress to stress axis dysregulation.

### Neuroprotective Effects of Methyl Donor Supplementation
- **Rat maternal separation + methyl donors (choline, betaine, folate, VitB12) for 18 weeks starting at PD60:** Reduced depressive-like behavior and increased total DNA methylation (5-mC) in hippocampus (ref 21, Paternain et al. 2016).
- **Chronic high-fructose rats + methyl donors for 8 weeks:** Reduced oxidative stress, anxiety-like behavior, and depressive-like behavior (ref 133).
- **Human maternal folate + VitB12 in first trimester:** Correlated with higher cognitive scores in children at age 3 (ref 128).
- **Norwegian study:** Folate maternal intake during early pregnancy reduced risk of language delay in children at age 3 (ref 129).
- **Folic acid in APP/PSNE1 AD mice:** Reduced amyloid beta via increased Dnmt1 activity and hypermethylation of PSEN1 and APP promoters (ref 143).
- **SAM supplementation in TgCRND8 AD mice:** Reversed PSEN1 hypomethylation, reduced tau phosphorylation and amyloid beta (refs 141, 142).
- **Betaine in AD patients:** Decreased homocysteine, phosphorylated tau, amyloid beta, inflammatory markers (IL-1beta, TNF-alpha); increased NMDA receptor subunits and synaptic proteins (ref 146).
- **Choline supplementation in APP/PS1 AD mice (perinatal):** Reduced amyloid beta, amyloid plaques; elevated ChAT; decreased GFAP (ref 150).
- **Choline supplementation in APP/PS1 AD mice (2-11 months):** Reduced anxiety, improved spatial/learning memory; reduced amyloid beta in cortex and hippocampus; restored choline and acetylcholine levels; increased synaptophysin and PSD95 (ref 151).

### VitB Deficiency Effects
- VitB deficiency elevated homocysteine in neurons/astrocytes in striatum, hippocampus, cerebellum with cell death markers, altered motor function and cognitive deficits in adulthood (ref 132).

## Methodology

- Narrative review synthesizing human and animal studies.
- Covers one-carbon metabolism biochemistry, epigenetic mechanisms (DNA methylation, histone modifications, microRNAs), HPA axis programming, and dietary intervention studies.
- Includes both prenatal/perinatal and adult supplementation paradigms.
- No meta-analysis; qualitative synthesis of evidence.

## Limitations

- Review format: no new primary data; relies on selection and interpretation of published studies.
- Most interventional evidence comes from animal models; human data is largely observational/correlational.
- Dosages and timing of supplementation vary across studies; optimal protocols not standardized.
- Author notes that dosage and timing should be monitored by health professionals to prevent overdosage.
- Some contradictions noted (e.g., Wistar Kyoto rats showing stress resilience with global hypermethylation after ELS, whereas most studies show adverse effects of ELS).

## Relevance to Q6

Highly relevant. This source provides comprehensive mechanistic evidence linking methyl-donor nutrients (including folate/methylfolate) to brain health through one-carbon metabolism and epigenetic regulation. It covers the full lifecycle from prenatal development through aging/neurodegeneration, with specific data on depression, anxiety, cognitive function, and Alzheimer's disease. The SAM/SAH ratio mechanism directly explains how methylfolate supplementation could influence brain health outcomes.

## Claims to Verify

- Betaine supplementation in AD patients showing decreased homocysteine, tau, amyloid beta, and inflammatory markers (ref 146) -- this is a strong clinical claim; the primary study should be checked for sample size and study design.
- Maternal folate + VitB12 in first trimester correlating with cognitive scores at age 3 (ref 128) -- sample size and confounders should be verified.
- The assertion that the dosage and timing of micronutrient intake should be monitored to prevent unwanted side effects is stated without specific evidence on what constitutes overdosage thresholds.
