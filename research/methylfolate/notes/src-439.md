# src-439: Dietary folate drives methionine metabolism to promote cancer development by stabilizing MAT IIA

**Citation:** Li JT, Yang H, Lei MZ, et al. Signal Transduct Target Ther. 2022;7:192. doi:10.1038/s41392-022-01017-8
**PMID:** 35729157 | **PMCID:** PMC9213445
**Venue:** Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy (Nature partner journal)
**Quality:** ok -- full text available from PMC

---

## Core Findings

This study demonstrates that dietary folate promotes hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) development through a mechanistic axis involving methionine adenosyltransferase 2A (MAT2A/MATIIalpha). High-folate diet (10x standard) significantly accelerated DEN/HFD-induced HCC in mice, while folate-free diet blunted tumor development. The effect is dependent on MATIIalpha: liver-specific Mat2a knockout abolished the cancer-promoting effect of high folate. The deubiquitylating enzyme VCIP135 stabilizes MATIIalpha protein in response to folate signals, providing a mechanistic link between dietary folate intake and cancer progression.

## Key Evidence

### Animal model results (DEN/HFD-induced HCC, C57BL/6J mice)
- **High-folate diet (20 mg/kg, 10x standard)** significantly increased liver/body weight ratio, total tumor number, and maximum tumor size vs standard folate (2 mg/kg) in DEN/HFD mice (n=10/group, 5 months post-DEN)
- **Folate-free diet** significantly decreased liver/body weight ratio, tumor number, and maximum tumor size
- No significant difference in tumor incidence, food intake, or body weight between groups
- Ki67 staining confirmed enhanced proliferation with high folate, suppressed with folate-free
- Serum ALT and AST elevated in high-folate group, decreased in folate-free group

### MATIIalpha is the critical mediator
- MATIIalpha protein upregulated in HCC tissues; further enhanced by high-folate diet; diminished by folate-free diet
- MAT1A (the adult liver isoform) showed inverse expression pattern
- **Liver-specific Mat2a knockout (LKO):** n=10 WT vs n=9 LKO, 32 weeks HFD -- dramatically reduced liver/body weight, tumor number, and maximum tumor size
- LKO protected against HFD-induced glucose intolerance and insulin resistance
- RNA-seq revealed LKO altered genes in metabolic pathways, ECM-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, and actin cytoskeleton

### Folate effect on HCC is Mat2a-dependent
- Folate-free diet attenuated HCC in Mat2a WT mice but NOT in KO mice (n=5-7/group)
- High-folate diet increased HCC in WT but NOT in KO mice
- This is the key genetic proof that folate's cancer-promoting effect requires MATIIalpha

### Mechanism: VCIP135 deubiquitylation of MATIIalpha
- VCIP135 identified as deubiquitylating enzyme (DUB) for MATIIalpha via TAP-MS
- VCIP135 WT (but not enzymatic-dead C218S mutant) decreases MATIIalpha ubiquitylation
- VCIP135 extends MATIIalpha half-life and rescues degradation upon folate deprivation
- Folate deprivation downregulates both MATIIalpha and VCIP135 protein levels
- MTX-decreased MATIIalpha rescued by excess folate dose-dependently
- Double knockdown of MAT2A and VCIP135 dramatically inhibits HCC cell growth
- MTX and FIDAS-5 (MAT2A inhibitor) synergistically repress cell proliferation

### Human HCC clinical data
- IHC on 58 paired HCC samples: both MATIIalpha and VCIP135 significantly upregulated in cancer vs adjacent tissues
- Western blot on 21 paired samples confirmed upregulation
- Positive Spearman correlation between VCIP135 and MATIIalpha protein levels
- High VCIP135 expression significantly associated with poor overall survival in liver cancer (TCGA)
- High MAT2A expression associated with poor prognosis in liver, breast, cervical, and gastric cancers
- Metabolomics on 44 paired samples: methionine and SAM enriched in cancer vs adjacent tissues
- Multiple one-carbon metabolism genes upregulated in HCC per TCGA and GEO data

## Methodology

- **Animal models:** DEN/HFD-induced HCC in C57BL/6J mice; hepatocyte-specific Mat2a knockout (Mat2a flox/flox; Alb-Cre+)
- **Diet:** AIN-93M based, standard folate 2 mg/kg, high 20 mg/kg, or folate-free; gut microflora not suppressed with succinylsulfathiazole
- **Cell lines:** HEK293T, HepG2, Huh7, PLC/PRF/5, Hep3B, MHCC-97L, MHCC-97H, MHCC-LM3
- **Omics:** RNA-seq (Illumina HiSeq 4000), targeted metabolomics (HPLC-MS), TAP-MS for interactome
- **Human samples:** 58 paired HCC/adjacent tissues from Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center; TCGA/GEO database analysis
- **Statistics:** Mean +/- SEM, one-way ANOVA or two-tailed t-test, Spearman correlation, P<0.05 threshold

## Limitations

1. Mouse model uses DEN/HFD which may not fully recapitulate human HCC etiology
2. Gut microflora were not suppressed, so folate-free mice still received some folate from microbiota
3. Mice had impure genetic background (acknowledged by authors)
4. Study focused on HCC only; generalizability to other cancers via MATIIalpha mechanism requires further validation
5. Human sample size modest (n=58 for IHC, n=21 for western blot, n=44 for metabolomics) from single center
6. The paper primarily used folic acid rather than methylfolate (5-MTHF) -- the synthetic oxidized form rather than the reduced biological form

## Relevance to Q5 (Risks: cancer promotion by folate)

**Highly relevant.** This is one of the most mechanistically detailed studies showing how dietary folate can promote cancer (specifically HCC) through a defined molecular pathway (folate -> VCIP135 stabilization of MATIIalpha -> accelerated methionine cycle -> tumor growth). The key finding that folate's cancer-promoting effect is abolished by Mat2a knockout provides strong causal evidence. The human clinical data corroborate the animal findings, showing MATIIalpha and VCIP135 upregulation in HCC and association with poor prognosis. This directly addresses cancer risk from folate supplementation, particularly in populations with existing liver pathology or high MAT2A expression.

**Important caveat for methylfolate context:** The study used folic acid (the synthetic oxidized form), not methylfolate/5-MTHF. The mechanism involves folate's role in one-carbon metabolism which is shared across folate forms, but the specific pharmacokinetics and metabolic effects may differ between folic acid and methylfolate.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample sizes are stated as n=10 for diet groups (Figure 1), n=10 WT vs n=9 LKO (Figure 3), n=5-7 for folate-free/high folate LKO experiments (Figure 4) -- these are plausible for mouse studies
- The 58 paired IHC samples and 44 metabolomics pairs are reasonable for a single-center study
- The diet formulations (2 mg/kg standard, 20 mg/kg high folate) are clearly stated and verifiable against AIN-93M standards
