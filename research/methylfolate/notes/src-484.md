# src-484: Holmes et al. 2011 (Lancet) - MTHFR, Folate, and Stroke

**Citation:** Holmes MV, Newcombe P, Hubacek JA, et al. "Effect modification by population dietary folate on the association between MTHFR genotype, homocysteine, and stroke risk: a meta-analysis of genetic studies and randomised trials." Lancet. 2011;378(9791):584-594. DOI: 10.1016/S0140-6736(11)60872-6. PMCID: PMC3156981.

## Core Findings

This is a landmark meta-analysis using Mendelian randomisation to demonstrate that the effect of MTHFR 677C->T on homocysteine and stroke risk is strongly modified by population folate status. In low-folate regions (Asia), the TT genotype increased homocysteine by 3.12 umol/L and stroke risk by 68% (OR 1.68); in high-folate/fortified regions, both effects were null (~0.13 umol/L homocysteine difference; OR 1.03 for stroke). The study triangulated genetic evidence with RCT data, showing concordance: both genetic studies and RCTs showed no benefit of homocysteine-lowering in folate-replete populations, while large genetic studies in Asia predicted a 22% stroke risk reduction that no RCT has tested.

## Key Evidence

### Gene-environment interaction: MTHFR x folate on homocysteine
- TT vs CC homocysteine difference by folate region (lines 896-905):
  - Asia (no fortification): 3.12 umol/L (95% CI 2.23-4.01)
  - Europe, low (pre-1996): 1.62 umol/L (95% CI 0.73-2.51)
  - Europe, mid (post-1996): 1.35 umol/L (95% CI 0.82-1.88)
  - AANZ, mid (pre-1996): 1.15 umol/L (95% CI 0.42-1.88)
  - AANZ, high (post-1996): 0.13 umol/L (95% CI -0.85 to 1.11)
- Metaregression: each one-category increase in folate status reduced MTHFR effect on homocysteine by 0.62 umol/L (95% CI 0.29-0.94)
- In 35 studies with plasma folate data (12,007 participants): each 1 ng/mL increase in plasma folic acid reduced TT vs CC homocysteine difference by 0.13 umol/L (95% CI -0.01 to 0.28)

### Gene-environment interaction: MTHFR x folate on stroke risk
- Stroke OR (TT vs CC) by folate region (lines 954-960):
  - Asia: OR 1.68 (95% CI 1.44-1.97)
  - Europe, low: OR 1.01 (95% CI 0.88-1.16)
  - Europe, mid: OR 1.33 (95% CI 1.14-1.55)
  - AANZ, high: OR 1.03 (95% CI 0.84-1.25)
- Overall (all regions): OR 1.37 (95% CI 1.25-1.50; I2=48.4%)
- Restricted to large studies (>=400 stroke events): overall OR 1.09 (95% CI 0.98-1.20)

### Sensitivity analyses for small-study bias
- Large Asian studies only (>=400 stroke events): OR 1.28 (95% CI 1.11-1.48)
- Trim-and-fill adjusted: OR 1.30 (95% CI 1.19-1.43)
- 41 hypothetical null studies (400 cases + 400 controls each) would be needed to reduce Asian OR from 1.68 to 1.15
- Egger test for Asian studies: coefficient 1.20 (p=0.002), indicating some funnel asymmetry
- Authors note they cannot fully exclude small-study bias as explanation for Asian findings

### RCT meta-analysis
- 13 RCTs, 45,549 participants, 2,314 stroke events, 269 TIAs
- Active treatment reduced homocysteine by 3.33 umol/L (24% proportional reduction)
- Pooled RR for stroke: 0.94 (95% CI 0.85-1.04; I2=18.8%)
- 91% of trial participants from high-folate regions; no RCT done exclusively in low-folate Asia
- Mean follow-up 4.7 years (range 2-7.3 years)

### Triangulation: genetic studies vs RCTs
- Non-Asian populations: RCTs showed 6% RRR (95% CI -4 to 15), large genetic studies showed 0% RRR (95% CI -11 to 10) -- concordant null
- Asian populations: large genetic studies predicted 22% RRR (95% CI 10-32), but no RCT available for comparison
- Homocysteine reduction magnitude similar across genetic (2.58 umol/L) and RCT (3.33 umol/L) contexts

## Methodology

- **Design:** Meta-analysis of genetic studies (Mendelian randomisation) combined with meta-analysis of RCTs
- **Genetic data:** 237 datasets from international collaboration, including unpublished data solicited from investigators
- **Participants:** 59,995 individuals for homocysteine analysis; 20,885 stroke events from 140 datasets (101 studies)
- **Folate classification:** Five categories based on geography, time period, and fortification policy (validated by Robert Clarke, CTSU Oxford)
- **Stroke subtypes:** 94 datasets ischemic stroke (17,909 cases), 20 hemorrhagic (1,615 cases), 25 total stroke (6,972 cases)
- **Small-study bias controls:** Funnel plots, Egger test, trim-and-fill, restriction to large studies, sensitivity analysis with hypothetical null studies
- **Confounding check:** In-silico analysis of long-range LD for rs1801133 across ethnic groups using GLIDERS
- **Statistical models:** DerSimonian-Laird random effects throughout

## Limitations

1. **Small-study bias cannot be fully excluded** as an explanation for the stronger effect seen in Asia, despite multiple sensitivity analyses
2. **Folate classification is ecological** -- based on population-level estimates, not individual participant plasma folate for most studies
3. **No RCT in low-folate Asia** -- the predicted 22% RRR is based on genetic data only and cannot be validated against trial evidence
4. **MTHFR poor specificity** -- the variant could affect stroke through mechanisms other than homocysteine (a limitation inherent to trans-instruments)
5. **Selective reporting bias** possible if stroke subtypes were differentially reported by continent
6. **RCT heterogeneity:** trials enrolled mainly secondary-prevention populations (vascular disease, CKD, diabetes), whereas genetic studies were mainly population-based
7. **Egger test significant in Asia** (p=0.002), though authors argue the magnitude of the Asian effect (41 null studies needed to deflate) makes pure publication bias unlikely

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (MTHFR and cardiovascular outcomes):** Directly addresses whether MTHFR 677C->T affects stroke risk, with strong evidence that the effect is modified by population folate status. The most comprehensive genetic meta-analysis of its era. Shows that in folate-replete populations, MTHFR has no meaningful effect on stroke.

**Q4 (Population differences in response):** Central paper for understanding why different populations show different MTHFR effects. Demonstrates a clear gradient of MTHFR effect by folate status across five geographical/temporal categories. Explains the null findings of RCTs conducted in fortified populations. Provides the key insight that population folate supplementation effectively eliminates the MTHFR-associated stroke risk.
