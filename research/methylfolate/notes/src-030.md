## Core Findings

The 2024 AHA/ASA guideline assigns a **Class 2b (not well established), Level of Evidence B-NR** recommendation to folic acid and B-complex vitamin supplementation for stroke prevention. This is the weakest positive recommendation class, reflecting uncertain benefit. While a systematic review of 12 RCTs showed B-complex vitamins reduced stroke in 9 of 12 trials with a borderline-significant pooled rate ratio of 0.90 (95% CI 0.81-1.00, P=0.04), the guideline notes critical limitations: none of the reviews stratified by vitamin deficiency status or isolated primary prevention populations.

## Key Evidence on Folate/B-Vitamins and Stroke

### Recommendation (Section 4.1, Diet Quality)
- **COR 2b, LOE B-NR**: "In adults, folic acid supplementation and B-complex (folic acid, B12, B6) vitamins supplementation for reducing the risk of stroke are not well established." (lines 1057-1061)
- This contrasts with the **Class 1** recommendation for Mediterranean diet and the **Class 3: No Benefit** recommendation for multivitamins, vitamin C, vitamin E, selenium, calcium, and antioxidants.

### Supporting Data
- Systematic review of 12 trials: B-complex vitamins (B6 + folic acid + B12) reduced stroke in 9/12 trials (lines 1151-1157)
- Pooled rate ratio: **0.90 (95% CI 0.81-1.00, P=0.04)** -- borderline significance
- Evidence quality: moderate
- Populations: mixed (healthy + end-stage renal disease + coronary artery disease + CVD risk factors)
- Key cited references: Jenkins et al. (JACC 2021), Khan et al. (Ann Intern Med 2019), An et al. (JACC 2022)

### Critical Limitations
1. **No vitamin deficiency stratification**: None of the systematic reviews included the proportion of patients who were vitamin deficient (lines 1164-1167)
2. **No primary prevention isolation**: Reviews did not present results solely for primary prevention of stroke (lines 1166-1167)
3. **Underpowered trials**: Most individual trials were not powered for examining differences in incident stroke (lines 1094-1095, 1266-1267)

### Knowledge Gaps Identified
The guideline explicitly lists "Whether folic acid or B-complex vitamins reduce the risk of incident stroke" as a future research priority (lines 1272-1273).

### Other B-Vitamin Findings
- **Vitamin B6 alone**: Did not reduce stroke risk (very low quality evidence) (lines 1208-1213)
- **Vitamin B3 (niacin)**: Did not reduce stroke risk (moderate quality); may increase all-cause mortality (RR 1.10, 95% CI 1.00-1.20, P=0.05) (lines 1222-1234)

## What the Guideline Does NOT Address

The guideline does **not** specifically discuss:
- Homocysteine as a risk factor or target for intervention
- MTHFR polymorphisms or genetic testing
- Methylfolate or 5-MTHF as distinct from folic acid
- Any genotype-stratified approach to B-vitamin supplementation

This is a significant gap for the research question on methylfolate and MTHFR -- the premier US guideline on primary stroke prevention treats folic acid/B-vitamins as a single population-level intervention without considering genetic variation in folate metabolism.

## Methodology

- Formal AHA/ASA guideline using Class of Recommendation (COR) and Level of Evidence (LOE) framework
- Systematic evidence review including RCTs and meta-analyses with stroke as an end point
- Eligible studies: general population without CVD, population with CVD but no stroke/TIA, or population with CVD including stroke/TIA (with no more than 50% having stroke history)
- Peer-reviewed by AHA/ASA committees

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Highly relevant** as the most authoritative current US guideline on primary stroke prevention. Its weak recommendation (2b) for B-vitamin/folate supplementation reflects the state of evidence. The complete absence of MTHFR/homocysteine/methylfolate discussion highlights a gap in guideline-level evidence that individual studies and meta-analyses may address.

## Claims to Verify

- Pooled rate ratio of 0.90 (95% CI 0.81-1.00, P=0.04) for B-complex vitamins -- this is a borderline result and the CI nearly includes 1.0. Verify against Jenkins et al. JACC 2021.
- "9 of 12 trials" showed reduction -- verify this characterization is consistent with the source meta-analysis.
