# src-441: Opposing Roles of Folate in Prostate Cancer

**Citation:** Rycyna KJ, Bacich DJ, O'Keefe DS. Urology. 2013;82(6). DOI: 10.1016/j.urology.2013.07.012. PMC3852144.

**Type:** Narrative review (peer-reviewed journal)

**Quality Assessment:** Full-text available, substantive content with methods discussion of epidemiological studies, in vitro experiments, animal models, and clinical correlations. Quality: OK.

## Core Findings

1. **Folate has a dual role in prostate carcinogenesis.** Before neoplastic transformation, folate is protective by maintaining nucleotide pools, proper DNA methylation, and genomic stability. After transformation, folate promotes tumor progression by fueling cancer cell proliferation and invasion. This "dual role" model is supported by animal studies, in vitro work, and human tissue data.

2. **Folic acid supplementation increases prostate cancer risk in key trials.** The Aspirin/Folate Polyp Prevention Trial (643 men, 1mg folic acid/day, 7-year median follow-up) found a 10-year prostate cancer probability of 9.7% in the folic acid group vs 3.3% in placebo (HR=2.63, p=0.01). Conversely, dietary folate intake showed a non-significant inverse trend (HR=0.65). Meta-analyses corroborate: RR=1.24 for folic acid supplementation >=0.4mg/day; OR=1.19 per 10nM serum folate increase.

3. **PSMA (prostate specific membrane antigen) mediates folate uptake in prostate cancer cells**, providing a growth advantage. PSMA expression increases both polyglutamyl folate hydrolysis and monoglutamyl folate transport, and high PSMA correlates with pathologic stage, tumor grade, and biochemical recurrence.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Epidemiological evidence (mixed)
- 14 studies reviewed: 4 positive correlation, 3 negative correlation, 7 null
- Major confounders: different countries/time periods, varying serum folate levels (e.g., 90% of Swedish men <11.1nM vs 2.5% of US men <10.4nM post-fortification), inconsistent screening methods, single time-point measurements

### In vitro evidence
- Bistulfi et al: 100nM vs 2uM folic acid environments showed that lower folate caused chromosomal rearrangements (24-37% of cells), CpG island hypermethylation (14 new regions), increased uracil misincorporation and DNA strand breaks
- Petersen et al: PC-3 and LNCaP cells showed significant growth increases at higher folate levels (100nM vs 20nM vs 4nM); all three cell lines showed significantly increased invasiveness at 100nM

### Animal model evidence (TRAMP)
- Folate-deficient diet: 1/23 progressed beyond HGPIN vs 10/22 control vs 7/21 high-folate (p=0.02)
- Folate-deficient group: significantly fewer lymph node metastases, retained E-cadherin staining
- However, high-folate diet did NOT significantly enhance progression over control

### Human tissue evidence
- Tomaszewski et al: Highest serum folate quintile (117+/-15nM) tumors had Ki67 index 6.17+/-3.2% vs 0.86+/-0.92% in lowest quintile (18+/-9nM), p<0.0001
- No difference in proliferation of normal adjacent glands between groups

### Case report
- Patient with GS 3+4=7 prostate cancer: PSA rising to 21.3ng/mL on high-dose folate supplements (8mg mixed folates + 5mg B12, serum folate 303.6nM). After stopping supplements, serum folate dropped to 9.06nM and PSA declined to 2.08ng/mL.

### NHANES data
- Post-fortification median serum folate more than doubled from 12nM (pre) to 30.14nM (1999-2000)
- U-shaped age curve: lowest folate ages 21-30 (31.3nM), highest in men 80+ (69.9nM) despite equal dietary intake across ages
- Mean serum folate 2007-2010: 42nM (95% CI 39.2-44.8) in ~69 million US males

## Methodology

This is a narrative review synthesizing epidemiological studies, in vitro experiments, animal models, and human tissue studies. The authors also conducted original analysis of NHANES 2007-2010 data in a subpopulation of fasting males with 2-day dietary recall.

## Limitations

- Narrative review (no systematic search protocol reported)
- Epidemiological studies are highly heterogeneous in populations, screening methods, and folate measurement approaches
- Many studies measured folate only at enrollment, not tracking changes over follow-up
- In vitro folic acid concentrations may not perfectly reflect in vivo physiology
- Case report is anecdotal (single patient, confounded by concurrent treatments)
- TRAMP model may not fully translate to human prostate cancer

## Relevance to Q5 (Folate dual effect on cancer)

This source is **highly relevant** to Q5. It provides the most direct and comprehensive discussion of folate's dual role in cancer (specifically prostate cancer) found in this source set. Key contributions:
- Clear articulation of the dual-role model with mechanistic explanations
- Quantitative evidence from a randomized trial showing folic acid increases prostate cancer risk
- In vitro and animal evidence that folate deficiency blocks tumorigenesis while high folate promotes growth/invasion
- Human tissue evidence that high serum folate correlates with increased cancer cell proliferation
- Discussion of PSMA as a mechanism linking folate uptake to cancer progression
- Recommendation against folic acid supplementation in men with prostate cancer

## Claims to Verify

- NHANES analysis (line 181): "Mean serum folate in this population was 42nM (95% CI 39.2 to 44.8)" -- this appears to be original analysis by the authors from NHANES 2007-2010 data; the claim about representing ~69 million men should be verified against NHANES sampling methodology.
- The assertion that prostate cells prioritize polyamine synthesis over methylation/nucleotide synthesis (line 162-163) comes from Bistulfi et al 2009 (ref 16) and would benefit from independent verification.
