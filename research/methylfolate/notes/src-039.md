## Core Findings

This is a sweeping review of homocysteine (Hcy) metabolism and its systemic pathological consequences, framed within Predictive, Preventive, and Personalized Medicine (PPPM/3PM). The paper covers the full Hcy metabolic pathway (transmethylation, remethylation, transsulfuration), hyperhomocysteinemia (HHcy)-associated disease mechanisms, B-vitamin interventions, and DNA methylation disruptions.

**Key message:** Impaired Hcy metabolism leading to HHcy is linked to oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, systemic inflammation, and increased risks across multiple disease domains (CVD, cancer, neurological disorders, pregnancy complications, eye diseases, impaired healing). B-vitamin supplementation (especially folate and B12) is the primary intervention.

## Homocysteine Metabolism Pathways

- **Transmethylation:** Methionine -> SAM -> SAH -> Hcy. SAH accumulation in HHcy decreases SAM/SAH ratio, reducing methylation capacity.
- **Folate-dependent remethylation:** Hcy -> methionine via methionine synthase, requiring folate (B9), B12, and B2 as cofactors, with MTHFR as a key enzyme.
- **Folate-independent remethylation:** Hcy -> methionine via betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase (BHMT), using choline/betaine, especially important in liver and kidney.
- **Transsulfuration:** Hcy -> cystathionine -> cysteine via CBS (requires B6) and cystathionine-gamma-lyase, producing glutathione.
- **SAM coordinates both pathways** as allosteric inhibitor of MTHFR and activator of CBS.
- **Optimal plasma tHcy:** 5.0-15.0 umol/L (HPLC) or 5.0-12.0 umol/L (immunoassay).
- **HHcy classification:** Moderate (16-30), Intermediate (31-100), Severe (>100 umol/L).
- **Hypohomocysteinemia** (<6 umol/L) in 0.5-1% of population; also a health risk.

## MTHFR Role

- MTHFR C677T polymorphism: most common variant in ovarian and breast cancer patients (meta-analysis of 19,260 patients and 23,364 controls). Significantly increases breast/ovarian cancer risk in Asians, breast cancer in Caucasians. Also linked to CVDs, AD, PD, epilepsy, esophageal cancer, neck cancer, lung cancer, age-related cataracts.
- MTHFR A1298C polymorphism: associated with breast and bladder cancer susceptibility.
- Altered MTHFR expression described in elderly patients with memory loss, cognitive impairments.
- Mild HHcy usually caused by mild methylation pathway impairment, associated with folate or B12 deficiencies or MTHFR thermolability.

## DNA Methylation

- HHcy causes SAH accumulation, decreasing SAM/SAH ratio and methylation capacity.
- Lack of one-carbon nutrients (Met, folate, choline) significantly reduces SAM and global DNA methylation.
- Long-term methyl-deficient diet (>18 weeks) causes **irreversible** DNA hypomethylation.
- Hcy thiolactone causes N-homocysteinylation of histone and non-histone proteins, altering gene expression.
- Global hypomethylation is a hallmark of cancer, destabilizing chromosomal integrity and promoting carcinogenesis.
- High folate status inversely correlated with Hcy and increased DNA methylation in colon tissue of colorectal cancer/adenoma patients.

## HHcy Disease Links

### CVD
- Independent risk factor for CVD, beyond conventional risk factors.
- Mechanisms: endothelial injury, NO dysfunction, oxidative stress, smooth muscle proliferation, inflammation.
- HHcy sensitivity 93.1%, accuracy 90.0%, specificity 86.1% for obstructive CAD (Asian population study).
- Meta-analysis: Hcy linked to elevated risk of ischemic stroke (RR=1.54, 95% CI 1.21-1.97) and stroke overall (RR=1.58, 95% CI 1.25-2.00).
- One Mendelian randomization study did NOT find causal linkage between CAD/myocardial infarction and Hcy (conflicting data).

### Pregnancy
- HHcy: 7.7-fold risk for preeclampsia.
- Associated with pre-term birth, low birth weight, placental abruption, recurrent pregnancy loss, neural tube defects.
- High Hcy is an independent NTD factor even when B12 and RBC folate are high.

### Cancer
- Elevated Hcy in breast, colorectal, ovarian, and lung cancers.
- HHcy is a risk factor for venous thromboembolism in cancer patients.
- Folate deficiency leads to uracil misincorporation into DNA and chromosomal damage.

### Neurological
- Elevated Hcy is independent risk factor for AD, PD, dementia, multiple sclerosis.
- In AD, Hcy promotes amyloid beta via demethylation of BACE-1 and Presenilin 1 promoters, and affects tau protein metabolism.
- Folate and B12 supplementation restored Hcy levels and antagonized Hcy-induced tau hyperphosphorylation in rats.

### Other
- Eye disorders (diabetic retinopathy, cataracts, macular degeneration, glaucoma).
- Impaired healing (chronic ulcers, fractures).
- Mitochondrial dysfunction via complex I-III inhibition, cytochrome c release, NOX4 upregulation.
- Cellular senescence acceleration via telomere shortening, DNA hypomethylation of hTERT.

## B-Vitamin Interventions

- **Folic acid + B12:** Synergistic effect in lowering Hcy. Recommended doses: folic acid 0.5-5 mg/day + B12 0.5 mg/day for vascular disease risk.
- **Pregnancy:** High-dose folic acid (5 mg/day) decreased Hcy at delivery.
- **Preeclampsia prevention:** 4 mg/day folic acid from 3 months before pregnancy through entire pregnancy.
- **Wound healing:** Folic acid 1-2 mg/day for 12 months accelerated healing in HHcy patients with chronic venous ulcers.
- **PD:** Folic acid 5 mg/day reduced Hcy; vitamin B6 mean 1.63 mg/day.
- **Whole grains:** 17% lower Hcy in highest vs lowest whole-grain consumers.
- **Mediterranean/fiber-rich diet:** 19% Hcy decline after 2 weeks.
- **CBS deficiency:** Low-Met diet + betaine (50 mg/kg/day twice for children; 3 g/day twice for adults).
- **Phytochemicals:** Genistein, EGCG, curcumin, quercetin, hesperidin showed protective effects against Hcy-induced damage.

## Limitations

- Narrative review (not systematic); potential selection bias in study inclusion.
- Many cited studies have small sample sizes (e.g., n=18-54 for several clinical studies).
- Heterogeneity in study designs, populations, and Hcy measurement methods.
- Some contradictory findings noted (e.g., Mendelian randomization not supporting causal CAD-Hcy link).
- No meta-analyses conducted by the authors; they report findings from existing meta-analyses.
- PPPM framework is promotional in nature.
