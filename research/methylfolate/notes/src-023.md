## Core Findings

The CDC directly and explicitly states that **MTHFR gene variants do not impair the ability to process folic acid**, and that the common recommendation in alternative health communities to avoid folic acid and take 5-MTHF instead is "not true." This is the most authoritative skeptical stance available on the MTHFR-folic acid question.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

1. **MTHFR variants do not block folate processing.** People with MTHFR variants can process all types of folate, including folic acid. Folic acid is the only form shown to prevent NTDs. (Lines 86-98)

2. **Modest blood folate difference.** Among people consuming the same amount of folic acid, those with the MTHFR 677 TT genotype have blood folate levels only about **16% lower** than those with the CC genotype. (Lines 100-109)

3. **Intake matters more than genotype.** Folic acid intake is more important than MTHFR genotype for determining blood folate levels. 400 mcg daily increases blood folate regardless of genotype. (Lines 110-118)

4. **Population prevalence is high.** More people in the US have one or two copies of the C677T variant than do not. It is more common in Hispanic individuals. (Lines 72-84)

5. **A1298C variant has insufficient evidence** of significant effect on folate processing. (Lines 120-124)

6. **CDC explicitly contradicts the 5-MTHF recommendation.** States that the advice to avoid folic acid and take 5-MTHF instead is "not true." (Lines 86-91)

7. **No harm from recommended folic acid intake.** Taking folic acid at recommended amounts has not been shown to cause harm. (Lines 145-147)

## Methodology

This is a CDC fact sheet synthesizing evidence from multiple peer-reviewed studies (7 primary references cited), including:
- Crider et al. 2012 (folate and DNA methylation review)
- Tsang et al. 2015 (systematic review and meta-analysis of MTHFR 677C>T and blood folate)
- Crider et al. 2011 (large population-based double-blind trial of folic acid supplementation)
- Crider et al. 2014 (Bayesian model for RBC folate and NTD prevention)

## Limitations

- Not a primary research study; it is a government synthesis/communication piece
- Does not provide detailed effect sizes or confidence intervals (these are in the cited primary studies)
- Focuses narrowly on NTD prevention and blood folate; does not address other health conditions sometimes attributed to MTHFR variants
- The 16% figure for TT vs CC blood folate difference is presented without confidence interval or context about clinical significance

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q7 (Skeptical perspective on MTHFR significance):** This is a **highly relevant, authoritative source** representing the official US government position. It directly:
- Rejects the claim that MTHFR variants require avoiding folic acid
- States that folic acid intake trumps genotype effects
- Identifies the 5-MTHF substitution advice as unsupported
- Positions MTHFR testing as clinically unnecessary for folic acid supplementation decisions

This source should be weighted heavily as it represents institutional consensus rather than individual study findings.
