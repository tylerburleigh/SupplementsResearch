What is Rapamycin?

Rapamycin is an immunosuppressant drug commonly used to prevent organ transplant rejection and is being studied for its potential anti-aging and longevity-promoting properties.

What is Rapamycin used for?

Rapamycin is primarily used to prevent rejection in organ transplant patients by suppressing the immune system. Additionally, it is being researched for its potential benefits in extending lifespan, improving healthspan, and treating age-related diseases by modulating cellular processes.

How does Rapamycin prolong life and youth?

Rapamycin may prolong life and youth by inhibiting the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway, which plays a crucial role in cell growth, proliferation, and survival. By inhibiting mTOR, Rapamycin helps reduce cellular senescence, promotes autophagy (the body's process of cleaning out damaged cells), and improves metabolic functions. These effects can contribute to increased lifespan and improved healthspan.
