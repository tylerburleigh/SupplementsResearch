# src-023: Rapamycin Longevity Guide (FormBlends Blog)

## Source Characteristics

- **Type:** Commercial blog article from FormBlends (telehealth company selling GLP-1, peptide therapy)
- **URL:** https://formblends.com/articles/longevity-hub/rapamycin-longevity
- **Quality:** Not peer-reviewed; marketing content with cited references
- **Caution:** This is a commercial source with inherent conflict of interest (the company sells longevity treatments). Claims should be cross-verified against primary literature. Some quantitative claims (e.g., 1.8-year biological age reduction from a "2025 Nature Aging" study) could not be immediately verified and may be inaccurate or refer to preliminary results.

## Core Findings

This article summarizes rapamycin's longevity potential for a consumer audience, covering mTOR mechanism, animal evidence, human clinical data, dosing protocols, and side effects. It cites legitimate primary studies (Harrison 2009 Nature, Mannick 2014 Sci Transl Med, Bitto 2016 eLife) but presents their findings alongside unverified claims and commercial recommendations.

## Key Evidence Points

### Animal Studies (Q2)
- Reports NIA ITP results: 9% lifespan extension in male mice, 14% in female mice starting at 20 months
- Claims up to 23% extension when starting at 9 months (attribution unclear -- may conflate multiple studies)
- References Harrison 2009, Wilkinson 2012, Bitto 2016

### Human Clinical Evidence (Q3)
- 2014 Mannick study: 6 weeks rapamycin improved vaccine responses in elderly by 20%
- Claims "2019 PEARL trial" reduced infection rates in adults >65 by ~25% -- PEARL trial attribution needs verification
- Claims "2025 Nature Aging" study showed 1.8-year biological age reduction after 12 weeks intermittent dosing -- **UNVERIFIED, potentially fabricated or misrepresented**
- States immune function improvements appear at 6-8 weeks

### mTOR Mechanisms (Q1)
- Standard mTORC1 inhibition mechanism described: suppresses mTORC1, enhancing autophagy
- Claims autophagy restored to youthful levels within 4-6 weeks (source unclear)
- Compares to caloric restriction mimetic effects

### Dosing Tradeoffs (Q4)
- Intermittent weekly dosing: 1-6mg (typical start 2-3mg)
- Pulse protocol alternative: 5-6mg every 2 weeks
- Target trough levels: below 5-8 ng/mL (lower than transplant levels)
- Claims intermittent dosing maintains benefits while reducing side effects

### Adverse Effects (Q6)
- Mouth ulcers: 10-15% at longevity doses
- Temporary cholesterol/triglyceride elevations
- Temporary blood glucose increases (normalize 8-12 weeks)
- CYP3A4 drug interactions noted (antifungals, antibiotics, grapefruit juice)

### Immune Paradox (Q7)
- Claims weekly dosing maintains normal vaccine responses
- No significant infection rate increase in healthy adults at intermittent doses
- Contra-indicated for active infections or immunocompromised patients

## Claims to Verify

1. **"2025 Nature Aging study showing 1.8-year biological age reduction"** -- Could not be verified. This may be a misattribution, preliminary result, or fabricated claim. No specific authors or DOI provided. HIGH PRIORITY for verification.
2. **"2019 PEARL trial" reducing infections by 25%** -- PEARL was a rapamycin study but needs confirmation of the specific infection reduction figure and year.
3. **"23% lifespan extension starting at 9 months"** -- This may conflate results from different studies. The Harrison 2009 paper primarily tested late-life intervention.
4. **Mouth ulcer rate of 10-15%** -- Needs corroboration from clinical trial data; commercial sources may understate side effects.
5. **"Autophagy restored to youthful levels within 4-6 weeks"** -- No primary citation provided for this specific timeline.
