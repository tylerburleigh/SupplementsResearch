# src-035: Target of Rapamycin Activation Predicts Lifespan in Fruit Flies

**Citation:** Scialo F, Sriram A, Naudi A, Ayala V, Jove M, Pamplona R, Sanz A. (2015) *Cell Cycle* 14:18, 2949-2958. DOI: 10.1080/15384101.2015.1071745

**Quality:** ok (full text, ~49,500 chars, 475 sentences, peer-reviewed open access)

## Core Findings

This study used wild type Dahomey Drosophila melanogaster cultured at three temperatures (10C, 18C, 29C) to test which physiological parameters predict lifespan. The central finding is that **Tor pathway activation (measured via phosphorylation of S6K1 and 4E-BP) correlates with lifespan but not with metabolic rate**, while mitochondrial ROS production and membrane unsaturation correlate with metabolic rate but not lifespan. This supports the hyperfunction theory of aging over the Mitochondrial Free Radical Theory of Aging (MFRTA).

## Key Evidence

1. **Lifespan by temperature:** Flies lived longest at 18C (median 162 days, max 175 days), followed by 10C (median 78, max 113) and 29C (median 41, max 49). n=200 per group, replicated twice. P<0.001 by log-rank test vs 10C.

2. **Tor activation predicts lifespan:** Phosphorylation of both S6K1 and 4E-BP (Tor downstream targets) showed consistent correlation with lifespan but not with metabolic rate. Tor signaling was lowest at 18C where flies lived longest, corroborated by reduced body weight at that temperature.

3. **mtROS does not predict lifespan:** mtROS correlated strongly with metabolic rate (R=0.97, P<0.001) but not with lifespan (R=0.11, P>0.05). Lipoxidative damage also did not correlate with lifespan (R=0.02, P>0.05).

4. **Metabolic rate does not predict lifespan:** Flies at 10C had lowest activity/food intake but intermediate lifespan; flies at 18C had lowest weight and longest lifespan. Activity correlated with temperature (R=0.55, P<0.001) but not lifespan (R=0.05, P>0.05).

5. **IIS components mixed:** AKT phosphorylation correlated with lifespan before normalization but not after, suggesting AKT concentration rather than activation matters. dFOXO phosphorylation did not correlate with AKT or lifespan.

6. **Rapamycin mechanism context:** Cites that rapamycin extends lifespan independently of dietary restriction, without altering IIS or AMPK. Two proposed mechanisms: autophagy boost and translation repression.

## Methodology

- **Model:** Wild type Dahomey female Drosophila, reared at 10C, 18C, 29C (12h light/dark)
- **Sample sizes:** n=200 per temperature for lifespan; n=3-9 for molecular assays
- **Measures:** Mitochondrial O2 consumption (Clark electrode), H2O2 production, qPCR for gene expression, Western blot for Tor/IIS pathway proteins (pS6K1, p4E-BP, pAKT, pdFOXO), fatty acid composition (GC/MS), protein damage markers (GC/MS)
- **Statistics:** ANOVA with Newman-Keuls post-hoc; Kaplan-Meier log-rank for survival; linear regression for correlations; Pearson correlation coefficients

## Limitations

- **Drosophila-only study:** Findings are in a single insect model; direct mammalian/human relevance is inferential
- **Correlational design:** Temperature manipulation changes many variables simultaneously; Tor-lifespan correlation does not establish causation
- **No rapamycin intervention:** The study tests Tor activation as a predictor but does not administer rapamycin; the mechanistic link to rapamycin is through cited literature
- **Hyperfunction theory support is indirect:** The paper argues results fit hyperfunction theory better than MFRTA but does not directly test the hyperfunction model
- **Temperature confounds:** 10C is below typical lab rearing temperature; physiological stress responses may confound results

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (mTOR mechanisms):** Directly relevant. Shows Tor activation (via S6K1/4E-BP phosphorylation) predicts lifespan in wild type flies. Supports two rapamycin mechanisms (autophagy, translation repression). Demonstrates Tor-IIS pathway partial overlap.
- **Q2 (animal studies):** Relevant as a Drosophila study. Shows Tor signaling is conserved as a lifespan predictor. Cites rodent data (Harrison et al. 2009, Wu et al. 2013) showing Tor inhibition extends mammalian lifespan.
- **Q5 (translational disagreement):** Relevant to the paradigm debate between damage-accumulation vs hyperfunction theories of aging. Argues hyperfunction theory better explains data, which would support rapamycin translation.
- **Q3, Q4, Q6, Q7:** Not directly addressed. No human data, no dosing information, no adverse effect data, no immune function analysis.

## Claims to Verify

- **Lifespan numbers at 3 temperatures:** Verified from Table 1 (lines 269-275). Combined experiment values: 10C median 78, mean 73, max 113; 18C median 162, mean 122, max 175; 29C median 41, mean 39, max 49.
