# src-024: Rapamycin modulates tissue aging and lifespan independently of the gut microbiota in Drosophila

**Citation:** Schinaman JM, Rana A, Ja WW, Clark RI, Walker DW. (2019) *Scientific Reports* 9:7824. doi:10.1038/s41598-019-44106-5. Cited 89 times.

## Core Findings

This study demonstrates that rapamycin's longevity and tissue-protective effects in Drosophila operate through autophagy-dependent, microbiota-independent mechanisms. Rapamycin (10 ug/ml in food) extended lifespan, delayed intestinal barrier dysfunction, and improved muscle proteostasis in both conventional and germ-free flies, with the effects being abolished by adult-onset inhibition of autophagy (Atg1 RNAi). Notably, germ-free flies showed an enhanced response to rapamycin compared to microbe-bearing controls.

## Key Evidence

- **Lifespan extension:** Rapamycin significantly increased mean and maximum lifespan in female wDahomey flies (p < 0.0001, log-rank test; n > 225 per condition, replicated across 4 trials).
- **Intestinal barrier function:** Rapamycin delayed age-onset intestinal barrier dysfunction (Smurf phenotype) in non-Smurf flies. This was observed in both conventional and axenic conditions.
- **Microbiota modulation:** Rapamycin reduced total bacterial load and specifically decreased Alphaproteobacteria (a taxon linked to health decline) in aged fly guts, but these microbiota changes were neither necessary nor sufficient for lifespan extension.
- **Germ-free experiments:** Rapamycin extended lifespan in germ-free (axenic) flies (p < 0.0001; n >= 200 per condition, 3 trials). Transferring microbiota from rapamycin-treated flies to germ-free flies had no effect on lifespan.
- **Enhanced efficacy without microbiota:** Lifespan extension and proteostasis improvements were greater in germ-free flies than in microbe-bearing controls, suggesting the microbiota may slightly impair rapamycin efficacy.
- **Autophagy dependency:** Adult-onset RNAi of Atg1 (via daGS Gene-Switch driver) completely abolished rapamycin-mediated lifespan extension, improvement in intestinal barrier function, and reduction in muscle protein aggregates.
- **TOR signaling confirmation:** Rapamycin reduced TOR signaling (p-S6K T398 / total S6K ratio) in both conventional and axenic flies (confirmed by western blot).

## Methodology

- **Model organism:** Drosophila melanogaster, wDahomey strain (female flies primarily used).
- **Rapamycin dose:** 10 ug/ml in standard cornmeal food media, administered from day 4 post-eclosion onward.
- **Germ-free generation:** Embryos dechorionated with bleach/ethanol; axenic status confirmed by plating swabs on MRS agar.
- **Autophagy inhibition:** Gene-Switch system (daGS driver) with UAS-Atg1-RNAi, induced by RU486 (25 ug/ml) in adults only.
- **Intestinal barrier assay:** Smurf assay (blue dye no. 1 at 2.5% wt/vol).
- **Proteostasis measurement:** Immunostaining of polyubiquitinated aggregates in indirect flight muscles (anti-FK2 antibody).
- **TOR activity:** Western blot for phospho-S6K (T398) and total S6K.
- **Microbiota analysis:** qPCR with universal and taxon-specific 16S rRNA primers.
- **Statistics:** Log-rank test for survival, one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni or Newman-Keuls post-hoc, Mann-Whitney U-test for pairwise comparisons.

## Limitations

- Single species model (Drosophila); authors note it is unknown whether findings generalize to mammals.
- Experiments conducted on a single diet composition; nutritional interactions with rapamycin and microbiota not explored.
- Only female flies were used for most experiments; sex-specific effects not assessed.
- Germ-free experiments used a specific strain (wDahomey); strain-specific microbiota interactions may differ.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (mTOR mechanisms):** Strong evidence that rapamycin's anti-aging effects require autophagy (Atg1-dependent pathway) and TOR signaling inhibition, but not microbiota. The mechanism is direct host action via autophagy induction.
- **Q2 (animal studies):** Primary data from Drosophila showing lifespan extension, improved gut barrier function, and improved muscle proteostasis. Multiple replicated trials with adequate sample sizes.
- **Q3 (human clinical evidence):** Not directly relevant; this is a Drosophila study.
- **Q4 (dosing tradeoffs):** Single dose used (10 ug/ml); no dose-response data. The study notes that published reports failing to observe rapamycin-mediated lifespan extension in flies may relate to microbiota interactions.
- **Q5 (translational disagreement):** Relevant in that microbiota-dependent effects of pro-longevity interventions (e.g., metformin in C. elegans) do not generalize to rapamycin in Drosophila, highlighting species-specific mechanism differences that complicate translation.
- **Q6 (adverse effects):** No adverse effects of rapamycin treatment reported at the dose used.
- **Q7 (immune paradox):** Not directly addressed; the study focuses on autophagy and microbiota rather than immune function.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample sizes: "n > 225 flies" for lifespan and "n >= 200" for germ-free lifespan are reported in figure legends but the exact numbers per condition are not specified. These appear to be adequate but should be cross-referenced with the original figures.
- The enhanced lifespan extension in germ-free flies is reported as an observation but specific percentage increases or median lifespan values are not provided in the text; these would need to be extracted from the survival curves.
