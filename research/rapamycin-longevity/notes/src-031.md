# src-031: Induction of autophagy by spermidine promotes longevity

**Citation:** Eisenberg, T. et al. (2009). Nature Cell Biology, 11, 1305-1314. DOI: 10.1038/ncb1975
**Citation count:** 1,435
**Venue:** Nature Cell Biology
**Content quality:** Abstract only (paywall). Full text not accessible.

## Core Findings

Spermidine, a natural polyamine whose intracellular concentration declines during human ageing, extends lifespan across multiple model organisms (yeast, flies, worms) and human immune cells. The mechanism involves epigenetic deacetylation of histone H3 via inhibition of histone acetyltransferases (HAT), which upregulates autophagy-related genes. Autophagy is shown to be essential for spermidine-induced longevity.

## Key Evidence

- **Multi-species lifespan extension:** Spermidine administration extended lifespan in yeast, Drosophila, C. elegans, and human PBMCs.
- **Mouse oxidative stress:** Spermidine potently inhibited oxidative stress in ageing mice.
- **Mechanistic pathway:** Spermidine inhibits HAT --> histone H3 deacetylation --> upregulation of autophagy transcripts --> autophagy induction --> necrosis suppression --> lifespan extension.
- **Loss-of-function confirmation:** Depletion of endogenous polyamines led to hyperacetylation, ROS generation, early necrotic death, and decreased lifespan.
- **Autophagy required:** Genetic ablation of autophagy genes abolished spermidine-induced lifespan extension in yeast, flies, and worms.

## Methodology

- Yeast chronological and replicative lifespan assays
- Drosophila and C. elegans lifespan experiments with autophagy-deficient mutants
- Human PBMC culture experiments
- Mouse oxidative stress measurements
- Histone acetylation analysis (western blot, mass spectrometry for polyamine levels)
- Microarray gene expression analysis

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (mTOR mechanisms):** Indirectly relevant. This paper establishes autophagy as a conserved longevity mechanism across species. It references TOR pathway literature (Powers et al. 2006, Bonawitz et al. 2007, Harrison et al. 2009 rapamycin paper) in its reference list, placing spermidine-induced autophagy alongside TOR-inhibition-mediated autophagy. However, the paper does not directly study mTOR or rapamycin -- spermidine induces autophagy via a distinct HAT-inhibition pathway rather than through mTOR.
