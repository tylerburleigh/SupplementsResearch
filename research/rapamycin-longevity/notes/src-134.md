# Source src-134: Long-term rapamycin treatment suppresses IL-17-producing gamma-delta T cells and blunts neuroinflammation in aging

**Citation:** Torrent C, Gagliardi C, Fulle N, et al. bioRxiv preprint (2026). doi:10.64898/2026.02.04.703808
**Type:** Preprint (not peer-reviewed), academic
**Quality note:** Metadata flagged as "degraded" (paywall_page EUR), but actual content is a full-text preprint with methods, results, and discussion. Content quality is good.

## Core Findings

Long-term low-dose dietary rapamycin (14 ppm, ~2.24 mg/kg) administered to aging C57BL/6J mice for 5 months did not cause major alterations in peripheral immune cell populations but selectively prevented the age-associated expansion of IL-17-producing CD27- gamma-delta T cells, particularly in the peritoneal cavity. Upon LPS challenge, rapamycin-fed mice showed attenuated systemic inflammation (reduced IL-17A, IL-12p40, IL-27) and reduced microglial inflammatory gene expression, suggesting neuroprotective anti-inflammatory effects mediated indirectly rather than via direct CNS penetration.

## Key Evidence

1. **mTORC1 target engagement confirmed:** Reduced p-S6 in CD11b+ myeloid cells from blood, comparable across two cohorts starting treatment at different ages (8 vs 17-19 months).
2. **No major immune remodeling:** Monocytes, neutrophils, B cell subsets, and most T cell populations were unchanged across blood, bone marrow, spleen, and peritoneal lavage.
3. **Selective gamma-delta T cell effect:** CD27- (IL-17-producing) gamma-delta T cells significantly reduced in peritoneal lavage, rescuing to young-mouse levels. Trend observed in spleen and lymph nodes but not statistically significant.
4. **Attenuated neuroinflammation after LPS:** Serum IL-12p40, IL-17A, IL-27, and IL-10 all significantly reduced in rapamycin group upon LPS challenge (2 mg/kg IP, 4 hours). Microglial Tnf-alpha, Il6, and Tlr2 gene expression moderately reduced.
5. **No effect at steady state:** Basal serum cytokines and microglial inflammatory markers were not different between rapamycin and control groups without LPS stimulation.

## Methodology

- **Animals:** C57BL/6J female and male mice from Charles River
- **Dosing:** Eudragit-encapsulated rapamycin at 152 ppm in food (14 ppm active, ~2.24 mg/kg body weight) for 5 months
- **Age groups:** Treatment started at 17-19 months (aging cohort) or 6-8 months (adult cohort); 3-month-old mice as young controls
- **Challenge model:** 2 mg/kg LPS (E. coli 0111:B4) IP, analyzed 4 hours post-injection
- **Readouts:** Flow cytometry (immune cell populations), RT-qPCR (microglial gene expression), LEGENDplex/ELISA (serum cytokines)
- **Sample sizes:** Very small (n=3-4 per group for flow cytometry; n=7 for LPS serum; n=8-9 for microglia qPCR)
- **Statistics:** One-way ANOVA and Welch's t-test (GraphPad Prism)

## Limitations

1. **Very small sample sizes** (n=3-4 per group for most experiments) -- limited statistical power; many observed trends may be real but undetectable at this sample size.
2. **Preprint** -- not yet peer-reviewed.
3. **Mechanism of CNS effect unclear:** Authors acknowledge rapamycin does not cross BBB efficiently and propose indirect mechanisms (reduced systemic inflammation or gut-brain axis) but do not test these directly.
4. **Single mouse strain** (C57BL/6J) -- may not generalize to other genetic backgrounds or species.
5. **Only one dose level tested** -- cannot speak to dose-response relationships.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (mTOR mechanisms):** Provides evidence that mTORC1 inhibition via PI3K-mTORC1 axis suppresses IL-17-producing gamma-delta T cells; links mTORC1 to Th17/gamma-delta T cell differentiation.
- **Q2 (animal studies):** Directly relevant -- aging mouse study showing immune and neuroinflammatory effects of chronic low-dose rapamycin. Dosing protocol follows established lifespan-extension regimens (Harrison et al. 2009, Zhang et al. 2014).
- **Q3 (human clinical evidence):** Not directly relevant -- mouse study only.
- **Q4 (dosing tradeoffs):** Supports the concept that low-dose chronic rapamycin achieves target engagement (reduced p-S6) without overt immunosuppression. Single dose tested.
- **Q5 (translational disagreement):** Relevant to the safety debate -- argues long-term rapamycin is well-tolerated and does not cause excessive immunosuppression, but small sample sizes limit strength of this conclusion.
- **Q6 (adverse effects):** Reports treatment was well-tolerated with no overt adverse effects; notes rapamycin can inhibit wound healing in rats and cause dermatologic adverse events in TSC patients (from literature review).
- **Q7 (immune paradox):** Directly addresses the paradox -- long-term rapamycin did not broadly suppress immune cells but selectively modulated inflammatory gamma-delta T cells and attenuated neuroinflammation.

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample sizes (n=3-4 per group):** These are very small for immunological studies. Verify whether any power analysis was performed (none mentioned in text). Results should be interpreted cautiously.
- **Dose of 14 ppm = 2.24 mg/kg:** This conversion appears in the Methods. It follows the established Zhang et al. 2014 protocol but the exact body weight assumption should be checked against original dosing references.
