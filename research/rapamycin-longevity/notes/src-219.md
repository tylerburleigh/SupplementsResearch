# src-219: eIF4E function in somatic cells modulates ageing in C. elegans

**Authors:** Popi Syntichaki, Kostoula Troulinaki, Nektarios Tavernarakis
**Venue:** Nature, Vol 445, 22 February 2007
**DOI:** 10.1038/nature05603
**Type:** Peer-reviewed primary research (Letter)
**Quality:** ok -- full text available from PDF conversion

## Core Findings

Loss of the somatic eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4E (eIF4E) isoform IFE-2 in *C. elegans* reduces global protein synthesis, increases resistance to oxidative stress, and extends lifespan through a mechanism independent of the insulin/IGF signalling pathway (DAF-16), dietary restriction, and mitochondrial electron transport chain function. TOR deficiency and IFE-2 depletion have additive effects on lifespan, indicating they operate through distinct mechanisms rather than a shared pathway.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

1. **IFE-2 depletion extends lifespan:** Both RNAi knockdown and deletion alleles of ife-2 significantly extend C. elegans lifespan. No other eIF4E isoform knockdown affects longevity (lines 42-55).

2. **Somatic specificity:** The lifespan extension is somatic in origin -- germline-specific isoforms (IFE-1, IFE-3, IFE-5) do not affect longevity when depleted. Confirmed using germline-deficient glp-4 mutants (lines 57-66).

3. **No overt phenotypic defects:** IFE-2-depleted animals at 20C are indistinguishable from wild-type for pharyngeal pumping, defecation, fecundity, development, locomotion, body size/shape, and organ morphology. Significant embryonic lethality only at 25C (lines 67-84).

4. **Independent of DAF-16/insulin signalling:** IFE-2 depletion further extends the lifespan of daf-2 and age-1 mutants. DAF-16 is not required for lifespan extension. ife-2 expression is not controlled by DAF-16 (lines 85-104).

5. **Additive with other longevity pathways:** ife-2 knockdown further extends the lifespan of clk-1 (mitochondrial), eat-2 (dietary restriction), and TOR-deficient mutants (lines 110-278).

6. **TOR-IFE-2 independence:** C. elegans lacks a structural 4E-BP homologue, so TOR cannot signal to eIF4E through 4E-BP in this organism. TOR and IFE-2 knockdown show additive effects, confirming distinct mechanisms (lines 261-279).

7. **S6K not required:** Knockdown of C. elegans S6K homologues (rsks-1, rskn-1) has no effect on lifespan and does not suppress ife-2 longevity (lines 280-289).

8. **Reduced protein synthesis confirmed:** Using FRAP-based measurement, protein synthesis rates are lower in IFE-2-deficient animals (P < 0.005) (lines 326-332).

9. **Oxidative stress resistance:** IFE-2-deficient animals are significantly more resistant to paraquat and NaN3 (P < 0.005, 6 experiments, 240 animals per strain). Also extends lifespan of short-lived, oxidative-stress-sensitive mev-1 mutants (lines 344-450).

## Methodology

- **Organism:** *C. elegans* (multiple mutant strains)
- **Genetic interventions:** RNAi knockdown, deletion alleles (ife-2(ok306)), double mutants
- **Lifespan assays:** Synchronous populations, 100-150 individuals per experiment, repeated at least 3 times, scored daily for movement and pharyngeal pumping
- **Protein synthesis measurement:** Novel FRAP-based method in live animals
- **Stress resistance assays:** Paraquat (superoxide generator) and NaN3 (cytochrome c oxidase inhibitor)
- **Statistical tests:** Unpaired t-tests for FRAP and stress resistance

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (mTOR mechanisms):** Directly relevant. Shows TOR signalling to eIF4E/4E-BP is absent in C. elegans (no 4E-BP homologue), TOR and IFE-2 have additive effects on lifespan, and S6K is not required for IFE-2-mediated longevity. These findings illuminate the complexity of TOR downstream effectors and species differences.

- **Q2 (Animal studies):** Highly relevant. Demonstrates lifespan extension via translation initiation factor manipulation in C. elegans, with detailed genetic interaction analysis across multiple longevity pathways.

- **Q5 (Translational disagreement):** Relevant. The absence of 4E-BP in C. elegans is a significant species difference that complicates translation of TOR/rapamycin findings from worms to mammals.

- **Q3, Q4, Q6, Q7:** Not directly addressed. This is a C. elegans genetics study with no human clinical data, dosing analysis, adverse effect profiling, or immune function assessment.

## Limitations

1. **Species difference:** C. elegans lacks 4E-BP, a key TOR effector present in mammals. This limits direct translational relevance of TOR-related findings.
2. **No rapamycin used:** The study uses genetic TOR knockdown (let-363 RNAi), not pharmacological rapamycin treatment.
3. **No quantitative lifespan values in text:** Lifespan data reported in Supplementary Table 1 (not available in this source file).
4. **PDF conversion quality:** Raw text extraction; some figure elements are fragmented in the text.
