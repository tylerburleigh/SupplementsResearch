# src-037: Inhibition of mTOR by Rapamycin Abolishes Cognitive Deficits and Reduces Amyloid-beta Levels in a Mouse Model of Alzheimer's Disease

**Authors:** Spilman P, Podlutskaya N, Hart MJ, Debnath J, Gorostiza O, Bredesen D, Richardson A, Strong R, Galvan V
**Year:** 2010 | **Venue:** PLoS ONE | **Citations:** 878
**DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pone.0009979

## Core Findings

Long-term rapamycin treatment (2.24 mg/kg/day for 13 weeks) abolished Alzheimer's-like cognitive deficits and significantly reduced soluble Ab42 levels in PDAPP [hAPP(J20)] transgenic mice, a well-established AD model. The beneficial effects appear mediated by mTOR-inhibition-driven autophagy, which was induced specifically in transgenic mice with high amyloid-beta levels but not in non-transgenic littermates despite equivalent mTOR inhibition in both genotypes.

## Key Evidence

1. **Cognitive rescue:** Rapamycin-fed PDAPP mice showed improved learning on the last day of training (P=0.036) and restored spatial memory retention to levels indistinguishable from non-transgenic controls. No effects on anxiety (thigmotaxis), helplessness (floating), or visual acuity -- confirming cognitive-specific effects.

2. **Ab42 reduction:** Soluble Ab42 was significantly decreased (P=0.02) in rapamycin-treated transgenic brains. Ab40 was unchanged. The reduction was NOT due to altered APP processing (C99, C83 unchanged), increased alpha-secretase cleavage, or increased degradation (neprilysin and IDE unchanged).

3. **Autophagy induction:** LC3-II/b-actin ratios decreased in hippocampi of rapamycin-treated PDAPP mice (P=0.0009), autophagic substrate p62SQSTM decreased (P=0.0015), and LC3-immunoreactive puncta increased in CA1 projections. Critically, none of these autophagy markers changed in rapamycin-treated non-transgenic mice.

4. **mTOR inhibition confirmed:** Phosphorylation of p70S6K was significantly reduced in brains of both genotypes (P=0.006 for non-Tg, P=0.01 for Tg), confirming mTOR pathway engagement.

## Methodology

- **Model:** PDAPP [hAPP(J20)] transgenic mice on C57BL/6J background, males only
- **Groups:** n=10 (non-Tg control), n=10 (non-Tg rapamycin), n=12 (Tg control), n=12 (Tg rapamycin)
- **Dose:** Microencapsulated rapamycin at 14 mg/kg food = 2.24 mg/kg body weight/day (same as Harrison et al. 2009 lifespan study)
- **Treatment period:** 13 weeks, starting at 4 months of age (testing at 7 months)
- **Behavioral test:** Morris water maze (6 trials/day x 4 days, 30-sec probe trial)
- **Biochemical measures:** Western blotting (p70, LC3-II, p62SQSTM), ELISA for Ab40/Ab42, immunohistochemistry for LC3 puncta
- **Statistics:** Two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post-hoc; one-way ANOVA with Tukey; Student's t-test

## Limitations

- All male mice; sex-specific effects not assessed
- Single AD mouse model (PDAPP); generalizability to other models or humans unknown
- Treatment started pre-pathology (4 months, before plaques or cognitive deficits); unclear if rapamycin would reverse established disease
- Autophagy link is correlative -- not directly demonstrated via autophagy inhibition experiments
- Sample sizes modest (n=10-12 per group)
- No assessment of rapamycin's effects beyond 7 months of age

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (mTOR mechanisms):** Strong -- details mTORC1/mTORC2 roles in autophagy regulation via ULK1/mAtg13 and Akt/FOXO3 pathways. Shows p70S6K phosphorylation as biomarker of mTOR inhibition in brain. Demonstrates genotype-specific autophagy induction.
- **Q2 (animal studies):** Strong -- primary data showing cognitive rescue and Ab42 reduction in a well-characterized AD mouse model. Dose matches lifespan-extending regimen.
- **Q3 (human clinical evidence):** Not directly relevant. Authors note rapamycin is "already used in clinical settings" and "may be a potentially effective therapeutic agent for the treatment of AD" but provides no human data.
- **Q4 (dosing tradeoffs):** Moderate -- documents specific dose (2.24 mg/kg/day), increased food consumption in rapamycin group, stable body weight. No dose-response comparison.
- **Q5 (translational disagreement):** Moderate -- explicitly bridges lifespan extension (Harrison et al. 2009) with disease prevention, suggesting shared autophagy mechanism. Notes that the lifespan-extending effect of TOR inhibition in C. elegans requires autophagy.
- **Q6 (adverse effects):** Not directly addressed. Notes rapamycin-fed mice consumed more food but maintained normal body weight.
- **Q7 (immune paradox):** Not addressed.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample sizes: n=10 non-Tg, n=12 Tg per treatment group. These are clearly stated in Methods and are reasonable for this type of study.
- Ab42 reduction P=0.02: Reported in figure legend text. Specific ELISA methodology described.
- LC3-II/b-actin P=0.0009: Very small p-value for a biochemical ratio comparison with n=5-6 hippocampal dissections. Worth confirming the exact n for this measurement.
- Food intake increase of 2.12 g/mouse/week (P<0.001): Seems plausible but is a secondary observation.
