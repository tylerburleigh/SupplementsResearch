# Source src-076: Alternative and Complementary Therapies in OA and Cartilage Repair

**Citation:** Fuggle NR, Cooper C, Oreffo ROC, et al. (2020). Aging Clinical and Experimental Research, 32:547-560. DOI: 10.1007/s40520-020-01515-1. Cited 101 times.

**Quality:** ok. Full-text review article with substantive methods, results, and discussion. PDF conversion was raw text extraction but content is complete and readable.

## Core Findings

This ESCEO Working Group review systematically assessed alternative OA therapies including ACI, MSCs, PRP, vitamin D, and "other" therapies (oral collagens, MSM, SAMe, curcuma, ginger, harpagophytum). **Regarding oral collagens (including UC-II), the review concludes there is only "low" grade evidence and does not support a positive recommendation to treat OA patients**, despite observing a mild effect on symptoms (pain) and function.

## UC-II / Undenatured Type II Collagen Evidence

### Specific UC-II study discussed (Lugo et al. 2016, ref 56)
- **Design:** Multicenter RCT, 190 patients, 3 arms: UC-II (40 mg) vs. glucosamine HCl + chondroitin sulfate vs. placebo
- **Duration:** 6 months
- **Primary outcome:** Total WOMAC change from baseline
- **Results:**
  - UC-II showed significantly greater reduction in WOMAC vs. placebo (551 vs. 414, p = 0.002)
  - UC-II showed significantly greater reduction in WOMAC vs. glucosamine + chondroitin (551 vs. 454, p = 0.009)
  - Lequesne index: greater reduction with UC-II (2.9 vs. 2.1, p = 0.009)
  - Pain VAS also recorded as secondary outcome
- **Note:** The WOMAC values reported (551, 414, 454) appear to be total WOMAC scores (not subscale); the absolute magnitude is unusual and should be verified against the original study.

### Second UC-II study discussed (Crowley et al. 2009, ref 59)
- **Design:** RCT, 26 patients per group
- UC-II compared to glucosamine + chondroitin
- **Result:** No inter-group differences in efficacy

### Systematic review cited (Van Vijven et al. 2012, ref 58)
- 8 trials total: collagen hydrosylates (3 vs placebo), gelatin (1 vs placebo), undenatured collagen (vs glucosamine + chondroitin)
- **Conclusion:** "low" grade evidence for collagens in OA
- Collagen hydrosylates (10 g/day): 313 treated vs. 297 placebo
  - Significant effect on WOMAC pain (-0.48), small effect size 0.17
  - No effect on joint structure (MRI)

## Mechanism Discussion

- **Very limited mechanism discussion specific to UC-II.** The review states oral collagens are "a rich source of amino acids" and are "purported to stimulate the joint to produce endogenous collagens in response to supplementation" -- this is the only mechanistic statement.
- **No discussion of oral tolerization** or immune modulation mechanism via gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT), which is the primary hypothesized mechanism for UC-II specifically.
- The review does not distinguish between mechanism of undenatured vs. hydrolyzed collagen -- treats all collagen preparations under a single umbrella.

## Positioning Among Alternative Therapies

The review organizes collagens among "other medical therapies" -- a lower tier compared to the more extensively reviewed ACI, MSCs, PRP, and vitamin D. Key comparative points:

| Therapy | Evidence Grade | Symptomatic Benefit | Structural Benefit |
|---------|---------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| ACI/MACI | Limited | Some (cartilage defects only) | Uncertain |
| MSCs | Poor quality studies | Some pain relief | Minimal, unconvincing |
| PRP | Mixed | Mild symptomatic benefits | Not demonstrated |
| Vitamin D | 4 negative RCTs | Inconclusive | Inconclusive |
| **Oral collagens** | **Low** | **Mild effect** | **None** |
| MSM | Small trials only | Possible short-term | N/A |
| SAMe | Cochrane: 4 RCTs, 656 pts | Very minor (4mm on 100mm VAS) | N/A |
| Curcuma | Sparse | Mild | N/A |

## Key Conclusions from Review

1. **For all alternative interventions:** studies are "often of small size, limited statistical power, uncertain blindness and using various methodologies."
2. **None of these therapies would currently clear regulatory approval** hurdles required for pharmaceutical interventions.
3. **Collagens specifically:** "current data do not support a positive recommendation to treat OA patients despite a mild effect on symptoms (pain) and function."
4. Summary table (Table 3) categorizes collagens/MSM/SAMe/curcuma/ginger together as having "very limited clinical trial data."

## Limitations of This Review for UC-II Specifically

1. UC-II receives only ~1 paragraph of dedicated discussion within the broader collagen section
2. Only 2 UC-II RCTs are cited (Lugo 2016 and Crowley 2009) -- other UC-II trials may exist but are not discussed
3. No mechanism distinction between undenatured and hydrolyzed collagen
4. The review does not cite any meta-analysis specific to UC-II
5. No safety data specifically for UC-II is discussed
6. Dose-response not explored

## Claims to Verify

- **WOMAC values (551 vs. 414 vs. 454):** These are unusual absolute values for WOMAC (typical total WOMAC range is 0-96 for the pain/function/stiffness subscales, or 0-2400 if using individual question scoring). Should verify against the original Lugo et al. 2016 paper whether these are total scores, change scores, or otherwise transformed.
- **Crowley et al. 2009 sample size:** 26 per group is reported here but should be verified.

## Relevance to Research Question

This source provides **moderate** evidence for UC-II positioning: it confirms UC-II is discussed in a major ESCEO-sponsored review alongside other alternative OA therapies, that it shows some symptomatic benefit in at least one trial, but that the overall evidence grade for collagens is rated "low." The review's dismissive stance on mechanism (no mention of oral tolerization) highlights a gap in how UC-II is evaluated in broader OA reviews.
