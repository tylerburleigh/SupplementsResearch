# src-080: Khatri et al. (2021) - Systematic review of collagen peptide supplementation + exercise

## Core Findings

This is a systematic review of 15 RCTs (656 participants) evaluating collagen peptide supplementation (COL) combined with exercise on joint health, body composition, muscle recovery, and protein synthesis. The review focuses overwhelmingly on hydrolyzed collagen peptides; only one of the 15 included studies (Lugo et al. 2013) used UC-II (undenatured type II collagen). The review found COL most beneficial for improving joint functionality and reducing joint pain, with more modest effects on body composition, strength, and muscle recovery.

## Relevance to UC-II / Undenatured Type II Collagen

This paper is **primarily about hydrolyzed collagen peptides**, not UC-II. However, it includes one UC-II study (Lugo et al. 2013) and discusses it in the context of joint health outcomes.

### UC-II Specific Data (from Lugo et al. 2013, as reviewed here)

- **Dose**: 40 mg/day undenatured type II collagen derived from chicken sternum
- **Population**: 55 participants with joint discomfort during physical activity (46 +/- 2 years COL, 47 +/- 2 years PLA)
- **Exercise**: Step-mill exertion test after 4 months supplementation
- **Outcomes**:
  - Increased knee extension with COL vs PLA (p = 0.011)
  - Increased pain-free strenuous exertion duration with COL from baseline (2.8 +/- 0.5 min vs 1.4 +/- 0.2 min; p = 0.019)
  - No significant changes in PLA group
  - Knee extension range: 81.0 +/- 1.3 degrees (COL) vs 73.2 +/- 1.9 degrees (PLA)
- **Proposed mechanism**: UC-II activates T regulatory cells specific to type II collagen; releases anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-10, TGF-beta) counteracting pro-inflammatory cascade from strenuous exercise, shifting toward ECM replenishment by chondrocytes
- **Quality score**: 85.71% (excellent)

### Key Quote on UC-II Mechanism
"Therefore, supplementing even 40 mg/day of UC-II may have the potential to improve joint functionality and range of motion."

## Broader Review Findings (Hydrolyzed Collagen)

### Joint Pain and Recovery (5 studies, avg quality 83%)
- Clark et al. 2008: 10 g/day collagen hydrolysate for 24 weeks in 97 athletes; decreased joint pain at rest (p=0.025), walking (p=0.007), standing (p=0.011); ES=0.36 for walking pain
- Zdzieblik et al. 2017: 5 g/day collagen peptides for 12 weeks in 139 athletes; 38.4% vs 27.9% pain reduction (COL vs PLA)
- Dressler et al. 2018: 5 g/day for 6 months in 50 athletes; CAIT score increased 5.28+/-1.16 (p<0.001)
- Praet et al. 2019: 5 g/day for 6 months in 20 Achilles tendinopathy patients; VISA-A score improved 12.6 points (COL) vs 5.3 points (PLA)

### Body Composition (4 studies, avg quality 82%)
- Zdzieblik et al. 2015: 15 g/day for 12 weeks in 53 elderly sarcopenic men; FFM +4.2 kg (COL) vs +2.9 kg (PLA); FM -5.4 kg (COL) vs -3.5 kg (PLA)
- Kirmse et al. 2019: 15 g/day for 12 weeks in 57 young men; increased FFM (p<0.05); no change in BFM with COL but increase with PLA
- Changes in FFM attributed to connective tissue, not myofibrillar protein (no fCSA hypertrophy differences)

### Muscle Recovery (2 studies, avg quality 82%)
- Clifford et al. 2019: 20 g/day for 7 days; reduced muscle soreness 48h post-exercise (ES=2.64)
- Lopez et al. 2015: 3 g/day BioCell collagen (contains chicken sternal cartilage collagen) for 6 weeks; improved perceived recovery (ES=0.66); reduced CK/LDH biomarkers

### Collagen and Muscle Protein Synthesis (4 studies, avg quality 85%)
- Shaw et al. 2017: 15 g/day vitamin C-enriched gelatine; collagen synthesis +153% from baseline (PINP markers)
- COL had lower MPS vs whey protein and lactalbumin in isonitrogenous comparisons (Oikawa et al. 2020a,b)
- COL has PDCAAS score of 0 (lacks tryptophan); whey/lactalbumin have PDCAAS of 1

## Methodology

- **Design**: Systematic review following PRISMA guidelines
- **Search**: PubMed, Web of Science, CINAHL (June 2020)
- **Included**: 15 RCTs from 856 screened articles
- **Quality assessment**: NHLBI tool; 11/15 rated excellent (>75%)
- **Populations**: Recreational athletes (12 studies), elderly (2), untrained pre-menopausal women (1)
- **No meta-analysis** was performed (heterogeneous outcomes)

## Limitations

- Only 1 of 15 studies used UC-II; the rest used hydrolyzed collagen peptides
- No meta-analysis; qualitative synthesis only
- Most studies funded commercially (13/15); potential publication bias
- Small sample sizes in many included studies (e.g., n=8 in Shaw et al., n=8 in Lopez et al.)
- Heterogeneous outcome measures across studies
- Few studies in females or elite athletes
- Baseline characteristics sometimes differed between groups

## Relevance Assessment

This source provides **tangential** evidence on UC-II specifically. It is primarily a review of hydrolyzed collagen peptides. The UC-II content is limited to a summary of the Lugo et al. (2013) study, which is one of the 15 reviewed RCTs. The proposed mechanism for UC-II (T-reg cell activation, anti-inflammatory cytokine release) is briefly discussed. For a review focused on UC-II, this paper's value is in contextualizing UC-II within the broader collagen supplementation landscape and noting the much lower effective dose (40 mg/day vs 5-15 g/day for hydrolyzed collagen).

## Claims to Verify

- Total participant count stated as 656 (325 males, 276 females, one study not reporting sex). The math checks: 325+276=601, meaning ~55 unaccounted from Lugo et al. which reported n=55 but did not break down by sex -- this is consistent.
- Shaw et al. 2017 collagen synthesis increase of 153% -- reported directly from the original paper, appears reliable.
- PINP increase of 30 ng/ml (0 ng/ml baseline vs 30 ng/ml at 4h) with 15g COL -- seems plausible but should be verified against the original Shaw et al. paper.
