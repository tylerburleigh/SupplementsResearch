# src-047: Undenatured type II collagen prevents and treats osteoarthritis and motor function degradation in T2DM patients and db/db mice

**Citation:** Rui et al., Food & Function, 2021, 12, 4373-4391. DOI: 10.1039/D0FO03011B
**Content quality:** Abstract-only (DOI landing page; full text behind paywall). Abstract is detailed with quantitative results.

## Core Findings

UC-II supplementation for 3 months improved motor function and reduced OA symptoms in both db/db mice and T2DM patients with knee OA. The mechanism involves reduction of inflammatory markers (hs-CRP, MDA), upregulation of anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-4, IL-10, TGF-beta), suppression of MMP-3 and MMP-13, and increased antioxidant activity (SOD).

## Key Evidence

### Animal Data (db/db mice)
- **Motor function:** Movement trajectory area: 163.25 +/- 20.3 vs 78.52 +/- 20.14 cm2 (UC-II vs old model); stride length: 7.2 +/- 0.9 vs 5.7 +/- 1.1 cm; cadence: 14.12 +/- 2.7 vs 7.35 +/- 4.4 steps/s
- **Anti-inflammatory:** IL-4, IL-10, CTX-II, TGF-beta levels were 1.74x, 2.23x, 1.67x, and 1.84x higher in UC-II group vs OM group, respectively
- **Catabolic suppression:** MMP-3 and MMP-13 in UC-II group were approximately half those in OM group
- **Groups:** UC-II intervention, old model (OM), positive control, normal control (db/m mice), young control (young db/db)

### Human Data (T2DM patients with knee OA)
- **Sample:** 55 T2DM patients diagnosed with knee OA, randomized to UC-II vs placebo
- **Duration:** 3-month intervention
- **Symptom scores:** Significant decreases in pain, stiffness, and physical function scores (p < 0.05)
- **Physical performance:** 6-minute walk test (6 MWT) and total MET distances increased significantly (p < 0.05)
- **Knee biomechanics:** Varus angle decreased from 4.6 +/- 2.0 deg to 3.0 +/- 1.4 deg; knee flexion range increased from 57.9 +/- 14.0 deg to 66.9 +/- 10.4 deg
- **Biomarkers:** hs-CRP and MDA decreased; SOD increased (trends consistent with mouse data)

## Mechanism

- **Immune modulation:** UC-II increased anti-inflammatory cytokines IL-4, IL-10, and TGF-beta; decreased hs-CRP (systemic inflammation marker)
- **Cartilage protection:** Increased CTX-II (collagen turnover marker); decreased MMP-3 and MMP-13 (matrix-degrading enzymes)
- **Oxidative stress:** Decreased MDA (lipid peroxidation); increased SOD (antioxidant enzyme)
- Note: The paper title in the metadata mentions "modulates immune response" but the actual published title focuses on "motor function degradation in T2DM patients and db/db mice." The immune modulation is a secondary finding.

## Methodology

- Dual-model study: aging db/db mice (T2DM model) + 55 human T2DM patients with knee OA
- Randomized design for both arms
- 3-month intervention period for both species
- Serum biomarkers: inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress factors, MMPs
- Gait and physical activity assessment

## Limitations

- Small human sample (n=55 total across both groups)
- Short intervention period (3 months) -- may not capture long-term effects
- Abstract-only available; cannot verify methods details, randomization quality, or statistical approaches
- T2DM-specific population limits generalizability to non-diabetic OA populations
- No dose-response data reported in abstract

## Relevance to Research Questions

This source provides direct evidence for UC-II's mechanism of action (immune modulation via anti-inflammatory cytokine upregulation and MMP suppression), supports both animal and human efficacy data, and demonstrates biomarker-level changes consistent with clinical improvement. The T2DM-specific population is a unique angle.
