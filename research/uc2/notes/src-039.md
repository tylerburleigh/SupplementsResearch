# src-039: Niacinamide and UC-II modulates inflammatory response in MIA-induced OA rats

**Citation:** Sahin et al. (2021). Scientific Reports, 11:14724. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-021-94142-3
**Quality:** ok -- full paper text available, peer-reviewed, 32 citations
**Design:** Animal study (rat), MIA-induced knee OA model

## Core Findings

Oral supplementation with niacinamide (NA) and undenatured type II collagen (UCII), alone and in combination, significantly reduced serum and joint-tissue inflammatory markers in rats with MIA-induced knee osteoarthritis. The combination of UCII (4 mg/kg) + NA (200 mg/kg) was the most effective treatment, nearly normalizing IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, NF-kB, and other markers. UCII alone was generally more effective than NA alone for gait/pain outcomes, while NA at higher doses was more effective for joint-tissue-level inflammatory protein expression.

## Mechanism of Action

1. **NF-kB pathway suppression:** Both supplements reduced NF-kB protein expression in knee joint tissue. NF-kB overexpression drives proinflammatory cytokine production (IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha) in OA. UCII+NA200 brought NF-kB levels back to control values.
2. **Pro-inflammatory cytokine reduction:** IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, COMP, and CRP were all significantly elevated in OA rats (p < 0.05) and reduced by all treatments, with combinations providing the greatest reduction.
3. **MMP-3 and TGF-beta1 downregulation:** OA elevated MMP-3 and TGF-beta1 protein in joint tissue; supplements reversed this (p < 0.05).
4. **Collagen type II preservation:** OA reduced collagen type II expression; UCII+NA200 most effectively restored it.
5. **Antioxidant restoration:** OA increased MDA and decreased SOD, CAT, GPx. NA-containing treatments significantly reversed these changes (UCII alone did not significantly reduce MDA vs. MIA group).
6. **Niacinamide-specific mechanisms:** NA inhibits cytokine-mediated induction of nitric oxide synthase, reduces inflammation. May elevate NAD/NADP levels in synovial fluid to support cartilage repair via pentose shunt. GPR109a receptor pathway may share synergy with UCII via Treg activation.

- OA-induced increases: IL-1beta +236%, IL-6 +442%, TNF-alpha +283%, COMP +441%, CRP +501%
- Combination treatments consistently produced significantly greater reductions than monotherapies

### Oxidative Stress Markers (Table 5)
- MDA: Control 0.71, MIA 2.94, UCII alone 2.81 (not sig. different from MIA), NA200 2.00, UCII+NA200 2.31
- SOD, CAT, GPx all significantly reduced in OA; partially restored by treatments

### Doses
- **UCII:** 4 mg/kg BW, oral gavage
- **Niacinamide:** 40 mg/kg BW (NA40) or 200 mg/kg BW (NA200), oral gavage
- Duration: Started 1 week before MIA injection, continued to day 30 (approximately 37 days total)
- Source: UCII from Lonza (chicken sternum cartilage derived)

### Functional/Pain Outcomes
- Stride length decreased in OA; UCII+NA200 restored stride length to exceed control values at d14 and d21
- Paw area and paw width decreased in OA; all supplements increased, combinations most effective
- Joint diameter (swelling/edema) increased in OA; all supplements equally reduced it

### Histological/Radiographic Outcomes
- Kellgren-Lawrence scores: OA rats reached grade 3; monotherapies reduced to grade 2; UCII+NA200 reduced to grade 1
- Mankin scores: Elevated in OA; decreased with all treatments, UCII+NA200 most effective (p < 0.05)

### Western Blot (Joint Tissue Proteins)
- IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, COMP, MMP-3, NF-kB, TGF-beta1 all elevated in OA joint tissue
- IL-10 (anti-inflammatory) decreased in OA
- UCII+NA200 brought IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, and NF-kB back to control levels

## Safety
- Serum glucose, BUN, creatinine, ALT, AST remained unchanged across all groups (p > 0.05)
- No adverse effects reported from any supplement dose

## In Vitro Supporting Evidence
- THP-1 monocyte-derived macrophages treated with UCII, NA, or combination
- Combination reduced IL-6, COX2, TNF-alpha, NF-kB inflammatory markers
- Doses used in vitro: 50 ug/mL each

## Methodology
- 49 male Wistar rats (8 weeks, 180-200g), 7 groups of n=7 each
- MIA model: 1 mg MIA in 50 uL saline injected into right knee
- Supplements delivered by oral gavage
- Power analysis: G*Power, alpha=0.05, 85% power, effect size 0.65
- Statistics: ANOVA + Tukey post-hoc for parametric; Kruskal-Wallis + Mann-Whitney U for nonparametric (radiologic/histopath scores)
- ELISA for serum markers, Western blot for joint tissue proteins, HPLC for MDA
- Histopathology: H&E staining, Mankin scoring by blinded evaluator
- Ethics: Approved by Firat University Animal Ethics Committee (2019/88-135)

## Limitations
1. **Animal model only** -- MIA-induced OA in rats differs from human OA pathophysiology
2. **Small sample size** -- n=7 per group; power analysis justified it but limits generalizability
3. **Short duration** -- ~37 days of supplementation; no long-term data
4. **Mechanism not fully elucidated** -- authors state "detailed mechanism of NA in arthritis" and "detailed molecular mechanisms of action of UCII and NA" require further work
5. **Industry involvement** -- Vijaya Juturu (co-author) affiliated with Lonza Inc., the UCII supplier
6. **No dose-response for UCII** -- only one dose (4 mg/kg) tested
7. **Combination not formally tested for synergy** -- additive vs. synergistic effects not statistically distinguished
8. **UCII alone did not significantly reduce MDA** vs. MIA group, suggesting limited antioxidant effect without NA

## Relevance
This paper provides direct evidence for anti-inflammatory mechanisms of UC-II (at 4 mg/kg) and niacinamide in OA. It documents NF-kB pathway suppression, reduction in key cytokines (IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha), and preservation of collagen type II. The combination data suggests potential additive/synergistic effects. Relevant for understanding UC-II mechanism of action, dose considerations, inflammatory marker profiles, and safety profile.

## Claims to Verify
- **n=7 per group with 85% power and effect size 0.65:** This is a small sample; the claimed power seems optimistic for a 7-group design. Verified from Methods text (line 141-142).
- **Percentage increases (235%, 438%, etc.) in Discussion:** Cross-checked against Table 4 data. IL-1beta: (47.62-20.13)/20.13 = 136.5%, not 235%. IL-6: (39.46-8.92)/8.92 = 342%, not 438%. These percentages in the Discussion appear to be inflated relative to the actual data in Table 4. This should be noted as a potential error in the manuscript.
