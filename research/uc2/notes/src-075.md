# src-075: A Review of the Effects of Collagen Treatment in Clinical Studies

**Citation:** Wang H. (2021). Polymers, 13(22):3868. DOI: 10.3390/polym13223868. PMCID: PMC8620403.
**Type:** Narrative review (not systematic, no PRISMA protocol reported)
**Author:** Single author, statistician (National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University, Taiwan)

## Core Findings

Broad narrative review covering collagen treatments across multiple conditions (skin, bone, sarcopenia, wound healing, dental, GERD, OA, RA). UC-II is discussed in the osteoarthritis section (Section 3.7) and RA section (Section 3.8), but this is not a UC-II-focused review. The paper concludes collagen-based treatments are broadly effective and safe, with collagen deficiency potentially underlying comorbidity across these conditions.

## UC-II Specific Content

### Osteoarthritis (Section 3.7)

1. **UC-II vs placebo vs glucosamine+chondroitin (GC):** A multicenter double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study compared UC-II with placebo and with glucosamine hydrochloride plus chondroitin sulfate. On day 180, the UC-II group demonstrated a significant reduction in overall WOMAC score compared with both placebo and GC. (Ref 117)

2. **UC-II vs glucosamine+chondroitin (Crowley et al.):** Evaluated effectiveness and safety of UC-II compared with a combination of glucosamine and chondroitin (G+C) in knee OA. At the end of 90 days of treatment, UC-II treatment reduced an index score by 20%, compared with 6% in the G+C treatment group. The daily activities of the UC-II group were significantly enhanced. (Ref 124)

3. **Type II collagen + acetaminophen vs acetaminophen alone:** RCT with 39 patients diagnosed with knee OA, randomly distributed into two groups: acetaminophen (n=19) vs acetaminophen plus type II collagen (n=20) for 3 months. Result: type II collagen + acetaminophen was superior to acetaminophen alone. Note: this study used type II collagen generally, not specifically UC-II. (Ref 116)

### Mechanism Discussion

- Collagen type II is the main component of cartilage tissue and has potential as OA treatment.
- In OA cartilage, hypertrophic differentiation is observed in degenerative chondrocytes.
- Collagen type II decrease causes chondrocyte hypertrophy. In a mouse model, loss of collagen type II promotes chondrocyte hypertrophy via the BMP-SMAD1 pathway (Ref 123).
- **Two distinct mechanisms proposed for collagen nutraceuticals:** Native collagen (like UC-II) may work through "oral induction" (oral tolerance/immune modulation) despite poor absorption. Hydrolyzed collagen peptides can reach target sites where collagen synthesis is needed. Both are effective for OA pain.
- UC-II described as "a nutritional supplement extracted from chicken breast cartilage."

### Rheumatoid Arthritis (Section 3.8) -- Relevant to UC-II Mechanism

- 274 RA patients randomized to placebo or different dosages of oral cartilage-derived collagen type II for 24 weeks. At the lowest tested dose, positive effects were observed with no side effects. (Ref 126)
- 503 RA patients in a 24-week randomized methotrexate-controlled study of chicken collagen type II (CCII). CCII was effective in treating RA and safe for consumption. CCII works by "inducing oral tolerance." (Ref 127)

## Safety Data

- The review characterizes collagen treatment broadly as safe with "no serious adverse reactions."
- Section 4.3 discusses disadvantages: allergic reactions possible (shellfish allergy with marine collagen; bovine gelatin sensitivity); disease transmission risk from animal sources (BSE from bovine collagen).
- No specific UC-II adverse events are detailed in this review.
- For the RA collagen type II study: "no side effects" reported at lowest dose.

## Evidence Quality Assessment

**Limitations of this review:**
- **Not a systematic review.** No PRISMA protocol, no registration, no formal search strategy described. This is a narrative review by a single author who is a statistician (not a clinician or collagen researcher).
- **UC-II evidence is very thin.** Only two studies specifically about UC-II are cited (Refs 117 and 124), both described briefly without detailed methodology, sample sizes, or effect sizes beyond WOMAC score changes.
- **No risk of bias assessment** for any included studies.
- **No meta-analysis or quantitative synthesis.**
- **No specific sample sizes reported** for the two UC-II trials (the Crowley et al. study and the multicenter study). The original papers would need to be checked for this.
- The 20% reduction in "index score" for Crowley et al. is vague -- likely WOMAC but not specified as such in the review text.
- The review conflates different forms of type II collagen (undamaged/undenatured UC-II, hydrolyzed collagen peptides, general type II collagen) which have different proposed mechanisms.

**Strengths:**
- Published in a peer-reviewed journal (Polymers, MDPI).
- Covers broad landscape of collagen applications.
- Good discussion of mechanism distinction between native collagen (oral tolerance) and hydrolyzed collagen (direct substrate).

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **UC-II efficacy for OA:** Provides secondary citations to two UC-II RCTs but does not add primary data or detailed analysis. Useful for identifying key studies to track down (Refs 117, 124).
- **UC-II mechanism:** Briefly discusses oral tolerance/induction mechanism for native collagen type II. The RA section provides additional support for oral tolerance as a mechanism.
- **UC-II safety:** Only general safety claims, no specific UC-II safety data.
- **UC-II vs glucosamine/chondroitin:** Both cited studies compare UC-II to GC, providing evidence of superior efficacy.

## Claims to Verify

1. **"Multicenter double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study" of UC-II (Ref 117):** No sample size reported in this review. The original study should be verified for participant count and specific WOMAC outcomes.
2. **"UC-II reduced an index score by 20% vs 6% for G+C" (Crowley et al., Ref 124):** The "index score" is not specified. This is likely WOMAC but should be confirmed from the original paper. Sample size not reported.
3. **"274 patients" in RA collagen type II study (Ref 126):** Should be verified -- this is a well-known study but the number should be confirmed.
4. **"503 RA patients" in CCII study (Ref 127):** This is a specific number that should be cross-checked with the original publication.
