# src-084: Role of Collagen Derivatives in Osteoarthritis and Cartilage Repair: A Systematic Scoping Review

**Authors:** Honvo G, Lengelé L, Charles A, Reginster J-Y, Bruyère O
**Year:** 2020 | **Venue:** Rheumatology and Therapy | **Citations:** 46
**DOI:** 10.1007/s40744-020-00240-5
**Quality:** ok (full text extracted, ~111K chars, 697 sentences)

---

## Core Findings

This systematic scoping review identified 41 published studies on collagen derivatives in OA and cartilage repair (16 preclinical, 25 clinical including 4 systematic reviews/meta-analyses). The two main collagen derivative types studied are collagen hydrolysate (CH, 28 individual studies) and undenatured collagen (UC, 9 studies). Despite all in vivo preclinical studies and clinical trials concluding beneficial effects regardless of quality, the authors find that evidence remains preliminary: studies are short-term, small-scale, and there are very few placebo-controlled RCTs specifically on UC. The review does not perform formal quality assessment (by design, as a scoping review).

## UC-II / Undenatured Collagen Evidence

### Preclinical Studies on UC
Two in vivo studies investigated undenatured collagen:

1. **Bagi et al. (2017)** [ref 25]: Oral UC-II (chicken, 0.66 mg/kg/day) in Lewis rats with PMMT-induced cartilage injury. N=40 rats (10 per group). UC-II improved mechanical function of injured knee and prevented excessive deterioration of articular cartilage over 8 weeks. Brand: UC-II (InterHealth). Funded by Pfizer Consumer Healthcare.

2. **Di Cesare Mannelli et al. (2013)** [ref 47]: Native type II collagen (porcine, oral 1/3/10 mg/kg/day) in MIA-induced OA rat model. N=48 rats (4 groups of 12). Reported protective effect on cartilage and pain relief at low doses over 13 days. Brand: NA (Bioiberica, Spain). Funded by Italian Ministry of Instruction/University of Florence.

Both concluded UC has protective effects on articular cartilage, though they used different rat models, different collagen sources (chicken vs. porcine), and different dosages.

### Clinical Trials on UC
Five clinical trials investigated UC:

| Study | Design | N | Intervention | Duration | Key Finding |
|-------|--------|---|--------------|----------|-------------|
| Bagchi (2002) | Open-label pilot | 5 | UC-II 10 mg/day oral | 42 days | UC-II may serve as novel therapeutic tool in OA/RA |
| Crowley (2009) | RCT double-blind | 52 (26+26) | UC-II 40 mg/day vs. G+C | 90 days | UC-II improved daily activities vs. G+C (glucosamine+chondroitin) |
| Bakilan (2016) | RCT single-blind | 39 (20+19) | Native type II collagen 10 mg/day + acetaminophen vs. acetaminophen | 3 months | Effective symptomatic treatment with acetaminophen; no evidence of reduced cartilage destruction |
| Lugo (2016) | RCT double-blind | 186 (63+58+65) | UC-II 40 mg/day vs. placebo vs. GH+CS | 180 days | UC-II significantly improved knee function vs. placebo and vs. glucosamine HCl+CS by day 180 |
| Stancik (2012) | RCT double-blind | 58 (29+29) | Undenatured type I collagen 8 mg/day vs. placebo | 3+1 months | Effective for symptomatic knee OA with carry-over effect |

### Observational Studies on UC-II
- **Azeem (2019)** [ref 70]: Open-label, 100 knee OA patients, 40 mg/day UC-II for 120 days. Significantly improved knee function.
- **Mehra (2019)** [ref 73]: Prospective multicenter real-life study, 291 patients, 40 mg/day UC-II for 90 days. Safe and effective; reduced pain, stiffness, improved functional mobility. Brand: DUPACT (Wockhardt Ltd).

### Key UC-II Dosage Observations
- Clinical trials used 10 mg/day (pilot) or 40 mg/day (standard dose)
- The 40 mg dose contained 10 mg bioactive undenatured type II collagen (Crowley 2009) or yielded 1.2 mg UC-II per capsule (Mehra 2019)
- Treatment durations: 42 days to 180 days
- All UC clinical trials used oral administration

## Mechanism of Action

The review notes that the real working mechanism of collagen derivatives in OA "remains roughly unclear." Proposed mechanisms include:
- Stimulation of collagen biosynthesis by chondrocytes (Oesser & Seifert)
- Use of collagen-specific peptides as building blocks for articular cartilage (Oesser et al. 1999, showing 14C-labeled gelatine hydrolysate accumulated in mouse cartilage after oral administration)
- Inhibition of apoptosis and hypertrophy of chondrocytes (Dai 2018)
- Gut absorption confirmed: collagen peptides accumulate in cartilage tissue a few hours after ingestion (Oesser et al. 1999; Taga et al. 2019)

Specifically for UC-II, the preclinical study by Bagi et al. noted the need for "adequate immunological, histo-chemical and molecular methods to address some of the lingering questions regarding mechanism of action of 'slow-acting' product such as UC-II."

## Comparison: UC vs. CH

| Feature | UC (Undenatured Collagen) | CH (Collagen Hydrolysate) |
|---------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| Total studies | 9 (2 preclinical + 5 clinical trials + 2 observational) | 28 (4 in vitro + 10 in vivo preclinical + 10 clinical trials + 4 observational) |
| RCTs against placebo | Only 2 (Lugo 2016 on UC-II; Stancik 2012 on UC-I) | Most of the 10 clinical trials were placebo-controlled |
| Typical dose | 10-40 mg/day | 1-10 g/day |
| Administration | All oral | Oral and intra-articular |
| Molecular weight | Native (not hydrolyzed) | Various (2000-5000 Da typical) |
| In vitro findings | Not tested in vitro in this review | 3 of 4 in vitro studies found CH ineffective or detrimental to OA cartilage |
| Safety | Confirmed safe [ref 77] | Confirmed safe [ref 78] |

## Evidence Quality Assessment

- Van Vijven et al. (2012) meta-analysis: evidence moderate to low quality due to methodological shortcomings
- Liu et al. (2018) meta-analysis with GRADE: quality of evidence was "very low" for supplements
- Garcia-Coronado et al. (2019) meta-analysis: concluded collagen effective on WOMAC and VAS but did not perform GRADE assessment
- Small sample sizes across trials (largest UC RCT: 186 in Lugo 2016)
- Most trials 3-6 months duration
- Many studies industry-funded (UC-II studies funded by InterHealth Nutraceuticals, Pfizer Consumer Healthcare, Bioiberica)

## Research Gaps Identified

1. Very few placebo-controlled RCTs on UC (only 2 of 5 UC clinical trials were RCTs vs placebo)
2. No long-term (>11 months) or large-scale trials
3. No studies involving hip or hand OA (almost all on knee OA)
4. No clinical studies investigating radiological changes after UC supplementation
5. Mechanism of action still unclear
6. No harmonization of preclinical endpoints
7. Need for studies on products manufactured per Good Manufacturing Practice

## Limitations of this Review

- Scoping review by design: no formal quality assessment of included studies
- Did not report outcomes of individual studies in tables (for readability)
- Excluded chondrocyte-only studies
- Language restricted to English and French
- Funded partly by Lonza Consumer Health Ingredient Inc. (industry sponsor, though authors state independence)

## Claims to Verify

- Lugo (2016) sample size: 186 total (63 UC-II, 58 placebo, 65 GH+CS) -- stated in table, consistent with RCT design
- Mehra (2019): 291 patients -- largest observational study on UC-II; no control group
- Crowley (2009): 52 patients total (26 per arm) -- small for an RCT
- Garcia-Coronado (2019) meta-analysis included 5 RCTs: only one was on UC-II (Lugo 2016), the rest were on CH -- this is a relevant limitation for meta-analytic conclusions about UC specifically

## Relevance to Research Question

This source provides a comprehensive overview of the UC-II evidence landscape up to early 2020. It is particularly valuable for mapping the full body of UC-II clinical studies (5 trials + 2 observational), identifying their design limitations, and contextualizing UC-II within the broader collagen derivatives field. It confirms the UC-II dose range (10-40 mg/day), administration route (oral), and the consensus that all published trials reported positive effects. However, it also highlights critical gaps: very few placebo-controlled RCTs, small sample sizes, short durations, and unclear mechanism. The comparison with CH (more studied but with concerning in vitro findings) is informative.
