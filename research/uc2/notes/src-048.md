# src-048: UC-II Collagen in MIA-Induced OA in Young and Old Rats

**Citation:** Sahin E, Orhan C, Erten F, Saiyed Z, Azari EK, Durkee S, Sahin K. (2023). Scientific Reports, 13:6499. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-33763-2. Cited 13 times.

## Study Type

**Animal study (preclinical).** Not a human clinical trial. Female Wistar albino rats with chemically-induced knee osteoarthritis (MIA model). This is a dose-response study of undenatured type II collagen (UC-II) in rats, not a human back pain or knee pain clinical trial.

## Core Findings

All doses of oral UC-II (0.66, 1.33, 2 mg/kg) reduced knee diameter (swelling) in both young (8-week) and old (18-month) female rats with MIA-induced knee OA. Higher doses (1.33 and 2 mg/kg) reduced Mankin histopathology scores and improved gait parameters as early as day 14. The 2 mg/kg dose showed the best efficacy overall by day 28. UC-II reduced inflammatory markers (IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, CRP, PGE2) and cartilage breakdown markers (COMP, MMP3) in serum and joint tissue. In young but not old rats, all doses reduced Kellgren-Lawrence radiographic scores. UC-II operated via oral tolerance mechanism involving Treg cells and TGF-beta.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **Subjects:** 70 female Wistar rats (35 young: 8 weeks, ~170g; 35 old: 18 months, ~340g); 7 per group, 5 groups per age cohort
- **Doses:** 0.66 mg/kg (HED ~40 mg/day), 1.33 mg/kg (HED ~80 mg/day), 2 mg/kg (HED ~120 mg/day)
- **Duration:** 37 days of oral UC-II via gastric gavage; OA induced on day 7 of supplementation
- **OA model:** Monosodium iodoacetate (MIA) intra-articular injection into right knee
- **Primary outcomes:** Gait parameters (paw area, stride length, pad width), knee diameter, Kellgren-Lawrence score, Mankin histopathology score
- **Biomarkers:** Serum IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, COMP, CRP, PGE2 (ELISA); joint tissue IL-1beta, IL-6, IL-10, TNF-alpha, COX2, NF-kB, collagen type 2, MMP3, COMP, TGF-beta (Western blot)
- **Key result:** 2 mg/kg dose was most effective, improving Mankin score and gait measurements by day 28
- **Dose-response:** Observed for CRP, IL-6 (serum), NF-kB, MMP3, COMP (tissue) in both age groups
- **Age difference:** All doses reduced Kellgren-Lawrence score in young rats; only 1.33 and 2 mg/kg in old rats

## Adverse Events / Safety

No adverse effects observed. No changes in body weight, serum glucose, BUN, creatinine, ALT, or AST levels across any dose group in either age group. No pathomorphological, hematological, or biochemical changes detected. Authors note supplementation appears safe at all tested doses.

## Population

Female Wistar albino rats only. Not a human study. Young rats were 8 weeks old (~170g); old rats were 18 months old (~340g). Female rats chosen due to higher susceptibility to experimental arthritis.

## Does It Address Back Pain?

**No.** This study exclusively examines knee osteoarthritis (MIA-induced in the right knee joint). There is no mention of back pain, spinal joints, or intervertebral disc issues. All outcomes pertain to knee joint health (knee diameter, knee radiographs, knee cartilage histopathology, gait).

## Funding and Conflicts of Interest

- **Funding:** Lonza Greenwood LLC (SC, USA) and Turkish Academy of Sciences (TUBA). Authors state funders were not involved in design, analysis, interpretation, writing, or publication decisions.
- **Competing interests:** Z. Saiyed, S. Durkee, and E.K. Azari are employees of Lonza Greenwood LLC -- the company that manufactures UC-II and funded the study. This is a significant conflict of interest for a dose-response study of their product.

## Methodology

- Randomized controlled design with 5 groups per age cohort
- Power analysis performed (G*Power, 0.05 type I error, 0.8 effect size, 90% power) yielding n=7/group
- Blinded assessment: radiologist and histopathologist blinded to groups
- Statistical: Shapiro-Wilk normality, Levene's test, one-way ANOVA + Tukey (parametric), Kruskal-Wallis + Mann-Whitney (non-parametric)
- Western blots repeated 3 times for reliability; beta-actin normalization
- Limitations noted: no estrogen evaluation, no healthy-animal control for UC-II effect, exact mechanism unclear

## Limitations for Human Relevance

1. **Animal study only** -- no human clinical data; results cannot be directly extrapolated to human OA or back pain
2. **MIA model** induces OA chemically, which differs from gradual degenerative OA in humans
3. **Short duration** (37 days total, 28 days post-OA induction)
4. **Industry-funded** by the UC-II manufacturer, with 3 co-authors being company employees
5. **Female rats only** -- sex-specific responses not assessed
6. **Knee-only** -- no data on axial joints, spine, or back pain

## Relevance to Research Question

This source provides preclinical evidence for UC-II dose-response in knee OA in rats. It does not address back pain. It supports the biological plausibility of UC-II for joint health via oral tolerance and anti-inflammatory mechanisms, and provides dose-response data (higher doses more effective). The human equivalent doses tested (40-120 mg/day) align with typical commercial UC-II supplement doses (40 mg/day). However, this is an animal study and cannot serve as evidence for clinical efficacy in humans.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample size claim: n=7/group with 90% power at 0.8 effect size -- this is a small n; the power analysis parameters should be verified for plausibility given the observed effect sizes
- HED conversion: Authors cite Shin et al. (ref 32) for the mg/kg to human dose conversion -- the conversion factor should be verified for accuracy
- The "1.66 mg/kg" dose mentioned on line 216 appears to be a typo for "1.33 mg/kg" based on the rest of the paper
