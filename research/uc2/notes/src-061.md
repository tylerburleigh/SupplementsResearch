# src-061: Parihar et al. (2025) - HTO vs UC-II + NSAIDs in Early Knee OA

## Core Findings

This non-randomized prospective cohort study compared high tibial osteotomy (HTO, n=27) against conservative management with UC-II 40 mg/day + NSAIDs (n=27) in active middle-aged adults (40-60 years) with Kellgren-Lawrence grade I-III medial compartment knee OA over 9 months. Both groups showed statistically significant within-group improvements in KSS and VAS, but **no between-group statistical testing was performed** due to the non-randomized design. HTO showed numerically larger gains (KSS +36.0 vs +22.7; VAS -5.3 vs -3.9), but these are associative trends only.

## UC-II Evidence Summary

- **Dose**: UC-II 40 mg/day (one capsule) for minimum 3 months, alongside NSAIDs (aceclofenac 100 mg BID) and calcium 500 mg
- **KSS improvement**: Conservative group improved from 53.0 +/- 6.7 to 75.7 +/- 9.0 at 9 months (+22.7 points, 95% CI +18.4 to +26.9, p < 0.001, paired t-test)
- **VAS improvement**: Conservative group improved from 7.0 +/- 1.2 to 3.1 +/- 1.1 at 9 months (-3.9 points, 95% CI -4.5 to -3.3, p < 0.001)
- **Progressive gains**: KSS at 3mo: 59.8 +/- 7.7; 6mo: 67.5 +/- 8.8; 9mo: 75.7 +/- 9.0
- **KSS grade shifts**: At baseline 22/27 poor, 5/27 fair; at 9 months 1 poor, 8 fair, 12 good, 6 excellent
- **Critical limitation**: UC-II was always co-administered with NSAIDs; the independent effect of UC-II cannot be isolated

## Methodology

- **Design**: Non-randomized, prospective, preference-based cohort study (NOT an RCT)
- **Setting**: Gajra Raja Medical College, Gwalior, India (Oct 2022 - May 2024)
- **Population**: 54 patients, 24M/30F, age 40-60, K-L grade I-III medial compartment knee OA
- **Allocation**: Based on patient preference after counseling -- introduces selection bias
- **Sample size**: Calculated from OA prevalence of 3.62%, yielding n=54 (27 per group)
- **Blinding**: None -- outcome assessors were aware of treatment allocation
- **Analysis**: Within-group paired t-tests only; no between-group statistical testing performed. Authors mention ANCOVA and linear mixed-effects models were added but results are not clearly reported
- **Outcomes**: KSS (1989 version) and VAS at 3, 6, and 9 months

## Safety Assessment

- **No systematic adverse event reporting was performed** -- explicitly acknowledged as a limitation
- No adverse events are reported in the results
- The study states "systematic adverse event reporting was not performed and should be incorporated in future studies"
- UC-II was given alongside NSAIDs, so safety of UC-II alone cannot be assessed
- No information on gastrointestinal, allergic, or other adverse reactions

## Evidence Gaps Identified

1. **Cannot isolate UC-II effect**: UC-II was always combined with NSAIDs (aceclofenac); independent efficacy unknown
2. **No between-group statistics**: Authors deliberately avoided intergroup testing due to non-randomized design
3. **Selection bias**: Patient-preference allocation means groups may differ systematically (though baseline demographics were comparable)
4. **Short follow-up**: Only 9 months; long-term durability, radiographic progression, and time to arthroplasty unknown
5. **No adverse event data**: Systematic safety reporting was absent
6. **No radiographic outcome data for conservative group**: X-rays taken at every visit but results not reported for UC-II group
7. **Small sample**: 27 per group limits statistical power
8. **No placebo control**: Cannot distinguish UC-II/NSAID effects from natural history

## Clinical Recommendations (from authors)

- Both HTO and conservative UC-II + NSAIDs are "viable options" for early OA in young active patients
- HTO provides mechanical correction and may delay TKA longer-term
- Conservative management may provide symptomatic benefit through "biochemical modulation"
- Authors call for future RCTs comparing collagen-only, NSAID-only, and combination therapy arms

## Referenced UC-II Studies

1. **Sadigursky et al. (2022)**: 53 UC-II patients, VAS 7.1 to 3.4 at 3 months (p < 0.001)
2. **Oentarini/Tjandra et al. (2023)**: RCT, VAS decreased 67.9% with UC-II vs 12.2% placebo
3. **Martinez-Puig et al. (2023)**: Review on collagen supplementation for joint health

## Relevance to UC-II Research

This study provides moderate-strength evidence that UC-II + NSAIDs improves knee OA symptoms over 9 months, but the lack of a UC-II-only arm means it cannot speak to UC-II's independent efficacy. It is one of the few studies evaluating UC-II outcomes using the KSS metric. The study is more useful as evidence for the comparative effectiveness question (surgical vs conservative) than for establishing UC-II's specific mechanism or standalone benefit.

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample size calculation**: Based on OA prevalence of 3.62% -- this is an unusual approach for a comparative study (typically powered on expected effect size difference). The resulting n=27 per group may be underpowered for detecting between-group differences.
- **UC-II dose**: Stated as 40 mg capsules, one per day. This is consistent with standard UC-II dosing in the literature.
- **KSS version**: Stated as 1989 version. The 2011 revision exists, so this is worth noting.
- **Between-group analysis claim**: The data analysis section mentions ANCOVA and linear mixed-effects models were added, but no specific results from these analyses appear in the Results section. This is a discrepancy.
