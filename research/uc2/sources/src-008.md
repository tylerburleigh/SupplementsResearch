What's the difference between types I, II, and III collagen?
There are at least 28 types of collagen, and three are particularly important for connective tissues.
Type I collagen
: Predominantly found in skin, bones, and tendons, this type supports tissue strength and resilience, making it popular in beauty supplements.
Type III collagen
: Often paired with Type I, Type III is found in muscles and blood vessels, aiding in tissue structure and elasticity.
Type II collagen
: Key to joint health, Type II collagen is found in cartilage: the protective layer at the ends of bones that cushions them and reduces friction. This type of collagen helps joints withstand the daily wear and tear of movement, making it vital for maintaining mobility.
Why is type II collagen better for joint health?
Type II collagen is needed for joint structure, as it forms the backbone of cartilage. Cartilage provides a smooth, cushioned surface for joint bones to move against each other without friction.
Type II collagen levels decrease naturally with age, which may lead to joint discomfort and reduced flexibility.
UC-II undenatured collagen (undenatured means it retains its original structure and has not been broken down in any way) can help to counteract this by enhancing joint function, comfort, and resilience, even as natural collagen levels decline.
How does UC-II collagen work?

UC-II interacts with immune cells in the gut, initiating a process that protects existing cartilage from breakdown, while promoting the body's own collagen synthesis.
This mechanism allows UC-II to support joint health at low doses, in contrast to hydrolysed collagen types, which require larger amounts to achieve a similar benefit.
