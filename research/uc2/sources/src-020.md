Which Type of Collagen Works Best for Joint Pain

Not all collagen supplements are equal. There are at least 28 types of collagen in the human body, but only a few matter for joint pain:

Type II collagen
is the primary structural protein in cartilage. This is the type with the strongest evidence for joint pain relief. A 40 mg daily dose of undenatured type II collagen (UC-II) showed significant results in multiple trials tracked by the Arthritis Foundation.

Type I collagen
is found in skin, bones, and tendons. Useful for bone density (a 2018 trial showed 5 grams daily increased bone mineral density in postmenopausal women) but less directly targeted at joint cartilage.

Hydrolyzed collagen peptides
are broken down for better absorption. A 2024 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in
Joint Diseases and Related Surgery
found that a combination of type I, III, and type II hydrolyzed collagen reduced osteoarthritis-related pain and improved physical function over 8 weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is collagen or glucosamine better for knee pain over 60?

Based on current evidence, type II collagen produces larger pain reductions than glucosamine for knee osteoarthritis. A clinical trial showed collagen reduced WOMAC pain scores by 33% versus 14% for glucosamine plus chondroitin over 90 days. That said, individual responses vary, and some people benefit from both.

How long does collagen take to work for joint pain?

Most clinical trials show improvements between 30 and 90 days. UC-II collagen at 40 mg daily often produces noticeable relief within 30-60 days. Hydrolyzed collagen peptides at 5-10 grams daily typically take 8-12 weeks for measurable improvement.

Can you take collagen and glucosamine together?

Yes. They work through different mechanisms, so taking both is safe. A 2025 study in
Joint Diseases and Related Surgery
examined a combination product containing both collagen and glucosamine sulfate and found positive outcomes for pain and function. However, starting with collagen alone for 90 days gives you a clearer picture of its individual effect.

What is the best type of collagen for osteoarthritis?

Undenatured type II collagen (UC-II) has the most direct evidence for osteoarthritis specifically. A 40 mg daily dose is the clinically studied amount. Hydrolyzed type II collagen peptides also show benefits at higher doses (5-10 grams daily) and offer the added advantage of supporting skin and bone health at the same time.

Does collagen help with hip pain in seniors?

The majority of clinical trials focused on knee osteoarthritis, so the evidence is strongest there. However, the mechanism (cartilage repair and immune modulation) applies to all synovial joints, including hips. Anecdotally, patients report hip pain improvement on the same timeline as knee pain improvement.

Are there any drug interactions with collagen supplements?

Collagen supplements have no widely documented drug interactions. This is an advantage over glucosamine, which can interact with warfarin and other blood thinners. Still, always inform your doctor about any supplements you take, especially if you’re on multiple medications.
