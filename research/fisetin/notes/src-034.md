# src-034: Intermittent supplementation with fisetin improves arterial function in old mice

**Source:** PubMed (PMID 38062873), Aging Cell, 2023. NIH-funded (NIA/NHLBI grants). Published by Anatomical Society / John Wiley & Sons.

**Content quality:** Abstract only (PubMed landing page, no full text). Abstract is detailed and informative with specific methods and outcomes.

---

## Core Findings

Intermittent oral fisetin supplementation improved both endothelial function and arterial stiffness in old mice, with causal evidence from p16-3MR transgenic mice demonstrating that these benefits were mediated by senolysis (elimination of p16INK4A-positive senescent cells). Fisetin also reduced cellular senescence and SASP-related inflammation in vitro (human endothelial cells) and in vivo (old mouse vasculature).

## Dosing Regimen

- **Protocol:** Oral intermittent dosing -- 1 week on, 2 weeks off, 1 week on (total 2 weeks of dosing over a 4-week period)
- **Species:** Old mice (age not specified in abstract)
- **Assessment timing:** Outcomes measured 1 week after the final dose
- **Note:** The specific daily dose (mg/kg) is not stated in the abstract

## Arterial Function Outcomes

### Endothelial function
- Old fisetin-supplemented mice had higher endothelium-dependent dilation (EDD) in carotid arteries
- Improved EDD was mediated by increased nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability
- Reduced whole-cell and mitochondrial oxidative stress (lower ROS, altered NADPH oxidase, SOD, p-p66SHC, MnSOD)
- Ex vivo genetic senolysis (ganciclovir in p16-3MR arteries) increased EDD in vehicle-treated mice but NOT in fisetin-treated mice, proving fisetin acted through senolysis

### Arterial stiffness
- Aortic pulse wave velocity (PWV) was lower post-intervention in fisetin-treated mice (n=9-10)
- Ex vivo intrinsic mechanical wall stiffness (elastic modulus) was lower in fisetin group (n=7)
- Ex vivo genetic (GCV) and pharmacological (ABT-263) senolysis did not further reduce stiffness in fisetin-treated mice, confirming senolytic mechanism
- Favorable arterial wall remodeling: lower advanced glycation end products (AGEs), altered collagen-1 and alpha-elastin

## Senolytic Mechanism Evidence

- **In vitro:** Fisetin reduced SA-beta-Gal signal in senescent HUVECs; reduced Cdkn2a and Cdkn1a expression in senescent HAECs at 1 uM
- **In vivo:** Lower aortic expression of senescence markers (Cdkn2a, Cdkn1a, p16INK4A protein) and SASP factors in fisetin-treated old mice
- **Causal evidence:** p16-3MR transgenic model allowed genetic clearance of senescent cells ex vivo. The lack of additive benefit when senolysis was applied to fisetin-treated arteries demonstrates fisetin had already cleared the senescent cells responsible for dysfunction

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q2 (healthspan):** Directly relevant -- fisetin improved two major age-related arterial dysfunction endpoints (endothelial function and stiffness) via senolysis. Arterial stiffness is a major predictor of cardiovascular events.
- **Q6 (dosing regimens):** Provides key evidence for intermittent dosing (1 week on / 2 weeks off / 1 week on). This protocol mirrors the "hit and run" senolytic approach advocated for clinical translation.
- **Q3 (PK):** Limited direct PK data in abstract, but the intermittent protocol design and assessment 1 week post-final dose suggest sustained effects beyond the dosing window.

## Limitations

- Only abstract available; specific dose (mg/kg), mouse age, and detailed results (effect sizes, p-values) are in the full text figures
- Sample sizes visible from figure captions (n=7-18 per group) appear adequate but modest
- Mouse study; translation to human dosing requires clinical trials
- No dose-response data visible from abstract

## Claims to Verify

- **Specific fisetin dose:** The mg/kg/day dose is not stated in the abstract. This is critical for human dose extrapolation. Needs full-text verification.
- **Mouse age:** "Old mice" is not quantified in the abstract. Typical vascular aging studies use ~24-30 month old mice.
- **Sample sizes:** n values in figure captions range from 5-18. The in vivo EDD comparison uses n=12-18 which is reasonable, but some endpoints (e.g., MitoQ experiment, n=5-7) are small.
