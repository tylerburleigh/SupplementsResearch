# src-042: Intermittent Supplementation With Fisetin Improves Physical Function in Aging Mice

**Source:** PubMed PMID 40437670 | *Aging Cell* (2025) | Wiley/Anatomical Society
**Content quality:** Abstract + figure legends only (PubMed landing page; no full text available)
**Type:** Comparative Study (MeSH) | Mouse model (C57BL)

---

## Core Findings

Intermittent oral fisetin supplementation (1 week on / 2 weeks off / 1 week on) mitigated age-related increases in frailty and decreases in grip strength in old mice, with no effect in young mice. These improvements were comparable to both genetic-based clearance of p16+ senescent cells (p16-3MR model) and intermittent treatment with the synthetic senolytic ABT-263. Transcriptomic analysis of quadriceps skeletal muscle showed fisetin reduced expression of senescence-associated genes including Cdkn1a (p21) and Ddit4 (Redd1).

## Dosing Regimen (Q6)

- **Protocol:** Oral intermittent treatment: 1 week on, 2 weeks off, 1 week on (3-week total cycle)
- **Route:** Oral administration
- **Population:** Old mice (age not specified in abstract) and young mice (as control/comparator)
- **Key observation:** Fisetin had no effect on physical function in young mice, suggesting the benefit is specific to the aged/senescent phenotype
- **Note:** Exact dose (mg/kg) and vehicle details are not available from the abstract/figure legends -- would need full text for precise dosing numbers

## Physical Function Outcomes (Q2)

- **Frailty index score:** Fisetin attenuated age-related increases in frailty index (Figure 1B)
- **Grip strength:** Fisetin attenuated age-related reductions in normalized grip strength (Figure 1C)
- **Sex analysis:** Both male (squares) and female (inverted triangles) mice included in analyses
- **Statistics:** One-way ANOVA with Tukey's multiple comparisons test (p < 0.05 threshold)
- **Age interaction:** No effect of fisetin in young mice, indicating benefit is specific to aged phenotype

## Comparative Efficacy (Q7)

### vs. Genetic clearance (p16-3MR model)
- p16-3MR mice allow ganciclovir-induced clearance of p16+ senescent cells
- Genetic clearance mitigated age-related changes in frailty index and grip strength (Figure 4)
- Fisetin efficacy was **comparable** to this genetic approach (Figure 6 comparison)

### vs. ABT-263 (synthetic senolytic)
- ABT-263 given as oral intermittent treatment
- ABT-263 lessened age-related differences in frailty and grip strength (Figure 5)
- Notable: ABT-263 showed effects in **both young and old mice** (Figure 5 legend), unlike fisetin which only affected old mice
- Fisetin efficacy was **comparable** to ABT-263 in old mice (Figure 6 comparison)

### Head-to-head comparison (Figure 6)
- Data presented relative to old vehicle-treated mice in each respective study
- All three approaches (fisetin, p16-3MR clearance, ABT-263) showed similar magnitudes of improvement in frailty and grip strength

## Transcriptomic Changes

- **Tissue:** Quadriceps skeletal muscle, bulk RNA-seq
- **PCA:** Global gene expression separated by age; fisetin shifted old mouse transcriptome toward young profile
- **DEG criteria:** Log2FoldChange > +/-0.5, p < 0.05
- **Pathway analysis:** KEGG enrichment, FDR < 0.2 threshold
- **Key downregulated genes with fisetin:**
  - **Cdkn1a (p21):** Major senescence marker; p < 0.001 (Figure 3C)
  - **Ddit4 (Redd1):** DNA damage inducible transcript 4; p < 0.01 (Figure 3F)
- **Interpretation:** Fisetin reduced abundance of cellular senescence-related genes in aged skeletal muscle

## Methodology

- Animal model: C57BL mice, both sexes
- Three parallel study arms: (1) fisetin, (2) p16-3MR genetic clearance, (3) ABT-263
- Outcomes: frailty index score, normalized grip strength
- Molecular: bulk RNA-seq of quadriceps, volcano plots, KEGG pathway analysis
- Sex considered as a variable in data presentation (male/female symbols)
