# src-059: Senolytic reduction of senescent cells mitigates atrial arrhythmia susceptibility in aged rabbits

**Citation:** Heart Rhythm Society / Elsevier, 2026. PMID: 41513056 (inferred from URL).
**Source type:** PubMed abstract with full figure legends (no full text body).
**Quality assessment:** Abstract + detailed figure legends provide substantial data; no methods body text available.

## Core Findings

This study demonstrates that senescent atrial cells (myocytes and myofibroblasts) create a proinflammatory, proarrhythmic substrate that predisposes to atrial fibrillation (AF) in aging. Fisetin, used as a senolytic agent at 20 mg/kg in aged New Zealand White rabbits, eliminated most senescent atrial cells and significantly reduced AF inducibility without impairing atrial function. Human translational data from atrial specimens confirmed increased senescence markers in AF patients and a correlation between SASP factor expression and left atrial diameter.

## Study Design

- **Animal model:** Young (<=1 year) and aged (>=4 years) New Zealand White rabbits
- **Aged cohort electrophysiology:** Optical mapping (Langendorff-perfused hearts), patch-clamp on isolated atrial myocytes, S1S2 stimulation protocol for AF inducibility
- **Senescence assessment:** SA-beta-Gal staining, immunofluorescence (gammaH2AX + desmin/alphaSMA colocalization), RT-qPCR (p16/CDKN2A, p21/CDKN1A), RNA-seq with GSEA (SenMayo gene set), Western blot (p53, phospho-p53, p21)
- **Fisetin dosing:**
  - Protocol A: 20 mg/kg for 2 consecutive days
  - Protocol B: 20 mg/kg, 2 days/week, for 2 weekly cycles
- **Human translational arm:** Left and right atrial appendage specimens from cardiac surgery patients with AF (n=6 LAA, n=6 RAA, mean age 63.8) vs sinus rhythm (n=4-5, mean age 63.6-65.5)

## Atrial Fibrillation Outcomes

- **Spontaneous AF:** 23.1% of aged rabbits (3/13) vs 1.8% of young (3/163); p<.01
- **Induced AF (S1S2 protocol):** 61.5% of aged (8/13) vs 0% of young (0/8)
- **Electrophysiological changes in aged atria:**
  - APD30 prolonged (p=.0009), APD50 prolonged (p=3.8e-5), APD90 prolonged (p=.002)
  - APD dispersion significantly increased (p=.03)
  - Conduction velocity trended lower (p=.24)
- **Fisetin treatment results:**
  - Spontaneous AF: absent in fisetin group (present in vehicle)
  - Induced AF: markedly reduced in fisetin vs vehicle (p<.05, Fisher's exact test)
  - Reentry activity reduced; premature atrial contractions did not trigger AF in fisetin group
  - n=5 per group for electrophysiology outcomes

## Senolytic Mechanism in Cardiac Tissue

- Senescent cells identified as both **myocytes** (desmin+) and **myofibroblasts** (alphaSMA+), with gammaH2AX+ nuclei colocalizing with both cell types
- SenMayo gene set significantly enriched in aged vs young atria (GSEA)
- SASP profile characterized by upregulation of inflammatory and profibrotic factors
- SA-beta-Gal+ cells significantly increased in aged vs young rabbit atria (p<.01)
- p16 (CDKN2A) and p21 (CDKN1A) upregulated at RNA level (p<.001); p21 protein and phospho-p53/p53 ratio increased at protein level
- Fisetin eliminated most SA-beta-Gal+ cells in both dosing protocols (significant vs vehicle, p<.05, 1-way ANOVA with post hoc Tukey)

## Human Translational Data

- AF patients showed higher SA-beta-Gal+ cell percentages in both LAA and RAA compared to SR patients
- gammaH2AX+ senescent cells colocalized with desmin (myocytes) and alphaSMA (myofibroblasts) in human AF atria
- CD68+ macrophages also showed some gammaH2AX positivity, suggesting immune cell senescence
- p16 IHC confirmed elevated senescence marker in AF human atrial tissue
- SASP factor expression correlated with left atrial diameter (a clinical AF risk factor)
- Sample sizes modest (n=4-6 per group)

## Safety Profile

- The study reports that fisetin treatment decreased inducible AF and reentry activity **without impairing atrial function**
- No detailed toxicity or adverse event data provided in available content
- Two dosing protocols tested; both showed senolytic efficacy
- n=3 per group for senescence quantification; n=5 per group for electrophysiology -- these are small sample sizes

## Limitations

1. **Small sample sizes** throughout: n=3 for senescence quantification after fisetin, n=5 for electrophysiology per group, n=4-6 for human samples
2. **Preliminary results** noted for fisetin electrophysiology data (APD, CV characterizations described as "preliminary")
3. Only abstract and figure legends available; no full methods/results body text to verify dosing details, statistical methods, or comprehensive safety data
4. Human translational arm uses appendage tissue from cardiac surgery patients -- a selected population that may not represent general elderly
5. Fisetin dosing (20 mg/kg) in rabbits requires translation to human-equivalent doses for clinical relevance
6. No long-term follow-up data on fisetin effects

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q2 (healthspan - cardiovascular):** Strongly relevant. Provides direct preclinical evidence that fisetin reduces AF susceptibility in aged hearts via senolytic action. Human data confirms senescence-AF link. Demonstrates potential cardiovascular healthspan benefit.
- **Q4 (senolytic mechanism):** Strongly relevant. Characterizes senescent cell types in cardiac tissue (myocytes, myofibroblasts), SASP gene enrichment (SenMayo), and shows fisetin eliminates these cells with functional improvement. Mechanistic pathway from senescence to arrhythmia via SASP-mediated inflammation and electrophysiological remodeling.

## Claims to Verify

1. **Fisetin dose of 20 mg/kg in rabbits** -- stated in figure legend but not confirmed in full methods text. Verify human-equivalent dose calculation.
2. **"n=3 for all groups" in fisetin senescence quantification** -- very small. The p<.05 may not be robust. Note that electrophysiology data uses n=5 per group, which is separate.
3. **APD30 p=.0009 with n=16-19 cells from 4 hearts** -- cell-level n is adequate but animal-level n (4 hearts) is small. The statistics appear to be computed at the cell level, which may inflate significance.
4. **Human SASP-LA diameter correlation** -- mentioned in abstract but no specific correlation coefficient, p-value, or sample size for this analysis is available in the figure legends. Would need full text to verify.
