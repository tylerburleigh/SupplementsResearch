# src-058: New Perspectives for Fisetin

**Authors:** Grynkiewicz G, Demchuk OM
**Venue:** Frontiers in Chemistry, 2019, Volume 7, Article 697
**DOI:** 10.3389/fchem.2019.00697
**Citations:** 136
**Quality:** ok (full review article, substantive content)

## Core Findings

This review covers fisetin's biological activities (senolytic, anticancer, neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory) and critically evaluates synthetic approaches for pharmaceutical-grade fisetin production. The paper emphasizes that fisetin is more effective than quercetin as a single-agent senolytic and has demonstrated lifespan extension across model organisms, but faces pharmaceutical development challenges due to poor solubility and low bioavailability.

## Chemical Properties and Structure-Activity Relationships

- Fisetin (CAS 528-48-3): 2-(3,4-dihydroxyphenyl)-3,7-dihydroxy-4H-1-benzopyran-4-one; also called 3,3',4',7-tetrahydroxyflavone or 5-deoxyquercetin
- The catechol ring (3',4'-dihydroxy) is critical for electrophilic character, enabling quinone/quinone methide isomerization that acts as cellular nucleophile acceptors (based on Awad et al., 2001)
- The 5-deoxy structural feature distinguishes fisetin from quercetin and is exploited in synthetic strategies
- Fisetin occurs in plant glycosylated forms but these are seldom mentioned in phytochemical literature

## Pharmacokinetic Data

- **Solubility:** 10.45 ug/mL (practically insoluble in water, soluble in polar organic solvents)
- **Oral bioavailability:** 44%
- **Metabolism:** Rapid metabolism producing 3'-O-methylated metabolite (geraldol), sulfates, and glucuronides in Sprague-Dawley rats (Mehta et al., 2018)
- Geraldol shows higher AUC concentrations and better distribution to distal organs including the brain vs. other metabolites
- Cyclodextrin and cyclosophoroase dimer complexation proposed to improve solubility and cytotoxicity against HeLa cells (Jeong et al., 2013; Zhang et al., 2015)

## Mechanism of Action

### Senolytic Activity
- More effective than quercetin as a single-agent senolytic; reduces senescence markers without combination therapy (Yousefzadeh et al., 2018)
- Overcomes senescent-cell anti-apoptotic pathways (SCAP) resistance
- Extends lifespan in S. cerevisiae, D. melanogaster, and vertebrate animals of both sexes (Wood et al., 2004; Si et al., 2011; Wagner et al., 2015)

### mTOR Inhibition
- Inhibits mTOR pathway in various cell models, affecting aging-related cellular pathways (Syed et al., 2013; Pallauf et al., 2016)

### Anticancer Targets
- Targets include: AMPK, COX, EGFR, ERK1/2, MMP, NF-kB, PSA, TCF, TRAIL, WIF-1, XIAP
- With ascorbic acid: 61% inhibition of carcinoma cell growth in 72h (Kandaswami et al., 1993)

### Antioxidant and Neuroprotective
- Induces glutathione synthesis (neuroprotective relevance)
- Enhances long-term memory, antidepressant effects, inhibits ischemic reperfusion injury (Khan et al., 2013; Maher, 2015; Currais et al., 2018)

## Clinical Trial Evidence

- **NCT03675724 (AFFIRM-LITE):** Mayo Clinic (Kirkland team); oral fisetin up to 20 mg/kg for "Alleviation by Fisetin of Frailty, Inflammation, and Related Measures in Older Adults"
- **NCT03430037:** Evaluated antioxidant, anti-apoptotic, hyperglycemia-alleviating, and kidney function-enhancing effects
- **AFFIRM trial:** Studied inflammation markers, insulin resistance, bone resorption, and frailty in older postmenopausal women
- Fisetin combined with red wine polyphenols, vitamin D3 showed synergistic effects on visual acuity in age-related macular degeneration patients (Ivanova et al., 2017)

## Safety and Dosing Considerations

- Average daily dietary intake estimated at 0.4 mg (Kashyap et al., 2018)
- Strawberries are the richest source at ~160 ug/g fisetin
- Fisetin is present in strawberries, apples, persimmons, grapes, onions, kiwi, kale in low concentrations (up to hundreds of ug/g fresh biomass)
- Clinical trials used doses up to 20 mg/kg -- far exceeding dietary intake
- Dietary supplement products exist but carry FDA disclaimers; clinical evaluation remains rare
- Iron complexes with fisetin derivatives show effects similar to desferrioxamine for beta-thalassemia (Yildiz et al., 2010)

## Synthetic/Manufacturing Considerations

- Focus of this paper is heavily on API manufacturing: Kostanecki synthesis (1904), Robinson synthesis (1926), Algar-Flynn-Oyamada (AFO) reaction, Pd-catalyzed arylation (Rao and Kumar, 2014)
- Biosynthetic pathway from L-tyrosine in E. coli and S. cerevisiae has been demonstrated (Jendresen et al., 2015; Stahlhut et al., 2015)
- Authors argue synthetic or biotechnological production will be needed for pharmaceutical-grade supply

## Limitations

- This is a review article, not a primary data source -- all quantitative claims derive from cited studies
- Pharmacokinetic data (44% bioavailability, 10.45 ug/mL solubility) cited without specifying the original study
- Much of the paper is devoted to chemical synthesis methods rather than biological activity
- The lifespan extension claims reference model organism studies but do not provide specific magnitudes of effect
