# src-022: Fisetin is a senotherapeutic that extends health and lifespan

**Citation:** Yousefzadeh MJ, Zhu Y, McGowan SJ, et al. eBioMedicine. 2018;36:18-28. doi:10.1016/j.ebiom.2018.09.015
**PMID:** 30279143 | **PMCID:** PMC6197652
**Venue:** eBioMedicine (open access, Elsevier)
**Quality:** ok - full text available, peer-reviewed

## Core Findings

This landmark study identifies fisetin as the most potent senolytic among 10 flavonoids screened, demonstrates its senolytic activity in vivo in both progeroid and aged wild-type mice, and provides the first evidence that a single flavonoid senolytic can extend both median and maximum lifespan when initiated late in life. Human adipose tissue explant data support translational potential.

## Study Design and Methods

### In vitro screening
- Screened 10 flavonoids (fisetin, luteolin, curcumin, quercetin, resveratrol, rutin, EGCG, apigenin, catechin, myricetin, pirfenidone) at 5 uM
- Cell models: Ercc1-/- MEFs (oxidative stress-induced senescence) and IMR90 human fibroblasts (etoposide-induced senescence)
- Readout: SA-beta-gal activity via C12-FDG fluorescence
- Fisetin showed dose-dependent reduction of senescent cells in both cell types

### In vivo - Progeroid mice (Ercc1-delta/-)
- p16Ink4a-luciferase reporter mice for non-invasive senescence monitoring
- Dietary fisetin: 500 ppm (500 mg/kg chow, approx. 60 mg/kg/day) ad libitum
- Intermittent schedule: 2 weeks on, 4 weeks off, 2 weeks on (ages 6-8 and 12-14 weeks)
- Also tested chronic treatment from 10 weeks of age for 10 weeks

### In vivo - Aged wild-type mice
- **Acute dosing:** 22-24 month-old C57BL/6 mice, 100 mg/kg oral gavage, 5 consecutive days
- **Lifespan study:** f1 C57BL/6:FVB mice, dietary fisetin (500 ppm) starting at 85 weeks of age (~20 months, human equivalent ~75 years), n=8-9 per group
- Tissues analyzed: fat, spleen, liver, kidney, brain, lung, forepaw

### Human ex vivo
- Greater omental adipose explants from 3 subjects (ages 55-66, BMI 25.5-45.6)
- 20 uM fisetin for 48h, then 24h drug-free culture

## Key Results

### Fisetin is the most potent flavonoid senolytic
- At 5 uM, fisetin was most effective at reducing SA-beta-gal+ Ercc1-/- MEFs
- Dose-dependent reduction in both MEFs and IMR90 cells (1-15 uM range)
- Luteolin and curcumin showed weak activity; quercetin was ineffective at this dose

### Senescence reduction in vivo
- Progeroid mice: p16-luciferase signal suppressed throughout treatment and during off-drug periods (consistent with hit-and-run senolytic mechanism)
- Reduced p16Ink4a, p21Cip1, and SASP markers (Il1b, Il6, Il10, Tnfa, Cxcl2, Mcp1, Pai1) in fat, spleen, liver, kidney
- Reduced senescence markers in peripheral CD3+ T cells

### Cell-type specificity (CyTOF)
- Fisetin cleared p16+ mesenchymal stem/progenitor cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, NK cells, and endothelial cells from subcutaneous fat
- Did NOT substantially affect macrophages or dendritic cells
- First demonstration of cell-type-specific senolytic activity in vivo

### Lifespan and healthspan extension
- Late-life fisetin (starting at 85 weeks) extended both median AND maximum lifespan (log-rank test)
- Reduced composite tissue lesion scores (histopathology) in brain, kidney, liver, lung
- Lower serum ALT and amylase (improved liver and pancreatic function)
- Reduced circulating MCP-1 levels
- Reduced hepatic oxidative stress (HNE adducts, GSH:GSSG ratio)

### Human adipose tissue explants
- 20 uM fisetin reduced SA-beta-gal+ cells
- Reduced SASP factors: IL-6, IL-8, MCP-1

## Senolytic Mechanism Evidence

- Hit-and-run mechanism: effects persist after drug withdrawal (p16-luciferase remained suppressed during 4-week off-drug period)
- Consistent with senescent cell clearance rather than senescence suppression requiring continuous drug presence
- Fisetin's terminal half-life is only 3.1 h, yet benefits persist days after dosing
- In HUVECs, fisetin induces apoptosis (caspase 3/7 activity); in MEFs, suppresses senescence markers without cell killing -- suggesting cell-type-dependent mechanisms
- PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway inhibition is a likely mechanism (fisetin blocks this pathway; disrupting PI3K/AKT by RNAi kills senescent cells)
- Also targets topoisomerase and activates SIRT1

## Safety/Toxicity Data

- No adverse effects reported even at high doses (cited: Maher 2015)
- Average dietary fisetin intake in Japan: ~0.4 mg/day with no adverse effects
- Natural product found in strawberries (highest concentration), apples, persimmon, grapes, onions, cucumbers
- Available as oral dietary supplement
- Authors note clinical trials were beginning based on these pre-clinical data

## Limitations and Caveats

1. **Small sample sizes in lifespan study**: n=8-9 per group, which limits statistical power for lifespan analysis
2. **Single genetic background for lifespan study**: f1 C57BL/6:FVB; not replicated in C57BL/6 inbred strain
3. **Human data is ex vivo only**: n=3 subjects, adipose tissue only, 48h treatment -- far from clinical evidence
4. **Mechanism not fully resolved**: Authors acknowledge healthspan extension could be due to mechanisms beyond senescence clearance, e.g., gut microbiome effects, antioxidant activity, anti-inflammatory effects
5. **Not all senescent cells cleared**: Macrophages and dendritic cells unaffected; p21+ cells less affected than p16+ cells
6. **No dose-response in vivo**: Only one dietary concentration (500 ppm) and one acute dose (100 mg/kg) tested
7. **Potential conflicts of interest**: Multiple authors hold patents on senolytic drugs (Mayo Clinic)
8. **Fisetin's very short half-life** (rapid: 0.09 h, terminal: 3.1 h) raises pharmacokinetic questions for human translation

## Claims to Verify

- **Lifespan extension**: Exact median and maximum lifespan values are reported only in figures (Fig. 5A-B), not in the text. The specific percentage or absolute extension cannot be verified from the text alone.
- **n=8-9 per group for lifespan study**: Small for a lifespan study; typical lifespan studies use 20-50 animals per group. This is a significant limitation.
- **~60 mg/kg/day from 500 ppm diet**: This estimate depends on food consumption, which varies by animal. Not directly measured in the text.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Human clinical trials vs animal models)**: Provides human ex vivo adipose tissue data (n=3) but no clinical trial data. Notes clinical trials were beginning.
- **Q2 (Healthspan vs lifespan)**: PRIMARY SOURCE. Demonstrates both lifespan extension and multiple healthspan improvements (histopathology, clinical chemistry, tissue homeostasis) from late-life intervention.
- **Q3 (Pharmacokinetic limitations)**: Reports terminal half-life of 3.1 h; argues hit-and-run mechanism overcomes PK limitations.
- **Q4 (Senolytic vs broader flavonoid effects)**: CENTRAL SOURCE. Demonstrates fisetin as most potent senolytic among 10 flavonoids. Shows cell-type specificity of clearance. But acknowledges other mechanisms may contribute.
- **Q5 (Counter-evidence and limitations)**: Notes cell-type specificity (not universal), small sample sizes, PK limitations, mechanism uncertainty.
- **Q6 (Dosing regimens)**: Reports two regimens: intermittent dietary (500 ppm) and acute oral (100 mg/kg x 5 days).
- **Q7 (Comparative effectiveness)**: Comparative data on 10 flavonoids; fisetin outperformed quercetin, luteolin, curcumin, and others at 5 uM.
