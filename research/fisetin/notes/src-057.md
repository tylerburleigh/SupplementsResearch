# src-057: Sexual Dimorphic Metabolic and Cognitive Responses of C57BL/6 Mice to Fisetin or D+Q

**Fang et al. (2023), GeroScience, 46 citations**

## Core Findings

Monthly oral senolytic treatment from 4-13 months of age in C57BL/6 mice produced strikingly sex-dependent results: Fisetin benefited males only (reduced SASP, improved glucose/energy metabolism, enhanced spatial learning), while D+Q harmed females (increased SASP, WAT accumulation, reduced energy metabolism, impaired object recognition). Fisetin had no effect in females and D+Q had minimal effect in males. This is a key cautionary study demonstrating that senolytic treatment in young adulthood, before significant senescent cell accumulation, can be detrimental depending on sex and compound.

## Study Design

- **Animals:** Male and female C57BL/6 mice from established breeding colony at Southern Illinois University
- **Age range:** 4-13 months (treatment initiated in young adulthood, before reported senescent cell accumulation at ~14 months)
- **Treatment groups:**
  - Fisetin: 100 mg/kg oral gavage, once monthly (n=8-12 per sex per group)
  - D+Q: 5 mg/kg Dasatinib + 50 mg/kg Quercetin oral gavage, once monthly
  - Vehicle: 2% DMSO in canola oil, once monthly
- **Duration:** 9 monthly treatments total
- **Key timepoints:** GTT/ITT after 6 treatments (age 9 mo); grip strength after 7 treatments; MWM/NOR after 8 treatments; indirect calorimetry at age 12 mo; sacrifice at 13 months after 10th treatment
- **Additional controls:** Treatment-naive young (4 mo) and old (21 mo) mice for aging comparison
- **Statistics:** Two-way ANOVA (Sex x Treatment), unpaired two-tailed Student's t-tests for pairwise comparisons

## Metabolic Outcomes

### Glucose Tolerance (GTT)
- Fisetin-treated males showed improved glucose clearance (reduced AUC, p<0.05) compared to vehicle
- D+Q-treated females had accumulated more WAT but did NOT show impaired glucose metabolism
- No differences in insulin sensitivity (ITT) between treated and control mice of either sex

### Plasma Markers
- Plasma adiponectin significantly increased in both Fisetin- and D+Q-treated males (p<0.05)
- No change in plasma adiponectin in females with either treatment
- Plasma insulin levels similar across all groups
- Males had lower baseline adiponectin and higher baseline insulin than females (consistent with known sex differences)

### Energy Metabolism (Indirect Calorimetry)
- Fisetin-treated males: increased VO2 and energy expenditure (EE), reduced respiratory quotient (RQ shift from ~0.8 to ~0.7, indicating shift to predominantly lipid oxidation)
- D+Q-treated females: decreased VO2 and EE, no change in RQ
- No changes in either parameter for Fisetin-treated females or D+Q-treated males

### Body Composition
- D+Q-treated females: significantly increased body weight due to elevated subcutaneous and perigonadal WAT depots
- No body weight changes in males with either treatment, or in females with Fisetin
- Mechanistically: D+Q females had increased ACC (lipid synthesis enzyme) in WAT, decreased UCP1 and PPARgamma in brown adipose tissue (reduced thermogenesis)

## SASP/Senescence Markers

### Gonadal WAT (primary tissue examined)
- Males had higher baseline TNF-alpha and IL-6 mRNA than females at 13 months
- **Fisetin in males:** Reduced p16Ink4a, p21Cip1, TNF-alpha, IL-6 mRNA levels
- **D+Q in males:** Reduced p16Ink4a only; minimal effects overall
- **Fisetin in females:** No effects on any SASP marker
- **D+Q in females:** INCREASED p21Cip1, MCP1, IL-6, IL-10 (worsened SASP profile, approaching or exceeding levels in 21-month-old untreated mice)

### Hippocampus
- Generally mirrored WAT patterns
- Fisetin reduced p16Ink4a and p21Cip1 in male hippocampus
- D+Q increased TNF-alpha and IL-10 in hippocampus of both sexes

### Plasma Cytokines
- Fisetin decreased plasma MCP1 in males; increased IL-10 in females
- D+Q increased plasma MCP1, IL-6, and IL-10 in females

## Cognitive Outcomes

### Morris Water Maze (Spatial Learning and Memory)
- **Fisetin in males:** Significantly improved spatial learning across 5 training sessions (increased path efficiency, reduced corrected integrated path length; p<0.01)
- Neither Fisetin nor D+Q affected MWM learning in females
- Probe challenge (memory recall): No treatment effects in either sex (platform entries unchanged)
- Swimming speed was similar across all groups (ruling out motor confounds)

### Novel Object Recognition (Object Memory)
- D+Q-treated females had significantly decreased retention index (impaired object recognition memory)
- Fisetin-treated males showed no NOR improvement (only MWM improvement)
- D+Q-treated females also showed reduced total distance in open field (potential anxiety confound)
- D+Q-treated males also showed reduced total distance in open field

### Hippocampal Molecular Markers
- Fisetin increased hippocampal Glut4 (glucose transporter 4) mRNA in males
- Fisetin increased hippocampal AdipoR1 (adiponectin receptor 1) mRNA in males
- Both Fisetin and D+Q increased liver Glut4 in males only
- D+Q decreased liver AdipoR1 and increased WAT AdipoR1 in females (contributing to fat accumulation)
- No changes in synaptic plasticity markers (PSD95, synaptophysin, etc.) with either treatment

## Fisetin vs D+Q Comparative Results

| Parameter | Fisetin-Males | Fisetin-Females | D+Q-Males | D+Q-Females |
|-----------|--------------|-----------------|-----------|-------------|
| SASP markers | Reduced | No effect | Minimal change | Increased |
| Body weight | No change | No change | No change | Increased |
| WAT accumulation | No change | No change | No change | Increased |
| Glucose clearance | Improved | No effect | No change | No change |
| Adiponectin | Increased | No effect | Increased | No change |
| Energy metabolism | Enhanced | No effect | No change | Reduced |
| Spatial learning (MWM) | Improved | No effect | No change | No change |
| Object recognition (NOR) | No change | No change | No change | Impaired |

### Proposed Mechanism for Differential Efficacy
- Fisetin has higher prooxidant activity than quercetin, independent of trace elements
- Quercetin's senolytic potency depends on copper/iron accumulation (which occurs with aging), so its potency was likely diminished in young mice without sufficient trace element buildup
- At young ages before significant senescent cell accumulation, Fisetin's mechanism may work primarily through anti-inflammatory (adiponectin-mediated) pathways rather than senescent cell clearance per se

## Sex-Specific Differences and Proposed Explanations

1. **Males biologically age faster metabolically:** 4-month-old males already had worse glucose tolerance, VO2, and EE compared to females. SASP became more prominent in males by 13 months (higher TNF-alpha, IL-6).
2. **Fisetin ineffective in females possibly because SASP not yet at harmful levels:** Female mice had lower baseline inflammation, giving Fisetin less room for improvement.
3. **D+Q detrimental in females possibly via estrogen disruption:** Quercetin may inhibit thyroid peroxidase, leading to reduced thyroid-stimulating hormone and lower estrogen levels, promoting metabolic dysfunction and fat accumulation.
4. **Glucocorticoid synergy in males:** Glucocorticoids (more effective anti-inflammatory in males) may synergize with senolytic compounds, amplifying anti-inflammatory outcomes in males.

## Limitations

- **No frailty or lifespan measurements** -- cannot conclude whether early Fisetin treatment extends lifespan
- **Single strain and age range** -- only C57BL/6 mice, treatment from 4-13 months; results may differ in other strains or with later-life initiation
- **n = 8-12 per group** -- moderate sample size; some analyses (especially indirect calorimetry at n=16-20) were better powered
- **Intermittent dosing only** -- monthly oral dosing; does not address continuous or different dosing schedules
- **No direct senescent cell quantification** -- relied on SASP gene expression markers rather than direct senescent cell counting
- **Anxiety confound in NOR** -- D+Q-treated females showed reduced locomotion in open field, potentially confounding NOR results
- **Mechanisms are correlative** -- the adiponectin/Glut4/AdipoR1 pathway is proposed but not causally demonstrated (no knockout or blockade experiments)

## Claims to Verify

- Sample sizes vary across assays (n=8-12 for RT-PCR/ELISA, n=16-20 for calorimetry/MWM); the paper states n=8-20 generally but specific group sizes per assay per sex are not explicitly tabulated
- The assertion that "cell senescence usually becomes prominent by approximately 14 months of age" (line 898) is attributed to Kirkland & Tchkonia 2020 (ref 6) but this reference is a review article, not a primary data source in C57BL/6 mice

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q2 (healthspan):** Strong evidence that Fisetin improves multiple healthspan parameters (metabolic, inflammatory, cognitive) in male mice when administered preventively from young adulthood. The adiponectin/Glut4 mechanism is a novel proposed pathway linking peripheral metabolic improvements to central cognitive benefits.
- **Q5 (adversarial):** Critical cautionary evidence that (a) senolytic treatment before significant senescent cell accumulation can be harmful (D+Q in females), (b) treatment can be completely ineffective in one sex (Fisetin in females), and (c) D+Q specifically worsened multiple health outcomes in females. Challenges the assumption that senolytics are universally beneficial.
- **Q7 (comparative):** Direct head-to-head comparison of Fisetin vs D+Q in same animals under identical dosing conditions. Fisetin clearly outperformed D+Q in males; D+Q was actively harmful in females. Provides mechanistic explanation (trace element dependence of quercetin vs Fisetin's independent prooxidant activity).
