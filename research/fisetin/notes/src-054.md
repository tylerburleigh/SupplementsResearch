# src-054: New Mito-Fisetin Derivative Compromises Mitophagy and Induces Apoptosis in Senescent Breast Cancer Cells

**Source:** PubMed PMID 39322603 (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39322603)
**Type:** Peer-reviewed research article (ACS journal, likely ACS Pharmacology & Translational Science based on formatting)
**Quality:** ok -- contains substantive abstract plus detailed figure captions with methods and results
**Content scope:** Abstract + figure legends with extensive mechanistic data. Full text not available (PubMed landing page only).

## Core Findings

1. A mitochondria-targeted fisetin derivative (mito-fisetin, mF3) was synthesized by conjugating a triphenyl phosphonium cationic group to fisetin at the 3-O position, creating a lipophilic cation that accumulates in mitochondria driven by membrane potential.

2. Mito-fisetin at low micromolar concentrations (1-5 uM) selectively kills senescent ER-positive breast cancer cells by disrupting mitochondrial membrane potential, impairing mitophagy, activating AMPK, suppressing AKT/HSP90, and triggering intrinsic apoptosis.

3. In vivo validation using zebrafish xenografts confirmed antitumor activity against ER-positive breast cancer models (MCF-7 and T-47D).

## Mito-Fisetin Synthesis and Design

- **Strategy:** Fisetin was transformed into lipophilic derivatives by attaching n-butyltriphenylphosphonium cation at position 3-O (mF3) or 7-O (mF7), with other hydroxy groups blocked (e.g., FCPh2 as intermediate).
- **Rationale:** Triphenyl phosphonium (TPP+) cations are well-established mitochondria-targeting moieties that exploit the negative mitochondrial membrane potential to accumulate several hundred-fold inside mitochondria.
- **Multiple derivatives tested:** mF3 (3-O linked), mF7 (7-O linked), and FCPh2 (intermediate). mF3 showed the most potent anticancer activity.
- Two independent synthesis batches of mF3 were used for reproducibility confirmation.

## Senolytic Activity Against Senescent Cancer Cells

- **Senescence model:** Tamoxifen-induced senescence in ER-positive breast cancer cell lines (HCC1500, CAMA-1, HCC1428, ZR-75-30).
- **Selectivity:** Senescent breast cancer cells were MORE sensitive to mito-fisetin than proliferating ones -- a key senolytic property.
- **Concentrations:** Effective at 1 and 5 uM (24 h treatment), which is in the low micromolar range comparable to or better than native fisetin.
- **Apoptosis markers:** Phosphatidylserine externalization (Annexin V), elevated caspase 9 and caspase 3.
- **Senescence markers affected:** SA-beta-galactosidase activity and IL-8 levels were modulated by mF3 treatment.

## Mechanism of Action

### Mitochondrial membrane potential and function
- mF3 promoted severe depolarization of mitochondrial membrane potential (Delta-psi-m) in senescent cells.
- Real-time OCR measurements showed changes in: basal respiration, ATP production, proton leak, maximal respiration, and spare respiratory capacity.
- Results: depletion of ATP pool and induction of energy stress.

### AMPK/AKT/HSP90 axis
- **AMPK:** Increased levels of phosphorylated AMPK (energy stress response).
- **AKT:** Decreased AKT levels and decreased phospho-AKT/AKT ratio (pro-survival signal suppression).
- **HSP90:** Decreased HSP90 levels (chaperone dysfunction, affects multiple survival pathways).
- **Bcl-2/Bcl-rambo:** Decreased levels (anti-apoptotic signal suppression).
- Cell-free interaction analysis confirmed direct binding of mF3 to AKT and Bcl-2 proteins.

### Mitophagy impairment
- mF3 affected markers of mitophagosome formation: PINK1, Parkin, ubiquitin, LC3B, ULK1, VPS34, beclin 1, BNIP3, and Rab9.
- Impaired mitophagic response prevents clearance of damaged mitochondria, amplifying cytotoxicity.

### Oxidative stress
- Increased mitochondrial superoxide production.
- Elevated levels of SOD1 (cytosolic) and SOD2 (mitochondrial) superoxide dismutases.

## Selectivity for Senescent vs Proliferating Cells

- Senescent breast cancer cells showed greater sensitivity to mF3 than proliferating cells across multiple assays (MMP depolarization, apoptosis induction).
- Normal cells tested: BJ fibroblasts and noncancerous mammary epithelial MCF10F cells.
- MCF10F cells (normal) showed less sensitivity than breast cancer cells, suggesting some cancer selectivity.

## In Vivo Validation

- **Model:** Zebrafish (Danio rerio) xenograft using ER-positive MCF-7 and T-47D cells.
- **Results:** mF3 inhibited tumor growth; upregulated BAX (pro-apoptotic) and CDKN1A (cell cycle arrest) mRNA levels.
- **Dose:** 5 uM treatment of cells prior to xenograft injection.
- Limitation: Zebrafish model is a screening-level model, not a mammalian tumor model.

## Implications for Fisetin Formulation/Derivatives

1. **Mitochondrial targeting is a viable strategy** to enhance fisetin's anticancer and senolytic activity.
2. **TPP conjugation** is a well-established chemistry that could be applied to other flavonols.
3. **Selectivity for senescent cells** makes this a potential senolytic strategy for drug-resistant senescent cancer cells (therapy-induced senescence).
4. mF3 outperformed native fisetin in cytotoxicity assays, demonstrating that structural modification can overcome fisetin's known limitations (poor bioavailability, low potency).
5. The study supports the broader principle that organelle-targeted derivatives can enhance flavonoid therapeutic potential.

## Limitations

- Full text not available -- analysis based on abstract and figure legends only.

- No pharmacokinetic or bioavailability data reported in available content.
