# src-036: Impact of Senolytic Treatment on Immunity, Aging, and Disease

**Citation:** Lorenzo EC, Torrance BL, Haynes L (2023). Frontiers in Aging 4:1161799. doi:10.3389/fragi.2023.1161799
**Type:** Review article | **Venue:** Frontiers in Aging | **Citations:** 24 | **Quality:** ok

---

## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive review examining the immunological consequences of senolytic drugs (fisetin, dasatinib+quercetin) in the context of aging, disease, and infection. The central thesis is that while senolytics may acutely benefit primary immune responses by clearing senescent cells, they may negatively impact immunological memory -- a critical concern for elderly and immunocompromised populations. The authors identify a major gap: almost no clinical trials evaluate immune cell function as an outcome measure in adults 65+, and no trials examine senolytics in infectious disease contexts in this age group.

## Key Evidence

### Fisetin and Innate Immunity
- **Stroke model:** Fisetin (50 mg/kg) reduced infarct size and prevented macrophage/dendritic cell/lymphocyte infiltration in ischemic brains; reduced TNF-alpha from macrophages and microglia (Gelderblom et al., 2012).
- **Macrophage viability:** Higher doses (10-20 uM) reduced macrophage viability in vitro; lower doses (0-8 uM) reduced inflammatory mediators (iNOS, COX-2, NO, PGE2, IL-6, TNF-alpha) without affecting viability (Molagoda et al., 2021).
- **Allergic asthma model:** 40-50 mg/kg fisetin over 47 days reduced lung tissue damage, macrophage/neutrophil/eosinophil infiltration, and co-stimulatory molecules CD80/CD86 on dendritic cells (Huang et al., 2018).

### Fisetin and Adaptive Immunity
- **CD4 T cells:** Fisetin suppresses proliferation in a dose-dependent manner (2-4 ug/mL significant reduction), shifts differentiation from Th1 toward Th2 cytokine profile (Song et al., 2013). High doses (8-16 ug/mL) significantly decrease splenocyte viability.
- **B cells:** Fisetin induces apoptosis in human B lymphoblastoid cells dose-dependently; 30 uM fisetin results in ~15% viability after 24h. Impairs PI3-kinase activity and mTOR signaling (Lim et al., 2015).
- **Senescence clearance in T cells:** Short-term treatment (100 mg/kg, 5 days oral gavage) in aged p16-ATTAC mice significantly decreased p16-expressing CD4+, CD8+ T cells, NK cells in adipose tissue, with effects persisting 3 days post-treatment despite fisetin's rapid half-life (0.09 h) (Yousefzadeh et al., 2018).
- **Lifespan extension:** Wild-type mice fed fisetin-supplemented chow from 85 weeks through end of life showed significant lifespan extension (Yousefzadeh et al., 2018).

### Fisetin in Infectious Disease
- **COVID-19:** Aged mice treated with fisetin showed decreased inflammation, SASP, p16/p21 expression, and significantly improved survival and antigen-specific antibody titers (Camell et al., 2021).
- **Viral-induced senescence (VIS):** Fisetin significantly reduced viability of SARS-CoV-2-infected cells in vitro (Lee et al., 2021).

### Dasatinib + Quercetin (D+Q) Comparative Findings
- **Dasatinib** impairs neutrophil functions (integrin signaling, migration, granule exocytosis) but not TNF-alpha signaling, phagocytosis, or bacterial killing (Futosi et al., 2012).
- **Dasatinib** inhibits TCR signaling in T cells, but this can be bypassed by IL-2 (Schade et al., 2008). Causes significant B cell apoptosis unlike T cells (Oksvold et al., 2015).
- **D+Q in flu infection:** Treatment of aged mice before flu infection reduced Tregs, increased Th2 cells, and lowered TGF-beta in lungs (Lorenzo et al., 2022). However, TGF-beta is required for proper CD4/CD8 memory formation, suggesting senolytics could impair memory responses.
- **Quercetin** inhibits T cell activation and proliferation (50-100 uM), and unlike dasatinib, this cannot be compensated by IL-2 (Lugli et al., 2009).

## Risks and Safety Considerations

1. **Memory impairment risk:** D+Q-associated reduction in TGF-beta could impair CD4 and CD8 memory formation, potentially dampening immunological recall and vaccination responses.
2. **Dose-dependent immunosuppression:** Higher doses of fisetin show significant immunosuppressive effects on both innate and adaptive immune cells.
3. **Tissue healing concerns:** Senescent fibroblasts are crucial for wound healing and tissue regeneration; premature elimination could impair recovery from infection or injury.
4. **Timing of intervention:** Treating with senolytics too early during active infection (e.g., flu, COVID) could impair lung tissue repair. Authors suggest intervening at peak infection or post-pathogen clearance.
5. **Heterogeneity of senescent cell populations:** Different SC types have different functions; blanket elimination may remove beneficial populations.
6. **Lack of long-term data:** Very little is known about long-term immunological consequences of repeated senolytic treatment.

## Methodology

This is a narrative review. The authors systematically surveyed pre-clinical (in vitro, ex vivo, in vivo murine models) and clinical trial data on fisetin, dasatinib, and quercetin effects on immune cell function. They searched ClinicalTrials.gov for active trials with immune cell outcome measures. The review spans cancer biology, immunosenescence, and infectious disease models.

## Limitations

- Narrative review rather than systematic review or meta-analysis
- No quantitative synthesis of effect sizes across studies
- Pre-clinical data largely from young animal models, not aged ones
- Doses and administration routes vary widely across studies, making direct comparisons difficult
- Many studies discussed did not use these drugs specifically for their senolytic properties
- No direct clinical data on senolytics and infectious disease outcomes in elderly

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q2 (healthspan):** Provides evidence that fisetin reduces senescence markers in multiple tissues and immune cells, extends lifespan in mice, and improves outcomes in infection models. Highlights immune-specific benefits and risks.
- **Q5 (adversarial):** Central paper for risk assessment. Documents dose-dependent immunosuppression, potential memory impairment, tissue healing concerns, and timing-of-treatment risks. Identifies that B cells are particularly vulnerable to fisetin-induced apoptosis.
- **Q7 (comparative):** Compares fisetin, dasatinib, and quercetin effects on innate and adaptive immunity. Fisetin has better safety profile than navitoclax but shows dose-dependent cytotoxicity to immune cells. Dasatinib impairs TCR signaling but this is reversible; quercetin's T cell suppression cannot be rescued by IL-2.

## Claims to Verify

- "30uM fisetin resulted in ~15% viability" (line 465): This is from an in vitro Raji cell study (Lim et al., 2015). The concentration is quite high for in vitro work and may not translate to achievable in vivo concentrations.
- "Fisetin (50 mg/kg of body weight) protected brain tissue" (line 284): Dose seems high but is standard for mouse models; verify human equivalent dose considerations.
- "Wild-type mice fed fisetin supplemented chow from 85 weeks through end of life also had significant lifespan extension" (line 522-524): Claims lifespan extension from Yousefzadeh et al., 2018 -- verify magnitude of extension in original paper.
