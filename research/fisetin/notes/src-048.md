# src-048: Differential Senolytic Inhibition of Normal vs A-beta-Associated Cholinesterases

**Citation:** Darvesh S, Cash MK, Forrestall K, Maillet H, Sands D. (2025). "Differential senolytic inhibition of normal versus Abeta-associated cholinesterases: implications in aging and Alzheimer's disease." *Aging*, 17. DOI: 10.18632/aging.206227

**Quality:** ok (full text available, peer-reviewed, 1 citation)

## Core Findings

This study evaluated six senolytic/nootropic compounds (dasatinib, nintedanib, fisetin, quercetin, GW2580, meclofenoxate hydrochloride) as cholinesterase (ChE) inhibitors, focusing on whether they could differentially inhibit ChEs associated with amyloid-beta plaques versus those associated with normal neural elements in human Alzheimer's disease brain tissue. The key finding is that dasatinib and nintedanib selectively inhibited plaque-associated ChEs without affecting normal neural ChEs -- a selectivity pattern not shared by the clinical AD drug galantamine. Fisetin and quercetin showed ChE inhibition in enzyme kinetics but could not be assessed histochemically due to metal chelation interference.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Enzyme Kinetics (Ellman's assay)
- **Fisetin (3):** AChE Ki = 109.81 uM (competitive); BChE Ki = 94.35 uM (uncompetitive). Fisetin was unique among tested compounds in showing competitive inhibition of AChE and uncompetitive inhibition of BChE; all other compounds showed mixed non-competitive inhibition.
- **Dasatinib (1):** AChE Ki = 28.22 uM; BChE Ki = 3.36 uM (both mixed non-competitive). Most potent BChE inhibitor of the group.
- **Nintedanib (2):** AChE Ki = 7.10 uM (mixed non-competitive); no inhibition of BChE. Most potent AChE inhibitor of the group.
- **Quercetin (4):** AChE Ki = 62.19 uM; BChE Ki = 51.49 uM (both mixed non-competitive).
- **GW2580 (5):** AChE Ki = 70.97 uM; no inhibition of BChE.
- **Meclofenoxate (6):** AChE Ki = 63.20 uM; BChE Ki = 76.30 uM.
- For reference, clinical ChEI galantamine: AChE Ki = 0.52 uM, BChE Ki = 2.09 uM (cited from prior work).

### Histochemical Studies (Karnovsky-Roots staining in human AD brain tissue)
- Tissues: Orbitofrontal cortex (plaques) and thalamus (normal neural elements) from 4 AD cases (age 84-98, Braak IV-VI).
- **Dasatinib:** Complete inhibition of BChE-associated plaques in all cases; moderate to strong inhibition of AChE-associated plaques; no inhibition of either ChE at normal neural elements.
- **Nintedanib:** Complete inhibition of both AChE and BChE associated with plaques; no inhibition of either ChE at normal neural elements.
- **GW2580:** No inhibition of plaque-associated AChE; slight to moderate inhibition of plaque-associated BChE; no inhibition of normal neural ChEs.
- **Meclofenoxate:** Moderate to strong inhibition of plaque-associated AChE; slight inhibition of plaque-associated BChE; no inhibition of normal neural ChEs.
- **Fisetin and quercetin:** Could NOT be evaluated histochemically. Both flavonoids chelated metal ions (iron/copper) required for the Karnovsky-Roots staining reaction, causing precipitation and precluding assessment.

### Molecular Docking
- Fisetin showed predominant affinity for the AChE active site gorge in blind docking experiments.
- Both fisetin and quercetin found the active sites of AChE and BChE.
- However, predicted Ki values at alternate allosteric sites (site II for AChE, site III for BChE) were closer to experimental Ki values than active site predictions, suggesting potential allosteric binding.
- Four main putative allosteric binding sites were identified on ChEs.

## Methodology

- **Enzyme kinetics:** Ellman's assay with Lineweaver-Burk plots, triplicate experiments, using recombinant human AChE and purified human serum BChE.
- **Histochemistry:** Modified Karnovsky-Roots method on post-mortem human AD brain tissue (orbitofrontal cortex and thalamus). pH 6.8 for plaque-associated ChEs, pH 8.0 for normal neural ChEs. Qualitative assessment (no inhibition, slight, moderate, strong).
- **Molecular docking:** MOE software with blind and site-directed docking on human AChE (PDB: 4M0E) and BChE (PDB: 4TPK) crystal structures.
- **Sample:** 4 AD brain tissue cases, sex- and age-matched, Braak stages IV-VI.

## Limitations

1. **Fisetin histochemistry gap:** The inability to assess fisetin's selectivity for plaque-associated vs normal ChEs due to metal chelation is a significant limitation for fisetin-specific conclusions. It remains unknown whether fisetin shares the selective plaque-only inhibition seen with dasatinib and nintedanib.
2. **Small sample size:** Only 4 AD brain tissue cases, precluding statistical analysis and examination of sex/age/plaque load effects.
3. **Qualitative histochemical assessment:** Staining was rated qualitatively (no/slight/moderate/strong inhibition) rather than quantitatively.
4. **Post-mortem tissue only:** Cannot assess anti-inflammatory or cognitive effects; in vivo studies needed.
5. **Metal chelation by fisetin:** This property itself may have biological implications (fisetin chelating iron/copper in vivo could affect metal homeostasis).

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q2 (Healthspan - cognitive):** The study connects senolytics to cognitive function via the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway (CAIP). Senolytics that inhibit ChEs may increase acetylcholine bioavailability, suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IL-6) via CAIP, potentially improving cognitive outcomes. The selective inhibition of plaque-associated ChEs (without affecting normal ChEs) suggests a potentially safer cognitive-enhancing mechanism than current ChE inhibitors.

**Q4 (Mechanism):** Provides a novel mechanism for senolytic benefits in AD -- cholinesterase inhibition, particularly of plaque-bound ChEs. Fisetin's unique competitive AChE/uncompetitive BChE inhibition and potential allosteric binding sites on ChEs are mechanistically distinct from other senolytics. Molecular docking identified 4 putative allosteric sites as potential targets for next-generation ChEIs.

**Q5 (Adversarial):** Fisetin's metal chelation property is a double-edged finding: it prevented experimental evaluation in this study, and it may indicate potential for interference with metal-dependent biological processes in vivo. The study also notes the CAIP pathway as a mechanism that could modulate inflammation but acknowledges other pathways may also be involved. The small sample size and lack of in vivo data limit conclusions about safety and efficacy.

## Claims to Verify

- Fisetin Ki values (109.81 uM AChE, 94.35 uM BChE) are consistent with cited literature values from Katalinic et al. (100.2 uM AChE, 94.2 uM BChE), which supports accuracy.
- The claim that fisetin/quercetin chelation "most likely" explains the histochemical failure is plausible but unproven -- alternative explanations could exist.
- The statement about no effect on AChE activity in wild-type mice treated with dasatinib+fisetin cocktail (cited as reference 106) is a secondhand claim that should be verified from the original source.
