# src-340: Senolytics decrease senescent cells in humans (Hickson et al., 2019)

**Citation:** Hickson LJ, Prata LGPL, Bobart SA, et al. "Senolytics decrease senescent cells in humans: Preliminary report from a clinical trial of Dasatinib plus Quercetin in individuals with diabetic kidney disease." eBioMedicine. 2019;47:446-456. PMID: 31542391. PMCID: PMC6796530.

**Venue:** eBioMedicine (Elsevier, open access, peer-reviewed)

**Trial registration:** NCT02848131

## Core Findings

This is the first peer-reviewed clinical trial to directly demonstrate that senolytic drugs decrease senescent cell abundance in humans. A single 3-day oral course of Dasatinib (100 mg/day) plus Quercetin (1000 mg/day total) in 9 subjects with diabetic kidney disease significantly reduced senescent cell markers in adipose tissue, skin, and circulating SASP factors by 11 days after the last dose, with no serious adverse events.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Open-label Phase 1 pilot study
- **Participants:** N=9 (plus 2 additional for adipocyte progenitor assays only); mean age 69 years (range 55-79); 7 males, 2 females; all with drug-controlled diabetes and CKD (eGFR 27 +/- 0.7 mL/min/1.73m2)
- **Inclusion:** Adults 50-80 years with diabetes mellitus on therapy and CKD (eGFR 15-45 mL/min/1.73m2)
- **Exclusion:** QTc prolongation >450 msec, liver dysfunction, non-diabetic kidney disease, dialysis, malignancy, active infection, morbid obesity (BMI >50), solid organ transplantation, interacting drugs (CYP3A4 substrates, etc.)
- **Intervention:** Single 3-day oral course: Dasatinib 100 mg daily + Quercetin 500 mg twice daily (1000 mg/day total)
- **Tissue sampling:** Abdominal subcutaneous adipose tissue and skin biopsies + blood collected at Day 0 (baseline) and Day 14 (11 days after last dose); post-treatment biopsy contralateral to baseline, same dermatome
- **Power calculation:** N=9 provided 80% power to detect 5% difference in SA-beta-gal activity (SD of differences = 4.0, two-sided alpha = 0.05)
- **Statistics:** Welch's unpaired t-test for unequal variances

## Key Results: Senescent Cell Reduction

### Adipose tissue (N=9)
| Marker | Reduction | p-value | Baseline (mean +/- SEM) |
|--------|-----------|---------|------------------------|
| p16INK4A+ cells | 35% | 0.001 | 3.18 +/- 0.64 per 100 adipocytes |
| p21CIP1+ cells | 17% | 0.009 | 3.82 +/- 0.65 per 100 adipocytes |
| SA-beta-gal+ cells | 62% | 0.005 | 8.76 +/- 2.51 per 100 nuclei |
| CD68+ macrophages | 28% | 0.0001 | 8.4 +/- 0.58 per 100 adipocytes |
| Crown-like structures | 86% | 0.001 | 0.27 +/- 0.05 per 100 adipocytes |

### Skin epidermis (N=9)
| Marker | Reduction | p-value |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| p16INK4A+ cells | 20% | 0.026 |
| p21CIP1+ cells | 31% | 0.016 |
| CD1a+ Langerhans cells | No significant change | 0.803 |

### Circulating SASP factors (significantly decreased)
- IL-1alpha, IL-2, IL-6, IL-9, MMP-2, MMP-9, MMP-12 (all p < 0.05)
- Trends (p=0.06): IL1-RA, FGF-2, GM-CSF
- Correlations: Adipose p16INK4A+ cells correlated with plasma IL-6 (R2=0.34), IL-1alpha (R2=0.56), MMP12 (R2=0.51), among others

### Adipocyte progenitor replicative potential (N=11)
- Post-treatment adipocyte progenitors showed 8% greater increase in cell density over time vs. baseline (cultured for 3 passages to exclude macrophages)

## Safety Data

- No serious adverse events (no hospitalization, no kidney injury requiring dialysis, no death)
- No subjects required drug discontinuation
- Authors note D+Q was well-tolerated in this short 3-day regimen

## Mechanistic Insights

- D targets senescent human adipocyte progenitors; Q targets senescent human endothelial cells. The combination broadens the range of senescent cell types eliminated.
- D+Q acts by disabling senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways (SCAPs), enabling senescent cells to undergo apoptosis from their own SASP.
- D half-life ~4 hours; Q half-life ~11 hours. Despite short half-lives, effects persist because senescent cells take weeks to months to re-develop.
- Intermittent "hit-and-run" dosing is sufficient; effects persist well beyond drug clearance.

## Relevance to Fisetin

1. **Direct mention of fisetin clinical trial:** The paper explicitly references NCT03325322, a trial of fisetin for frailty in elderly women, as part of the broader senolytic clinical trial landscape. This is from the same Mayo Clinic group (Kirkland, Tchkonia).

2. **Comparative senolytic framework:** The paper establishes the senolytic mechanism shared by flavonoid polyphenols -- quercetin (used here) and fisetin both target BCL-2 family members and PI3-kinase anti-apoptotic pathways. Reference 64 (Zhu et al., 2017) identifies fisetin as a senolytic agent, and Reference 65 (Yousefzadeh et al., 2018) reports fisetin extends health and lifespan in mice.

3. **Proof-of-concept for flavonoid senolytics in humans:** Since quercetin is a flavonoid like fisetin, the demonstration that quercetin-containing senolytic regimens reduce senescent cell burden in humans provides indirect support for the biological plausibility of fisetin as a senolytic in clinical settings.

4. **Dosing paradigm:** The "hit-and-run" intermittent dosing concept validated here (3-day course with effects measurable at 11 days) is the same paradigm being tested in fisetin clinical trials.

## Limitations

- Very small sample size (N=9 for primary analyses; N=11 for progenitor assays)
- Open-label design with no placebo control
- No randomization
- Preliminary report from an ongoing trial (no long-term outcomes data)
- Single dose regimen tested; dose-response not explored
- Only adipose tissue and skin assessed (not kidney, the disease-relevant organ)
- Short follow-up (11 days post-treatment)
- Biomarker endpoints only; no clinical efficacy endpoints reported in this interim analysis
- Predominantly white male subjects (88.9% white, 77.8% male)
- Competing interests declared (patents on senolytic drugs held by Mayo Clinic)

## Claims to Verify

- The power calculation states N=9 provides 80% power to detect a 5% difference in SA-beta-gal with SD of differences = 4.0. The observed SA-beta-gal reduction was 62%, far exceeding the minimum detectable effect, which supports the statistical validity.
- Baseline values reported in Table 1 use SEM rather than SD (age: 68.7 [1.0], BMI: 33.9 [0.8], eGFR: 27.0 [0.7]). These appear consistent with the abstract values (68.7 +/- 3.1, BMI 33.9 +/- 2.3, eGFR 27.0 +/- 2.1) if the abstract reports SD and the table reports SEM, but the discrepancy between abstract and table formatting should be noted.
