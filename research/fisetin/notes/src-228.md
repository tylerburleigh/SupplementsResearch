# src-228: ITP Lifespan Study -- Fisetin Null Result

**Citation:** Harrison DE, Strong R, et al. "Astaxanthin and meclizine extend lifespan in UM-HET3 male mice; fisetin, SG1002 (hydrogen sulfide donor), dimethyl fumarate, mycophenolic acid, and 4-phenylbutyrate do not significantly affect lifespan in either sex at the doses and schedules used." *GeroScience* 46(1):795-816, 2023. DOI: 10.1007/s11357-023-01011-0. PMCID: PMC10828146.

## Core Findings

This is the definitive NIA Interventions Testing Program (ITP) study demonstrating that **fisetin does not extend lifespan in UM-HET3 mice** at 600 ppm in the diet, started at 20 months of age, under either continuous or cyclic (3 days on / 11 days off) feeding schedules. In the same study, astaxanthin extended male median lifespan by 12% (p=0.003) and meclizine by 8% (p=0.03), confirming the ITP protocol can detect lifespan-extending interventions. Fisetin also failed to reduce p16Ink4a senescence markers in liver, kidney, or brain, and was not reliably detectable in plasma, suggesting severe bioavailability issues with chow-based delivery.

## Study Design

**ITP Protocol:**
- Multi-site: Jackson Laboratory (JAX), University of Michigan (UM), University of Texas San Antonio (UTSA)
- Mouse strain: UM-HET3 (genetically heterogeneous, CByB6F1 x C3D2F1 cross)
- Sample sizes: ~140-150 mice per treatment group per sex, ~286-298 controls per sex per cohort
- Statistics: Log-rank test stratified by site, Wang-Allison test for 90th percentile survival
- Two cohorts: C2018 (fisetin, SG1002) and C2019 (astaxanthin, meclizine, DMF, MPA, PBA, SG1002_2)
- Fisetin proposed by Kirkland, Tchkonia, and Robbins (Mayo Clinic / University of Minnesota)

## Fisetin-Specific Results

**Dosing:** 600 ppm target in chow, 99% +/- 9% of target actually achieved (5 preparations). Start age: 20 months. Two arms: continuous feeding (Fis_On) and cyclic 3 days on / 11 days off (Fis_Cyc).

**Male results (pooled across 3 sites):**
| Group | n | Median (days) | % change | Log-rank p |
|-------|---|---------------|----------|------------|
| Control C2018 | 292 | 750 | -- | -- |
| Fis_Cyc | 150 | 799 | +7% | 0.54 |
| Fis_On | 142 | 715 | -5% | 0.85 |

**Female results (pooled):**
| Group | n | Median (days) | % change | Log-rank p |
|-------|---|---------------|----------|------------|
| Control C2018 | 286 | 876 | -- | -- |
| Fis_Cyc | 134 | 871 | -1% | 0.75 |
| Fis_On | 144 | 883 | +1% | 0.37 |

**Key observation:** Fis_Cyc showed a non-significant 7% increase in males, while Fis_On showed a non-significant 5% *decrease*. Neither was remotely significant.

**90th percentile survival:** No significant effects (Wang-Allison p = 0.74 for Fis_Cyc males, 1.00 for Fis_On males).

## Why Fisetin Failed

1. **Bioavailability failure:** Fisetin was not reliably detectable in mouse plasma. Continuous chow-based delivery may not produce adequate systemic exposure, unlike bolus oral gavage used in the prior Yousefzadeh study.

2. **No senescence marker reduction:** Fisetin did not significantly lower p16Ink4a mRNA in liver, kidney, or brain after 2-4 months of feeding. p21Cip1 also showed no age-related increase in old UM-HET3 mice, limiting the ability to detect senolytic effects.

3. **Mouse strain differences:** The positive Yousefzadeh study used (B6 x FVB)F1 hybrids, while the ITP uses UM-HET3 (CByB6F1 x C3D2F1). Senescent cell burden and senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways (SCAPs) may differ between strains.

4. **Route of administration:** Yousefzadeh used oral gavage for initial treatment; ITP used chow-based feeding. Plasma levels may be dramatically different between these routes.

5. **Dose was NOT the problem:** The chow contained 99% of the target 600 ppm, slightly higher than the 500 ppm that worked in Yousefzadeh. The null result is not attributable to underdosing in the food.

6. **Study power:** The ITP study was ~30x larger than the Yousefzadeh study, suggesting the earlier positive result may have been a false positive from small sample size.

## Comparison with Successful Compounds

| Compound | Target dose | Actual dose | Start age | Male median lifespan change | p-value |
|----------|-------------|-------------|-----------|---------------------------|---------|
| Astaxanthin | 4000 ppm | 1840 ppm (46%) | 12 mo | +12% | 0.003 |
| Meclizine | 800 ppm | 544 ppm (68%) | 12 mo | +8% | 0.03 |
| Fisetin | 600 ppm | 594 ppm (99%) | 20 mo | -5% to +7% | 0.54-0.85 |

Notably, astaxanthin worked despite receiving only 46% of target dose, while fisetin at 99% of target still failed. This compounds the bioavailability concern -- even at full dose in food, fisetin may not reach effective plasma concentrations.

## Implications for Human Translation

1. **Senolytic hypothesis under strain:** The failure of fisetin to clear senescent cells (no p16Ink4a reduction) in the most rigorous lifespan model available raises questions about whether dietary fisetin can function as an effective senolytic in vivo.

2. **Route matters critically:** Human clinical trials using oral fisetin supplementation (e.g., the MAYO-FRUIT study) may face the same bioavailability limitations seen here. Pharmacokinetic optimization (bolus dosing, enhanced formulations) may be essential.

3. **Positive results may reflect model-specific effects:** The strain-dependent discrepancy suggests fisetin efficacy may be context-dependent, which complicates extrapolation to genetically diverse human populations.

4. **Dose is not the lever:** Increasing dietary fisetin concentration may not help if the bottleneck is systemic bioavailability rather than intake amount.

5. **Other senolytics may fare differently:** The authors suggest testing other senolytic agents via gavage to address whether the null result reflects fisetin-specific pharmacokinetics or a broader failure of the senolytic approach.

## Methodological Strengths

- Gold-standard multi-site ITP protocol with independent site controls
- Large sample sizes (~150 per treatment per sex)
- Measured actual drug concentrations in diet (revealed the 99% delivery)
- Attempted plasma level measurements (revealed poor bioavailability)
- Tested senescence biomarkers (p16Ink4a, p21Cip1) in tissues
- Both continuous and intermittent dosing schedules tested

## Limitations

- Only one dose level tested (600 ppm)
- Only one start age (20 months)
- Only chow-based delivery, no gavage arm
- Plasma fisetin levels could not be reliably measured
- p16Ink4a whole-tissue mRNA is an imperfect senescence marker
- Did not assess tissue-level fisetin concentrations

## Claims to Verify

- The "30x larger" sample size claim compared to Yousefzadeh: ITP had ~150 mice per fisetin group vs. the Yousefzadeh study which reportedly had 8 controls and some treated mice. The ratio depends on how one counts, but 30x is plausible if Yousefzadeh had ~10 mice total.
- The actual diet concentrations for fisetin (99% +/- 9%) come from only 5 diet preparations, compared to 9 preparations for other drugs. This is a smaller quality-control sample but still adequate.
