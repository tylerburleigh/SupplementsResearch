# src-077: Cellular senescence and senolytics: the path to the clinic

**Citation:** Chaib S, Tchkonia T, Kirkland JL. *Nature Medicine* 28, 1556-1568 (2022). DOI: 10.1038/s41591-022-01923-y. PMCID: PMC9599677.

**Type:** Comprehensive review article from Mayo Clinic senolytics research group.

**Quality Assessment:** Full-text available from PMC. Substantive review with detailed methods discussion, extensive preclinical and clinical trial data, and authoritative positioning on the senolytic field. This is a landmark review from the lab that pioneered fisetin as a senolytic.

---

## Core Findings

1. Over 20 senolytic clinical trials are completed, ongoing, or planned, including at least 10 fisetin-specific trials spanning frailty (AFFIRM), COVID-19 (3 trials), osteoarthritis (4 trials), diabetic kidney disease, and skeletal health -- all in Phase 1/2 or Phase 2, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled designs.

2. Fisetin is positioned as a first-generation senolytic alongside dasatinib and quercetin, selected because it is an orally bioavailable natural flavonoid that targets multiple senescent cell antiapoptotic pathways (SCAPs) and has a known safety profile with rapid elimination (3-4 hour half-life in humans), supporting intermittent "hit-and-run" dosing.

3. Preclinical data show fisetin extends median and maximum lifespan in mice, delays progeria progression, reduces COVID-19-related mortality in mice, and improves frailty/sarcopenia outcomes, providing the rationale for current human trials.

- UBX0101 (nutlin-3a) for knee OA (NCT04349956): Phase 2 failed to achieve primary endpoint. Authors note nutlin-3a shows weak/no senolytic activity and can even induce senescence, attributing failure to the agent rather than the senolytic concept.

## Translation Challenges from Animal to Human

1. **No FDA-recognized gerodiagnostics** to serve as primary trial endpoints. The first gerodiagnostic score (blood composite) sensitive to senolytic administration was published in 2019.

2. **No ICD codes** for frailty, multimorbidity, or sarcopenia, hindering regulatory approvals, reimbursement, and epidemiological studies.

3. **Cell-type specificity**: Different senolytics target different senescent cell types. Fisetin is effective against senescent endothelial cells but not fat cell progenitors, while dasatinib shows the opposite pattern. This heterogeneity complicates translation.

4. **Genetic vs. pharmacologic models diverge**: p16-based genetic clearance models (INK-ATTAC, p16-3MR) also eliminate non-senescent cells like activated macrophages, while small-molecule senolytics do not. Genetic model results may not predict pharmacologic outcomes.

5. **Industry disincentive**: Natural products like fisetin lack IP protection, limiting pharmaceutical company interest and funding.

6. **Inevitable trial failures** may create psychological barriers and tarnish the field. The authors advocate parallel smaller trials across multiple indications with shared outcome variables.

## Fisetin-Specific Data and Positioning

- **Mechanism**: Targets multiple SCAPs (senescent cell antiapoptotic pathways). Unlike single-target agents (e.g., navitoclax/BCL-2 inhibitor), fisetin's multi-target approach may reduce toxicity risk at lower doses.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 3-4 hour half-life in humans, supporting intermittent dosing.
- **Cell-type sensitivity**: Effective against senescent HUVECs but not senescent human fat cell progenitors (preadipocytes/MSCs).
- **Preclinical achievements**: Extended lifespan in mice; reduced mortality in murine coronavirus model; delayed progeria progression; improved frailty/sarcopenia.
- **Rationale for clinical use**: Natural product, orally bioavailable, favorable safety profile, brief half-life enabling "hit-and-run" strategy.

## Safety Considerations

1. **Intermittent dosing rationale**: Senolytics can be administered intermittently (as effective as continuous), reducing side-effect profiles. Dasatinib side effects generally appear only after weeks-months of continuous use.

2. **Advantage of brief half-lives**: Fisetin (3-4h), dasatinib (4h), and quercetin (11h) are rapidly eliminated, minimizing prolonged exposure.

3. **Single-target agent risks**: Navitoclax (BCL-2 inhibitor) causes thrombocytopenia and neutropenia even after brief exposure, raising concern about agents targeting a single SCAP node at high doses.

4. **Potential for eliminating beneficial senescent cells**: The paper notes that eliminating all senescent cells could be detrimental since some have pro-growth, tissue-repair functions. First-generation senolytics preferentially target cells with proapoptotic, tissue-destructive SASP.

5. **CAR T/vaccine risks**: Unlike small molecules, these approaches cannot be readily discontinued if senescent cells become needed (e.g., wound healing, pregnancy).

## Future Clinical Directions

1. Large RCTs needed to validate early-phase results for safety, benefit, and target engagement.
2. Progression from serious diseases (DKD, Alzheimer's, IPF) to less severe conditions, then to prevention in older adults.
3. Gerodiagnostic development (panels of SASP factors, senolysis markers, gerodiagnostic ratios).
4. Combination geroscience strategies (senolytics + NAD+ precursors, exercise, dietary interventions).
5. Ex vivo organ perfusion with senolytics to rescue organs from older donors currently discarded.

## Limitations

- This is a review, not a primary data source. All fisetin clinical trial results are listed as "current" (no published results yet for fisetin trials as of August 2022).
- Mayo Clinic authors hold patents on senolytic drugs; potential competing interest acknowledged and managed.
- Fisetin-specific dosing information for human trials is not provided (likely in individual trial protocols).
- No specific fisetin pharmacokinetic data from the trials themselves; the 3-4h half-life is referenced from prior literature (refs 120-122).

## Claims to Verify

- **Fisetin half-life of 3-4 hours in humans**: Stated without primary data in this review, attributed to refs 120-122. This is an important pharmacokinetic parameter that should be verified from the primary sources.
- **"Over 20 clinical trials"**: Count from Table 1 yields approximately 20 distinct trials; the exact count depends on how multi-arm studies are tallied.
