# src-071: Exploring the effects of Dasatinib, Quercetin, and Fisetin on DNA methylation clocks

**Citation:** Lee, E., Carreras-Gallo, N., Lopez, L., et al. (2024). *Aging*, 16. DOI: 10.18632/aging.205581. Cited 38 times.

**Quality:** ok -- full text available from Aging (open access).

## Core Findings

This longitudinal clinical study (N=28 total, split into two arms) is the first to assess the impact of senolytic drugs on DNA methylation (DNAm) epigenetic aging clocks. DQ treatment (Dasatinib 50mg + Quercetin 500mg, 3 days/month for 6 months) transiently increased first-generation epigenetic clock readings at 3 months, but these largely returned to baseline by 6 months. Adding Fisetin (500mg) to DQ attenuated most epigenetic clock changes compared to DQ alone. Critically, the more biologically meaningful second-generation (GrimAge) and third-generation (DunedinPACE) clocks showed no significant changes in either arm, suggesting the transient first-generation clock increases may reflect CpG composition artifacts rather than true biological aging acceleration.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Prospective non-randomized longitudinal pilot study, registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04946383)
- **Study 1 (DQ):** 19 participants (ages 43-86.6, mean 59.6), 57.9% male; blood draws at baseline, 3 months, 6 months
- **Washout:** 1 year between studies
- **Study 2 (DQF):** 19 participants (10 continued + 9 new; ages 44.5-88.0, mean 60.9), 42.1% male; blood draws at baseline and 6 months
- **Dosing (both studies):** 3 consecutive days per month for 6 months. DQ = 50mg Dasatinib + 500mg Quercetin. DQF added 500mg Fisetin. 8 participants received strawberry-based Fisetin, 11 received non-strawberry Fisetin
- **Platform:** Illumina Infinium HumanMethylationEPIC BeadChip (850K CpG sites)
- **Conflicts of Interest:** Multiple authors are employees of TruDiagnostic, which funded data analysis

## Key Evidence

### Comparative Effects (DQ vs DQF)

| Metric | DQ (3mo vs baseline) | DQ (6mo vs baseline) | DQF (6mo vs baseline) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PC Horvath pan tissue EAA | p=6.7e-6 (increase) | p=0.234 (NS) | p=0.017 (increase) |
| PC Horvath Skin & Blood EAA | p=0.025 (increase) | p=0.297 (NS) | p=0.083 (NS) |
| PC Hannum EAA | p=0.002 (increase) | p=0.020 (increase) | p=0.093 (NS) |
| PC GrimAge EAA | p=0.988 (NS) | p=0.170 (NS) | p=0.890 (NS) |
| PC DNAmPhenoAge EAA | p=0.005 (increase) | p=0.038 (increase) | p=0.288 (NS) |
| DunedinPACE | p=0.699 (NS) | p=0.912 (NS) | p=0.323 (NS) |
| IntrinClock | p=0.484 (NS) | p=0.624 (NS) | p=0.125 (NS) |

**Interpretation:** Fisetin addition blunted most first-generation clock increases. Only the Horvath pan tissue clock remained significant under DQF. Second/third-generation clocks were stable in both arms.

### DNA Methylation Changes (EWAS)

- DQ at 3 months: 11 differentially methylated CpGs (4 hyper, 7 hypo); enriched for senescence-related processes (glycolysis, vesicle recycling, cytoskeletal organization)
- DQ at 6 months: only 2 significant CpGs; no aging/senescence enriched pathways
- DQF at 6 months: 208 significant CpGs (roughly 50/50 hyper/hypo); enriched for senescence-related pathways (epithelial cell proliferation, platelet dense granule membrane, cell junction, cardiac muscle cell apoptosis)

### Immune Cell Composition

DQ produced significant changes: decreased CD4T Naive cells (p=0.039), decreased B Naive cells at 3mo then significant rebound increase 3-6mo (p=0.001), and increased monocytes (p=0.003). DQF produced only one significant change: B Naive cells decreased (p=3.0e-4), opposite to the DQ trend. Importantly, adjusting EAA for immune cell changes did not alter the significance or direction of epigenetic clock results, indicating clock changes were not driven by immune cell redistribution.

### Telomere Length and Mitotic Clocks

DQ significantly decreased DNAm-predicted telomere length (age-adjusted p=4.4e-4 at 6mo) and increased mitotic clock values (p=2.4e-4 for stem cell divisions 3-6mo). DQF showed no significant changes in either metric.

### Inflammatory Proteomic EpiScores

Several inflammatory mediators (CRP, CXCL9, CXCL11, CCL17, TGF-alpha) showed increased predicted levels at 3 months of DQ treatment but decreased from 3 to 6 months. Complement C4 and C5a showed opposite trends.

## Safety/Adverse Events

- 3 of 19 DQ participants dropped out after 3 months: nausea, prostate cancer diagnosis, and concern about the drug
- No specific adverse event data reported for DQF arm
- The significant decrease in DNAm telomere length and increase in mitotic clocks under DQ raises potential concerns about increased cell replication

## Methodology

- DNAm measured via Illumina EPIC array with minfi preprocessing, Genome-wide Median Normalization, BMIQ normalization, and kNN imputation
- 12-cell immune deconvolution via EpiDISH
- EWAS via limma with adjustment for sex, age, batch, 3 PCs; participant ID as random effect
- FDR correction for multiple comparisons
- Paired t-tests for longitudinal comparisons

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size** (N=19 per arm) -- pilot study design
2. **No control group** -- cannot rule out time-related drift or confounding
3. **Blood-only measurement** -- tissue-specific effects unknown
4. **Non-randomized, open-label design**
5. **Multiple authors are employees of TruDiagnostic** (funding source) -- potential conflict of interest
6. **Cannot isolate Fisetin effect** -- always administered with DQ, not alone
7. **Mixed Fisetin formulations** (strawberry-based vs non-strawberry) with no subgroup analysis reported
8. **EpiScore protein predictions have limited accuracy** acknowledged by authors

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q4 (senolytic vs broader):** Strongly relevant. Shows DQ and DQF affect epigenetic clocks and immune cell composition in blood, but the more biologically meaningful clocks (GrimAge, DunedinPACE) were unchanged. Fisetin's addition modulated DQ's effects, and its neuroprotective/cognitive benefits may represent broader non-senolytic mechanisms. However, Fisetin was never tested alone.
- **Q5 (adversarial):** Highly relevant. The transient increase in first-generation epigenetic clock readings, significant telomere length decrease, increased mitotic clock values, immune cell perturbations, and 3 dropouts (including nausea and prostate cancer) constitute concerning signals, though the authors argue second/third-generation clock stability suggests these are not clinically meaningful.
- **Q7 (comparative):** Directly relevant. This is one of the few human studies directly comparing DQ vs DQF regimens. DQF showed attenuated epigenetic clock effects compared to DQ, fewer immune cell changes, and no telomere/mitotic clock effects. However, the comparison is confounded by the sequential rather than parallel design and by carryover effects (10 of 19 DQF participants had prior DQ exposure).

## Claims to Verify

- **N=19 per arm:** Consistent throughout (Table 1, methods). 3 dropped from DQ arm; analysis presumably used available data at each timepoint.
- **Fisetin described as "safest and most potent natural senolytic":** This claim (Introduction, citing Yousefzadeh 2018) is widely cited but from a single mouse study. The present study does not independently validate this.
- **DQF had 208 differentially methylated CpGs:** This is a much larger number than DQ alone at 6 months (only 2). This could suggest Fisetin addition increases epigenomic impact, which seems counterintuitive given the attenuated clock effects. Worth examining whether the larger EWAS signal could be partly due to the mixed Fisetin formulations or carryover from prior DQ treatment.
