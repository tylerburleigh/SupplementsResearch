If you’ve ever read the fine print on an energy drink label, you might have heard of taurine. This compound naturally occurs throughout the body and is also present in animal products, like meat, seafood, and dairy.

But what exactly is taurine, and is it worth it to consume it as a supplement? At
NOVOS
, we follow the research closely, and the evidence is mounting: Taurine may be one of the most exciting compounds for supporting longevity and healthy aging.

Here’s what you need to know about taurine benefits and how it may fit into your longevity regimen.

What Is Taurine and Where Do You Get It?

Taurine is a sulfur-containing compound found in high concentrations in the brain, heart, eyes, and muscles. As one of the
body’s most essential substances
, taurine supports a wide range of functions in the body, including:

Mitochondrial energy production

Metabolic efficiency

Electrolyte balance

Reduction of inflammation and cell damage, two key drivers of aging

Heart health

Exercise performance, recovery, and muscle health

Cognitive performance

Immune resilience

In contrast, taurine deficiency may result in:

Mitochondrial dysfunction

Deregulated nutrient sensing

Oxidative stress

Chronic inflammation

Cellular senescence

Metabolic dysregulation

Given its role in everything from energy production to inflammation, keeping taurine levels stable is essential. While you can get taurine from foods like shellfish, tuna, salmon, and organ meats, the average Western diet only provides 40 to 400 mg per day. That’s far below the recommended clinical dose, and plant-based eaters may get virtually none from diet alone.

What Are the Benefits of Taurine for Longevity?

A major 2023 study published in
Science
and
led by Columbia researchers
found that age-related declines in taurine levels may speed up aging in animals. When researchers gave taurine supplements to mice, they lived up to 12% longer. What’s more, the benefits were seen in both males and females — a noteworthy finding, as most longevity interventions tend to be sex-specific.

The same study also found that taurine helped protect cells in several important ways. It improved how mitochondria function, preserved the ends of DNA (telomeres), reduced damage to genetic material, and boosted stem cell activity. These effects weren’t limited to mice — similar results were seen in simpler organisms like worms and yeast, which suggests that taurine supports healthy aging across many forms of life.

Human Evidence: Healthspan Benefits of Taurine

Taurine may not only extend lifespan in animal models — it also supports healthspan in humans. A
2025 meta-analysis
reviewed nine randomized controlled trials involving overweight and obese adults. Participants who supplemented with 1.5 to 3 grams of taurine per day consistently experienced improvements in key health markers, including better insulin sensitivity, lower fasting glucose, improved lipid profiles (higher HDL, lower LDL), and reduced HbA1c (a blood test that measures average blood sugar levels).

One of those trials found that taurine supplementation at 1.5 grams per day led to a significant drop in hs-CRP — a blood marker of chronic, low-grade inflammation closely tied to aging and disease. This reduction was observed after just eight weeks of use, reinforcing taurine’s anti-inflammatory potential and its role in healthy aging.

Systemic Taurine Benefits: From Metabolism to Muscle Health

Outside of aging-specific data, taurine has several
notable benefits
that support whole-body health:

Cell hydration and electrolyte balance
: Taurine helps cells maintain hydration, particularly in tissues like skin and hair.

Liver and digestion
: It supports bile salt formation, which helps break down dietary fats and regulate cholesterol.

Mineral regulation
: Taurine helps manage calcium levels in certain cells.

Brain and nervous system
: Animal studies suggest
neuroprotective effects
against dementia and support for central nervous system development.

Vision
: Taurine is the most abundant amino acid in the retina, supporting eye health and
protecting against degenerative eye diseases
.

Immune system
: As an antioxidant, taurine helps protect cells from oxidative stress and inflammation.

Diabetes management:

Research suggests
taurine may improve insulin sensitivity and blood sugar control, potentially helping reduce the risk of obesity and type 2 diabetes.

Enhanced exercise performance:
Research shows
that taurine may support better oxygen use during workouts and reduce muscle damage, which could aid recovery and strength gains.

Put it all together, and taurine is doing a lot behind the scenes to keep your body running smoothly — and potentially helping you live not just longer, but healthier.

Supplementing With Taurine: Dosage & Effectiveness

Taurine is classified as Generally Recognized As Safe (GRAS) by the
U.S. FDA
, and daily intakes up to 3 grams are well-tolerated.

To match the 1.5 to 3 grams shown to improve health markers in studies, you’d need to eat over 1 kilogram of beef liver, 360 grams of scallops, or 900 grams of raw tuna daily. That’s why we consider supplementation to be the most practical way to access taurine’s full potential and address age-related taurine declines.
