# src-067: Taurine supplementation in conjunction with exercise modulated cytokines and improved scWAT plasticity in obese women

**Citation:** De Carvalho et al. (2021). Amino Acids, 53, 1391-1403. DOI: 10.1007/s00726-021-03041-4
**Venue:** Amino Acids (peer-reviewed)
**Citations:** 22
**Quality:** ok (metadata); content is abstract-only due to paywall

## Content Quality Note

Only the abstract is available from the publisher landing page. The full text is behind a paywall. The abstract is substantive and reports key outcomes, but detailed methods, statistics, effect sizes, and quantitative results cannot be verified. Claims below are drawn from the abstract only.

## Core Findings

1. Taurine supplementation (3 g/day for 8 weeks) decreased plasma IL-6 in obese women (Tau group, n=8), even without exercise.
2. When combined with exercise (Tau + Exe, n=8), taurine increased anti-inflammatory interleukins IL-15 and IL-10 and reduced IL-1-beta gene expression in subcutaneous white adipose tissue (scWAT).
3. Both groups (Tau and Tau + Exe) showed reduced adipocyte size, increased connective tissue, and increased multilocular droplets in scWAT, suggesting improved adipose tissue plasticity.
4. Despite these biochemical improvements, no changes in anthropometric characteristics (body weight/composition) were observed.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized controlled trial (RCT), two-arm parallel
- **Population:** 16 obese women, randomized 1:1
  - Tau group: n=8 (taurine supplementation alone)
  - Tau + Exe group: n=8 (taurine + exercise training)
- **Intervention:** Daily taurine supplementation (3 g) for 8 weeks; exercise training (details not available from abstract)
- **Outcomes measured:** Anthropometry, body fat composition, inflammatory markers (IL-6, IL-10, IL-15, IL-1-beta), oxidative stress markers -- assessed in both plasma and scWAT biopsy samples (pre/post)
- **Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04279600 (retrospectively registered August 18, 2019)
- **Ethics:** Approved by Ethical Committee of the School of Physical Education and Sport of Ribeirao Preto, University of Sao Paulo (protocol 62643516.7.0000.5659)
- **Funding:** Sao Paulo Research Foundation (FAPESP); taurine powder supplied by Ajinomoto

## Key Evidence

- **Inflammatory markers (plasma):** IL-6 decreased with taurine alone; IL-10 and IL-15 increased when taurine was combined with exercise.
- **Inflammatory markers (scWAT):** IL-1-beta gene expression reduced in Tau + Exe group.
- **Adipose tissue histology:** Both groups showed reduced adipocyte size, increased connective tissue, and multilocular droplets (suggesting browning/beiging).
- **Null findings:** No anthropometric changes observed despite biochemical improvements.
- **Taurine levels:** Plasma taurine increased with supplementation, confirming compliance.

## Limitations

- **Very small sample size:** n=16 total (8 per group), limiting statistical power and generalizability.
- **No placebo control group** mentioned in the abstract (both arms received taurine; the comparison is taurine vs. taurine+exercise, not taurine vs. placebo).
- **Retrospective trial registration** (registered after study commencement).
- **Abstract-only access:** Full methodology, statistical analyses, and detailed results are unavailable for verification.
- **Population specificity:** Obese women only; results may not generalize to men, non-obese individuals, or other demographics.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (efficacy of taurine supplementation):** Provides direct RCT evidence that 3 g/day taurine for 8 weeks reduces IL-6 and, with exercise, increases anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-10, IL-15) in obese women. Also shows scWAT plasticity improvements.
- **Q3 (optimal dose):** Uses 3 g/day, a commonly studied dose, but dose optimization is not addressed.
- **Q5 (exercise interaction):** Directly examines taurine + exercise vs. taurine alone, showing synergistic effects on anti-inflammatory cytokines and IL-1-beta expression in scWAT. Exercise appears to be necessary for the anti-inflammatory cytokine increases.
