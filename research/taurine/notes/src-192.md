# src-192: PTER is a N-acetyltaurine hydrolase that regulates feeding and obesity

**Authors:** Wei, Lyu, Markhard, Fu, Mardjuki, Cavanagh, Zeng, Rajniak, Lu, Xiao, Zhao, Moya-Garzon, Truong, Chou, Wat, Chidambaranathan-Reghupaty, Coassole, Xu, Shen, Huang, Ramirez, Jang, Li, Svensson, Fischbach, Long

**Venue:** Nature (2024), 49 citations

**Quality:** ok -- full text available from DOI landing page with substantive methods, results, and discussion.

---

## Core Findings

1. **PTER identified as the principal N-acetyltaurine hydrolase.** Using activity-guided fractionation from mouse kidney, the orphan enzyme PTER (phosphotriesterase-related) was shown to catalyze the hydrolysis of N-acetyltaurine into taurine and acetate. PTER has a narrow substrate range largely restricted to N-acetyltaurine (Kcat = 2,600 s-1, Km = 430 uM). PTER is expressed predominantly in kidney, liver, and brainstem.

2. **Pter knockout mice accumulate N-acetyltaurine and show resistance to diet-induced obesity -- but only under conditions of elevated taurine flux.** When Pter KO mice were placed on high-fat diet with taurine supplementation (2.5% w/v in drinking water), they exhibited reduced food intake (~7%), lower body weight, reduced fat mass, and improved glucose homeostasis compared to WT. This phenotype was absent on chow diet or without taurine supplementation, revealing a gene-by-environment interaction.

3. **Exogenous N-acetyltaurine administration reduces food intake and body weight via GFRAL-dependent signaling.** Administration of N-acetyltaurine (1-50 mg/kg/day i.p.) to diet-induced obese wild-type mice dose-dependently reduced food intake and body weight. The effect required intact GFRAL receptors (blocked by neutralizing anti-GFRAL antibody) but not GLP-1R or MC4R signaling. N-acetyltaurine increased circulating GDF15 levels by ~40%.

4. **Multiple pathways produce N-acetyltaurine: reverse PTER reaction, non-enzymatic acetyl-CoA condensation, and gut microbiome.** Antibiotic-treated mice showed ~30% reduction in circulating N-acetyltaurine; germ-free mice colonized with hCom2 microbiome showed ~80% increase. Individual gut bacterial strains produce N-acetyltaurine in vitro.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

- **Enzymology:** Recombinant PTER: Kcat = 2,600 s-1, Km = 430 uM, Vmax = 3.7 nM/min/mg. PTER activity abolished by mutations in active-site residues (H300, R233, R204, H26, H28, E169).
- **Pter KO tissue metabolomics:** N-acetyltaurine increased 2-fold (spleen) to 10-fold (blood). Taurine levels unchanged.
- **HFD + taurine water study:** N=10/group, 8 weeks. Pter KO mice had lower body weight (P = 7x10-4), reduced iWAT (P = 4.97x10-4), improved glucose tolerance. Female mice showed similar phenotypes.
- **N-acetyltaurine dosing in DIO mice:** Dose-dependent weight loss at 1-50 mg/kg/day i.p. Head-to-head: N-acetyltaurine (15 mg/kg) reduced food intake and body weight, while taurine alone or acetate alone at the same dose had no effect.
- **GFRAL dependency:** Anti-GFRAL antibody abrogated the anorexigenic effect of N-acetyltaurine. GLP-1R antagonist (exendin-3) did not block the effect. N-acetyltaurine still suppressed food intake in Mc4r KO mice.
- **Human genetics:** PTER locus shows "strong" Human Genetic Evidence score for BMI association in the Type 2 Diabetes Knowledge Portal; polymorphism near PTER linked to early-onset and morbid adult obesity in European ancestry.

## Methodology

- Activity-guided fractionation of mouse kidney cytosol (anion-exchange + size-exclusion chromatography) to identify PTER
- Recombinant enzyme kinetics (Michaelis-Menten) with panel of substrates
- Global Pter knockout mice (KOMP consortium)
- Targeted LC-MS metabolomics for N-acetyltaurine quantification across tissues
- Metabolic phenotyping: HFD feeding with/without taurine supplementation, treadmill exercise, metabolic chambers
- Pharmacological N-acetyltaurine administration in DIO mice
- GFRAL antibody blockade, GLP-1R antagonist, Mc4r KO experiments

## Relevance to Taurine-Longevity Hypothesis

This paper is a critical mechanistic follow-up to Singh et al. (2023) on taurine and aging. Key implications:

- **Mechanistic pathway:** Establishes that taurine's metabolic effects are mediated at least partly through N-acetyltaurine, not taurine itself. When taurine levels rise (via supplementation, exercise, or other stimuli), N-acetyltaurine levels increase, and in the absence of PTER (or when PTER is overwhelmed), this metabolite acts as an anorexigenic signal via GFRAL.
- **Taurine supplementation context:** The anti-obesity phenotype in Pter KO mice only manifests when taurine flux is elevated (supplementation or exercise). This suggests that taurine supplementation's benefits on body weight may depend on the N-acetyltaurine/PTER axis.
- **N-acetyltaurine vs. taurine:** Head-to-head comparison showed N-acetyltaurine (but not taurine at the same dose) reduces food intake and body weight in DIO mice. This challenges the assumption that taurine itself is the direct effector.
- **Gut microbiome connection:** The microbiome contributes to N-acetyltaurine production, linking taurine supplementation effects to the gut microbiome.
- **Broader relevance to aging:** The authors explicitly note that "taurine has been linked to multiple age-associated phenotypes" and suggest that "secondary taurine metabolites, such as N-acetyltaurine, are not simply biomarkers or inert by-products but in fact chemical effectors of the increased taurine state." This opens the possibility that other taurine supplementation effects on longevity could also be mediated through N-acetyltaurine or other secondary metabolites.

## Limitations

- Mouse-only data; no human intervention studies with N-acetyltaurine
- Pter KO phenotype is stimulus-dependent (requires both HFD and taurine elevation); absent on chow diet
- The precise molecular mechanism of N-acetyltaurine-GFRAL crosstalk is unclear (N-acetyltaurine does not structurally resemble GDF15, suggesting intermediate steps)
- GFRAL signaling is associated with nausea, raising the possibility that the anorexigenic effect may partly reflect malaise rather than healthy appetite regulation
- No direct assessment of longevity or aging endpoints
