# src-048: Taurine reduces the risk for metabolic syndrome: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

**Authors:** Tzang, Chi, Lin, Lin, Chang, Wu, Ozcakar
**Year:** 2024
**Venue:** Nutrition & Diabetes
**DOI:** 10.1038/s41387-024-00289-z
**Citations:** 23
**Quality:** ok (full text extracted via PDF fallback; content is substantive)

---

## Core Findings

This meta-analysis of 25 RCTs (N=1,024 participants) found that taurine supplementation significantly reduces multiple metabolic syndrome risk factors: systolic blood pressure (WMD -4.0 mmHg), diastolic blood pressure (WMD -1.5 mmHg), fasting blood glucose (WMD -5.9 mg/dL), and triglycerides (WMD -18.3 mg/dL). Taurine did not significantly affect HDL-C or body weight/BMI. Meta-regression revealed dose-dependent reductions for DBP and FBG. No significant adverse effects were observed.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Study characteristics
- **RCTs included:** 25
- **Total participants:** 1,024
- **Study dates range:** 1983-2021, conducted in 13 countries (Russia, Iran, Japan, Spain, Brazil, Canada, Ireland, China, Austria, Iraq, Denmark, USA, Egypt)
- **Age range:** 8-113 years
- **Populations:** healthy individuals, heart failure, hypertension, coronary heart disease, T1DM, T2DM, obesity, alcoholism, homocystinuria, post-surgical patients, prehypertensive individuals

### Dosing
- **Daily dose range:** 0.5 g/day to 6 g/day
- **Most common doses:** 1.5 g/day and 3 g/day and 6 g/day
- **Duration range:** 5 days to 365 days (most studies <= 8-12 weeks)
- **Total dose range:** 3 g to 168 g over the treatment period

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs
- **Registration:** INPLASY2023120081
- **Guidelines:** PRISMA 2020
- **Databases searched:** Embase, PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane CENTRAL, ClinicalTrials.gov
- **Search cutoff:** December 1, 2023
- **Initial yield:** 2,517 publications; 25 included after screening
- **Statistical method:** WMD with random-effects model (Comprehensive Meta-Analysis v3)
- **Heterogeneity:** I-squared and Cochran's Q
- **Bias assessment:** Cochrane RoB 2 (per-protocol approach)
- **Sensitivity analysis:** One-study removal
- **Publication bias:** Funnel plots + Egger's test (all p > 0.05, no bias detected)

## Quality Assessment

- 18/25 studies had "some risk" of bias due to lack of allocation concealment details
- 7/25 studies had low risk of bias across all domains
- No studies had high risk of bias
- Heterogeneity was high for SBP (84.9%) and FBG (75.5%), moderate for TG (35.5%), low for DBP (14.1%) and HDL-C (7.7%)
- Publication bias was not detected for any outcome

## Limitations

1. High heterogeneity for SBP and FBG outcomes, reflecting diverse populations and protocols
2. Most studies had short durations (<= 2 months), limiting conclusions about long-term effects
3. Mixed populations (healthy, heart failure, diabetes, obesity) -- not a uniform MetS cohort
4. Concurrent medications in cardiovascular patients may confound lipid results
5. No direct waist circumference data; BMI used as proxy
6. Some studies used crossover design with varying washout periods
7. Doses ranged widely (0.5-6 g/day), making optimal dose determination difficult
8. The meta-regression used total dose (duration x daily dose), which conflates dose and duration effects

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: What mechanisms drive taurine's metabolic effects?
This study provides high-level evidence that taurine improves multiple MetS parameters but does not directly investigate mechanisms. The discussion proposes: (1) increased nitric oxide availability and hydrogen sulfide production for BP reduction, (2) reduced hepatic glucose production, inhibited glucagon activity, enhanced insulin clearance, and beta-cell support for glycemic control, (3) enhanced bile acid synthesis via CYP7A1, increased LDL-C receptor activity for lipid effects. The dose-response relationships support a genuine physiological effect rather than placebo.

### Q6: What is known about taurine's effects on metabolic syndrome markers?
This is the most comprehensive and directly relevant meta-analysis for MetS outcomes. It demonstrates statistically significant improvements in 4 of 5 diagnostic MetS criteria (SBP, DBP, FBG, TG), with HDL-C being the exception. Secondary outcomes showed additional benefits for TC, LDL-C, HOMA index, and fasting insulin. No effect on BW or BMI. The study is the first to perform dose-response meta-regression for taurine and MetS, showing dose-dependent effects for DBP and FBG (total dose) and SBP, DBP, and TG (daily dose).

## Claims to Verify

1. **Total N = 1,024 participants from 25 RCTs.** Verified by counting participants in Table 1. Note that two entries (Ahmadian 2017a and 2017b) share the same subjects reporting different outcomes, and Spohr 2005 had 18 taurine vs 44 placebo (asymmetric crossover), which may complicate the total count.
2. **FBG meta-regression coefficient reported as both -0.0445 and -0.495.** The abstract states -0.0445 mg/dL per g (p = 0.0273) while the Results section reports -0.495 mg/dL per g (p = 0.0011). These may reflect different models (daily vs total dose) but the abstract attributes -0.0445 to total dose. This discrepancy should be noted.
3. **HbA1c p-value in Results section listed as 0.070** but the text says "significant reductions" and includes it among significant findings. The p-value exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold, so this is not statistically significant by standard criteria. The authors acknowledge it as "marginal."
