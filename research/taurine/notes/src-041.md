# src-041: Maleki et al. (2020) - Taurine Supplementation on Glycemic Control and Lipid Profile in T2DM

## Metadata
- **Title:** The effects of taurine supplementation on glycemic control and serum lipid profile in patients with type 2 diabetes: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **Authors:** Maleki V, Alizadeh M, Esmaeili F, Mahdavi R
- **Year:** 2020
- **Venue:** Amino Acids, 52, 905-914
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s00726-020-02859-8
- **Citations:** 28
- **Quality Note:** Full text behind paywall; only abstract, abbreviations, references, and acknowledgements available. Abstract is substantive.

## Core Findings (2-3 sentences)
Taurine supplementation at 3000 mg/day for 8 weeks significantly improved fasting blood sugar (FBS), insulin, HOMA-IR, total cholesterol (TC), and LDL-C in patients with type 2 diabetes compared to placebo. However, there were no significant changes in HbA1c, triglycerides, HDL-C, or anthropometric indicators. The study supports a modest but measurable benefit of taurine on glycemic and lipid markers in T2DM patients.

## Study Design
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **Population:** 45 patients with T2DM (Tabriz Sheikhor-raees Polyclinic and Imam-Reza Hospital Endocrine Center, Iran)
- **Intervention group:** n = 23, taurine 3000 mg/day
- **Placebo group:** n = 22, crystalline microcellulose/day
- **Duration:** 8 weeks
- **Outcome assessment:** Fasting blood samples at baseline and after 8 weeks

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Significant improvements in taurine group vs. placebo:
- **FBS:** p = 0.01
- **Insulin:** p = 0.01
- **HOMA-IR:** p = 0.003
- **Total cholesterol (TC):** p = 0.013
- **LDL-C:** p = 0.041

### Null findings (no significant changes):
- **HbA1c:** not significant
- **Triglycerides:** not significant
- **HDL-C:** not significant
- **Anthropometric indicators:** not significant
- **Dietary intakes:** not significant

### Statistical methods:
- Independent t-test, paired t-test, Pearson's correlation, ANCOVA

## Methodology
- Double-blind, placebo-controlled design
- Random allocation to taurine or placebo
- Fasting blood samples collected at baseline and endpoint
- Ethical approval: Medical Ethics Committee of Tabriz University of Medical Sciences (IR.TBZMED.REC.1397.682)
- Written informed consent obtained
- Funded by Vice Chancellor for Research at Nutrition Research Center, Tabriz University of Medical Sciences

## Limitations
1. **Small sample size:** Only 45 patients total (23 taurine, 22 placebo) -- limited statistical power
2. **Short duration:** 8 weeks may be insufficient to detect changes in HbA1c (which reflects ~3 months of glycemic control)
3. **Single-center study:** Conducted at two related clinical sites in Tabriz, Iran -- limited generalizability
4. **No effect sizes reported in abstract:** Only p-values provided, no mean changes or confidence intervals visible
5. **Full text behind paywall:** Unable to verify methodological details, exact inclusion/exclusion criteria, adverse events, or magnitude of changes
6. **HbA1c null finding:** The absence of HbA1c improvement despite FBS improvement may reflect the short study duration relative to HbA1c's averaging window

## Relevance to Research Questions
- **Q1 (What clinical evidence exists for taurine supplementation in humans?):** Directly relevant -- human RCT in T2DM patients showing benefits on FBS, insulin, HOMA-IR, TC, and LDL-C.
- **Q6 (Are there null/negative findings for taurine?):** Relevant -- null findings for HbA1c, triglycerides, HDL-C, and anthropometric measures despite positive effects on other markers.

## Claims to Verify
- **Exact effect sizes and confidence intervals** are not available from the abstract; these would be needed to assess clinical significance of the reported p-values.
- **The p-values** (FBS p=0.01, insulin p=0.01, HOMA-IR p=0.003, TC p=0.013, LDL-C p=0.041) should be verified against actual mean changes in the full text when available.
- **Dropout/attrition** data not available from abstract -- unclear whether all 45 participants completed the study.
