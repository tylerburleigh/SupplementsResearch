## Core Findings

1. Taurine, a sulfur-containing non-protein amino acid, exerts cardioprotective and anti-aging effects through multiple mechanisms including modulation of ion channels (Ca2+, K+, Na+), antioxidant activity, anti-inflammatory actions, and activation of SIRT1. Taurine accounts for approximately 50% of total free amino acids in the heart.

2. Evidence from both human and animal studies supports beneficial cardiovascular effects (blood pressure reduction, improved cardiac contractility, enhanced endothelial function), but the review notes that large-scale clinical trials are still needed to confirm efficacy and establish optimal dosing.

3. Taurine levels decline with age, and supplementation in mice extended lifespan by approximately 10% (via the Singh et al. 2023 Science study). Taurine influences multiple hallmarks of aging: cell senescence, telomere attrition, loss of proteostasis, stem cell depletion, and sirtuin activation.

## Mechanisms of Taurine in Cardiovascular Health

- **Cardiac function:** Enhances myocardial contractility, stroke volume, cardiac output. Modulates Ca2+ and Na+ handling in cardiomyocytes. Improves myocardial energetics. Attenuates angiotensin II signaling. Promotes natriuresis and diuresis via osmoregulatory activity in the kidney. Approved as heart failure treatment in Japan since 1985.
- **Vascular function:** Promotes nitric oxide (NO) production, reduces endothelial dysfunction, regulates ion channels and Ca2+ homeostasis. Beneficial effects on dysfunctional endothelium are more consistent than on healthy endothelium.
- **Signaling pathways:** Influences glucose transport, 3-phosphoinositide-dependent protein kinase-1, AKT, SIRT1, FOXO3, p38, NFkappaB.
- **Antioxidant:** Scavenges ROS; stabilizes SOD and glutathione. Two taurine-containing modified uridines (tm5u, tm5s2u) identified in mitochondrial tRNA may contribute to antioxidant actions.

## Mechanisms of Taurine in Aging

- **Cell senescence:** Taurine deficiency correlates with increased senescence markers (p16, p21, SA-beta-Gal). Slc6a6 (taurine transporter) knockout mice show drastically shortened lifespan with high senescence markers. Taurine supplementation for 10 months in aged mice reduced senescent cells by ~2x in brain/gut/muscle and ~3x in liver/fat.
- **Unfolded protein response:** Slc6a6 knockout triggers ER stress in skeletal muscle. Taurine's beneficial effects on lifespan in drosophila were abolished by silencing Ero1 or Xbp1 (ER stress genes).
- **Telomere attrition:** Taurine increases TERT expression in dental-pulp-derived stem cells. Correlation between liver telomere length and plasma taurine in mice. Prevented premature death in Tert-deficient zebrafish.
- **Sirtuins:** Activates SIRT1 in liver, heart, and brain. Molecular docking suggests direct interaction with SIRT1 at a different binding site than resveratrol (amino acid 441-445 vs 289-304).
- **Stem cells:** Increases stem cell survival and regenerative capacity. Slc6a6 knockout abrogates embryonic stem cell development.

## Evidence from Human Studies

### Cardiovascular
- Clinical trials demonstrate potential to reduce blood pressure, improve left ventricular function, and enhance exercise capacity in heart failure patients.
- **Key trial:** 120 patients with T2D randomized to 1 g taurine or placebo 3x/day (3 g/day total) for 8 weeks. Taurine group showed significant decreases in serum insulin, HOMA-IR, and markers of inflammation, oxidative stress, and endothelial dysfunction.
- **Meta-analysis (2018):** Concluded taurine ingestion can reduce blood pressure to a clinically relevant magnitude without major adverse side effects.
- Endothelial effects on healthy vasculature remain controversial (some studies show enhancement with ACh, others do not confirm); effects on dysfunctional endothelium are more consistent.

### Aging
- KNHANES data: Taurine supplementation decreased cardiometabolic risk in male elderly subjects aged >=75.
- Double-blind study: 24 women, 1.5 g taurine or placebo for 16 weeks. Taurine prevented decrease in SOD plasma levels, suggesting control of oxidative stress during aging.

### Athletic performance
- Energy drinks contain ~750-1000 mg taurine per 240 mL can, but confounded by caffeine. Conflicting findings on performance enhancement; concerns raised about energy drink abuse.

## Evidence from Animal Studies

- **Mice:** Taurine supplementation increased lifespan by ~10% (Singh et al. 2023, Science). Doses: 15-30 mg/day depending on age, equivalent to 3-6 g for an 80-kg human. Improved muscle endurance and strength. Shaped gut microbiota positively.
- **C. elegans:** Taurine-fed worms lived longer and were healthier.
- **Rhesus monkeys:** Lower body weights, reduced liver damage signs, denser bones with taurine supplementation.
- **Slc6a6 knockout mice:** Drastically shortened lifespan, high senescence markers. Treatment with senolytics increased lifespan, linking taurine deficiency causally to cell senescence.
- **Heart:** Taurine deficiency induces atrophic cardiac remodeling; supplementation increases myocardial contractility.

## Human Supplementation Doses Mentioned

- Typical dietary intake: ~40-400 mg/day from average Western diet
- Supplement doses: 500-2000 mg per serving (most common range)
- Clinical trial in T2D: 1 g three times daily (3 g/day total) for 8 weeks
- Aging study in women: 1.5 g/day for 16 weeks
- Mouse-to-human equivalent for longevity: 3-6 g/day for 80-kg person
- Upper level of supplementation (per risk assessment by Shao et al.): 3 g/day
- Minimum dose used in clinical trials reviewed: 3 g/day

## Negative/Null Findings and Caveats

- Effects of taurine on healthy endothelium remain **controversial** -- some studies show enhanced endothelium-dependent relaxation, others do not confirm.
- Evidence for taurine supplementation in infant formula is **mixed** on whether it is beneficial.
- Conflicting findings on athletic performance enhancement; caution warranted.
- The review acknowledges that **exact mechanisms of antioxidant action remain unclear**.
- Authors explicitly call for **large-scale clinical trials** to confirm cardiovascular benefits and establish optimal dosing/periods.
- Potential drug interactions noted: medications lowering blood pressure, targeting Ca2+ or angiotensin signaling, or modulating heart/CNS function.
- Side effects at high doses: GI disturbances (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea), neurological symptoms (dizziness, tremors, headache).

## Limitations

- Narrative review, not systematic -- selection bias possible in included studies.
- No formal quality assessment of included studies reported.
- Much of the aging mechanistic evidence is from animal/cell models with limited human translational data.
- Longevity data relies heavily on a single Science paper (Singh et al. 2023).
- No meta-analytic synthesis of effect sizes.
