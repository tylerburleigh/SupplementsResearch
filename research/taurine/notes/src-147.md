# src-147: Taurine supplementation as a therapeutic strategy for cellular senescence and chronic inflammation in long COVID

**Citation:** Wang K, Ma CH, Khoramjoo M, Kung JY, Oudit GY. BMC Infect Dis 26, 774 (2026). DOI: 10.1186/s12879-026-13009-y

**Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA-compliant, PROSPERO: CRD420251011508)

**Quality note:** Metadata flagged as "degraded" due to paywall detection, but the article is open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0) and full text was available. The content is substantive and authoritative.

## Core Findings

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 27 clinical trials (n=1,030 participants) demonstrates that taurine supplementation significantly improves metabolic markers, inflammatory markers, and oxidative stress markers relevant to PASC pathophysiology. A parallel analysis of 6 studies (n=308) shows significantly lower plasma taurine levels in individuals with PASC compared to recovered counterparts. No clinical trials to date have directly tested taurine in PASC patients; the evidence is drawn from populations with conditions sharing core pathological features (chronic inflammation, metabolic dysfunction, endothelial dysfunction).

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Study characteristics
- 27 clinical trials, 1,030 total participants
- Sample sizes ranged from 14 to 120, median 30 per study
- Median taurine dose: 3,000 mg/day (range 1,000-6,000 mg/day)
- Median treatment duration: 8 weeks (range 1-16 weeks)
- 15 studies in metabolic disorders, 5 in cardiovascular disease, 4 in neurocognitive, 3 in GI disorders
- 17 studies used placebo control; 6 used non-intervention/standard of care

### Inflammatory markers (critical for Q1 and Q3)
- **CRP**: 7 studies, SMD -0.77 (95% CI: -1.01 to -0.54) -- strong, significant reduction
- **TNF-alpha**: 6 studies, SMD -0.42 (95% CI: -0.65 to -0.19) -- significant reduction
- **IL-6**: 6 studies, SMD -0.54 (95% CI: -1.07 to -0.01) -- trend toward reduction, wide CI, borderline significant
- **IL-10**: 3 studies, SMD 0.62 (95% CI: -0.11 to 1.34) -- non-significant increase
- **Dose-response**: Meta-regression identified dose-response for TNF-alpha (beta -0.006, p=0.03) and IL-6 (beta -0.0008, p=0.02)

### Oxidative stress
- **Malondialdehyde (MDA)**: 6 trials, SMD -0.71 (95% CI: -1.17 to -0.24) -- significant reduction
- Other markers assessed but not consistently reported across studies

### Metabolic parameters
- **HbA1c**: 10 studies, SMD -0.23 (95% CI: -0.42 to -0.03) -- significant
- **Fasting blood glucose**: 8 studies, SMD -0.38 (95% CI: -0.59 to -0.17) -- significant
- **Fasting insulin**: 7 studies, SMD -0.40 (95% CI: -0.63 to -0.16) -- significant
- **HOMA-IR**: 7 studies, SMD -0.36 (95% CI: -0.59 to -0.13) -- significant
- **Total cholesterol**: 12 studies, SMD -0.50 (95% CI: -0.85 to -0.15) -- significant
- **Triglycerides**: 12 studies, SMD -0.50 (95% CI: -0.98 to -0.03) -- significant
- **LDL**: 8 studies, SMD -0.33 (95% CI: -0.55 to -0.11) -- significant
- **HDL**: 10 studies, SMD 0.49 (95% CI: -0.09 to 1.08) -- NOT significant

### Cardiopulmonary
- **Systolic BP**: SMD -0.30 (95% CI: -0.56 to -0.05) -- significant
- **Diastolic BP**: SMD -0.29 (95% CI: -0.49 to -0.10) -- significant
- **Heart rate**: SMD -0.15 (95% CI: -0.59 to 0.30) -- NOT significant
- **Exercise capacity**: SMD 0.50 (95% CI: 0.02 to 0.98) -- significant (only 3 studies)

### Null findings
- **Neurocognitive function**: SMD 0.07 (95% CI: -0.24 to 0.38) -- no significant effect. Studies were mostly in elderly institutionalized women at high risk of bias, using brief screening tools (MoCA, MMSE). Only 4 studies total.
- **HDL**: No significant effect
- **Heart rate**: No significant effect

### Plasma taurine in PASC vs recovered
- 6 studies, 308 participants (104 recovered, 204 with PASC)
- After sensitivity analysis (excluding one outlier study with younger mean age of 32): SMD -0.35 (95% CI: -0.63 to -0.08, I^2=11.70%) -- significantly lower taurine in PASC
- Full pooled estimate including outlier: SMD -0.20 (95% CI: -0.60 to 0.20) -- not significant due to heterogeneity

### Optimal dose
- Based on dose-response for TNF-alpha and IL-6, 3,000 mg/day recommended as optimal balance of efficacy and tolerability
- Adverse events: generally well-tolerated; mild GI discomfort, fatigue, flatulence at 6,000 mg/day in one study

## Senescence Mechanism (Q3 relevance)

The paper provides mechanistic discussion linking taurine to anti-senescence pathways:
- Taurine interacts with CDKN2AIP, a positive regulator of p53 pathway that promotes p53 degradation, thereby suppressing p53-p21 and p16INK4a signaling axes
- In vitro: taurine treatment significantly reduced SA-beta-gal activity in irradiated osteoblasts
- Network analysis: taurine-related genes most frequently interact with SASP genes p16 and p21
- Taurine inhibits NF-kB signaling cascade, reducing SASP-driven inflammation
- Taurine modulates NLRP3 inflammasome activation (a canonical inflammaging pathway)
- SARS-CoV-2 triggers virus-induced cellular senescence with cell-cycle arrest, impaired mitochondrial energetics, and pro-inflammatory SASP that persists after viral clearance

## Methodology

- PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis
- Databases: MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, Scopus (supplementation); plus CINAHL, Web of Science (taurine levels)
- Search through March 21, 2025
- Random-effects restricted maximum likelihood models (R metafor package)
- RoB 2 risk of bias: 12/27 low risk (44.4%), 11/27 some concerns (40.7%), 4/27 high risk (14.8%)
- Meta-regression explored dose, age, sex, treatment duration
- Sensitivity analyses using Cook's distance and one-study-removal

## Limitations

1. **No PASC-specific trials**: All 27 trials were in non-PASC populations (obesity, diabetes, heart failure, etc.) -- findings require validation in PASC patients
2. **Small sample sizes**: Median 30 participants per study; total n=1,030
3. **Heterogeneous populations**: Various comorbidities pooled together; substantial heterogeneity in many analyses (e.g., triglycerides I^2=82%, HDL I^2=86.5%)
4. **6 non-randomized trials** included, introducing potential confounding
5. **No direct senescence measurements**: No clinical measurement of cellular senescence markers; anti-senescence mechanism is inferred from inflammatory/metabolic outcomes and preclinical evidence
6. Studies excluded if taurine was co-administered with other nutrients -- cannot assess synergistic effects
7. Neurocognitive null finding is based on few, low-quality studies in atypical populations

## Claims to Verify

- N=1,030 across 27 trials: stated in abstract and results; consistent across the paper
- N=308 for plasma taurine analysis (104 recovered, 204 PASC): stated in abstract and results
- Median 30 participants per study, range 14-120: stated in results
- Optimal dose of 3,000 mg/day: based on meta-regression dose-response for TNF-alpha and IL-6; the regression coefficients are small and the clinical significance of the dose-response requires careful interpretation
- IL-6 SMD -0.54 with 95% CI crossing near zero (-1.07 to -0.01): this is borderline significant and should be interpreted cautiously

## Relevance

- **Q1 (taurine health effects/dose)**: Highly relevant. Provides pooled effect sizes for taurine supplementation on CRP, TNF-alpha, IL-6, MDA, metabolic markers, BP, exercise capacity, and neurocognition. Identifies 3,000 mg/day as optimal dose. Strong evidence for anti-inflammatory and metabolic effects.
- **Q3 (senescence mechanism)**: Highly relevant. Discusses mechanistic pathways linking taurine to anti-senescence effects (p53-p21/p16 suppression, NF-kB inhibition, SA-beta-gal reduction in vitro). Positions PASC as a condition of accelerated aging/senescence and taurine as a potential therapeutic. However, direct clinical senescence evidence is limited to preclinical data.
