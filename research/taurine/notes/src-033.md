# src-033: Ohsawa et al. 2019 - Taurine for MELAS Phase III Trial

## Core Findings

Oral high-dose taurine supplementation (9-12 g/day for 52 weeks) significantly reduced the annual relapse rate of stroke-like episodes in MELAS patients from 2.22 to 0.72 (P=0.001), with 60% of patients achieving complete prevention. This is the first clinical trial to demonstrate that taurine can partially restore the taurine modification defect in mitochondrial tRNA-Leu(UUR) in vivo, providing direct mechanistic evidence that taurine ameliorates a specific mitochondrial translation defect.

## Study Design

- **Type:** Multicentre, open-label, phase III trial (UMIN000011908)
- **Population:** 10 MELAS patients with recurrent stroke-like episodes, identified via nationwide survey in Japan (291 total MELAS patients screened; prevalence estimated at 0.22 per 100,000)
- **Age range:** 14-46 years (mean 29.1); 7 male, 3 female
- **Mutations:** 9 patients with 3243A>G, 1 with 3271T>C in mitochondrial tRNA-Leu(UUR)
- **Dose:** Weight-based: 12 g/day (>=40 kg), 9 g/day (25-39 kg), 6 g/day (15-24 kg), 3 g/day (<15 kg). Target: achieve plasma taurine > 0.3 mM (concentration shown in prior in vitro work to improve mitochondrial dysfunction)
- **Duration:** 52 weeks; first 8 weeks excluded from evaluation period
- **Primary endpoint:** 100% responder rate (complete prevention of stroke-like episodes)
- **No placebo control** due to rarity and ethical considerations (patients continued prior therapies including CoQ10, L-arginine)

## Key Evidence

### Clinical Outcomes
- **100% responder rate:** 60% (95% CI 26.2%-87.8%), lower CI exceeded null hypothesis threshold of 5%
- **50% responder rate:** 80% (95% CI 44.4%-97.5%)
- **Annual relapse rate:** reduced from 2.22 +/- 0.73 to 0.72 +/- 0.62 (P=0.001)
- Focal neurological deficits: 33 events pre-trial vs 8 during evaluation period
- 9 of 10 patients were already on L-arginine; stroke-like episodes still decreased with taurine addition

### Plasma/CSF Taurine Levels
- Plasma taurine: 57.57 +/- 20.29 -> 945.67 +/- 406.18 nmol/mL (P=0.0001)
- CSF taurine: 11.24 +/- 2.88 -> 42.11 +/- 13.77 nmol/mL (P=0.0007)
- Confirms high-dose oral taurine crosses blood-brain barrier

### Mitochondrial Mechanism (Critical for Q3)
- Taurine modifies the first anticodon nucleotide (uridine) of mitochondrial tRNA-Leu(UUR) -- this "wobble" modification is essential for accurate codon-anticodon pairing during mitochondrial protein translation
- MELAS mutations (3243A>G, 3271T>C) cause a taurine modification defect, leading to failure in decoding UUG and UUA codons, impairing mitochondrial translation
- **First-in-human measurement** of taurine modification rate using primer extension method on peripheral blood leukocyte tRNA
- 5 of 9 tested patients showed significant increase in taurine modification rate of mitochondrial tRNA-Leu(UUR) (P<0.05)
- Proposed mechanism: high-dose taurine substrate enables MTO1 (mitochondrial translation optimization 1) enzyme to partially restore the modification

### Metabolic Markers
- No significant changes in serum/CSF lactate, pyruvate, or lactate:pyruvate ratio
- CSF lactate:pyruvate ratio showed trend toward decrease (28.47 -> 26.03, P=0.0521)
- No significant change in Japanese Mitochondrial Disease Rating Scale scores

### Safety
- No patients discontinued; 84 adverse events total (10 patients)
- 10 treatment-related adverse events, all mild-moderate (diarrhea, constipation, appetite loss, insomnia, oral aphtha, reflux esophagitis, elevated gamma-GTP, pollakiuria, hiatal hernia, gastroenteritis)
- 2 serious adverse events (hyperCKemia, gastritis) deemed unrelated to taurine
- No severe adverse events associated with taurine

## Mechanistic Insights (Q3 Relevance)

1. **tRNA wobble modification:** Taurine directly modifies mitochondrial tRNA-Leu(UUR) at the wobble position. This is a specific molecular mechanism by which taurine supports mitochondrial protein translation. Deficiency in this modification impairs synthesis of mitochondrial-encoded respiratory chain subunits.

2. **Dose-response for mitochondrial effect:** The 0.3 mM plasma threshold was derived from prior in vitro work showing improved VO2 and mitochondrial membrane potential in patient-derived cells at that concentration.

3. **Substrate-driven rescue:** The data support a model where high-dose taurine acts as a substrate to push the MTO1 modification enzyme to overcome the modification defect caused by pathogenic mtDNA mutations.

4. **Limited metabolic effect:** Despite clinical improvement, standard metabolic markers (lactate, pyruvate) did not significantly change, suggesting the benefit may operate through more subtle translational optimization rather than gross metabolic correction.

## Limitations

- Small sample size (n=10) -- inherent to rare disease
- Open-label, no placebo control
- First 8 weeks excluded from evaluation (potential early effects missed)
- Pre-trial stroke-like episodes not always MRI-confirmed (asymmetric comparison)
- 9/10 patients on concurrent L-arginine; cannot fully isolate taurine effect
- Taurine modification measured only in leukocytes, not in affected brain tissue
- Only 5/9 patients showed significant tRNA modification improvement (partial mechanistic response)

## Relevance to Research Question

Highly relevant for Q3 (mechanisms of taurine action on mitochondrial function):
- Provides direct human evidence that taurine participates in mitochondrial tRNA modification, a fundamental mechanism for mitochondrial protein translation
- Demonstrates that oral taurine supplementation can reach target tissues (including CSF) and partially correct a specific mitochondrial molecular defect
- Shows clinical efficacy in a disease defined by mitochondrial dysfunction, supporting the broader concept that taurine supports mitochondrial health

## Claims to Verify

- Prevalence of MELAS stated as "at least 0.22 per 100,000" -- derived from 291 patients identified from 126 million population. Straightforward arithmetic confirms this.
- Annual relapse rate reduction (2.22 to 0.72): these are calculated over the evaluation period (44 weeks, not 52), so the annualized rates depend on the exact time window used. The pre-trial period frequency was based on 78 weeks of retrospective data.
- Sample size justification: n=10 to test H0: responder rate <= 5% vs H1: >= 50%, with >90% power and alpha=0.05. This is a standard exact binomial calculation and is reasonable.
