## Core Findings

This is a Columbia University medical center press release summarizing the Singh et al. 2023 *Science* paper led by Vijay Yadav (Columbia). It presents the key findings in accessible language for a general/clinical audience and emphasizes the need for human clinical trials before recommending taurine supplementation.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Animal findings (from Singh 2023)
- Taurine levels in blood decline substantially with age across species. In people, 60-year-olds had roughly one-third the taurine levels of 5-year-olds.
- ~250 14-month-old mice (roughly equivalent to 45 human years) were supplemented daily with taurine or control.
- Average lifespan increased by 12% in females and 10% in males (3-4 extra months, equivalent to ~7-8 human years).
- Health improvements in supplemented mice at age 2 (~60 human years): suppressed age-associated weight gain, increased energy expenditure, increased bone mass, improved muscle endurance and strength, reduced depression-like and anxious behaviors, reduced insulin resistance, younger immune system profile.
- Cellular-level improvements: fewer senescent ("zombie") cells, increased survival after telomerase deficiency, more stem cells, improved mitochondrial performance, reduced DNA damage, improved nutrient sensing.
- Rhesus monkeys given daily taurine for 6 months: prevented weight gain, reduced fasting blood glucose and liver damage markers, increased spine/leg bone density, improved immune health.

### Human correlational evidence
- 12,000 European adults aged 60+: higher taurine levels associated with fewer cases of type 2 diabetes, lower obesity, reduced hypertension, lower inflammation. Yadav emphasizes these are associations, not causal evidence.
- Exercise study: taurine levels increased significantly after strenuous cycling in all groups (sprinters, endurance runners, bodybuilders, sedentary individuals).

## Expert Physician Perspective (Vijay Yadav, PhD)

Yadav is quoted extensively:
- Describes taurine as potentially "an elixir of life within us."
- Argues taurine should be considered alongside other anti-aging candidates (metformin, rapamycin, NAD analogs) for clinical trials.
- Notes advantages: naturally produced in the body, obtainable from diet, no known toxic effects (though concentrations used in the study are rarely tested), and boostable through exercise.

## Caveats About Human Applicability

- Yadav explicitly states: "These are associations, which do not establish causation."
- "Only a randomized clinical trial in people will determine if taurine truly has health benefits."
- At the time of publication, taurine trials for obesity were underway but none measured a wide range of health parameters.
- The article does not specify human-equivalent dosages from the mouse/monkey studies.
- Columbia University filed provisional patent applications with Yadav listed as inventor (disclosed competing interest).

## Doses Discussed

- No specific human dose recommendations are made.
- Animal doses described only as "a bolus of taurine" daily for mice and "daily taurine supplements" for monkeys -- no mg/kg or absolute amounts specified in this article.
- The article notes concentrations used in the study are "rarely used" in human contexts, suggesting the animal doses may be high relative to typical human supplementation.
