# src-002: Singh et al. (2023) "Taurine deficiency as a driver of aging" - Science

**Citation:** Singh P, Gollapalli K, Mangiola S, et al. Science. 2023 Jun 9;380(6649):eabn9257. doi:10.1126/science.abn9257. PMID: 37289866. PMCID: PMC10630957.

**Quality:** Full text available (PMC author manuscript). Peer-reviewed, published in Science.

## Core Findings

1. Circulating taurine concentrations decline with age in mice, monkeys, and humans (by >80% in elderly humans vs. young). Reversing this decline through supplementation extended lifespan in mice (10-12% median increase) and worms (10-23%), and improved multiple healthspan metrics in mice and rhesus monkeys.

2. Taurine supplementation impacted all established hallmarks of aging: reduced cellular senescence, protected against telomerase deficiency, suppressed mitochondrial dysfunction (via mitochondrial tRNA taurinylation / tau-m5U pathway), decreased DNA damage, attenuated inflammaging, improved stem cell maintenance, modulated nutrient sensing (decreased p-RS6P, increased autophagy/LC3A/B), and affected epigenetic patterns.

3. In humans (EPIC-Norfolk cohort, n=11,966), lower taurine and hypotaurine levels were associated with higher BMI, waist-to-hip ratio, type 2 diabetes prevalence, inflammation (CRP), and other age-related pathologies. Taurine levels increased after acute endurance exercise in athletes.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Age-related taurine decline
- Mice (C57Bl/6J): serum taurine dropped from 132.3 +/- 14.2 ng/mL (4 weeks) to 40.2 +/- 7.1 ng/mL (56 weeks); slope = -25.7, p < 3.4e-10
- Monkeys: 85% lower in 15-year-old vs. 5-year-old females
- Humans: >80% decrease in elderly vs. younger individuals

### Lifespan extension (mice)
- Dose: 1000 mg/kg BW/day (T1000), administered orally once daily starting at 14 months
- Median lifespan increase: 10-12% (both sexes)
- Life expectancy at 28 months increased by 18-25%
- Two independent cohorts showed consistent control median lifespans (females: 871-885 days; males: 785-815 days)
- Dose rationale: pilot study showed this regimen restored peak blood taurine to young (4-week-old) baseline levels
- Both sexes tested; effect was greater in females

### Lifespan extension (worms)
- Dose-response tested: 0, 10, 50, 100, 150, 300 uM
- Median lifespan extended 10-23% at higher concentrations
- Replicated in two independent labs (U. Washington, USA; NII, India)

### No effect in yeast
- Replicative lifespan unaffected by taurine (0-100,000 uM) in S. cerevisiae
- Authors attribute this to evolutionary divergence in taurine metabolism enzymes

### Healthspan in mice (females, 14-month-old, treated 10-12 months)
- Two doses tested: 500 mg/kg/day (T500) and 1000 mg/kg/day (T1000)
- Body weight gain suppressed ~10% (T1000); fat pad weight/BW% dose-dependent reduction
- Bone: increased bone mass (BV/TV%) in spine and femur; improved femur maximal load and stiffness; cured osteoporosis in ovariectomy model
- Muscle: increased hanging time, rotarod distance (T500 and T1000); grip strength (both doses); wire hang latency (T1000)
- Behavior: reduced depression-like behavior (tail suspension); reduced anxiety (light-dark box); enhanced exploration/memory (Y maze)
- Metabolic: improved glucose tolerance; improved insulin sensitivity; faster GI transit
- Immune: decreased WBCs, monocytes, granulocytes (T1000); ameliorated myeloid-leukocyte prominence
- Energy expenditure: increased O2 consumption, CO2 production, RER, energy expenditure in males

### Healthspan in mice (males, 14-month-old, T1000 for 8-16 weeks)
- Reduced fat pad weight/BW% but not body weight over 16 weeks
- Improved muscle strength, neuromuscular coordination, bone density, glucose tolerance, memory; reduced anxiety

### Mechanistic pathways
1. **Senescence suppression:** SA-beta-Gal staining reduced; p16/p21 SASP network top hit in RNA-seq; irradiation-induced senescence reduced in culture; senolytics (D+Q) improved Slc6a6-/- healthspan and extended lifespan ~21% but did not fully rescue, indicating other mechanisms involved
2. **Telomerase deficiency protection:** tert-/-(G2) zebrafish: taurine (300 uM, 10 mM) suppressed senescence and rescued lethality
3. **DNA damage reduction:** Serum 8-OH-dG reduced in supplemented mice; phospho-gamma-H2Ax increased in taurine-deficient muscle; improved survival after paraquat challenge
4. **Epigenetic effects:** CpG methylation in muscle and cerebral cortex shifted toward young patterns; H3K9me3 and H3K27me3 changes tissue-specific
5. **Nutrient sensing/proteostasis:** Decreased p-RS6P (mTOR pathway); increased LC3A/B (autophagy); rapamycin partially rescued taurine-deficient phenotypes
6. **Mitochondrial function:** Decreased mitochondrial ROS (MitoSOX); increased Pgc1alpha and Ucp1 in brown fat; reduced lipid peroxidation (~22%) and protein carbonylation (~11%) in liver
7. **Mitochondrial tRNA taurinylation:** tau-m5U content declined >60% in aged liver; taurine supplementation prevented decline (only ~20% reduction); increased ND6 (complex I subunit) protein; rotenone (complex I inhibitor) blocked taurine's health benefits in worms
8. **Inflammaging:** Reduced TNF-alpha, IL17-alpha, RANTES, IL1-alpha, GM-CSF in supplemented mice
9. **Stem cells:** Increased Lgr5+ cells in gut epithelium and hair follicles

### Human observational data (EPIC-Norfolk)
- n = 11,966 subjects
- Higher taurine/hypotaurine associated with: lower BMI, lower waist-to-hip ratio, less abdominal obesity, lower type 2 diabetes prevalence, lower glucose, lower CRP (inflammation)
- Positive associations with hemoglobin, platelets, WBC count
- Mixed associations with liver/lipid traits (taurine positive for AST/cholesterol; hypotaurine negative)
- Data from open-access metabolomics web server (Metabolon HD4 platform)
- Important caveat: association does not establish causation

### Exercise data
- Athletes (sprinters, endurance, bodybuilders) and sedentary subjects
- Taurine increased 1.16-fold post graded exercise test in all athlete groups (p values: 0.046, 0.0021, 0.0017)
- Sedentary: trend toward increase, not significant (p = 0.067)
- Hypotaurine increased 1.36-fold in all subjects
- N-acetyltaurine increased in endurance (1.18-fold) and sprinters (1.28-fold)

### Non-human primate study
- Rhesus monkeys, 15 +/- 1.5 years old (equivalent to 45-50 human years)
- Dose: 250 mg/kg BW/day (T250) for 6 months (equivalent to T1000 in mice via allometric scaling)
- Results: 0.75 kg less weight gain; increased bone density (lumbar spine, legs); reduced fasting glucose by 19%; reduced AST by ~36%, ALT by ~20%; WBC/monocytes/granulocytes decreased ~50%; serum 8-OH-dG decreased ~36%, lipid peroxide decreased ~11%, protein carbonyls decreased ~20%
- Only female monkeys tested (acknowledged limitation)

## Limitations (acknowledged by authors)

1. Male monkeys were not tested (only females); human association analyses did not distinguish between sexes
2. The study does not identify the initial/primary molecular events that taurine elicits
3. Senolytic treatment did not fully rescue taurine-deficient mouse lifespan, suggesting additional mechanisms beyond senescence
4. No human clinical trial data -- authors explicitly call for human trials
5. The paper notes that taurine supplementation in subjects with metabolic abnormalities does not affect BMI (citing Guan & Miao 2020, ref 85), which tempers the observational associations
6. Congenitally taurine-deficient (Slc6a6-/-) mice have more severe taurine depletion than aged animals, making some comparisons difficult
7. Taurine had no effect on yeast replicative lifespan, limiting cross-species generalizability
8. Conflicting interest: Columbia University filed provisional patent applications (VKY inventor)

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (human RCTs):** This paper has NO human RCT data. It explicitly calls for human clinical trials. It is the primary preclinical evidence motivating such trials.
- **Q2 (observational evidence):** EPIC-Norfolk cohort (n=11,966) provides strong observational evidence linking lower taurine to age-related diseases. Also exercise metabolomics data. However, these are associations only.
- **Q3 (mechanisms):** THE primary mechanistic paper. Covers senescence, telomere function, DNA damage, epigenetics, nutrient sensing/proteostasis, mitochondrial function (tau-m5U-tRNA / ND6 / complex I pathway), inflammaging, and stem cell maintenance. Most comprehensive mechanistic study available.
- **Q4 (expert debate):** Not directly, but represents the core position in the debate -- that taurine deficiency is a "driver" (not merely passenger) of aging.
- **Q5 (negative evidence):** Notes that taurine supplementation does not affect BMI in metabolically abnormal humans (ref 85); no effect on yeast lifespan; senolytics only partially rescued taurine-deficient phenotypes.
- **Q6 (doses):** Detailed dose information: 1000 mg/kg/day in mice (lifespan); 250 mg/kg/day in monkeys; 10-300 uM in worms. Dose rationale based on restoring youthful blood taurine levels.

## Claims to Verify

1. **Mouse serum taurine values** (132.3 and 40.2 ng/mL): These seem low for whole serum; typical reported values are in the ug/mL range. May reflect specific assay methodology. Worth cross-referencing with supplementary materials.
2. **EPIC-Norfolk n=11,966**: This is a well-established cohort; the number is plausible but should be verified against the original cohort description (ref 66: Pietzner et al., Nat Med 2021).
3. **Monkey age equivalence** (15 years = 45-50 human years): Standard allometric conversion; reasonable for rhesus macaques.
4. **Dose scaling** (T250 in monkeys = T1000 in mice): The authors state this is an equivalent dose based on body surface area scaling. This is a standard FDA approach (divide by ~4 for mouse-to-monkey), and 250 = 1000/4 is consistent.
