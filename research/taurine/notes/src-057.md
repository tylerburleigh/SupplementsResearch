# src-057: Taurine supplementation on inflammatory markers and clinical outcomes in TBI patients

**Citation:** Vahdat et al. (2021), Nutrition Journal, 20:53. DOI: 10.1186/s12937-021-00712-6
**Quality:** Full paper text available (open access). Content quality is good.

## Core Findings

Taurine supplementation (30 mg/kg/day, max 3 g/day, for 14 days) significantly reduced serum IL-6 levels and improved Glasgow Coma Scale scores in traumatic brain injury (TBI) patients in a double-blind RCT (n=32 completers). No significant effects were found for IL-10, hs-CRP, TNF-alpha, ICU length of stay, ventilator days, or 30-day mortality.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Double-blind randomized controlled trial
- **Population:** 32 TBI patients (out of 44 enrolled) admitted to ICU, GCS 6-12, aged >18, receiving enteral nutrition. Baseline characteristics well-matched. Most were male (~80%), mean age ~33, GCS 6-9 at enrollment.
- **Intervention:** Taurine 30 mg/kg/day (max 3 g/day) added to Standard Entera Meal for 14 days (n=16 taurine, n=16 control)
- **Duration:** 14 days supplementation, outcomes tracked through 30-day mortality
- **Setting:** ICU of Golestan Hospital, Ahvaz, Iran (April-November 2018)
- **Trial registration:** IRCT20180514039657N1

## Inflammatory Markers Measured

| Marker | Between-group change (p-value) | Within-group significance |
|--------|-------------------------------|--------------------------|
| **IL-6** | **p = 0.04** (significant) | Decreased in both groups; taurine group: -70.75 +/- 71.89; control: -20.09 +/- 59.51 |
| **TNF-alpha** | p = 0.07 (not significant) | Increased significantly in control group (p = 0.004); no significant change in taurine group |
| **hs-CRP** | p = 0.24 (not significant) | Decreased significantly in taurine group (p = 0.02); no significant change in control |
| **IL-10** | p = 0.81 (not significant) | No significant within-group changes reported |

## Clinical Outcomes

| Outcome | Between-group p-value | Notes |
|---------|----------------------|-------|
| **GCS change** | **p = 0.03** (significant) | Median change: taurine +2 (IQR 0,3) vs control 0 (IQR -1,1) |
| **APACHE II change** | p = 0.05 (marginal) | Decreased in taurine group; median change -4 vs -1 |
| **SOFA change** | p = 0.06 (not significant) | Decreased significantly within taurine group (p=0.003) but not between groups |
| **30-day mortality** | p = 0.33 | 6.3% taurine vs 25% control (1 vs 4 deaths) -- not significant |
| **Ventilator days** | p = 0.26 | 18 +/- 9 vs 23 +/- 14 days |
| **ICU length of stay** | p = 0.96 | No difference |
| **Weight loss** | **p = 0.03** (significant) | Taurine: -4.80 +/- 0.93 kg; Control: -5.61 +/- 1.09 kg |

## Key Evidence / Effect Sizes

- IL-6 reduction was the primary significant inflammatory finding: taurine group had approximately 3.5x greater reduction in IL-6 than control (-70.75 vs -20.09 pg/mL), p=0.04
- TNF-alpha trended toward significance (p=0.07): taurine prevented the increase seen in controls
- hs-CRP decreased significantly within the taurine group (p=0.02) but between-group difference was not significant (p=0.24)
- GCS improvement of +2 points (median) in taurine vs 0 in control is clinically meaningful

## Methodology

- Inflammatory markers measured via ELISA kits from serum samples at day 1 and day 14
- Randomization via computer-generated sequence with block method (block size 6), sealed envelopes held by independent third party
- Blinding: patients, recruiters, intervention assigners, and outcome evaluators all blinded; gavage solutions identical in appearance, taste, color, odor
- Statistical analysis: SPSS 13.0; normal data via independent/paired t-tests; non-normal via Mann-Whitney U / Wilcoxon; qualitative via chi-squared / Fisher's exact
- Sample size calculation based on IL-6 with alpha=0.01, beta=0.1, targeting 30 patients plus 30% dropout = 44

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size** (n=32 completers from 44 enrolled; 27% dropout) -- the study was underpowered for clinical endpoints like mortality
2. **Short supplementation duration** (14 days only)
3. **Plasma taurine levels not measured** -- cannot confirm bioavailability or compliance
4. **Single-center study** in Iran -- limited generalizability
5. No resting energy expenditure measurements
6. Several outcomes (SOFA, TNF-alpha, mortality) showed non-significant trends that might reach significance with larger samples

## Null Findings

- IL-10: no effect (p=0.81)
- hs-CRP: no between-group difference (p=0.24), despite within-group decrease in taurine arm
- TNF-alpha: no between-group difference (p=0.07), though taurine prevented the increase seen in controls
- SOFA score: no between-group difference (p=0.06)
- 30-day mortality: no difference (p=0.33)
- ICU length of stay: no difference (p=0.96)
- Ventilator days: no difference (p=0.26)

## Relevance to Research Question (Q1: Inflammatory Endpoints)

This study is directly relevant to Q1. It provides primary clinical trial evidence that taurine supplementation reduces IL-6 in a critically ill population (TBI patients). The dose was 30 mg/kg/day (max 3 g/day) for 14 days. The IL-6 finding was statistically significant (p=0.04) with a substantial effect size (~3.5x greater reduction vs control). Null findings for hs-CRP, TNF-alpha, and IL-10 between groups suggest taurine's anti-inflammatory effects may be marker-specific. The within-group hs-CRP decrease and TNF-alpha prevention in the taurine arm hint at broader effects that the study was underpowered to detect between groups.

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample size discrepancy:** Abstract mentions "32 patients" randomized. Methods says n=22 per group (44 total) were randomized, but Table 1 shows baseline n=22 per group. Tables 2 and 3 show n=16 per group (32 completers). The 12 dropouts are explained by the flow diagram (not fully visible). The numbers are internally consistent.
- **IL-6 change values:** Mean change reported as -70.75 +/- 71.89 (taurine) vs -20.09 +/- 59.51 (control). These are large changes with high variability; the p=0.04 is plausible but the effect is driven by large SDs relative to the mean difference.
- **Mortality rate:** 1/16 (6.3%) vs 4/16 (25%). With n=16 per group, this difference is not statistically significant, which is correctly reported (p=0.33 by Fisher's exact test). The absolute difference (18.7 percentage points) is clinically notable but underpowered.
