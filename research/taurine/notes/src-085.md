# src-085: Cross-sectional study of functional and metabolic changes during aging in male mice

**Citation:** Petr et al. (2021), eLife 10:e62952. 84 citations.

**Quality:** Full text available (eLife open access). Content is substantive with methods, results, discussion.

## Core Findings

This cross-sectional study characterized functional decline and organ-specific metabolic remodeling across the lifespan (3-36 months) of male C57BL/6N mice. The key finding is that organs remodel their metabolism differently with age: liver and heart upregulate redox-related pathways (pentose phosphate, NAD+ salvage, transsulfuration) to cope with increased oxidative stress, while skeletal muscle and brain do not, making them more vulnerable. Taurine appears specifically within the transsulfuration pathway as one of the metabolites exhibiting significant age-dependent changes.

## Taurine-Specific Findings

Taurine is mentioned explicitly in the context of the transsulfuration pathway, alongside cysteine, glutamate, glycine, and glutathione. The paper identifies a "consistent meta-pattern of metabolic change" involving metabolites from redox-related pathways including the transsulfuration pathway (cysteine, taurine, glutamate, glycine, glutathione). Specifically:

- Taurine is part of the 46 shared metabolites significantly different across age groups in serum and multiple organs
- The transsulfuration pathway (which produces taurine as an end product) is upregulated in liver and heart of old mice
- This upregulation is interpreted as a protective metabolic remodeling response to increased oxidative stress
- In skeletal muscle, the opposite pattern was observed -- metabolites from this pathway were lower in old mice
- The brain showed intermediate behavior

No quantitative data on taurine levels by age group is reported in the text (likely in the heatmap figures not fully rendered in markdown).

## Key Evidence and Data Points

**Sample:** Male C57BL/6N mice, N=71 total: Young (3-8 mo, n=23), Adult (13-23 mo, n=26), Old (27-36 mo, n=22). Metabolomics on n=11-12 per group (young and old), n=7 (adult) per tissue.

**Aging biomarkers documented:**
- Frailty index increased linearly (Y: 0.13 +/- 0.01, A: 0.24 +/- 0.01, O: 0.31 +/- 0.02; p=3.80e-16, r2=0.615)
- Gait speed declined (p=2.73e-16, r2=0.608); fully explained by age + frailty
- Energetic cost of walking increased exponentially from ~20 months
- Work capacity decreased sustainedly
- Bone cortical thickness declined (p=7.20e-07, r2=0.458)
- Body weight and fat peaked at ~19 months then declined
- RER decreased from ~0.85 to ~0.80 (shift toward lipid oxidation in older mice)
- Fasting blood glucose, insulin, and HOMA-IR lower after 19 months (survivorship bias)

**Metabolomics findings:**
- 133-177 metabolites detected per organ; 46 shared significantly different across age
- 5 tissues analyzed: brain, heart, liver, skeletal muscle, serum
- Liver: highest metabolite abundance, active glycolysis/glycogenolysis, amino acid degradation, urea cycle
- Heart: BCAA accumulation, active glucose catabolism, active mitochondrial function, reduced 3-HB
- Brain: linoleic acid, stearic acid, 3-HB accumulation; active urea cycle
- Skeletal muscle: high glucose catabolism, amino acid degradation, urea cycle
- Serum: BCAAs accumulated; glucose, 3-HB, glycerol depleted in old mice

## Methodology

- Cross-sectional design (not longitudinal)
- Untargeted metabolomics via GC-TOF MS (West Coast Metabolomics Center, UC Davis)
- Multivariate statistics: PCA, PLS-DA, VIP scores, MSEA
- Correlation matrices linking metabolites to phenotypic outcomes (energetic cost, RER, frailty)
- Metabolite set enrichment analysis (MSEA) with MetaboAnalyst

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional, not longitudinal -- cannot infer causal relationships
- Male mice only, single strain (C57BL/6N)
- No correction for multiple comparisons
- No taurine supplementation intervention -- taurine is observed as part of broader metabolic remodeling
- Metabolomics data is relative (normalized peak amplitude), not absolute quantification
- Gut-heart-brain axis is not explicitly studied; the paper focuses on organ-specific metabolic profiling

## Relevance to Research Question (Q2: observational evidence on aging)

**Highly relevant.** This paper provides strong observational evidence of age-related metabolic changes across multiple organs in a controlled mouse model. Taurine is identified as part of the transsulfuration pathway remodeling that occurs differentially across organs during aging -- upregulated in liver and heart (protective) but not in skeletal muscle or brain. This supports the hypothesis that taurine-related pathways are involved in the aging process, though it does not test taurine supplementation directly. The organ-specific nature of the findings (some organs compensate, others don't) is an important nuance for understanding taurine's potential role in aging.

Note: Despite the assigned title mentioning "gut-heart-brain axis," the actual paper title is "A cross-sectional study of functional and metabolic changes during aging through the lifespan in male mice" and does not study the gut specifically.
