# src-036: Systematic Review of Taurine for DCM and Heart Failure

**Citation:** McGurk KA, Kasapi M, Ware JS. Wellcome Open Research 2022, 7:9. DOI: 10.12688/wellcomeopenres.17505.3
**Quality:** ok (full paper text available, peer-reviewed, 2 approved reviews)

## Core Findings

This systematic review identified 11 human studies examining taurine supplementation in heart failure patients, none of which were specific to dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM). Meta-analysis of the only two parameters measured consistently enough across studies (ejection fraction and stroke volume) found **no significant effect** of taurine supplementation in all-cause heart failure. Individual studies reported improvements in NYHA class, diastolic/systolic function, exercise capacity, and hemodynamic parameters, but these could not be pooled due to heterogeneity. The authors conclude a formal clinical trial in DCM-specific patients is needed.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **Studies reviewed:** 285 articles identified across 4 databases (PubMed, Cochrane, Web of Science, BioMed Central); 11 met inclusion criteria
- **Study quality:** Only 1 of 11 studies rated "high quality" (NOQAS score 8/9); 7 rated "fair" (6-7); 3 rated "low" (5 or below). 91% were fair or lower quality.
- **Author overlap:** 63% of included studies (7/11) came from just two research groups, with duplicate reporting of values across papers
- **Doses:** 500 mg to 6 g per day
- **Frequency:** Once to three times daily
- **Delivery methods:** Tablet (n=1), capsule (n=4), powder (n=1), sachet (n=1), drink (n=1), not described (n=3)
- **Duration:** 2 weeks to 12 months (median 6 weeks)
- **Placebo control:** Only 55% of studies (6/11) included a placebo group
- **Meta-analysis results:** EF and SV pooled across 3 studies -- neither reached statistical significance. SV showed high heterogeneity (I2 = 93%)
- **NYHA class:** Improved in studies by Azuma et al.; 5/7 patients (71%) improved to NYHA class II after 4 weeks, sustained for 3-12 months; another study found 4/14 improved on taurine vs 0/14 on placebo
- **Long-term data (Azuma 1994, n=103):** At 3 months, 60% of taurine group had symptomatic improvement vs 20% non-taurine; at 1 year, 39% had improved breathlessness vs 15% non-taurine
- **Safety:** No significant safety concerns attributable to taurine. Adverse events in the multivitamin supplement study (Jeejeebhoy 2002) were likely due to other components. One patient died of hepatoma in another study.
- **DCM-specific gap:** No study enrolled DCM-specific patients; all were all-cause HF with mixed aetiologies (predominantly ischemic)

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review with meta-analysis, registered on PROSPERO (CRD42021241114)
- **Databases:** PubMed, Cochrane Central Register, Web of Science, BioMed Central (searched through March 11, 2021)
- **Eligibility:** Human studies of participants with cardiomyopathy/HF/altered LV function, administering taurine in any formulation. Non-English excluded.
- **Quality assessment:** Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOQAS) for non-randomized studies
- **Meta-analysis:** Random-effects model in R (v3.6.0) using meta and metafor packages; required at least 3 studies with similar traits
- **Limitations:** Exclusion of non-English articles; single reviewer for initial screening; high author overlap among included studies; small sample sizes; heterogeneous HF aetiologies; most studies lacked placebo controls

## Limitations

- No study was specific to DCM patients (the primary condition of interest)
- Overwhelmingly low study quality (91% fair or lower)
- Small sample sizes across all studies
- High heterogeneity in HF aetiologies (mostly ischemic)
- 63% of studies from just two research groups with overlapping data
- Only EF and SV could be meta-analyzed; many outcomes reported only in single studies
- Non-English articles excluded

## Relevance to Research Questions

This is a key source for cardiovascular outcomes of taurine supplementation. It provides the most comprehensive evidence synthesis available for human heart failure and indirectly addresses DCM. The meta-analytic null findings for EF and SV are important counterpoints to positive individual study reports. The review highlights the critical gap: no DCM-specific trials exist.

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample sizes in individual studies** are small (n=4 to n=48 in most cases; the Azuma 1994 long-term study claims n=48 in Table 2 but n=103 in the text discussion -- this discrepancy should be noted; the text says "preliminary report of a long-term study of taurine administration in 103 chronic HF patients" but Table 2 lists Azuma 1994 as n=48 from 24 study centres)
- **P-value notation:** The abstract states "the association was not significant with all-cause HF (P<0.05)" -- this appears to be stating the significance threshold used, not the actual p-value. The results section and figures confirm non-significance, but the exact p-values are not clearly reported in the text.
- **Quality of life improvement (22.6% vs 16.6%)** is attributed to Averin 2015 but only reported briefly; could not be independently verified from the data presented.
