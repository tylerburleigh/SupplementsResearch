# src-016: New research challenges belief that taurine supplements support longevity

**Source type:** News article (Fortune Well)
**Author:** Beth Greenfield
**Date:** June 9, 2025
**URL:** https://fortune.com/well/2025/06/09/taurine-supplements-longevity

## Core Findings

A 2025 study published in *Science* challenges the foundational claim that taurine declines with age, which had been the basis for supplementing taurine for longevity. The study found that taurine levels either increased or remained unchanged with age in humans, rhesus monkeys, and mice, undermining the rationale that declining taurine is a universal aging biomarker warranting supplementation.

## Key Evidence

1. **Methodological critique of the 2023 study:** The original 2023 research that popularized taurine supplementation used a cross-sectional design (data from animals of different ages at a single point in time). The new study used a longitudinal design, sampling the same individuals over time, producing different results.

2. **Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging data:** The researchers examined taurine concentrations in blood from humans (ages 26+), rhesus monkeys, and mice. Taurine concentrations **increased** in all age groups except male mice, where it remained unchanged.

3. **Corroborating datasets:** Similar age-related taurine concentration patterns were observed in data from two additional studies.

4. **Study conclusion (quoted):** "On the basis of these findings, we conclude that low circulating taurine concentrations are unlikely to serve as a good biomarker of aging."

## Expert Commentary

- **Rafael de Cabo** (coauthor, gerontologist at National Institute on Aging): "We clearly show that there's no need for taurine supplementation as long as you have a healthy diet."
- **Julia Zumpano** (registered dietitian, Cleveland Clinic): "For the most part, people are either consuming enough protein or your body is adjusting to make sure you're getting what you need by creating and making taurine out of the other amino acids." This applies to vegans as well.
- De Cabo noted the results "yield a more complex picture" than previously thought, and that the effectiveness of taurine supplements for longevity likely depends on individual factors.

## Context on Taurine

- Taurine is a "conditionally essential" amino acid — the body typically produces enough on its own.
- Found in animal-based foods (beef, poultry, shellfish, eggs, dairy) and some vegan sources (seaweed, red algae).
- Popularized by biohackers (Bryan Johnson includes it in his Longevity Mix) and social media.
- Supports brain function, metabolism, vision, immune system; potential benefits for heart function, diabetes, exercise performance.

## Methodology

- Longitudinal study design (tracking same individuals over time), contrasting with the cross-sectional design of the original 2023 study.
- Published in *Science* (high-impact journal).
- Used data from the Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging plus two additional datasets.
