# src-076: A human-origin probiotic cocktail ameliorates aging-related leaky gut and inflammation via modulating the microbiota/taurine/tight junction axis

**Citation:** Ahmadi et al., JCI Insight 2020; 5(9):e132055. DOI: 10.1172/jci.insight.132055
**Citations:** 210 (as of metadata fetch)
**Quality:** ok -- full paper text with methods, results, discussion

## Core Findings

This study demonstrates a mechanistic chain linking gut microbiota modulation to taurine release to tight junction enhancement, showing that a 10-strain probiotic cocktail (5 Lactobacillus + 5 Enterococcus, isolated from healthy infant gut) ameliorated aging-related leaky gut, inflammation, and metabolic decline in older mice. The critical mechanism is that probiotic bacteria with bile salt hydrolase (BSH) activity deconjugate tauro-bile salts, liberating free taurine in the gut, which then directly stimulates tight junction protein expression (Zo1, Ocln) in intestinal epithelial cells, reducing permeability and downstream inflammation. Taurine alone extended lifespan in C. elegans by >2 days (equivalent to >10-12 human years) and reduced adiposity and leaky gut.

## The Microbiota/Taurine/Tight Junction Axis (Key Mechanism)

1. **Probiotics increase BSH activity:** The probiotic strains possess bile salt hydrolase (BSH) activity. BSH deconjugates tauro-bile salts (e.g., taurocholate) by breaking the covalent bond between taurine and bile acids, releasing free taurine into the lower gut.
2. **Taurine is the dominant metabolite increased:** Untargeted global metabolomics (NMR-based) showed taurine had the highest fold change (3.9x) in probiotic-fed vs. control older mice feces. The KEGG pathway most affected was "taurine and hypotaurine metabolism." Other elevated metabolites included total bile acids, butyrate, propionate, glucose, glycine, acetate, isoleucine, phenylalanine, and tyrosine.
3. **Taurine directly enhances tight junctions:** In vitro, taurine supplementation (200 uM) in cecal conditioned media significantly increased Zo1 and Ocln mRNA expression and reduced TEER decline in Caco-2 cell monolayers, demonstrating direct action on intestinal epithelial barrier integrity.
4. **Tight junction enhancement is primary, not inflammation suppression:** Using a coculture system (Caco-2 epithelial cells + THP-1 macrophages), the study showed probiotic-modulated microbiota primarily act on intestinal epithelial cells to increase tight junctions, which then reduces inflammation -- not the reverse. When CCM was applied to macrophages alone, no significant changes in TEER or tight junction expression were observed.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Mouse studies (80-week-old C57BL/6J, ~65 human years equivalent)
- **Sample sizes:** n=6-8 per group (control vs. probiotic), fed HFD for 10 weeks
- **Metabolic improvements:** Probiotic feeding significantly improved oral glucose tolerance (OGTT) and insulin sensitivity (ITT), reduced hepatic steatosis and WAT inflammation (crown-like structures)
- **Inflammation reduction:** Decreased IL-6, TNF-alpha, IL-1beta in LPS-stimulated peritoneal macrophages and colon tissue; increased anti-inflammatory IL-10 and TGF-beta in colon
- **Leaky gut markers:** Serum LBP significantly reduced; sCD14 decreased but not statistically significant
- **Tight junctions:** Zo1 mRNA and protein significantly increased; Ocln mRNA significantly increased (protein marginally increased)
- **RNAseq:** 856 genes upregulated, 1053 downregulated; top pathways: cell adhesion molecules (CAMs) and cytokine signaling
- **Microbiome:** Enhanced alpha-diversity (Shannon index); distinct beta-diversity clustering; increased Firmicutes, Ruminococcaceae, Clostridiales; decreased Verrucomicrobia, Akkermansia muciniphila

### C. elegans studies (taurine supplementation)
- **Lifespan extension:** >2 days increase (n=150-195 worms/group), equivalent to >10-12 human years
- **Reduced adiposity:** Oil Red O staining showed reduced fat accumulation
- **Reduced leaky gut:** Smurf assay showed decreased intestinal permeability
- **Improved physical function:** Increased body bend activity

### In vitro evidence
- **Caco-2 + THP-1 coculture:** CCM from probiotic-fed mice applied directly to Caco-2 cells significantly reduced TEER decline and FITC-dextran permeability; increased Zo1 and Ocln. No effect when applied to macrophages only, establishing tight junctions as primary target.

## Methodology

- **Animal model:** 80-week-old male C57BL/6J mice on HFD, 10-week probiotic intervention (1x10^9 CFU/mL in drinking water)
- **Probiotic cocktail:** 10 strains from healthy infant gut (5 Lactobacillus + 5 Enterococcus), characterized for safety and probiotic attributes
- **Metabolomics:** Untargeted NMR-based (400 MHz Bruker), analyzed with Chenomx 8.4 and MetaboAnalyst 3.0
- **Microbiome:** 16S rRNA gene sequencing (V4 region), QIIME 1.9.1, Greengenes database
- **BSH activity assay:** Ninhydrin-based colorimetric assay using taurodeoxycholic acid as substrate
- **Coculture system:** Caco-2/THP-1 Transwell model with cecal conditioned media
- **C. elegans lifespan:** Liquid culture in 96-well plates, taurine at 200 uM

## Limitations

1. **Mouse study only -- no human clinical trial data.** The probiotic cocktail and taurine effects have not been tested in human elderly subjects.
2. **Small sample sizes:** n=5-8 per group in mouse studies limits statistical power.
3. **Male mice only:** No female animals included, limiting generalizability.
4. **Taurine mechanism incompletely resolved:** The precise molecular signaling pathway by which taurine enhances tight junction protein expression remains unknown.
5. **BSH source attribution unclear:** Cannot distinguish whether BSH activity comes from the probiotic strains themselves or from probiotic-stimulated native microbiota.
6. **C. elegans translational gap:** Lifespan extension in nematodes has limited direct translatability to humans.
7. **Other metabolites also elevated:** Butyrate, propionate, and bile acids were also significantly increased and may contribute independently or synergistically -- their individual contributions were not dissected.
8. **No direct taurine supplementation in mice:** The study did not test whether direct taurine supplementation (rather than probiotic-induced taurine release) produces the same benefits in the aging mouse model.

## Relevance to Research Question (Q3: Gut Microbiome/Taurine Axis)

**Highly relevant.** This paper provides the most detailed mechanistic account of the microbiota-taurine-tight junction axis available in the source set:

- Establishes that taurine is a key microbial metabolite mediating gut barrier health
- Identifies BSH activity as the enzymatic mechanism by which gut bacteria liberate taurine from bile salts
- Demonstrates taurine's direct action on tight junction proteins (Zo1, Ocln)
- Provides causal evidence (coculture experiments) that the microbiota-taurine-tight junction pathway is the primary mechanism, not inflammation suppression
- Shows taurine extends lifespan and reduces aging phenotypes in C. elegans
- Directly relevant to how taurine supplementation might benefit gut health and aging, though human data are lacking

## Claims to Verify

- **"equivalent to >65 years human age"** for 80-week-old C57BL/6J mice -- this is a standard conversion but the exact equivalence varies by method.
- **"extended life span of C. elegans by >2 days, which is equivalent of >10-12 years of human age"** -- C. elegans wild-type lifespan is ~2-3 weeks at 20C; 2 days is approximately 10% of lifespan. The human equivalent calculation is approximate.
- **Taurine fold change of 3.9** in probiotic-fed vs. control mice feces -- stated in results but the actual quantitative values (concentrations) are not reported in text; would need to verify from figures.
- **Sample sizes vary across experiments** (n=5-8 for mice, n=5 for metabolomics subset) -- the metabolomics analysis appears to have been done on a smaller subset, which may limit the metabolite findings.
