# src-127: Significance of Taurine Transporter (TauT) in Homeostasis and Its Layers of Regulation

**Authors:** Baliou S, Kyriakopoulos AM, Goulielmaki M, Panayiotidis MI, Spandidos DA, Zoumpourlis V
**Venue:** Molecular Medicine Reports 22: 2163-2173 (2020)
**DOI:** 10.3892/mmr.2020.11321
**Citations:** 75
**Quality:** ok (full text available, raw PDF extraction with OCR artifacts)

## Core Findings

This review comprehensively details how the taurine transporter (TauT/SLC6A6) regulates intracellular taurine levels, which in turn mediate osmoregulation, antioxidant defense, and anti-inflammatory actions essential for cellular homeostasis. The TauT transporter is the primary (non-redundant) taurine transporter; its knockout eliminates up to 95-100% of taurine in cardiac/skeletal muscle and causes multi-organ dysfunction that worsens with age. The review establishes that taurine deficiency phenotypes closely parallel mitochondrial diseases (MELAS) and accelerate aging hallmarks.

## Key Mechanisms Described

### 1. Osmoregulation (Primary TauT Function)
- TauT uses a Na+/Cl- gradient to transport taurine with stoichiometry 2 Na+:1 taurine:1 Cl- (Hill model)
- Under hypertonic stress, TonEBP/NFAT5 binds the TonE element in the TauT promoter, upregulating expression
- mTOR signaling enhances TonEBP-dependent TauT transcription in human trophoblasts and mouse fibroblasts
- In TauT KO hearts, compensatory upregulation of alternative osmolyte transporters (Slc38a2) and osmolytes (betaine, glycerophosphocholine) occurs
- TauT is regulated by PKC phosphorylation at Ser-332, which neutralizes ionic binding at Arg-324 and abolishes transport activity

### 2. Antioxidant/Anti-inflammatory Properties
- Taurine prevents protein carbonyl content under high-glucose oxidative stress
- N-chlorotaurine (TauCl) formation provides protection against oxidants and pro-inflammatory cytokine damage
- Inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-1-beta) induce TauT activity in intestinal epithelial cells
- Anti-inflammatory properties demonstrated via TauT regulation in leukocytes of depression patients (mirtazapine reduced TauT-containing CD4+ T and CD8+ T cells to 77% and 80%, respectively)
- TauT KO mice show characteristics mimicking human diabetic nephropathy (mesangial expansion, glomerular basement thickening, nodular sclerosis)

### 3. Mitochondrial Function and Energy Metabolism
- Taurine is required for post-translational modification of mitochondrial tRNA-Leu(UUR) at the wobble position (5-taurinomethyluridine formation)
- Taurine deficiency impairs UUG codon decoding, reducing synthesis of Complex I subunits
- TauT KO hearts show impaired fatty acid beta-oxidation, Krebs cycle dysfunction, elevated NADH, and inefficient acetyl-CoA utilization
- Mass spectrometry confirmed downregulation of long-chain acyltaurines and upregulation of short-chain acyltaurines in TauT KO hearts

## Evidence: Human vs. Animal Studies

### Human Evidence
- Humans have reduced CSAD biosynthetic enzyme activity; dietary intake is the primary taurine source
- TauT transcription levels higher in healthy subjects vs. type 1 diabetes patients, with lower plasma homocysteine
- Reduced TauT expression in mononuclear peripheral blood cells of patients with diabetic retinopathy
- TauT in leukocytes of depression patients responds to antidepressant therapy
- 2% of type 2 diabetic patients harbor the MELAS-linked tRNA-Leu(UUR) mutation (cited from Vionnet et al)
- HASC (human adipose-derived stem cell) differentiation requires TauT activity

### Animal Evidence (Predominantly Mouse)
- TauT KO mice: 95-100% taurine loss in cardiac/skeletal muscle, 74% decrease in plasma/kidney/liver/eye
- CSAD KO mice (3rd generation) died within 24 hours of birth; rescued by 0.05% taurine in drinking water
- CDO KO mice: severe taurine elimination, increased hydrogen sulfide catabolism, pulmonary/pancreatic toxicity
- TauT KO mice on high-fat diet or genetic obesity models (ob/ob, db/db) show upregulated TauT in fat tissue
- Extensive renal cell line data (LLC-PK1, MDCK) for transporter regulation mechanisms

## Relevance to Aging

This is a strong section of the paper with specific mechanistic evidence:

- **Aging biomarker p16INK4A:** Aged TauT KO mice (18-22 months) showed up to 10x increased p16INK4A levels vs. aged wild-type; no difference in young mice, confirming the increase is aging-dependent
- **Sarcopenia:** TauT KO mice developed excessive formation of muscle fibers with central nuclei, diminished mitochondrial Complex I activity
- **Unfolded protein response (UPR):** Both aging and taurine deficiency decreased GRP78 expression and downregulated ER membrane sensor proteins (PERK, eIF2-alpha, IRE-alpha)
- **Accelerated aging phenotype:** Taurine-deficient hearts resemble accelerated aging with susceptibility to oxidation-mediated protein aggregates
- **Age-dependent organ dysfunction:** Within 1 year -- vision, auditory, olfactory, muscle impairment; beyond 15 months -- liver fibrosis, hepatitis, tumor formation
- **Cardiac aging:** Signs of dilated cardiomyopathy (ANP, BNP levels) more intense in 9-month-old vs. 5-month-old TauT KO mice
- **Liver aging mechanisms:** 3-month-old TauT KO: inactivation of RhBG ammonium transporter; 12-month-old: tyrosine nitration-mediated elimination of glutamine synthesis from oxidative/nitrative stress
- **SMAD3 and beta-catenin:** Transcriptome data from old TauT KO skeletal muscle showed induction of these signaling pathways

## MELAS Syndrome Connection
- Taurine deficiency phenotype nearly indistinguishable from MELAS syndrome
- Both involve impaired UUG decoding, diminished respiratory chain flux, decreased ATP synthesis, superoxide overproduction, anaerobic metabolism stimulation
- MELAS additionally impairs aminoacylation, reducing Complexes I and III-V (vs. only Complex I in pure taurine deficiency)

## Methodology
- Comprehensive narrative review (not systematic)
- Synthesizes data from TauT KO mouse studies, cell culture models, human clinical observations
- Uses renal system as primary model for transporter regulation
- Mass spectrometry (LC-MS) and H-NMR spectroscopy for metabolomic analysis

## Limitations
- Review article; no original experimental data presented
- Most mechanistic evidence comes from mouse KO models, with limited direct human mechanistic studies
- No quantitative meta-analysis of the evidence
- Some human data is observational (correlations, not causal)
- The paper focuses heavily on TauT transporter biology rather than direct supplementation outcomes
