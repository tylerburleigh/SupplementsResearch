# src-156: MTHFR 677C>T Meta-Analysis and Coronary Heart Disease

**Title:** Meta-analysis of MTHFR 677C->T polymorphism and coronary heart disease: does totality of evidence support causal role for homocysteine and preventive potential of folate?

**Authors:** Lewis SJ, Ebrahim S, Smith GD

**Year:** 2005

**Venue:** BMJ (doi: 10.1136/bmj.38611.658947.55)

**Citation Count:** 214

**Study Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of genetic association studies (Mendelian randomization approach)

---

## Core Findings

This meta-analysis of 80 studies (26,000 cases, 31,183 controls) found a modest overall association between the MTHFR 677C>T polymorphism and coronary heart disease (OR 1.14, 95% CI 1.05-1.24 for TT vs CC). However, this was driven by strong heterogeneity (I²=38.4%, P<0.001). After stratification by geography, the association disappeared in Europe (OR 1.08), North America (OR 0.93), and Australia (OR 1.04). Only Middle Eastern (OR 2.61) and some Asian (especially Japanese) studies showed significant associations, but these may reflect publication bias rather than true effects.

## Key Evidence

1. **Overall OR 1.14 (95% CI 1.05-1.24)** for TT vs CC genotype across all 80 studies, with strong heterogeneity (I²=38.4%, P<0.001)
2. **Null in high-income countries:** Europe OR 1.08 (0.99-1.18, 42 studies), North America OR 0.93 (0.80-1.10, 15 studies), Australia OR 1.04 (0.73-1.49, 3 studies)
3. **Positive in low-folate regions:** Middle East OR 2.61 (1.81-3.75, 5 studies), Japan OR 1.71 (1.23-2.37)
4. **Homocysteine differences by genotype** varied geographically: overall 2.24 umol/l (1.55-2.94), North America only 1.35 umol/l (consistent with mandatory folate fortification), Middle East 8.80 umol/l
5. **Publication bias detected:** Egger test P=0.03, and largest Japanese and Middle Eastern studies gave null results
6. **Clinical trials negative:** Recent folate supplementation trials in North America, Europe, and Australia showed no benefit for cardiovascular outcomes

## Methodology Notes

- Literature search: Medline and Embase, January 2001 to August 2004, no language restrictions
- Study design: Case-control and prospective studies; random effects models used throughout
- Primary comparison: TT genotype vs CC genotype (unadjusted odds ratios from published frequencies)
- Heterogeneity assessed via I² statistic and Cochran's Q test
- Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium checked in controls; 5 studies deviated (P<0.05) but exclusion did not change results
- Meta-regression used to assess whether homocysteine differences by genotype predicted CHD risk (beta=0.103, P=0.02)
- More than doubled the sample size compared to previous meta-analyses (Wald et al. 2002, Klerk et al. 2002)

## Limitations

- Did not have individual-level data; could not account for uncertainty in homocysteine differences between genotypes in meta-regression
- Observational genetic epidemiology lacks systematic review infrastructure (compared to RCTs)
- Publication bias likely inflates estimates despite comprehensive search
- Homocysteine measurements included some diseased individuals, potentially clouding genotype effects
- Small study bias could not be formally tested for Japanese and Middle Eastern subgroups due to few studies

## Relevance to TMG/Betaine Research

This paper is highly relevant to the TMG supplementation research question through several pathways:

1. **MTHFR and methylation (Q3):** The MTHFR 677C>T polymorphism directly impairs the conversion of 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate, which is the methyl donor for remethylating homocysteine to methionine via methionine synthase. TMG (betaine) provides an alternative remethylation pathway via betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase (BHMT). Understanding the CHD risk associated with the MTHFR variant helps define the population that might benefit from TMG as an alternative methyl donor.

2. **Clinical endpoints (Q2):** The study directly addresses whether homocysteine-lowering translates to cardiovascular benefit. The null findings in high-income countries suggest that simply lowering homocysteine may not reduce CHD risk, which would qualify claims about TMG's cardiovascular benefits derived from its homocysteine-lowering mechanism.

3. **Adversarial/contradicting evidence (Q5):** This paper is a key piece of contradicting evidence against the homocysteine-cardiovascular disease hypothesis. If homocysteine is not causally related to CHD (as this Mendelian randomization analysis suggests in well-nourished populations), then TMG supplementation's primary proposed mechanism of action for cardiovascular protection may not translate to clinical benefit.

4. **Folate interaction (Q4):** The geographical variation in MTHFR-CHD associations, with null effects in folate-fortified populations, raises the possibility that TMG supplementation may only be beneficial in populations with low folate intake or in MTHFR TT homozygotes with elevated homocysteine that cannot be adequately addressed by folate alone.
