# src-140: Modulation of DNA methylation by one-carbon metabolism: a milestone for healthy aging

- **Authors:** Choi, Sang-Woon; Friso, Simonetta
- **Year:** 2023
- **Venue:** Nutrition Research and Practice (Vol 17(4):597-615)

- **Citation Count:** 29
- **Type:** Narrative review (not systematic)

## Core Findings (2-3 sentences)

This review articulates the mechanistic axis linking one-carbon metabolism to DNA methylation and aging, arguing that modulating one-carbon nutrient intake (including betaine/choline as folate-independent methyl donors) can reshape age-associated DNA methylation changes and potentially mitigate age-associated diseases. The authors synthesize evidence from epigenetic clock studies, C. elegans longevity models, calorie/methionine restriction studies, and MTHFR polymorphism research to argue that one-carbon metabolism is a tractable target for healthy aging interventions. Notably, they highlight that clinical trial results for B-vitamin homocysteine reduction on cardiovascular and cognitive endpoints have been inconsistent, while paradoxically, reduced folate metabolism (as in C. elegans longevity mutants) can extend lifespan.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

1. **Betaine's role in one-carbon metabolism:** Choline and betaine catalyze the remethylation of homocysteine to methionine via betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase (BHMT), independent of folate and vitamin B12. This is the primary mechanism by which TMG/betaine supplementation could influence DNA methylation.

2. **C. elegans longevity and folate metabolism (Annibal et al., 2021):** Long-lived mutant strains showed substantially reduced 5-methyltetrahydrofolate levels. Supplementing 5-MTHF reversed longevity phenotypes, suggesting a causal link between reduced one-carbon metabolite flux and extended lifespan.

3. **MTHFR 677C>T polymorphism and methylation response:** Folate/B12 supplementation increased global DNA methylation only in wild-type 677CC individuals, not in variant 677TT carriers. This directly addresses Q3 -- genetic variation modulates response to methyl donor nutrients.

4. **Epigenetic clock and one-carbon nutrients (VA Normative Aging Study, N=715):** Vitamin B6 associated with decreased PhenoAge; folate associated with increased PhenoAge and GrimAge; cumulative 4-metabolite mixture (folate, B6, B12, homocysteine) associated with decreased GrimAge. This is an important observational finding with mixed directions.

5. **RCT on epigenetic age reversal (Fitzgerald et al., 2021):** Healthy lifestyle + dietary supplementation reduced Horvath DNA methylation age and significantly increased 5-MTHF levels, suggesting DNA methylation age can be lowered with one-carbon nutrients.

6. **Inconsistent clinical trial results:** Multiple large RCTs (NORVIT, HOPE-2) failed to show cardiovascular or cognitive benefits from B-vitamin homocysteine lowering, despite successful homocysteine reduction.

7. **Paradoxical methionine restriction:** Methionine restriction extends lifespan across species; SAH supplementation also extends lifespan in yeast via mTORC1 inhibition -- same pathway as methionine restriction. This creates a paradox where reducing methyl donor availability may promote longevity through methylation-independent pathways.

8. **Hyperhomocysteinemia with aging:** Plasma homocysteine increases with age due to reduced renal excretion, decreased nutrient intake, and dysregulated methionine cycle, leading to elevated SAH and DNA hypomethylation.

## Methodology Notes

- This is a narrative review, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. No PRISMA protocol; selection bias in included studies is possible.
- The review draws on animal studies (yeast, C. elegans, mouse, rat), observational human studies, and a limited number of RCTs.
- Key methodological approaches discussed: whole-genome bisulfite sequencing, DNA methylation microarrays (450K, EPIC), epigenetic clock models (Horvath, PhenoAge, GrimAge), liquid chromatography/mass spectrometry for one-carbon metabolites.
- The authors are established researchers in nutritional epigenetics (Choi at CHA University/UMass Amherst; Friso at University of Verona).

## Relevance to TMG/Betaine Supplementation

- **Direct relevance:** Betaine is explicitly positioned as a folate-independent methyl donor that remethylates homocysteine to methionine via BHMT. This is the primary mechanistic pathway by which TMG supplementation would influence DNA methylation and aging.
- **Indirect relevance:** The review provides the broader metabolic context (SAM/SAH ratio, homocysteine metabolism, epigenetic clocks) needed to understand how betaine supplementation might influence aging trajectories.
- **Limitation for TMG specifically:** The review discusses betaine among several one-carbon nutrients but does not focus on betaine supplementation trials or dose-response data. Most cited intervention studies use folate/B12 rather than betaine.
- **Paradox to consider:** If reduced one-carbon metabolite flux extends lifespan (as in C. elegans), then increasing methyl donor availability via betaine supplementation could theoretically counteract longevity mechanisms. This tension is not fully resolved in the review.

## Claims to Verify

1. **VA Normative Aging Study N=715 (line ~416-423):** The claim that plasma vitamin B6 decreases PhenoAge while folate increases PhenoAge and GrimAge seems counterintuitive (folate worsening epigenetic age). This should be cross-checked against the original paper (Nwanaji-Enwerem et al., 2021, ref 62).

2. **C. elegans 5-MTHF reversal of longevity (line ~237-245):** The claim that restoring folate reverses longevity phenotypes is attributed to Annibal et al., 2021 (Nat Commun). This is a key mechanistic claim that deserves verification.

## Limitations

- Narrative review without systematic methodology; potential for selective citation.
- No original data presented; synthesizes published evidence.
- Betaine is discussed as one of several methyl donors, not the primary focus.
- Does not address TMG dosing, pharmacokinetics, or safety.
- Does not directly address TMG supplementation RCTs for health endpoints.
