# src-362: Betaine Supplementation Decreases Plasma Homocysteine in Healthy Adults (Meta-analysis)

## Metadata

- **Title:** Betaine supplementation decreases plasma homocysteine in healthy adult participants: a meta-analysis
- **Author:** Marc P. McRae
- **Year:** 2013
- **Venue:** Journal of Chiropractic Medicine, 12(1), 20-25
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jcm.2012.11.001
- **PMCID:** PMC3610948
- **Study type:** Meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
- **Quality:** ok (full text available, substantive content)

## Core Findings

This meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (206 participants total) found that betaine supplementation at 4-6 g/d significantly reduces plasma homocysteine by 1.23 umol/L (95% CI: -1.61 to -0.85, P = .01), an 11.8% reduction from baseline. The author estimates this translates to a 5-8% reduction in cardiovascular disease risk and 10-15% reduction in stroke risk. However, the author also identifies a significant tradeoff: betaine increased total and LDL cholesterol by approximately 5 mg/dL in 2 of 5 studies, which could offset cardiovascular benefits. Folic acid is recommended as the first-line homocysteine-lowering agent, but betaine may be particularly useful for MTHFR 677C->T TT homozygotes (~12% of the population) who have impaired folate-dependent remethylation.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Random-effects meta-analysis of 5 RCTs published 2002-2010
- **Search strategy:** MEDLINE (1966-August 2012) plus manual reference search; Boolean: ("betaine" OR "trimethylglycine") AND "homocysteine"
- **Inclusion criteria:** Randomized placebo-controlled; intervention >= 4 weeks and <= 24 weeks; reported mean plasma homocysteine changes
- **Exclusion reasons:** Non-randomized (5 trials), disease populations (4 trials), insufficient data (2 trials), methionine loading (1 trial)
- **Statistical method:** Random-effects model; studies weighted by reciprocal of variance; variance calculated using Follmann et al. method with assumed correlation coefficient of 0.5
- **Population:** Healthy adults, age 27-59, 60.2% women, normal baseline homocysteine (8.4-12.7 umol/L)
- **Doses:** 4 of 5 trials used 6 g/d; 1 trial used 4 g/d
- **Duration:** 6-24 weeks (median 6 weeks)
- **Study designs:** 4 parallel double-blind, 1 crossover double-blind

## Key Evidence Points

1. **Primary outcome:** Pooled reduction of 1.23 umol/L (P = .01), all 5 trials showed trend toward reduction, 4 of 5 statistically significant individually
2. **Dose-response:** Doses of 3-6 g/d decrease homocysteine within 2 hours; maximum effect at 4-6 weeks
3. **Cardiovascular risk projection:** Extrapolated from Wald et al. 2002 meta-analysis linking 5 umol/L homocysteine reduction to 20-30% CVD risk and 40-60% stroke risk reduction
4. **Lipid tradeoff:** 2/5 studies showed significant increases in total and LDL cholesterol (~5 mg/dL); mechanism proposed as increased VLDL synthesis from choline sparing
5. **MTHFR relevance:** TT homozygotes have ~40% higher homocysteine; betaine-dependent BHMT pathway serves as alternative remethylation route when folate pathway is impaired
6. **Folate comparison:** Folic acid (800 ug/d) achieves ~3 umol/L homocysteine reduction vs. 1.23 umol/L for betaine; folate has no adverse lipid effects
7. **Two pathways interconnected:** Folic acid supplementation increases plasma betaine by 15%

## Limitations

1. All 5 studies used participants with normal baseline homocysteine -- effect may be larger in hyperhomocystinemia
2. Only MEDLINE was searched (single database); possible unidentified studies
3. Single author performed article selection and data analysis (no dual review)
4. No direct evidence that betaine supplementation reduces cardiovascular disease or stroke -- risk reduction is extrapolated from homocysteine-lowering effect
5. Small total sample size (206 participants across 5 trials)
6. Short study durations (median 6 weeks); no long-term outcome data

## Claims to Verify

- **206 participants total (124 betaine, 119 control):** These numbers sum to 243, not 206. This appears to be an inconsistency in the original paper, likely due to crossover design where some participants count in both groups, but the arithmetic warrants flagging.
- **11.8% reduction from baseline:** Calculated from 1.23 umol/L divided by average baseline (~10.4 umol/L). Reasonable but not explicitly verified in each trial.
- **Estimated CVD risk reduction of 5-8%:** This is an extrapolation from the Wald et al. 2002 meta-analysis, not directly measured. The actual clinical impact is uncertain.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (dietary intake):** Minor relevance -- mentions dietary betaine sources (spinach, beets, wheat) and Framingham Offspring Study showing inverse association between dietary betaine and homocysteine.
- **Q2 (clinical endpoints):** Moderate -- no direct clinical endpoint data, but provides cardiovascular risk projections based on homocysteine reduction. Explicitly states no studies show direct CVD/stroke reduction from betaine.
- **Q3 (methylation cofactors/MTHFR):** Strong -- discusses BHMT pathway, MTHFR 677C->T polymorphism, and the interplay between betaine and folate-dependent remethylation pathways. Identifies TT homozygotes as primary candidates for betaine supplementation.
- **Q4 (tradeoffs vs alternatives):** Strong -- directly compares betaine to folic acid, recommends folic acid as first-line, and identifies the LDL cholesterol increase as a significant concern. Discusses when betaine may be preferred (MTHFR polymorphism, low folate status).
- **Q5 (adversarial/contradicting evidence):** Strong -- highlights that betaine raises LDL cholesterol in some studies, that the lipid effect may negate homocysteine benefits, that no direct evidence exists for CVD reduction, and that folic acid is more effective. Also notes that some studies failed to replicate the LDL increase.
- **Q6 (dose-response/safety):** Strong -- provides specific dose ranges (4-6 g/d), timing of effects (2 hours acute, 4-6 weeks maximum), and notes no serious adverse effects but modest lipid changes.
- **Q7 (animal/mechanistic longevity):** Minimal -- focused on human clinical data only. Brief mention of BHMT catalyzing up to 50% of hepatic homocysteine metabolism.
