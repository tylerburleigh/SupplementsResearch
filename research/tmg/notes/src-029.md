## Study Design

- **Model:** Double-knockout mouse: ApoE-/-/SAHH+/- (heterozygous SAH hydrolase deficiency on atherosclerosis-prone ApoE-/- background)
- **Groups:** (1) ApoE-/-/SAHH WT (control), (2) ApoE-/-/SAHH+/- (SAHH-deficient), (3) ApoE-/-/SAHH+/- + betaine
- **Diet:** AIN-93G base diet with or without betaine at 4 g/100 g diet (approx 4% w/w)
- **Duration:** 8 weeks of dietary intervention, starting at 8 weeks of age
- **Sample sizes:** n=10 per group for in vivo endpoints; n=6 for biochemical/HPLC measurements; n=3 for cell culture experiments
- **Measurements:** Plasma SAH and SAM (HPLC-MS/MS), total homocysteine (HPLC-FD), aortic sinus lesion area (Oil Red O), immunohistochemistry for macrophages (CD68), smooth muscle actin (SMA), PCNA, inflammatory markers (MCP-1, VCAM-1, ICAM-1), Western blot for BHMT, p-NFkB/p65, p-ERK, MMP2/MMP9

## Core Findings

1. **SAH accumulation drives atherosclerosis:** Heterozygous SAHH knockout in ApoE-/- mice caused plasma SAH accumulation, decreased SAM/SAH ratio, and decreased homocysteine levels, promoting atherosclerotic lesion development.

2. **Betaine lowers SAH and increases SAM/SAH ratio:** Betaine supplementation in SAHH-deficient mice lowered plasma SAH levels, increased the SAM/SAH ratio, and increased homocysteine levels (consistent with betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase activity remethylating homocysteine to methionine).

3. **Betaine attenuates atherosclerosis:** Aortic sinus lesion area was significantly reduced in betaine-supplemented SAHH-deficient mice compared to unsupplemented SAHH-deficient mice.

4. **Mechanism via BHMT upregulation:** Betaine increased BHMT protein expression in aortas; BHMT siRNA knockdown abolished betaine's SAH-lowering effect in isolated vascular cells, confirming BHMT mediation.

5. **Anti-inflammatory mechanism:** Betaine reduced expression of NFkB pathway components (p-NFkB/p65) and inflammatory adhesion molecules (MCP-1, VCAM-1, ICAM-1) in atherosclerotic lesions.

6. **Anti-proliferative mechanism:** Betaine reduced smooth muscle cell proliferation (PCNA), migration (wound healing/transwell assays), and MMP2/MMP9 expression, with associated reduction in p-ERK signaling.

## Methodology Notes

- Appropriate use of double-knockout model to simultaneously create atherosclerosis susceptibility (ApoE-/-) and SAH accumulation (SAHH+/-)
- Statistical analysis by one-way ANOVA with post-hoc comparisons
- Both in vivo and in vitro (cell culture with siRNA knockdown) evidence for BHMT-mediated mechanism
- HPLC-MS/MS for SAH/SAM quantification is gold-standard methodology

## Limitations

- **Animal model only** -- findings may not translate directly to humans
- **High dose:** 4 g/100 g diet is a very high pharmacological dose; human equivalent would be substantially different
- **Disease model:** SAHH deficiency is a specific genetic condition; relevance to normal physiology or typical human hyperhomocysteinemia is uncertain
- **Full text not available** -- only abstract and figure legends were captured; methods details, exact quantitative results, and discussion are not available
- Betaine increased homocysteine levels in this model (unexpected; may reflect altered SAHH pathway flux rather than typical betaine-homocysteine remethylation)
