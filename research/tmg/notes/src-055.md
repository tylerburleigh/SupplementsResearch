# Source src-055: Extreme Urinary Betaine Losses in Type 2 Diabetes Combined with Bezafibrate Treatment

## Bibliographic Details

- **Title:** Extreme Urinary Betaine Losses in Type 2 Diabetes Combined with Bezafibrate Treatment are Associated with Losses of Dimethylglycine and Choline but not with Increased Losses of Other Osmolytes
- **Authors:** Lever M, McEntyre CJ, George PM, Slow S, Elmslie JL, Lunt H, Chambers ST, Parry-Strong A, Krebs JD
- **Year:** 2014
- **Venue:** Cardiovascular Drugs and Therapy, 28, 459-468
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s10557-014-6542-9
- **Citation Count:** 16
- **Quality:** Abstract/metadata only (paywall -- full text not available)

## Study Type

Cross-sectional analysis of samples from a previous RCT (the DEWL trial). Case-control design comparing type 2 diabetes patients on bezafibrate (n=32) vs. matched comparators not on fibrates (n=64). Total n=96.

## Key Findings

1. **Massive betaine excretion in diabetes + fibrate:** Median urinary betaine excretion in bezafibrate-treated diabetes patients was 5-fold higher than matched non-fibrate comparators (p < 0.001). The comparator group itself had 3.5-fold higher excretion than reported healthy population medians, indicating diabetes alone substantially increases betaine loss.

2. **Betaine deficiency as cardiovascular risk factor:** The authors frame betaine deficiency as a probable cardiovascular risk factor and a cause of elevated homocysteine, linking it to the homocysteine-methylation pathway.

3. **Dimethylglycine (DMG) excretion elevated:** DMG excretion was 9-fold higher in the bezafibrate group (p < 0.001), consistent with betaine being metabolized to DMG via BHMT (betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase) and then excreted.

4. **Net betaine depletion in some patients:** Some participants excreted more betaine than typical dietary intakes, with fractional clearances exceeding 100%, indicating they were in net negative betaine balance.

5. **Other osmolytes unaffected:** Excretions of choline, myo-inositol, taurine, and glycerophosphorylcholine were not significantly different between groups, suggesting the fibrate effect is specific to betaine/DMG rather than a general osmolyte effect.

6. **Supplementation recommendation:** Authors conclude that concurrent betaine supplementation may be beneficial for diabetes patients on fibrates who are experiencing excessive betaine losses.

## Methodology Notes

- Secondary analysis of stored samples from the DEWL trial (a 2-year RCT of high-protein vs. high-carbohydrate diets in type 2 diabetes)
- Bezafibrate group (n=32) identified from the larger cohort; matched 1:2 with non-fibrate comparators (n=64) by age and gender
- Measured betaine, DMG, choline, and osmolytes (myo-inositol, taurine, glycerophosphorylcholine) in both plasma and urine
- Full methods not available (paywall); abstract provides only high-level description

## Limitations

- **Paywall restriction:** Full text not accessible; analysis based on abstract and metadata only. Cannot verify methods details, statistical approaches, or full results.
- **Small sample size:** n=96 total (32 bezafibrate + 64 comparators)
- **Cross-sectional:** Samples analyzed at a single time point; cannot establish causality or temporal trends
- **Confounding:** Bezafibrate was prescribed clinically, not randomized in this analysis; patients on fibrates likely had different lipid profiles (the indication for fibrate use)
- **Population specificity:** Type 2 diabetes patients; generalizability to healthy populations or other disease states is unclear
- **No supplementation arm:** Study did not test betaine supplementation; the recommendation is based on inference from observed losses

## Relevance to TMG/Betaine Supplementation Research

- **Q1 (dietary intake):** Indirectly relevant -- establishes that some diabetes patients lose more betaine in urine than typical dietary intakes provide, creating a case for supplementation.
- **Q2 (clinical endpoints):** Relevant -- links betaine deficiency to cardiovascular risk and elevated homocysteine, and shows that a common medication (fibrates) can dramatically worsen betaine status in diabetes patients.
- **Q3 (methylation cofactors/MTHFR):** Tangentially relevant -- betaine is a methyl donor via BHMT, and losses could impair methylation; the elevated DMG excretion is consistent with active betaine metabolism. However, no direct methylation or MTHFR data.
- **Q4 (tradeoffs vs alternatives):** Relevant -- suggests fibrates may deplete betaine, which could be a consideration in choosing lipid-lowering strategies or could be addressed by co-supplementation.
- **Q5 (adversarial/contradicting evidence):** Relevant -- identifies a pharmacologically-induced betaine deficiency state, which contradicts any assumption that endogenous betaine levels are always adequate.
- **Q6 (dose-response/safety):** Relevant -- demonstrates that betaine losses can exceed intake, and authors recommend supplementation as potentially beneficial, but no dosing data.
- **Q7 (animal/mechanistic longevity):** Not directly relevant -- human clinical study.

## Claims to Verify

- The 3.5-fold comparison vs. healthy populations relies on external reference data (not directly measured controls); the actual healthy reference was not specified in the abstract.
- The assertion that betaine deficiency is a "probable cardiovascular risk factor" should be cross-referenced with the Lever et al. 2012 PLoS One study cited (ref 1 in the paper).
- The statement that some participants had fractional clearances >100% is striking but the exact number/proportion of such participants cannot be determined from the abstract.
