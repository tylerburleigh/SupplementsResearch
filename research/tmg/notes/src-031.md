## Core Findings (2-3 sentences)

Betaine functions as a methyl donor in the conversion of homocysteine to methionine via betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase (BHMT), providing a folate-independent remethylation pathway. Plasma betaine (~30 micromol/L) is a stronger determinant of post-methionine load (PML) total homocysteine than vitamin B6 or folate, particularly in subjects with low folate status. The review argues that betaine status is an underappreciated component of one-carbon metabolism with implications for cardiovascular disease, cancer, and neural tube defects.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

1. **Plasma betaine levels:** Mean ~30 micromol/L; inter-individual range 9-90 micromol/L (10-fold variation); small intra-individual variability.
2. **Determinants of plasma betaine:** Choline, dimethylglycine, folate in plasma; folic acid intake; gender.
3. **Betaine vs. B-vitamins for PML tHcy:** Betaine is a stronger determinant of post-methionine load tHcy than B6 or folate.
4. **Folate interaction:** Betaine-tHcy relationship is most pronounced in low-folate subjects and attenuated after B-vitamin supplementation.
5. **Pregnancy findings:** Plasma betaine declines until gestational week 20, then plateaus. After week 20, betaine is a stronger predictor of fasting tHcy than folate.
6. **Clinical use:** Betaine has been used for years to lower tHcy in homocystinuria patients.
7. **MTHFR interaction (from abstract of ref 58):** Betaine's effect on fasting homocysteine is modified by MTHFR 677C->T genotype.
