# Source src-285: Encapsulation of curcumin into beta-cyclodextrins inclusion: A review

**Citation:** Guo S (2019). E3S Web of Conferences 131, 01100. DOI: 10.1051/e3sconf/201913101100
**Venue:** E3S Web of Conferences (ChinaBiofilms 2019 conference proceedings)
**Type:** Review article (conference paper)

## Core Findings

Beta-cyclodextrin (beta-CD) and its derivatives (notably hydroxypropyl-beta-CD) can effectively encapsulate curcumin through hydrophobic interaction between curcumin's nonpolar structure and the internal hydrophobic cavity of cyclodextrin. This inclusion complex dramatically improves curcumin's water solubility (up to ~1052-fold with freeze-drying method) and oral bioavailability (3-fold increase with HP-beta-CD), while also enhancing stability against light, heat, and oxygen degradation. Three main carrier formats are reviewed: liquid-type, solid-type, and gel-type.

## 1. Cyclodextrin Encapsulation Approach

- **Mechanism:** Curcumin contains two benzene rings and a carbon chain, giving it low polarity and hydrophobic character. Beta-CD has an external hydrophilic / internal hydrophobic truncated-cone cavity. Curcumin migrates into the cavity via hydrophobic interaction, self-assembling into an inclusion complex (lines 112-119).
- **Beta-CD structure:** Cyclic oligosaccharide of 7 D-glucopyranose units; MW ~1135 Da; cavity height ~7.9 A; cavity diameter ~6.5 A; water solubility 1.85 g/100 mL at room temperature (lines 62-82).
- **Derivatives:** Hydroxyl groups at C2, C3, C6 can be modified to produce esterified or etherified cyclodextrin derivatives (e.g., hydroxypropyl-beta-CD) with improved properties (lines 86-103).
- Beta-CD is GRAS and safe for food use; it has good embedding, digestion, and absorption performance in the body (lines 43-47).

- Curcumin baseline water solubility: ~11 ng/mL (line 30).
- Solid granule carriers show excellent storage stability: 90% retention after 3 months at 25 C (lines 180-183).
- Maximum curcumin load in solid-type beta-CD can reach one-half of total weight (lines 170-171).

## 3. Preparation Methods

### Liquid-type (Section 3.1, lines 126-154)
- Prepare aqueous beta-CD solution, add curcumin (or curcumin in organic solvent) in proportion, mix thoroughly.
- Optimization variables: solvent, temperature, pH, ultrasonic-assisted methods.
- Saturated aqueous solution method is the primary approach.

### Solid-type (Section 3.2, lines 155-183)
- **Route 1:** Direct grinding/milling of beta-CD with curcumin (ball mill or mortar).
- **Route 2:** Form inclusion complex in solution first, then convert to solid via solvent evaporation, low-temperature drying, freeze-drying, or spray drying.

### Gel-type (Section 3.3, lines 184-228)
- Beta-CD dispersed in solvent, heated; dimers form head-on/tail-to-tail, assembling into fiber clusters and 3D network.
- Two subtypes: hydrogels (aqueous) and organogels.
- Hydrogel example: beta-CD-curcumin nanoparticles by saturated solution method, freeze-dried, mixed into acrylic polymer solution, gelled by pH adjustment.
- Chitosan-based thermosensitive hydrosol also described (beta-CD-curcumin solid particles blended with chitosan solution, cooled).
- Gel-type carriers offer good permeability and slow-release in gastrointestinal digestion.

## 4. Relevance to Q1 (Bioavailability Strategies)

This source is **directly relevant** to Q1 on bioavailability enhancement strategies for curcumin. It provides:

- Specific quantitative data on solubility improvements (100x to 1052x depending on method).
- Quantitative bioavailability improvement (3-fold with HP-beta-CD oral delivery).
- Multiple preparation methods applicable to food and pharmaceutical industries.
- A clear mechanistic explanation (hydrophobic interaction-driven self-assembly).
- Evidence that beta-CD is food-safe and already used in food industry applications.

## Limitations

- **Conference proceeding, not a high-impact journal.** This is a relatively short review presented at ChinaBiofilms 2019. It cites other studies for all quantitative claims but provides limited methodological detail of its own.
- **No original experimental data.** This is purely a review; all quantitative results are attributed to cited references.
- **Narrow focus.** Only covers beta-cyclodextrin approaches; does not compare with liposomal, nanoparticle, emulsion, or other delivery strategies.
- **Some text quality issues.** The PDF-to-text conversion shows scattered formatting artifacts and the English writing quality is uneven, suggesting possible translation issues.
- **Gel-type section is descriptive without quantitative performance data.** The gel-type carriers are discussed as promising but lack the specific solubility/bioavailability figures provided for liquid and solid types.

## Claims to Verify

1. **Solubility of 11 ng/mL for curcumin in water (line 30):** This is a commonly cited figure but some sources report higher values (~600 ng/mL). Worth cross-checking with the original reference [4] (Yu et al., 2012).
2. **1052-fold solubility increase via freeze-drying (line 179):** Sourced from reference [19] (Ghanghoria et al., 2013). This is a large effect size; verify the original paper measured comparable conditions.
3. **3-fold bioavailability increase with HP-beta-CD (line 153-154):** Sourced from reference [15] (Li et al., 2018). Verify this was an in vivo study and the exact measure used (AUC, Cmax, etc.).
