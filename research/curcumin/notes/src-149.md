# src-149: Nano-curcumin for Cardiometabolic Risk in Coronary Slow Flow Phenomenon

**Citation:** Soltani M, Hosseinzadeh-Attar MJ, Rezaei M, et al. Effect of nano-curcumin supplementation on cardiometabolic risk factors, physical and psychological quality of life, and depression in patients with coronary slow flow phenomenon: a randomized double-blind clinical trial. *Trials*. 2024;25:515. doi:10.1186/s13063-024-08354-9

**Quality:** ok (full open-access text available, peer-reviewed, 16 citations)

---

## Core Findings

In a 12-week randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial, 80 mg/day nano-curcumin supplementation in patients with coronary slow flow phenomenon (CSFP) prevented deterioration of glycemic control (FBG and HbA1c remained stable vs. significant increases in placebo) and improved both physical and psychological quality of life and depression severity. However, inflammatory biomarkers visfatin and hs-CRP were unchanged.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group clinical trial
- **Registration:** IRCT20131125015536N8 (Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials), registered June 19, 2019
- **Population:** 50 patients with angiographically confirmed CSFP (corrected TIMI frame count >27, coronary stenosis <40%, LVEF >=45%), aged 35-70, BMI 25-40 kg/m2
- **Randomization:** Stratified permuted block randomization (block sizes 2 or 4), stratified by sex and disease risk level; performed by statistician not involved in recruitment
- **Intervention:** 80 mg/day nano-curcumin (Exir Nano Sina Co., Iran); curcuminoid mixture with 79.4% curcumin, 17.6% DMC, 3% BDMC, purity >=95%; in polysorbate nanomicelle formulation with vitamin E, vitamin C, natural oil
- **Placebo:** Matching capsules with polysorbate 80, soy oil, sorbitol 70, methylparaben, propylparaben, purified water
- **Duration:** 12 weeks, with visits at baseline, week 6, and week 12; compliance assessed by capsule count (>90% threshold)
- **Blinding:** Patients and assessors blinded; containers identical in appearance, coded A/B by uninvolved colleague

## Primary Outcomes and Results

### Inflammatory Biomarkers (null results)
- **Visfatin:** No significant change (intervention: -0.4 +/- 3.6 ng/ml; placebo: -0.1 +/- 3.3 ng/ml; p = 0.91)
- **hs-CRP:** No significant change (intervention: -0.1 +/- 0.4 mg/l; placebo: 0.0 +/- 0.4 mg/l; p = 0.87)
- Note: Baseline hs-CRP was ~1 mg/l in both groups, considered low inflammatory status

### Glycemic Indices (positive)
- **FBG:** Intervention group stable (-0.9 +/- 12.2 mg/dl) vs. placebo group increased (7.7 +/- 12.4 mg/dl); p = 0.02 between groups; within-group increase significant in placebo (p = 0.01) but not intervention (p = 0.49)
- **HbA1c:** Intervention group stable (-0.1 +/- 0.8%) vs. placebo group increased (0.5 +/- 0.8%); p = 0.04 between groups; within-group increase significant in placebo (p = 0.008) but not intervention (p = 0.74)
- **Insulin and HOMA-IR:** No significant between-group differences (p = 0.10 and p = 0.21, respectively)

### Quality of Life (SF-36) (positive)
- **Physical component summary:** Intervention +8.2 +/- 8.1 vs. placebo -1.2 +/- 6.5 (p < 0.001)
- **Mental component summary:** Intervention +6.8 +/- 11.8 vs. placebo -1.1 +/- 10.4 (p = 0.02)
- Significant improvements also in: physical functioning (p < 0.001), role physical (p = 0.04), bodily pain (p < 0.001), social functioning (p = 0.01)
- Mental health score significantly higher at endpoint in intervention (p = 0.04 by ANCOVA)

### Depression (BDI-II) (positive)
- Distribution of depression severity categories shifted favorably in intervention group (p = 0.046)
- Minimal depression category: intervention increased from 57.1% to 76.2%; placebo decreased from 61.9% to 38.1%

## Cardiometabolic Biomarker Changes

No significant between-group differences were observed for:
- Systolic blood pressure (change: 3.2 +/- 15.7 vs. 4.1 +/- 14.0 mmHg, p = 0.84)
- Diastolic blood pressure (change: 2.8 +/- 7.2 vs. 3.2 +/- 10.5 mmHg, p = 0.88)
- Weight, BMI, or waist circumference

Dietary intakes (energy, carbohydrate, protein, fat, fiber) were not significantly different between groups at baseline or week 12.

## Sample Size and Attrition

- **Randomized:** 50 (25 per arm)
- **Analyzed:** 42 (21 per arm)
- **Dropouts:** 4 per group (16% total), primarily due to COVID-19 or other medical conditions
- **Sample size calculation:** Based on hs-CRP (primary endpoint), detecting 0.46 difference with 80% power, alpha 0.05, 20% dropout rate

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size** (n = 42 analyzed) -- limited power to detect modest effects and evaluate covariates
2. **Dose may be suboptimal** -- 80 mg/day was chosen as a starting dose; may be insufficient for inflammatory biomarker changes
3. **No follow-up angiography** -- invasive procedure not repeated at endpoint for ethical reasons; clinical improvement in coronary flow could not be confirmed
4. **Low baseline inflammation** -- mean hs-CRP ~1 mg/l offered limited room for improvement
5. **No active comparator** -- only placebo-controlled
6. **Short duration** -- 12 weeks may be insufficient for structural/angiographic changes

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: Nano-curcumin efficacy
Directly relevant. Provides RCT evidence for nano-curcumin (nanomicelle formulation, 80 mg/day) in a cardiovascular population. Shows glycemic stabilization (prevention of deterioration) and QOL/depression improvements, but null results for inflammatory markers visfatin and hs-CRP. Supports the bioavailability advantage of nano-formulations at low doses (80 mg vs. typical 500-1000 mg of standard curcumin).

### Q3: Clinical trials of curcumin
Highly relevant. A well-designed (randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled) clinical trial in a specific cardiovascular population (CSFP). Adds to the evidence base for curcumin in cardiometabolic health. Demonstrates that even low-dose nano-curcumin can influence patient-reported outcomes and glycemic trends, though inflammatory biomarker effects may require higher doses or different populations.
