## Core Findings

This is an umbrella meta-analysis (meta-analysis of meta-analyses) pooling results from 11 previously published meta-analyses of curcumin RCTs for osteoarthritis. It found that curcumin supplementation significantly improved all measured OA outcomes: VAS pain scores, WOMAC-total, WOMAC-Function, WOMAC-Pain, and WOMAC-Stiffness (all p <= 0.007). The authors conclude results "strongly support curcuminoid supplementation in relieving pain, improving joint mobility and stiffness, and shortening medication usage of OA patients."

## Methodology

- **Design:** Umbrella meta-analysis (second-order meta-analysis synthesizing existing meta-analyses)
- **Included studies:** 11 meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials
- **Databases searched:** PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science
- **Search period:** Up to September 2023
- **Language:** English-language meta-analyses only
- **Statistical model:** Random-effects model
- **Heterogeneity assessment:** I-squared statistics and Cochrane Q-test
- **Effect sizes:** Both WMD (weighted mean difference) and SMD (standardized mean difference) reported

This source is **highly relevant** to Q3 as it provides a top-level synthesis of the entire meta-analytic evidence base for curcumin in osteoarthritis. It confirms that across 11 separate meta-analyses, curcumin consistently shows significant benefits for pain (VAS), overall OA symptoms (WOMAC-total), function, and stiffness. The umbrella design provides stronger evidence than any single meta-analysis. However, without full-text access, the exact effect sizes needed for precise quantitative comparison are unavailable.

## Limitations

1. **Abstract-only content:** The PubMed page provides the abstract but not full text. Exact SMD/WMD values, confidence intervals, I-squared values, and detailed subgroup analyses are not accessible.
2. **No participant-level data:** As an umbrella review, it synthesizes aggregate data from meta-analyses, not individual patient data.
3. **English-language restriction:** Only English-language meta-analyses were included.
4. **Potential overlap:** The 11 included meta-analyses likely share underlying RCTs, introducing correlated evidence rather than independent replication.

## Claims to Verify
