# src-205: Curcumin-QingDai Combination for Active Ulcerative Colitis

**Citation:** Ben-Horin et al., *Clinical Gastroenterology and Hepatology*, 2024. DOI: 10.1016/j.cgh.2023.05.023
**Citations:** 59 | **Quality:** ok | **Content:** Abstract only (PubMed record)

## Core Findings

In a two-part RCT (Part I open-label, Part II placebo-controlled), enteric-coated curcumin-QingDai (CurQD) 3 g/d for 8 weeks significantly outperformed placebo for induction of clinical response (85.7% vs 30.7%, P < .001) and remission (50% vs 8%, P = .01) in active UC. The combination uniquely activated the aryl-hydrocarbon receptor pathway (CYP1A1 up-regulation), suggesting a mechanistic basis beyond anti-inflammatory effects.

## Study Design

- **Part I (Open-label lead-in):** 10 patients with active UC (SCCAI >= 5, Mayo endoscopic subscore >= 2) received CurQD. 7/10 responded, 3/10 achieved remission.
- **Part II (Placebo-controlled RCT):** 42 patients randomized 2:1 to enteric-coated CurQD 3 g/d (n=28) or placebo (n=13) for 8 weeks, conducted at sites in Israel and Greece. Double-blinded.
- **Maintenance phase:** Responding patients continued curcumin or placebo alone for an additional 8 weeks (total 16 weeks).
- **Co-primary outcome:** Clinical response (SCCAI reduction >= 3) AND objective response (Mayo endoscopic subscore improvement >= 1 OR 50% fecal calprotectin reduction).
- **Registration:** NCT03720002.

## Key Outcomes (Part II, Week 8)

| Outcome | CurQD (n=28) | Placebo (n=13) | P-value |
|---------|-------------|----------------|---------|
| Co-primary outcome | 43% | 8% | .033 |
| Clinical response | 85.7% | 30.7% | <.001 |
| Clinical remission | 50% (14/28) | 8% (1/13) | .01 |
| Endoscopic improvement | 75% | 20% | .036 |
| Calprotectin >=50% reduction | 46.4% | 15.4% | .08 |

## Maintenance Phase (Week 16)

Among CurQD responders maintained on curcumin alone for 8 additional weeks:
- Clinical response maintained: 93%
- Clinical remission maintained: 80%
- Clinical biomarker response: 40%

## Mechanistic Finding

CurQD up-regulated mucosal CYP1A1 expression (a marker of aryl-hydrocarbon receptor activation). This effect was not observed with placebo, mesalamine, or biologics, suggesting a novel therapeutic pathway.

## Safety

Adverse events were comparable between CurQD and placebo groups. No specific serious adverse events detailed in the abstract.

## Limitations

- **Small sample size** (n=42 total in RCT phase; only 13 in placebo arm) limits generalizability and statistical power, particularly for secondary endpoints.
- **Imbalanced randomization** (2:1) resulted in a small placebo group, making safety comparisons less robust.
- **Abstract-only content available** -- full methods, detailed safety data, and subgroup analyses could not be verified.
- The calprotectin reduction did not reach statistical significance (P = .08).
- The combination therapy (curcumin + QingDai, a Chinese indigo plant extract) makes it impossible to attribute effects to curcumin alone.

## Claims to Verify

- **Clinical response 85.7% (24/28):** The abstract states 85.7% but does not give the exact numerator; 85.7% of 28 = 24, which is consistent.
- **Remission 50% (14/28) vs 8% (1/13):** These numbers are internally consistent.
- **Endoscopic improvement 75% vs 20%:** Exact numerators not given. 75% of 28 = 21; 20% of 13 = 2.6 (likely 2 or 3). Cannot fully verify without full text.
- **Co-primary outcome 43% vs 8%:** 43% of 28 = ~12; 8% of 13 = ~1. These are plausible but should be confirmed against full text.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q3 (Clinical trials of curcumin for ulcerative colitis):** Highly relevant. This is a placebo-controlled RCT specifically testing a curcumin-containing regimen in active UC. However, the intervention is a curcumin-QingDai combination, not curcumin monotherapy, which limits direct attribution of effects to curcumin alone. The strong clinical and endoscopic outcomes, mechanistic data on AhR pathway, and durable maintenance response make this one of the more compelling curcumin-related UC trials, but the confound of QingDai must be noted.

**Strengths:** Multi-center (Israel + Greece), double-blind, placebo-controlled, pre-registered (NCT03720002), published in high-impact GI journal (CGH), 59 citations suggest strong uptake by the field. Accompanied by an editorial comment ("Is it Time for the Alternative to Move to the Mainstream?").

**Key caveat:** QingDai (indigo naturalis) has its own anti-inflammatory properties and has been independently studied for UC. The observed effects cannot be attributed solely to curcumin.
