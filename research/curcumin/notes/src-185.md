# src-185: Curcumin Supplementation in Metabolic Syndrome - Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

**Citation:** Qiu L, Gao C, Wang H, et al. (2023). Frontiers in Endocrinology, 14:1216708. doi: 10.3389/fendo.2023.1216708
**Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs
**PROSPERO:** CRD42022362553
**Citations:** 59
**Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

Curcumin supplementation significantly improved multiple metabolic syndrome markers (waist circumference, fasting blood sugar, diastolic blood pressure, HDL cholesterol), inflammatory markers (TNF-alpha, CRP), and the oxidative stress marker MDA in a meta-analysis of 13 RCTs with 785 participants. However, no significant effects were found for SBP, TG, IL-6, or hsCRP, and overall evidence quality was rated LOW to VERY LOW by GRADE assessment.

## Study Characteristics

- **Included studies:** 13 parallel RCTs (published 2014-2022)
- **Total participants:** 785 (373 male, 412 female)
- **Intervention duration:** 4-12 weeks
- **Geographic distribution:** Iran (10), Pakistan (1), China (1), Italy (1)
- **Curcumin doses ranged:** 80 mg/day (nano-curcumin) to 2,400 mg/day (turmeric powder)
- **Diagnostic criteria:** NCEP-ATP III or IDF definitions of MetS

## Risk of Bias Assessment

- **Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool** used
- Only 3/13 RCTs rated as low risk of bias
- 10/13 RCTs had uncertain risk of bias due to insufficient reporting
- 10 RCTs had insufficient information on random sequence generation
- 10 RCTs had imperfect allocation concealment information
- 3 RCTs had unclear blinding design/implementation

## Adverse Events

- 3 of 13 studies reported mild gastrointestinal effects (diarrhea, nausea, constipation)
- Attributed to high daily curcumin doses
- No serious adverse events reported

## Key Limitations

1. Geographic concentration: 12/13 studies from Asian countries (10 from Iran)
2. Very few studies for inflammatory/oxidative markers (only 2-3 per marker)
3. Sex-specific cohorts introduce bias (2 male-only, 2 female-only studies)
4. Bioavailability enhancers (piperine, alkaloids) not excluded
5. Unable to perform dosage subgroup analyses due to limited data
6. One study used curcumin combined with black seed extract (Nigella sativa), potentially confounding results

## Subgroup Findings

- HDL-C improvement was significant only in trials with duration <= 6 weeks
- WC improvement was significant only in trials with duration <= 6 weeks or sample size >= 60
- Sensitivity analysis: Osali (2020) study was a major contributor to heterogeneity in FBS and SBP outcomes; removal resolved heterogeneity

## Relevance to Q3 (Clinical Trials)

This meta-analysis is directly relevant to Q3 on clinical trials of curcumin. It synthesizes evidence from 13 RCTs examining curcumin's effects on comprehensive metabolic, inflammatory, and oxidative stress markers specifically in metabolic syndrome patients. The significant effects on WC, FBS, DBP, HDL-C, TNF-alpha, CRP, and MDA provide quantitative effect sizes for clinical trial outcomes. However, the LOW/VERY LOW GRADE ratings and geographic concentration limit the strength of conclusions.

## Claims to Verify

- The participant count of 785 is stated in multiple places and is consistent with the individual study sample sizes listed in Table 2 (though exact verification requires summing all groups).
- The male/female ratio (373/412) is stated once; this should be cross-checked against Table 2 entries.
- Only 2 studies contributed data for TNF-alpha, IL-6, and CRP analyses, and only 3 for MDA -- these very small evidence bases mean individual study results heavily influence pooled estimates.
