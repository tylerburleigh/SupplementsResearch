# src-189: Effects of Curcumin on Weight Loss in Metabolic Syndrome (Meta-Analysis)

**Citation:** Akbari et al. (2019). Frontiers in Pharmacology, 10:649. doi:10.3389/fphar.2019.00649
**Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs
**Citations:** 88 | **Quality:** ok | **Year:** 2019

## Core Findings

This is the first meta-analysis specifically evaluating curcumin's effect on weight-related outcomes in patients with metabolic syndrome and related disorders. Pooling 18 articles (21 RCTs, ~1,604-1,646 participants), curcumin supplementation significantly reduced BMI, body weight, waist circumference, and leptin, while increasing adiponectin. Hip ratio was not significantly affected. All effect sizes are standardized mean differences (SMDs), meaning they represent relative rather than absolute reductions.

## Effect Sizes for Weight-Related Outcomes

| Outcome | SMD | 95% CI | P-value | N studies | I2 (%) |
|---------|-----|--------|---------|-----------|--------|
| BMI | -0.37 | -0.61, -0.13 | <0.01 | 14 | 69.7 |
| Body weight | -0.23 | -0.39, -0.06 | <0.01 | 15 | 45.7 |
| Waist circumference | -0.25 | -0.44, -0.05 | 0.01 | 9 | 42.1 |
| Hip ratio | -0.17 | -0.42, 0.08 | 0.18 | 5 | 0.0 |
| Leptin | -0.97 | -1.18, -0.75 | <0.001 | 4 | 0.0 |
| Adiponectin | 1.05 | 0.23, 1.87 | 0.01 | 5 | 94.5 |

**Note:** SMDs are unitless; an SMD of ~0.2-0.3 is generally considered a small-to-moderate effect. The leptin and adiponectin effects are large but based on few studies.

## Study Design and Methods

- **Databases searched:** MEDLINE, EMBASE, Web of Science, Cochrane Central Register (up to January 2018)
- **Eligibility:** Human RCTs, curcumin intervention vs. placebo, reporting BMI/weight/WC/HR/leptin/adiponectin with SD
- **Statistical approach:** Random-effects model (inverse variance method), Cohen's SMD, RevMan 5.3 and Stata 12.0
- **Heterogeneity assessment:** Cochran's Q and I-squared
- **Publication bias:** Egger's regression test; Duval and Tweedie trim-and-fill for HR
- **Subgroup analyses:** By dose (<=500 vs >500 mg/day) and duration (<=8 vs >8 weeks)
- **Sensitivity analysis:** One-by-one exclusion of studies

## Population Details

- **Conditions:** MetS, T2DM, NAFLD, obesity, hyperlipidemia
- **Dose range:** 70 to 2,400 mg/day curcumin/curcuminoids
- **Duration range:** 4 to 36 weeks
- **Geographic spread:** Primarily Iran, plus Thailand, Egypt, Italy, Germany, Pakistan, Taiwan, USA
- **Study designs:** 14 parallel, 4 crossover; 15 double-blind, 3 single-blind
- **Individual study sizes:** 25 to 234 participants

## Subgroup Findings

- **BMI:** Significant for >500 mg/day (SMD -0.33) and <=8 week studies (SMD -0.42), but not for <=500 mg/day or >8 week durations. Heterogeneity remained high across subgroups.
- **Body weight:** Significant for >500 mg/day (SMD -0.19) and <=8 weeks (SMD -0.25); low dose subgroup borderline (SMD -0.43, CI crosses 0 at lower bound -0.85).
- **Waist circumference:** Significant only for >500 mg/day (SMD -0.31); not significant at lower doses.

## Key Limitations

1. **Low bioavailability formulations:** Most studies used unformulated curcumin with poor absorption and rapid metabolism/elimination.
2. **Methodological quality:** Included RCTs rated as low to moderate quality.
3. **Substantial heterogeneity:** For BMI, I2=69.7%; for adiponectin, I2=94.5%. Different methodologies, dosages, durations, and populations contributed.
4. **Small number of studies for some outcomes:** Only 4 studies for leptin, 5 for adiponectin and HR.
5. **Fragile WC finding:** Sensitivity analysis showed WC became non-significant (SMD -0.20; CI -0.42, 0.00) after omitting one study (Panahi et al., 2017b).
6. **Publication bias detected for HR** (Egger's P<0.01); trim-and-fill changed HR from non-significant to significant.

## Claims to Verify

- **Participant count discrepancy:** The abstract states "1,604 individuals" (line 66) while the Results section states "1,646 participants" (line 324). The difference of ~42 may reflect counting of crossover arms vs. unique individuals. Both numbers appear in the source without explicit reconciliation.
- **Dose range:** Table 1 shows studies ranging from 70 mg/day (not explicitly listed but referenced in discussion for Rahmani 2016 at 500 mg/day) up to 2,400 mg/day (Amin 2015). The 70 mg/day figure appears in the discussion referencing Rahmani et al. 2016 at 500 mg/day; the actual lowest dose may be the 80 mg/day nano-curcumin from Rahimi et al. 2016 or ~112 mg/day from Nieman et al. 2012.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q3 (clinical trials):** Highly relevant. This is a comprehensive meta-analysis of 21 RCTs directly assessing curcumin's effects on weight-related outcomes in metabolic syndrome populations. Provides pooled effect sizes with CIs for BMI, weight, WC, leptin, and adiponectin. Key for establishing the magnitude and consistency of curcumin's anti-obesity effects in clinical settings. The subgroup analyses by dose and duration are valuable for identifying optimal dosing regimens.

## Mechanisms Discussed

The paper reviews several proposed mechanisms for curcumin's weight-loss effects:
- Suppression of adipogenesis via inhibition of key transcriptional proteins
- Downregulation of preadipocyte differentiation and upregulation of adipocyte apoptosis
- Activation of AMPK/SIRT1 pathway improving insulin sensitivity
- Inhibition of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, PAI-1, MCP-1)
- Downregulation of JAK enzyme involved in obesity pathogenesis
- Suppression of 11beta-HSD1 enzyme (reduces cortisol activation in adipocytes)
- Inhibition of PPAR-gamma reducing adipocyte differentiation
- Modulation of ERalpha/ERbeta ratio decreasing leptin and leptin receptor expression
