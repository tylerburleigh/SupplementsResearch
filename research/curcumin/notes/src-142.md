# src-142: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Curcuminoids for Knee Osteoarthritis

**Source type:** Systematic review with meta-analysis (PubMed abstract)
**PMID:** 28470851
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28470851
**Content quality:** Abstract only (no full text). Structured abstract with quantitative results.
**Published:** 2017, Asia Pacific League of Associations for Rheumatology / Wiley

---

## Core Findings

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (797 participants) found that curcuminoids provided statistically significant improvements in knee pain and quality of life vs placebo in knee OA patients, but were **inferior to ibuprofen** on pain relief, knee stiffness, and physical function. No serious adverse events were reported. However, all studies were conducted in Asia with small sample sizes and moderate overall risk of bias, limiting generalizability.

## 1. Study Design and Risk of Bias

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis; searched Medline, Embase, AMED, Cinahl, Cochrane Library.
- **Inclusion criteria:** RCTs of orally-administered curcuminoids for OA in adults.
- **Analysis:** Random-effects model for meta-analysis.
- **Risk of bias:** Assessed via Cochrane risk of bias criteria. Overall risk of bias rated **moderate**.
- **Limitations:** Small sample sizes across studies, variable reporting quality, all studies conducted in Asia (geographic restriction).

## 2. Effect Sizes vs Placebo

### VAS Knee Pain
- **SMD: -3.45** (95% CI: -5.52 to -1.38)
- I-squared = 95% (very high heterogeneity)
- P = 0.001
- **Note:** The SMD of -3.45 is unusually large for a standardized mean difference. This could reflect the original studies reporting on different VAS scales, or there may be a unit issue in the abstract (the value may represent a raw mean difference on a 0-10 or 0-100 scale rather than a true standardized mean difference).

### Lequesne Pain-Function Index (Quality of Life)
- **MD: -2.69** (95% CI: -3.48 to -1.90)
- I-squared = 0% (no heterogeneity)
- P < 0.00001
- This is a strong, consistent finding across studies.

### WOMAC Total Score
- Significant improvements reported (exact effect size not provided in abstract).
- Significant reduction in rescue medication use also observed.

## 3. Comparison with Ibuprofen

- Curcuminoids had **significantly fewer effects** on pain relief, knee stiffness, and physical function compared with ibuprofen.
- This means curcuminoids were **less effective** than ibuprofen across these outcomes.
- Exact effect sizes for the ibuprofen comparison are not available from the abstract.

## 4. Safety and Adverse Events

- **No serious adverse events** were reported across all 7 included RCTs.
- Curcuminoids appear safe in the short-term.
- The review notes possible reduction in rescue medication need, which indirectly supports a tolerability advantage.

## 5. Relevance to Q3 (Strongest Clinical Trials)

This source is **highly relevant** to Q3 as a meta-analytic synthesis of clinical trial evidence for curcumin in knee OA. Key contributions:

- Provides pooled effect estimates across 7 RCTs, offering more robust evidence than any single trial.
- Demonstrates statistically significant benefit over placebo for pain and function.
- Critically, shows curcuminoids are **inferior to ibuprofen**, which tempers enthusiasm about curcumin as a standalone pain treatment.
- Highlights that the evidence base is limited by Asian-only studies, small samples, and moderate risk of bias.

## Claims to Verify

1. **SMD of -3.45 for VAS pain:** This is an extremely large standardized mean difference (Cohen's convention: 0.2 = small, 0.8 = large). Values >2 are rare in clinical research. This may be a raw mean difference mislabeled as SMD, or the VAS scales in included studies may have varied widely (e.g., 0-10 vs 0-100). The full text should be consulted to clarify the metric.
2. **797 participants across 7 studies:** Average ~114 per study, which is plausible but should be verified from the full text.
3. **I-squared = 95% for VAS pain:** This extreme heterogeneity suggests the pooled estimate may be unreliable. The direction of effect is likely robust but the magnitude should be interpreted very cautiously.
