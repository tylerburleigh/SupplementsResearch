# src-155: Add-on Curcumin for MDD in Thai Patients (Kanchanatawan et al., 2018)

**Citation:** Kanchanatawan B, Tangwongchai S, Sughondhabhirom A, et al. "Add-on Treatment with Curcumin Has Antidepressive Effects in Thai Patients with Major Depression: Results of a Randomized Double-Blind Placebo-Controlled Study." *Neurotoxicity Research* 33, 621-633 (2018). DOI: 10.1007/s12640-017-9860-4

**Venue:** Neurotoxicity Research | **Year:** 2018 | **Citations:** 60 | **Quality:** ok (abstract only; full text behind paywall)

---

## Core Findings

Adjunctive curcumin (500-1500 mg/day escalating dose) showed significant antidepressant effects compared to placebo in a 12-week RCT of 65 Thai MDD patients, with significant MADRS improvement emerging at weeks 12 and 16. The effect was more pronounced in males. Curcumin was safe and well-tolerated with no significant adverse effects, blood chemistry changes, or ECG abnormalities.

**Content note:** Only the abstract is available; full-text methods, statistics, and detailed results are behind a paywall. Quantitative effect sizes, p-values, and exact group sizes are not accessible.

## 1. Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **Population:** 65 participants diagnosed with MDD (Thai patients), confirmed by M.I.N.I. Thai version
- **Intervention:** Adjunctive curcumin added to existing antidepressant treatment
  - Dose escalation: 500 mg/day increasing to 1500 mg/day
  - Duration: 12 weeks of active treatment
- **Control:** Placebo (also adjunctive to antidepressants)
- **Follow-up:** Week 16 (4 weeks post-treatment cessation)
- **Rationale framing:** Based on immune-inflammatory and oxidative-nitrosative (IO&NS) stress pathways in MDD; curcumin has strong anti-IO&NS properties

## 2. Primary Outcomes

### MADRS (primary outcome)
- Rated at baseline and weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, and 16
- **No significant difference at early timepoints** (weeks 2, 4, 8)
- **Significant improvement with curcumin vs. placebo at weeks 12 and 16**
- The delayed onset of significant separation (not until week 12) is clinically notable and consistent with anti-inflammatory mechanisms requiring longer treatment durations
- Benefit persisted at week 16 (4 weeks after treatment ended), suggesting sustained effects

### HAM-A (secondary outcome)
- Rated at same timepoints as MADRS
- Abstract does not report whether HAM-A improvements reached statistical significance
- Full text needed to determine anxiety-specific effects

## 3. Sex Differences

- Effects of curcumin were **more pronounced in males than females**
- Abstract does not provide quantitative interaction statistics
- Authors recommend future trials specifically examine treatment-by-sex interactions for immune-modifying compounds

## 4. Safety Data

- **No statistically significant treatment-emergent adverse effects**
- **No significant effects on blood chemistry**
- **No significant effects on ECG measurements**
- Curcumin was described as safe and well-tolerated even when combined with antidepressants

## 5. Relevance to Q3 (Clinical Trials)

This study is directly relevant to Q3 on clinical trials of curcumin for depression:

- **Key contribution:** One of the few curcumin trials with a 12-week treatment period (most prior trials were 4-8 weeks), demonstrating that significant antidepressant effects may require longer treatment durations
- **Dose:** Higher dose range (up to 1500 mg/day) than many prior studies (typically 500-1000 mg/day)
- **Adjunctive design:** Tests curcumin as add-on to standard antidepressants, which reflects real-world clinical usage
- **Delayed onset of effect:** The finding that significance only emerged at week 12 has important implications for trial design -- shorter trials may produce false negatives
- **Sex-specific response:** The male-female difference is a novel finding that could explain heterogeneity across prior trials
- **Sustained benefit:** Effects persisting 4 weeks post-treatment suggest disease-modifying rather than purely symptomatic effects
- **Limitation:** Abstract-only access prevents extraction of effect sizes, p-values, exact per-group sample sizes, and detailed safety data

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample size (N=65):** Cannot verify from abstract whether this was the randomized sample or the completer sample. Dropout rates are unknown.
- **Dose escalation details:** Abstract states 500-1500 mg/day but the escalation schedule (timing, increments) is not specified without full text.
- **HAM-A results:** The abstract omits any mention of whether the secondary anxiety outcome was significant -- this may indicate a null finding that was not highlighted.
- **Sex difference statistical test:** The abstract states effects were "more pronounced in males" but does not confirm this was a formal treatment-by-sex interaction test vs. a post-hoc observation.
- **Effect sizes and p-values:** None reported in the abstract; critical for meta-analysis and cross-study comparison.
