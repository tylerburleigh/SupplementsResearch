# src-058: Phase I PK Study of Nanoparticle Curcumin (THERACURMIN)

**Source:** PubMed PMID 21603867 (abstract only; full text behind paywall)
**Quality note:** Metadata fields (title, authors, year, venue) are empty. Content is the PubMed abstract with substantive PK data. Publication type: Controlled Clinical Trial, Research Support Non-U.S. Gov't. Referenced by a comment by Gescher AJ in Cancer Chemother Pharmacol 2012.

## Core Findings

THERACURMIN, a nanoparticle curcumin formulation with increased water solubility, achieved substantially higher plasma curcumin levels than previously reported for standard curcumin preparations at much lower doses. The formulation demonstrated linear, dose-dependent pharmacokinetics across the 150-210 mg dose range with a favorable safety profile in healthy volunteers.

## Study Design and Sample Size

- **Design:** Open-label, sequential crossover Phase I study
- **N = 6** healthy volunteers
- **Doses:** 150 mg oral THERACURMIN, followed after 2-week washout by 210 mg
- **Sampling:** Plasma curcumin measured at 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, and 24 hours post-dose via HPLC
- **Population:** Healthy adults (MeSH: Adult, Middle Aged, Male, Female)

## Key Pharmacokinetic Parameters (mean +/- SEM)

| Parameter | 150 mg dose | 210 mg dose |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Cmax (ng/ml) | 189 +/- 48 | 275 +/- 67 |
| AUC(0-24h) (ng/ml x h) | 2,649 +/- 350 | 3,649 +/- 430 |
| t1/2 (h) | 9.7 +/- 2.1 | 13.0 +/- 3.3 |

**Dose-response observation:** Increasing dose from 150 to 210 mg (1.4x) increased Cmax by ~1.45x and AUC by ~1.38x, consistent with linear (non-saturable) pharmacokinetics in this range. The half-life also appeared to increase with dose, though the wide SEMs make this difficult to interpret conclusively.

## Safety Findings

- 1 of 6 subjects reported **grade 1 diarrhea** after 150 mg dose
- No other toxicities observed at either dose
- No adverse events reported at the 210 mg dose
- The authors conclude THERACURMIN can safely increase plasma curcumin levels at least up to 210 mg

## Relevance

### Q1 (Bioavailability Enhancement Strategies)

**Directly relevant.** This is a primary source demonstrating that nanoparticle formulation (THERACURMIN) meaningfully improves curcumin bioavailability in humans. At 150 mg, Cmax reached 189 ng/ml -- a level the background section notes is difficult to achieve even with gram doses of standard curcumin. This provides quantitative evidence that nanoparticle-based solubility enhancement is an effective bioavailability strategy. The study claims THERACURMIN is the first nanoparticle curcumin formulation demonstrating improved bioavailability in human subjects.

### Q3 (Clinical Trials)

**Directly relevant.** This is a Phase I clinical trial (controlled clinical trial designation) providing foundational safety and PK data for nanoparticle curcumin. It establishes dosing parameters and demonstrates a favorable safety profile that supports proceeding to efficacy trials. The authors explicitly state the compound could be a promising tool for testing anticancer effects in clinical trials.

## Limitations

1. **Very small sample size (n=6)** -- limits generalizability and statistical power
2. **Abstract only available** -- full methods, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and detailed results are behind a paywall
3. **No comparator arm** -- no direct comparison with standard curcumin in the same subjects; the bioavailability improvement claim relies on comparison with published literature values
4. **Open-label, unblinded design** -- potential for bias in safety assessments
5. **Single-dose study** -- no data on steady-state pharmacokinetics or accumulation with repeated dosing
6. **Healthy volunteers only** -- PK may differ in patients with cancer or other conditions
7. **Metadata incomplete** -- title, authors, year, and venue not captured in source metadata

## Claims to Verify

- The "first nanoparticle formulation of curcumin that demonstrates improved bioavailability in human subjects" claim (line 29) should be verified against the chronology of other nanoparticle curcumin publications circa 2011.
- The AUC values (2,649 and 3,649 ng/ml x h) are reported with only 2 significant figures for SEM, suggesting modest precision. The abstract does not specify whether AUC was calculated via trapezoidal rule or another method.
- The apparent increase in t1/2 with dose (9.7 vs 13.0 h) is noted but not discussed; with n=6 and large SEMs, this may not be statistically meaningful.
