# src-145: Curcumin for Depression in Obese Type 2 Diabetes Patients

**Source:** PubMed 39125295 (MDPI journal, likely Nutrients or similar open-access)
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39125295
**Quality flag:** Degraded -- only PubMed landing page captured, not full text. Abstract is complete and substantive.

## Core Findings

In a 12-month randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 227 obese type 2 diabetes patients, curcumin supplementation significantly improved depression severity on PHQ-9 (p = 0.000001). The curcumin group showed increased serotonin (p < 0.0001), decreased pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1beta, IL-6, and TNF-alpha (p < 0.001 for all), and improved antioxidant markers (total antioxidant status, glutathione peroxidase, superoxide dismutase elevated; malondialdehyde reduced; p < 0.001 for all).

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **Participants:** 227 obese patients with type 2 diabetes
- **Duration:** 12 months
- **Assessment timepoints:** Baseline, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months
- **Primary outcome:** Depression severity via Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9)
- **Biomarkers measured:** Serotonin, IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, total antioxidant status (TAS), glutathione peroxidase (GPx), superoxide dismutase (SOD), malondialdehyde (MDA)

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q3 (clinical trials):** Directly relevant. One of the largest and longest-duration curcumin depression RCTs identified. Demonstrates significant antidepressant effect with concurrent biomarker changes (serotonin up, inflammation down, antioxidant capacity up) in a comorbid population (obesity + T2DM). The 12-month duration is notably longer than most curcumin RCTs, which typically run 6-12 weeks.
