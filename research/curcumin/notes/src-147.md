# src-147: Curcumin for Postpartum Depression and Anxiety in Primiparous Women

**Citation:** Hamlbar EP, Mirghafourvand M, Shaseb E, Kamalifard M. (2025). BMC Complementary Medicine and Therapies, 25:157. DOI: 10.1186/s12906-025-04798-x

**Quality:** ok | **Type:** RCT (open-access) | **Citations:** 7

## Core Findings

This double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT found that 500 mg/day curcumin supplementation for 8 weeks significantly reduced both postpartum depression (EPDS) and anxiety (PSAS-RSF) scores in primiparous women compared to placebo. The study population had low baseline depression scores (EPDS ~2/30), suggesting curcumin may have preventive rather than treatment effects in this context. Notably, the placebo group's depression scores increased from baseline during the study period, which drove much of the observed between-group difference.

## Study Design and Sample Size

- **Design:** Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial
- **Registration:** Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT20110524006582N36), registered 20/09/2022
- **Setting:** Al-Zahra and Taleghani educational centers, Tabriz, Iran (April-August 2023)
- **Randomization:** Block method (blocks of 4 and 6), 1:1 allocation, allocation concealed via numbered identical bottles
- **Blinding:** Triple-blind (researcher, participants, analyst unaware of allocation)
- **Sample size calculation:** G-Power software based on previous depression data (alpha=0.05, beta=0.2, 10% dropout)
- **Screened:** 120 women; **Randomized:** 96 (48 per group); **Completed:** 83 (42 curcumin, 43 placebo)
- **Dropouts:** 11 total -- 6 curcumin (4 GI side effects, 2 unwillingness), 5 placebo (1 GI, 4 unwillingness)

## Dosage and Administration

- **Intervention:** Curcumin (Curcuma longa extract) 500 mg capsules once daily
- **Placebo:** Identical capsules prepared by Adineh Company under GMP
- **Timing:** Started on day 7 postpartum, continued for 8 weeks
- **Administration:** With a glass of water, 2 hours after meals
- **Monitoring:** Weekly phone calls to check baby weight, remind capsule use, and record side effects

## Primary Outcomes

### Depression (EPDS, range 0-30)
| Group | Pre (SD) | Post (SD) | MD (95% CI) | P |
|-------|----------|-----------|-------------|---|
| Curcumin (n=42) | 2.1 (1.9) | 1.8 (1.3) | -2.5 (-3.3 to -1.7) | <0.001 |
| Placebo (n=43) | 2.2 (2.0) | 4.4 (2.6) | | |

**Key observation:** The curcumin group showed minimal change (-0.3), while the placebo group worsened substantially (+2.2). The treatment effect is largely driven by the placebo deterioration rather than dramatic curcumin improvement. Baseline EPDS scores were very low (2/30), meaning participants were not clinically depressed at enrollment (threshold: >=12 indicates depression).

### Anxiety (PSAS-RSF, range 16-64)
| Group | Pre (SD) | Post (SD) | MD (95% CI) | P |
|-------|----------|-----------|-------------|---|
| Curcumin (n=42) | 20.5 (1.7) | 18.6 (1.3) | -1.8 (-2.3 to -1.3) | <0.001 |
| Placebo (n=43) | 20.1 (1.6) | 20.1 (1.7) | | |

**Anxiety subscales:** Significant improvements in practical anxiety (MD: -0.5), attachment anxiety (MD: -0.6), and safety anxiety (MD: -0.4). Mental adaptation subscale was not significant (MD: -0.1, P=0.313).

### Inclusion Criteria
- Primiparous women aged 18+, Tabriz residents
- No chronic disease, no history of depression/mental disorders
- No alcohol/drug use, delivery within last 12 hours
- Low-risk pregnancy, EPDS score <=12 at enrollment
- Excluded: prenatal depression, NICU baby, maternal hospitalization, anticoagulant use

## Safety Findings

- **Adverse events:** 4 curcumin vs 1 placebo reported gastrointestinal symptoms (nausea, stomach pain)
- These were the only side effects reported; severity not further quantified
- No serious adverse events reported
- **Breastfeeding relevance:** Women were initially hesitant about curcumin during breastfeeding but were reassured about safety. The study does not report specific breastfeeding outcomes or infant safety data.
- No specific data on transfer to breast milk or infant effects

## Methodology Notes

- **Strengths:** Double-blind design, random allocation with concealment, standard validated questionnaires (EPDS validated in Iran, Cronbach's alpha 0.86; PSAS-RSF validated in Iran)
- **Analysis:** ANCOVA adjusting for baseline scores; appropriate statistical approach
- **Population:** Primiparous women only; mean age ~28; ~90% housewives; high BMI (~29.5); mostly rental housing

## Limitations

1. **Very low baseline depression scores** (EPDS ~2/30, threshold for depression is >=12) -- raises questions about floor effect and clinical significance
2. **Small sample size** (83 completers) -- acknowledged by authors
3. **Short follow-up** (8 weeks only) -- no longer-term data
4. **Placebo group worsening** is unusual and unexplained -- could reflect natural postpartum course
5. **No bioavailability data** -- standard curcumin (not a bioenhanced formulation) has poor absorption
6. **Single-center study** in Tabriz, Iran -- limited generalizability
7. **No infant outcome data** despite breastfeeding population

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample size justification:** Authors cite G-Power calculation with m1=12.6, m2=10.08 (from Gary et al.), but the actual baseline scores were ~2.1-2.2. The study was powered for a much higher depression population than actually enrolled. The effect size observed (MD=2.5) is large relative to baseline scores (~2), which may reflect the unusual placebo deterioration.
- **Abstract vs. body anxiety MD discrepancy:** The abstract states anxiety MD of -1.4 (95% CI: -2.1 to -0.7), but Table 3 and the Results section report MD of -1.8 (95% CI: -2.3 to -1.3). These are inconsistent -- the abstract numbers do not match the body text.

## Relevance to Q3 (Clinical Trials)

Directly relevant. This is a recent (2025) double-blind RCT specifically examining curcumin for postpartum depression and anxiety. It provides primary trial data with significant results, though the very low baseline depression scores and placebo-group worsening introduce interpretive complexity. The use of standard (non-enhanced) curcumin at a moderate dose (500 mg/day) for 8 weeks adds to the clinical trial evidence base. The anxiety finding is more straightforward (curcumin improved while placebo was stable), while the depression finding requires caution due to the unusual placebo trajectory.

## Proposed Mechanisms (from Discussion)

- MAO inhibition leading to increased serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine
- HPA axis regulation
- Anti-inflammatory effects (IL-4, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-alpha modulation)
- Antioxidant properties (free radical scavenging, glutathione boost)
- Upregulation of BDNF
