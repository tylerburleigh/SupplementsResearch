## Core Findings

This meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (599 patients) found that curcumin significantly improved both WOMAC and VAS scores in osteoarthritis patients, with effect sizes favoring curcumin. The side effect rate of curcumin was comparable to ibuprofen (RR ~0.81), suggesting a favorable safety profile.

## Study Design and Inclusion Criteria

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
- **Databases searched:** PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library (through November 2015)
- **Inclusion criteria:** RCTs reporting the effect of curcumin on osteoarthritis
- **Two independent reviewers** performed study selection
- **Total included:** 5 studies, 599 patients
- **Quality assessment:** Cochrane Collaboration's tool for risk of bias
- **Statistical methods:** SMD with 95% CI for continuous data; RR with 95% CI for dichotomous data; sensitivity analysis via Stata 12.0

## Effect Sizes

### WOMAC Score
- SMD = -0.96 (95% CI: -1.81, -0.10; P = 0.03)
- Large effect size, statistically significant but with a wide confidence interval that barely excludes zero

### VAS Pain Score
- SMD = -1.65 (95% CI: -2.11, -1.19)
- Very large effect size; no P-value reported for VAS specifically
- Note: SMD of -1.65 is unusually large and may indicate substantial heterogeneity or small-study effects

## Safety Comparison with Ibuprofen

- The side effect rate of curcumin was 0.81 times that of ibuprofen (reported as "0.81 times higher" but contextually this means curcumin had a lower or comparable rate)
- The abstract's wording ("0.81 times higher") is ambiguous -- it likely means RR = 0.81, i.e., curcumin had 81% of the side effect rate of ibuprofen
- Conclusion: curcumin's side effect profile was not worse than ibuprofen

## Risk of Bias Assessment

- Cochrane Collaboration's tool was used for quality assessment
- No detailed results of the risk of bias assessment are reported in the abstract
- With only 5 studies, funnel plot analysis for publication bias may not have been feasible

## Limitations

1. **Small number of studies** (only 5 RCTs) limits the reliability of meta-analytic estimates
2. **Very large effect sizes** (SMD = -1.65 for VAS) are unusual and may reflect publication bias, heterogeneity, or methodological issues in the primary studies
3. **Wide confidence intervals** (WOMAC CI: -1.81 to -0.10) indicate substantial uncertainty
4. **Abstract-only source** -- cannot assess heterogeneity metrics (I-squared), publication bias tests, or individual study quality ratings
5. **Search date** is November 2015, so newer trials are not included
6. Ambiguous reporting of safety comparison (RR = 0.81 phrased unclearly)
