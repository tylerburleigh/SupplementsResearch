# src-224: Curcumin supplementation increases longevity and antioxidant capacity in C. elegans

**Authors:** Xu J, Du P, Liu X, Xu X, Ge Y, Zhang C
**Venue:** Frontiers in Pharmacology (2023)
**DOI:** 10.3389/fphar.2023.1195490
**Citations:** 28

## Core Findings

Curcumin significantly extends lifespan in wild-type C. elegans (N2 strain), with the optimal concentration of 25 uM increasing mean lifespan by 2.91 days (14.44 to 17.34 days, p=0.0001). The lifespan extension mechanism involves upregulation of antioxidant enzymes (SOD-1/2/3, GST-4) and downregulation of the p38 MAPK signaling pathway (nsy-1, sek-1, pmk-1), reducing ROS accumulation and preserving mitochondrial integrity. Four concentrations (10, 25, 50, 100 uM) all extended lifespan significantly, but 25 uM was optimal across all endpoints.

## Experimental Design

- **Organism:** C. elegans wild-type N2 strain, cultured at 20 C on NGM/OP50
- **Curcumin concentrations:** 10, 25, 50, and 100 uM (dissolved in DMSO, final DMSO 0.1% v/v)
- **Lifespan assay:** ~30 nematodes per group, repeated at least 3 times; FUdR used to prevent progeny
- **Stress resistance:** Heat stress (35 C, 4 h) and paraquat stress (50 uM, 6 h)
- **ROS measurement:** H2DCFDA fluorescence assay (microplate reader and confocal microscopy)
- **Antioxidant capacity:** T-AOC commercial kit
- **Mitochondrial copy number:** qPCR of mtDNA relative to nDNA
- **Transcriptomics:** RNA-seq (12,000 worms per group, 25 uM curcumin vs control, 3 replicates each)
- **Gene validation:** qRT-PCR for sod-1/2/3, gst-4, pmk-1, sek-1, nsy-1, daf-2, daf-16 (act-1 reference)
- **Statistics:** Log-rank test for lifespan; Student's t-test for other measures; significance at p<0.05

## Key Evidence

### Lifespan Extension
| Concentration | Mean lifespan (days) | SD | p-value (log-rank) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control (0 uM) | 14.435 | 4.9797 | -- |
| 10 uM | 15.967 | 4.9513 | 0.0461 |
| 25 uM | 17.344 | 5.5019 | 0.0001 |
| 50 uM | 16.429 | 5.0949 | 0.0113 |
| 100 uM | 16.163 | 4.9175 | 0.0322 |

- All concentrations significantly extended lifespan vs control
- Optimal at 25 uM: +20.2% mean lifespan increase (+2.91 days)
- Curcumin had no inhibitory effect on E. coli OP50 growth (ruling out bacterial metabolism confound)

### Antioxidant Effects
- ROS significantly reduced under both heat stress and paraquat stress at all concentrations
- 25 uM most effective at reducing ROS
- T-AOC significantly increased at all doses (10 uM p=0.0056, 25 uM p=0.0021, 50 uM p=0.046, 100 uM p=0.039)

### Mitochondrial Effects
- mtDNA copy number increased with curcumin treatment, strongest at 25 uM
- Supports "free radical-mitochondria" aging theory

### Mechanism: MAPK Pathway
- RNA-seq KEGG analysis identified 5 significantly enriched pathways: MAPK, FOXO, Wnt, mTOR, TGF-beta
- MAPK was the most representative pathway for environmental information processing
- MAPK-related genes were predominantly downregulated, concentrated in the p38 MAPK kinase pathway
- qRT-PCR confirmed:
  - **Upregulated:** sod-1, sod-2, sod-3, gst-4 (antioxidant defense)
  - **Downregulated:** sek-1, pmk-1, nsy-1 (p38 MAPK signaling cascade)
- daf-2 and daf-16 expression was also examined (primers listed) but results not discussed in detail

### Safety
- No effect on locomotion (head swing, body bending) at any concentration (p>0.05)
- No effect on egg-laying (reproductive toxicity marker) at any concentration

## Methodology Notes
- RNA-seq used edgeR for differential expression analysis (FDR<0.01, FC>=2)
- 2,516 GO items significantly enriched
- Data deposited at NCBI (PRJNA949877)
- FUdR (50 uM) used in lifespan assays to prevent progeny hatching

## Limitations
- Study conducted only in C. elegans; translational relevance to mammals unclear
- Curcumin bioavailability issues (well-documented in mammals) not addressed
- Specific mitochondrial function assays (e.g., respiration, membrane potential) not performed
- The exact molecular link between MAPK downregulation and lifespan extension was not mechanistically dissected (correlative)
- daf-2/daf-16 results from qRT-PCR were not discussed despite primers being listed
- No genetic mutants (e.g., pmk-1 knockout) were used to confirm pathway causality

## Relevance to Q4 (Longevity/Aging Biomarkers)
This study is directly relevant to Q4 on longevity and aging biomarkers. It provides:
1. **Quantitative lifespan extension data** across a dose-response curve (10-100 uM curcumin)
2. **Mechanistic pathway data** linking curcumin to MAPK signaling and oxidative stress genes
3. **Biomarker candidates:** mtDNA copy number, T-AOC, ROS levels, and expression of sod-1/2/3, gst-4 as measurable endpoints affected by curcumin
4. **Transcriptomic evidence** (RNA-seq) identifying multiple aging-related pathways (MAPK, FOXO, mTOR, Wnt, TGF-beta)

The study's strength is in its multi-level approach (physiological, biochemical, transcriptomic, molecular). Its weakness is the absence of genetic validation (knockout/mutation studies) to establish causal pathway involvement.
