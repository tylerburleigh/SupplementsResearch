# src-217: Curcumin for IBD - Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (Mohseni et al., 2025)

## Core Findings

This is the most comprehensive meta-analysis to date of placebo-controlled RCTs evaluating curcumin for IBD. Curcumin demonstrated statistically significant efficacy for clinical remission and clinical response in ulcerative colitis (UC) patients when used as adjunctive therapy, but showed no significant benefit over placebo in Crohn's disease (CD). Safety profile was comparable to placebo.

## Number of Included Studies and Patients

- **13 placebo-controlled RCTs** included (from 507 records screened, 362 after deduplication, 25 full-text reviewed)
- **11 RCTs in UC patients**, **2 RCTs in CD patients**
- Aggregated sample sizes: UC 322 curcumin vs 308 placebo; CD 51 curcumin vs 41 placebo
- Individual trial sizes ranged from 30 to 89 participants
- Most trials published after 2010
- PROSPERO registered: CRD42024567247

## Key Outcomes for UC

| Outcome | Analysis | RR (95% CI) | p-value | I2 | Participants (C/P) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical remission | ITT | 2.45 (1.09, 5.51) | 0.03 | 88% | 220/206 |
| Clinical remission | PP | 3.04 (0.93, 9.96) | 0.07 | -- | -- |
| Clinical response | ITT | 1.93 (1.15, 3.25) | 0.01 | 69% | 190/177 |
| Clinical response | PP | 2.04 (1.30, 3.20) | 0.002 | -- | -- |
| Endoscopic remission | PP | 2.11 (1.23, 3.62) | 0.007 | 55% | 148/142 |
| Endoscopic remission | ITT | 3.81 (0.95, 15.18) | 0.06 | -- | -- |

- All UC analyses used random-effects models due to high heterogeneity
- Clinical remission and response favored curcumin significantly by ITT; PP analysis was significant for response but marginally non-significant for remission
- Endoscopic remission significant only by PP analysis

## Key Outcomes for CD

| Outcome | Analysis | RR (95% CI) | p-value | I2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical remission | ITT | 2.23 (0.24, 20.51) | 0.48 | 65% |
| Endoscopic remission | ITT | 0.91 (0.34, 2.45) | 0.86 | 13% |

- Only 2 RCTs available for CD; wide confidence intervals reflect low statistical power
- Curcumin did not demonstrate superiority over placebo for any CD outcome

## Effect Sizes and Heterogeneity

- UC clinical remission: substantial heterogeneity (I2=88%, p<0.0001)
- UC clinical response: substantial heterogeneity (I2=69%, p=0.004)
- UC endoscopic remission: moderate heterogeneity (I2=55%, p=0.04)
- CD outcomes: moderate heterogeneity for clinical remission (I2=65%), low for endoscopic (I2=13%)
- Heterogeneity likely driven by variation in curcumin formulations, doses (0.1-10 g/day), treatment durations (1-6 months), and disease severity
- All pooled analyses used random-effects models

## Safety Assessment

- Adverse events: 7.80% curcumin vs 7.29% placebo (RR 1.03, 95% CI 0.55-1.92, p=0.93)
- Withdrawal rates: 15.16% curcumin vs 13.85% placebo (RR 1.04, 95% CI 0.65-1.66, p=0.87)
- No significant heterogeneity in safety outcomes (AEs I2=0%, withdrawal I2=18%)
- Conclusion: curcumin safety profile comparable to placebo

## Methodology

- Databases: Medline/PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Knowledge, Cochrane, Google Scholar (through May 2024)
- English-language restriction; snowball reference searching
- Data extraction by 4 independent researchers; consensus resolution
- Quality assessment via Cochrane Collaboration RoB tool
- Statistical analysis in RevMan v5.4.1; dichotomous outcomes reported as risk ratios
- Both ITT and PP analyses conducted

## Limitations

1. Notable proportion of low-quality pooled studies
2. Varying curcumin dosage forms and regimens across trials
3. Small individual trial sizes (30-89 participants)
4. Only 2 CD trials available, severely limiting CD conclusions
5. Samples may not represent broader IBD population
6. No ethnicity data reported in included trials
7. Heterogeneity in disease severity definitions and outcome measures
8. Funnel plots / publication bias assessment not explicitly reported

## Relevance to Q3 (Clinical Trials)

**Highly relevant.** This is the most recent and comprehensive meta-analysis specifically focused on placebo-controlled RCTs of curcumin in IBD. It provides:
- Pooled effect sizes with confidence intervals for all major clinical outcomes
- Direct evidence that curcumin as adjunctive therapy benefits UC (but not CD)
- Safety data confirming favorable tolerability profile
- Clear identification of formulation heterogeneity as a major confound
- Explicit recommendations for future trial design (standardize doses/formulations, diverse recruitment, longitudinal designs, mechanistic endpoints)

## Claims to Verify

- Total participant counts (322/308 UC, 51/41 CD) were stated in aggregate but individual trial ITT numbers from Table 1 should be summed to confirm
- The I2=55% for endoscopic remission is described as "moderate" with p=0.04, which is consistent
- AE percentages (7.80% and 7.29%) appear consistent with the raw counts (16/205 and 14/192 approx) but denominators should be cross-checked against individual trial totals
