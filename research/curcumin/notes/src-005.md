# src-005: BioTurm Piperine-Free Curcumin Bioavailability Study (2025)

**Source:** Nutritional Outlook article by Erin McEvoy (Dec 18, 2025), reporting on a study published in *Cureus* (Nov 2025)

**Citation:** Malhotra VK, Deshpande AC, Tamoli S, Shinde RS, Soman AD. "An Open Label, Randomized, Comparative Bioavailability Study of BioTurm Extract Versus Curcuma longa Extract With Piperine in Healthy Adult Volunteers." *Cureus*. 2025;17(11):e97127. DOI: 10.7759/cureus.97127

**Quality Notes:** This is an industry trade publication article summarizing a peer-reviewed study, not the primary study itself. The PK numbers and study design details appear to be faithfully reported from the original paper, but this is a secondary source. The underlying study was published in Cureus, an open-access peer-reviewed journal.

---

## Core Findings

BioTurm (NuAxon Bioscience), a curcumin ingredient standardized to 45% curcuminoids and 4.5% ar-turmerone, achieved comparable bioavailability to a high-dose piperine formulation (10% piperine) and vastly outperformed a low-piperine formulation (1% piperine). The mean Cmax for BioTurm was 647.97 ng/mL vs. 663.60 ng/mL for the 10% piperine group and only 10.94 ng/mL for the 1% piperine group. This suggests ar-turmerone -- a natural turmeric oil compound -- can replace piperine as a bioavailability enhancer.

## 1. BioTurm Formulation Approach (Ar-Turmerone)

- BioTurm is standardized to **45% curcuminoids** and **4.5% ar-turmerone**
- Ar-turmerone is a turmeric oil compound naturally present in turmeric
- Mechanism: enhances curcumin's cellular uptake and regulates inflammatory signaling pathways
- This is a "whole-extract" approach -- using compounds native to turmeric rather than adding an exogenous enhancer (piperine from black pepper)
- Ar-turmerone was detectable in plasma for 8 hours post-dose, peaking at 2 hours

## 2. PK Comparison Data vs Piperine Formulations

| Parameter | BioTurm | 1% Piperine | 10% Piperine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cmax (ng/mL) | 647.97 | 10.94 | 663.60 |
| Tmax (hours) | ~2 (ar-turmerone) | 4 | 4 |

- BioTurm's Cmax was **59x higher** than the 1% piperine formulation
- BioTurm's Cmax was essentially equivalent to the 10% piperine formulation (only ~2.4% lower)
- Both piperine groups reached maximum concentrations at 4 hours; BioTurm's ar-turmerone peaked at 2 hours (suggesting potentially faster absorption)

## 3. Safety and Adverse Events

- **No adverse events or serious adverse events** reported in any group
- The article notes that higher doses of piperine can cause gastrointestinal discomfort in consumers
- The absence of piperine is framed as a safety advantage, particularly for long-term use
- Note: with only 14 participants in a single-dose design, the safety data is limited

## 4. Implications for Clean-Label Products

- BioTurm uses only compounds naturally found in turmeric (curcuminoids + ar-turmerone), avoiding exogenous piperine
- Aligns with clean-label product development trends
- Absence of piperine could result in cost savings for manufacturers (no need to source/blend black pepper extract)
- Marketed as enabling "more effective products for consumers" without piperine-related GI side effects
- Showcased at SupplySide Global 2025 as an ingredient in a hangover prevention supplement (Party Fit)

## 5. Relevance to Q6 (Isolated vs Whole Extract Tradeoffs)

This source is directly relevant to Q6. It provides evidence that a **whole-turmeric-extract approach** (retaining ar-turmerone alongside curcuminoids) can achieve bioavailability comparable to formulations that rely on an isolated exogenous enhancer (piperine). Key points:

- Supports the case that whole extract components (ar-turmerone) have synergistic value beyond the primary active (curcumin)
- Demonstrates that the "entourage effect" concept has pharmacokinetic support -- ar-turmerone enhances curcumin absorption
- Shows that removing piperine need not sacrifice bioavailability, if other turmeric-native compounds are retained
- However, this is still a semi-purified extract (45% curcuminoids, 4.5% ar-turmerone), not truly "whole turmeric" -- it is a selectively enriched formulation

## Methodology

- **Design:** Open-label, randomized, comparative bioavailability study
- **Participants:** 14 healthy adults, ages 21-30
- **Groups:** Three arms (BioTurm, 1% piperine combo, 10% piperine combo)
- **Dosing:** Single oral dose
- **Sampling:** Blood samples taken multiple times over 8 hours
- **Journal:** Cureus (open-access, peer-reviewed)
- **DOI:** 10.7759/cureus.97127

## Limitations

- **Very small sample size** (14 participants total, likely ~4-5 per group) -- limits statistical power
- **Open-label design** -- participants and researchers knew which treatment was being administered
- **Single-dose study** -- does not address steady-state pharmacokinetics or long-term effects
- **Narrow age range** (21-30) -- results may not generalize to older populations most likely to use curcumin supplements
- **Industry-funded research** (NuAxon Bioscience) -- potential conflict of interest
- **Secondary source** -- this is a trade publication article, not the primary study; PK numbers should be verified against the original Cureus paper

## Claims to Verify

1. **Cmax values (647.97, 10.94, 663.60 ng/mL):** These are striking numbers, especially the ~60x difference between BioTurm and 1% piperine. Should be verified against the primary Cureus paper. The precision (two decimal places) suggests they come from the original publication.
2. **Sample size (14 total):** With three groups, this yields ~4-5 per group, which is very small for a PK study. Statistical significance of the comparisons should be verified.
3. **The Cmax for 1% piperine (10.94 ng/mL) seems remarkably low** compared to published literature for curcumin + piperine combinations. This may reflect formulation-specific differences, dosing levels, or analytical methodology that should be examined in the primary paper.
