Microdosing psychedelics – Does it have an impact on emodiversity?

January 19, 2023

Microdosing Research

This naturalistic study (n=18) on microdosing found that it led to a decrease in positive and overall emotional diversity (emodiversity). Participants experienced more "awe, wonder, or amazement" and "ashamed, humiliated, or disgraced" emotions during microdosing days and fewer "joyful, glad, or happy" emotions.
Microdosing has been associated with a decrease in positive emotions and overall emotional diversity, contrary to some anecdotal reports of enhanced mood.
Feelings of awe, wonder, and amazement were reported during microdosing, along with negative emotions like shame, while typical feelings characterized as joyful were notably lower.
The cumulative dosage of psychedelics did not correlate with the expected increase in emotional variety, suggesting that microdosing may prioritize certain emotional experiences rather than broadening them.
Microdosing Research Overview
This naturalistic study, which included 18 participants, explored the effects of microdosing on emotional states. Microdosing refers to the practice of taking sub-threshold doses of serotonergic hallucinogens, such as LSD or psilocybin, to enhance well-being, mood, and cognitive functions without experiencing perceptual changes associated with higher doses. The findings indicate that engaging in microdosing results in a diminished range of positive and overall emotional diversity, showcasing a complex relationship between microdosing and emotional experiences.
Microdosing and Emodiversity
Previous anecdotal evidence and qualitative research have suggested that microdosing can improve mood, leading to higher levels of happiness, well-being, and reduced feelings of anxiety or depression. Despite these observations, the current study highlights a different outcome, revealing that microdosing days are linked with a reduced expression of positive emotions. The emphasis on emotional diversity is crucial, as a broader range of emotional experiences typically correlates with a healthy emotional landscape. The contrast between prior studies and current findings invites further examination of how microdosing might influence emotional awareness and expression.
Methods Overview
The authors gathered data over a 28-day period using Experience Sampling Methods (ESM) to collect real-time emotional responses from participants. The participant sample included university students already practicing microdosing, who were prompted five times daily to report on their emotional states. Responses were scored on a scale to assess various emotions experienced, providing a granular view of emotional dynamics associated with microdosing.
Key Findings of the Study
Results indicated that while participants engaged in microdosing, both positive and overall emotional diversity declined. This was surprising considering earlier studies reported mood improvements during microdosing. The researchers found specific emotions, such as those symbolizing awe and negative feelings like shame, rose in prominence during microdosing periods. Nonetheless, the expected correlation between increased frequency of microdosing and a wider emotional range did not materialize, leading to questions about the nuances of emotional experiences during such practices.
Discussion and Limitations
The outcomes of this study pose challenges to the narrative that microdosing unequivocally enhances mood. Analysis indicates certain emotions were emphasized, potentially limiting the diversity of emotional experience on microdosing days. While the study benefitted from a detailed sampling method over 28 days, the limited number of participants constrains broader generalizations and calls for caution. Additionally, emotional awareness was not quantitatively assessed, obscuring the theoretical underpinnings linking microdosing practices to emotional variability. Insights gained from this research underscore the need for further investigation into how microdosing uniquely influences emotional experiences, intentionally focusing on the intricate relationship between emotional awareness and microdosing practices.
