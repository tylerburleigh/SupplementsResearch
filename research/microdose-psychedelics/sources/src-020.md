Twenty percent better with 20 micrograms? A qualitative study of psychedelic microdosing self-rapports and discussions on YouTube

January 6, 2021

Microdosing Research

This qualitative study (n=198) of opinions on microdosing found that they were generally well-tolerated and were used for therapeutic or enhancement purposes. The study analysed 32 videos and the self-report reactions to those videos.
Microdosing of psychedelics like LSD and psilocybin demonstrates therapeutic effects, including improvements in mental health conditions and enhanced cognitive performance.
Most participants reported positive outcomes, but some experienced adverse effects, indicating the need for careful dosage and administration practices.
The motivations for microdosing were largely therapeutic rather than merely for enhancement, with many users integrating the practice into a holistic health approach.
Introduction to Microdosing
The authors Martin Andersson and Anette Kjellgren explore the popular practice of psychedelic microdosing, which involves using minute, repeated doses of substances like LSD and psilocybin to promote various psychological and physiological benefits. Although significant research has been conducted on the effects of full doses of psychedelics, microdosing remains relatively underexplored. Social media, particularly platforms such as YouTube and online forums, has significantly contributed to the increased visibility and discussion surrounding this phenomenon. The researchers’ own interest in microdosing emerged from prior work on drug discussion forums that highlighted its use as a self-treatment method for conditions like headaches.
Research Methods
To investigate microdosing, the researchers utilized specialized software along with a YouTube API to gather relevant video content. Initial searches led to 84 relevant videos, which after careful screening resulted in a total of 32 videos for analysis. The videos featured self-reports from 34 individuals, and their accompanying comments were extracted for examination. In total, the researchers analyzed content from a significant number of voices, gathering a wealth of data concerning personal experiences with microdosing.
Themes Identified in Results
Upon analysis, eight main themes emerged from the data, illustrating both the motivations behind microdosing and the experiences reported by users. Firstly, individuals tended to microdose for reasons related to self-treatment of mental health disorders like depression, as well as for achieving personal enhancement. Many integrated microdosing with other health practices such as diet or meditation.
Enhanced Experiences and Benefits
Users described microdosing as providing subtle but impactful changes in their psychophysiological states. Many reported enhanced sensory perception and improved focus, accompanied by a boost in mood and energy. Reflective insights and transformative experiences were also noted, linking the practice to personal growth and a reassessment of life priorities.
Performance and Health Effects
Users claimed a broad range of cognitive improvements, from enhanced creativity to better physical performance in sports and daily activities. Reports also indicated successful alleviation of various health issues, including anxiety and PTSD. Nonetheless, some users experienced negative side effects, like increased anxiety and gastrointestinal distress, underscoring the importance of personalized dosage strategies.
Perspectives on Microdosing
The general views on microdosing were mostly positive, with users often favoring it over standard psychiatric treatments. They emphasized that microdosing offers a unique approach to addressing personal struggles, diverging from conventional therapies that tend to mask symptoms. Many users, however, acknowledged the importance of integrating microdosing with other lifestyle enhancements such as mindfulness and fitness.
Discussion and Future Research
The authors conclude that the motivations for microdosing lean more towards therapeutic benefits rather than solely for productivity enhancement. While microdosing appears to offer significant mental health and cognitive benefits, the promising results should be approached with caution due to potential biases in self-reported data. The authors call for future research to examine the influences of individuals’ intentions and personalized dosing practices, which could be vital for drawing reliable conclusions about the efficacy of microdosing.
