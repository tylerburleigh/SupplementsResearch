The Viability of Microdosing Psychedelics as a Strategy to Enhance Cognition and Well-being – An Early Review

April 8, 2021

Microdosing Research

This review (2020) examines the viability of microdosing psychedelics as a method to improve cognition and well-being. Available evidence indicates a variety of (perceived) benefits, including improvements in mood, focus, and creativity, with some people experiencing no discernible effects or expressing concerns about selective negative consequences like increased anxiety. However, most insights stem from observational studies using convenience samples that are biased or unrepresentative of the general population.
The majority of evidence surrounding microdosing comes from observational studies that may lack generalizability due to biased sampling methods.
While some studies indicate that microdosing may improve mood, creativity, and cognitive function, results are inconsistent, and some individuals report negative effects, including increased anxiety.
Future research is essential to explore long-term effects and establish standardized methodologies concerning microdosing to ensure reliability and validity.
Overview of Microdosing Psychedelics
This review analyzes the potential benefits and drawbacks of microdosing psychedelics—defined as taking doses significantly lower than typical recreational or therapeutic levels. The researchers observe a rising interest in these substances for therapeutic and recreational purposes, highlighting that full doses, while promising for some medical applications, can also lead to adverse psychological experiences. In response, microdosing has emerged as a strategy to maximize benefits while reducing risks associated with higher doses.
Research Methodology
The authors describe a thorough literature search conducted across various academic databases. This review included studies that reported findings on the human effects of microdosing different psychedelics. Out of the original search, they identified a total of eleven observational studies, one quasi-experimental study, and nine experimental studies, most of which provided qualitative insights due to the limited scope of research available at this time.
Findings on Effects
General Effects
The researchers summarize various observational studies revealing that a significant portion of participants reported neutral to positive experiences with microdosing. Experiences of negative effects, primarily psychological, were noted by about 20% of individuals surveyed. Additionally, interviews highlighted concerns such as the long-term consequences of microdosing, with some instances of benefits reversing over extended use.
Impact on Creativity
Improvements in creativity were frequently reported among microdosers according to qualitative feedback. One quasi-experimental study recorded substantial gains in two forms of creative thinking, although several other studies presented opposing findings, adding complexity to understanding the relationship between microdosing and creativity.
Focus and Productivity
Observations from qualitative studies revealed perceived enhancements in productivity and focus among microdosers. However, experimental studies reported contradicting outcomes, with some failing to show any significant cognitive benefits. This inconsistency highlights the need for more targeted research in this area.
Effects on Mood and Well-being
Positive improvements in mood and overall well-being were documented in both qualitative and quantitative research. Participants frequently described enhancements in self-efficacy and life perspective, although some studies found minimal or negligible effects on mood changes, suggesting variability in personal responses to microdosing.
Mental Health Effects
The potential of microdosing to alleviate certain mental health issues was highlighted, with some reports suggesting benefits in conditions such as PTSD and OCD. However, the effects on depression and anxiety showed significant variability, showcasing the nuanced nature of microdosing’s impact across different individuals.
Physiological Effects
Physiological responses to microdosing were largely documented on a qualitative basis, indicating some potential benefits while also raising concerns about mild adverse effects. The authors note the absence of comprehensive studies investigating long-term physiological consequences, marking a critical area for future exploration.
Discussion of Limitations
The researchers articulate several methodological weaknesses in the current body of literature. The predominant reliance on convenience samples raises questions regarding the broader applicability of findings. Furthermore, the lack of long-term studies, consistency in the definition of microdoses, and the necessity of understanding placebo effects are pivotal areas identified for future investigation.
Conclusion and Future Directions
The authors call for more robust, scientifically rigorous studies to ascertain the effects of microdosing while recognizing the challenges posed by legal and social stigmas associated with psychedelics. Although preliminary exploratory evidence suggests potential benefits, they urge caution and rigorous methodological approaches as the field advances.
