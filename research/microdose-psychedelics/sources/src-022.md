Can Psychedelic Microdosing Benefit Women During Menopause?
Menopause is a natural stage of aging, usually occurring between
ages 45 and 55
, when periods cease and fertility ends. The end of cyclic hormonal fluctuations can usher in a new
hormonal normal
, often accompanied by symptoms that significantly impact physical and mental well-being.
Common Menopausal Symptoms
Women may experience:
Mood swings, anxiety, and depression
Insomnia and sleep disturbances
Hot flashes and night sweats
Low energy, low libido, and vaginal dryness
Trouble focusing, dry skin, increased abdominal fat, and weight gain
Beyond these immediate effects,
hormonal decline
—particularly oestrogen—can lead to chronic and systemic changes, including:
Reduced bone density
Increased inflammation
Higher risk of certain cancers and cardiovascular diseases
With limited relief from current treatments, researchers are exploring
psychedelic microdosing
as a potential therapeutic avenue for menopausal women.
What is Microdosing?
Microdosing
involves consuming
small, sub-perceptual amounts
of psychedelics, typically 10–20% of a regular dose, such as
psilocybin
(from “magic mushrooms”) or
LSD
. Unlike a full psychedelic experience, microdosing aims to
enhance cognitive, emotional, and spiritual functioning
over time.
Once taboo, psychedelics are gaining societal acceptance and have even been recognized as a
“breakthrough therapy”
by the FDA, suggesting they may soon receive medical approval.
Preliminary studies and anecdotal reports indicate potential benefits:
Reduced anxiety and depression
Improved mood
Increased focus and productivity
Enhanced cognitive function and overall well-being
In a conversation with
Dr. 1Dreah
, a physician specializing in hormone optimization, age-related disease prevention, and maintaining cognitive and sexual health, she shared her insights:
“I started listening to how psychedelics, might increase blood flow to the brain, neuroplasticity, BDNF. And I’m like, wait, estradiol does that… there are a lot of parallels between what psychedelics do on a mechanistic level and what estradiol does. So, for women where estradiol is not an option for them due to a cancer status, psilocybin might be incredibly helpful.”
Microdosing Protocols
Two popular microdosing protocols are widely cited:
Fadiman Protocol
Developed by
Dr. James Fadiman
and inspired by
Albert Hoffman
Involves taking a small dose every
three days for 4–6 weeks
Stamets Protocol
Developed by
Paul Stamets
Involves microdosing for
4–5 consecutive days
followed by a
2–3 day break
over 4–6 weeks
Often includes supplements like
Niacin
and
Lion’s Mane mushroom
Typical microdoses:
LSD: 10–26 μg (vs. 100–300 μg recreationally)
Psilocybin mushrooms: 0.1–0.7 g (vs. 3–5 g recreationally)
Studies show
20 μg of LSD
can reduce pain perception and increase tolerance [6].
Evidence for Benefits
Dr. Fadiman’s 2019 study, based on online microdosing forums, reported improvements such as:
Better mood and reduced anxiety
Facilitated habit formation
Enhanced learning and focus [1]
Other qualitative studies and surveys support
reduced anxiety and depression
and enhanced mental health, sometimes surpassing conventional treatments [1,7–10].
However, limitations include:
Self-selected online participants
Lack of control groups
Effects sometimes driven by
participant expectations
[11,12]
Neuroimaging studies show LSD microdosing
modifies brain connectivity
, particularly in the thalamus and amygdala, which correlates with improved mood [16].
Potential Beyond Symptom Relief
Menopause-related
inflammation
increases risks for autoimmune and cardiovascular conditions. Declining
oestrogen
, an anti-inflammatory hormone, contributes to this.
Microdosing psychedelics may also
reduce inflammation
in the brain, where it contributes to depression, cognitive decline, and brain fog [17,18]. Activation of
5-HT2A receptors
by psychedelics can promote anti-inflammatory effects [19].
A study found
psilocybin reduced TNF-α
, with further reductions in IL-6 and CRP after seven days
Improvements in mood and social behavior correlated with these reductions [20]
The
mechanism
may involve changes in methylation and histone acetylation, promoting anti-inflammatory gene expression [21]
Further research is needed to understand:
Peripheral inflammation effects
Psychedelic half-life in the body
Other receptor pathways involved
Safety Considerations
Long-term safety of microdosing
is not well-established. Considerations include:
Cardiovascular impact (already a concern in menopausal women)
Minor side effects: altered thoughts, fatigue, sleep disturbances (~6% of users) [7]
LSD: slight increase in blood pressure, no major heart rate changes [2,16]
Psilocybin: potential heart valve concerns via 5-HT2B activation [22]
Interactions with medications
remain largely unknown. Any microdosing regimen should account for:
Overall health
Specific symptoms
Contraindications and medical history
Conclusion
Psychedelic microdosing represents a
promising yet experimental avenue
for menopausal women. While anecdotal evidence and preliminary studies suggest benefits—mental health improvements, cognitive enhancements, and potential anti-inflammatory effects—comprehensive clinical trials are urgently needed.
Balancing
potential benefits
against safety concerns like cardiovascular impact and drug interactions requires
caution, patient-centricity, and scientific rigor
. As research unfolds, psychedelic microdosing may emerge as a
groundbreaking tool for women navigating menopause
, but decisions should be informed, cautious, and evidence-based.
