# src-038: Szigeti et al. (2023) - Activated Expectancy Bias in Psychedelic Microdosing

**Citation:** Szigeti, B., Nutt, D., Carhart-Harris, R., & Erritzoe, D. (2023). The difference between 'placebo group' and 'placebo control': a case study in psychedelic microdosing. *Scientific Reports*, 13, 12107.

**Venue:** Scientific Reports (Nature) | **Citations:** 27 | **Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

This paper introduces **Activated Expectancy Bias (AEB)** -- the phenomenon whereby weak blinding combined with positive treatment expectancy creates an uneven distribution of expectancy effects across treatment arms, inflating treatment effect estimates and potentially generating false positive findings. Using a novel statistical tool called the **Correct Guess Rate Curve (CGRC)**, the authors re-analyze the self-blinding psychedelic microdose trial dataset (n=233) and find that nearly all previously significant microdose-placebo differences become non-significant after CGR adjustment, with the exception of energy VAS. The authors argue that microdosing functions as an **active placebo** -- producing perceivable subjective effects but no meaningful health benefits beyond expectancy.

## Key Concepts

### Activated Expectancy Bias (AEB)
- When blinding fails (patients can deduce their allocation via side effects), and patients have positive expectancy for the active treatment, expectancy effects become unevenly distributed
- This is a "residual expectancy bias" that persists even in nominally "blinded" trials
- Distinguished from general placebo effect: AEB specifically arises from the *interaction* of failed blinding and directional expectancy bias

### Correct Guess Rate Curve (CGRC)
- A resampling-based statistical method that estimates what a trial's outcome would be under perfect blinding (CGR=0.5)
- Separates data into four strata by treatment x guess combinations, builds KDE models, then resamples to simulate perfect blinding
- Procedure repeated 100 times; mean treatment estimate/p-value reported as CGR-adjusted result
- Can also estimate outcomes at intermediate CGR values (e.g., CGR=0.6)

### Active Placebo Framework
- Microdosing produces perceivable effects (energy, body sensations) but these do not translate to mental health benefits
- The perceivable effects enable unblinding, which then drives AEB
- Evidence: 55% of participants reported body/perceptual sensations as primary unblinding cue; effect sizes are too small to be self-detectable (natural day-to-day variability is 500-750% larger than mean placebo-microdose differences on PANAS)

## Re-analysis Results (Self-Blinding Microdose Trial)

### Before CGR Adjustment (traditional analysis)
| Outcome | p-value | Effect (Hedges' g) |
|---------|---------|-------------------|
| PANAS (acute) | 0.01* | 0.32 |
| Energy VAS (acute) | <0.001*** | 0.58 |
| Mood VAS (acute) | 0.02* | 0.31 |
| Creativity VAS (acute) | 0.01* | 0.34 |
| QIDS depression (post-acute) | 0.04* | -0.27 |

### After CGR Adjustment
| Outcome | p-value | Effect (Hedges' g) |
|---------|---------|-------------------|
| PANAS (acute) | 0.43 | 0.11 |
| Energy VAS (acute) | 0.04* | 0.34 |
| Mood VAS (acute) | 0.42 | 0.13 |
| Creativity VAS (acute) | 0.48 | 0.11 |
| QIDS depression (post-acute) | 0.10 | -0.25 |

- 4 of 5 previously significant outcomes became non-significant after CGR adjustment
- Energy VAS retained significance but with ~40% reduced effect size (g=0.34)
- Equivalence testing confirmed that adjusted outcomes were equivalent between groups

## Simulation Results (AEB Model)

| Configuration | Traditional significant rate | CGR-adjusted significant rate | Traditional effect estimate | CGR-adjusted effect estimate |
|--------------|------------------------------|------------------------------|---------------------------|-----------------------------|
| DTE off, AEB off | 5% | 6% | 0.0 | 0.02 |
| DTE on, AEB off | 86% | 84% | 3.02 | 3.01 |
| DTE off, AEB on | 78% (false positive) | 3% | 2.91 | 0.01 |
| DTE on, AEB on | 99% | 82% | 5.69 (inflated) | 3.04 (accurate) |

Key insight: When AEB is present without a true effect, traditional analysis produces false positives 78% of the time. When AEB co-occurs with a true effect, traditional analysis overestimates effect size by ~90%.

## Methodology

- **AEB Model**: Bayesian network with 3 binary nodes (Treatment, Perceived Treatment, Treatment Expectancy) and continuous outcome; parameters calibrated to microdosing/antidepressant trial data (CGR=0.7, effect size=0.4 Hedges' g)
- **Simulation**: 500 trials per configuration, n=230 per trial; 4 configurations (DTE on/off x AEB on/off)
- **Empirical re-analysis**: Self-blinding microdose trial, first week only (n=233), to ensure independence; outcomes ~ treatment linear models
- **CGR Adjustment**: KDE-based resampling to CGR=0.5, repeated 100 times

## Limitations

1. Binary treatment guess data cannot capture the complexity of treatment belief (confidence ratings would improve precision)
2. Unblinding source treated as binary (benign vs. malicious); in reality, both may coexist
3. Source of unblinding assessment relies on retrospective self-reports (cannot establish causation)
4. AEB model assumes additive effects of DTE and expectancy; may not hold universally
5. Resampling cannot guarantee equal distribution of confounders
6. Method reliability decreases with smaller samples, more extreme CGR, and smaller effects
7. Self-selected healthy sample; may not generalize to clinical populations

## Claims to Verify

- **n=233 datapoints**: This is the first-week-only subsample. The original trial had 191 completers. The 233 likely reflects repeated measurements (2 doses/week) from fewer unique participants in week 1. Worth noting for interpretation.
- **CGR = 0.72**: Reported in Figure 4 caption (line 386). Consistent with the ~0.7 used in model calibration.
- **"500-750% larger" natural variability claim** (line 323-326): PANAS placebo-microdose difference was 2.1/0.8 (positive/negative); day-to-day variability cited as ~10/~6 from von Stumm (2016). Ratio: 10/2.1 = 4.76x and 6/0.8 = 7.5x. The "500-750%" claim (5x-7.5x) checks out but uses two different subdomains and a reference to a study with no intervention -- the comparison is illustrative rather than rigorous.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q3 (Methodological limitations):** Directly relevant. Introduces a novel methodological concern (AEB) specific to microdosing and psychedelic trials where blinding is inherently difficult. Provides both theoretical framework and empirical demonstration. The CGRC method itself is a methodological contribution. The finding that only 2-7% of psychiatric trials assess blinding integrity contextualizes the broader problem.

**Q5 (Evidence challenging benefit narrative / microdosing as active placebo):** Strongly relevant. Provides the most rigorous empirical case for the "active placebo" interpretation of microdosing. Shows that previously reported significant benefits (mood, creativity, depression) become non-significant when expectancy bias is controlled. Only energy VAS survives adjustment, with small effect size (g=0.34). The regulatory implication -- that the unadjusted results would meet FDA approval standards -- underscores the practical significance of this methodological critique.
