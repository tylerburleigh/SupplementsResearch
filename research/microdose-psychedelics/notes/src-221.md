## Core Findings

This is a foundational review article that systematically addresses 11 key questions about psychedelic microdosing. As of 2019, the authors found very few controlled studies and no agreed scientific definition of microdosing. The paper provides the first comprehensive framework for microdosing research, covering definitions, schedules, existing evidence, pharmacology, safety, receptor mechanisms, biological plausibility, legal status, regulatory issues, and future research needs. The authors conclude that while anecdotal reports emphasize positive effects, future research must investigate potential risks of repeated low-dose psychedelic administration, particularly cardiac valvulopathy from 5-HT2B receptor activation.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Definition and Dose Ranges (Q1, Q7)
- Microdosing defined by three components: (1) sub-perceptual dose not impairing normal functioning, (2) multiple dosing sessions, (3) intention to improve well-being/cognition.
- **Psilocybin microdose**: ~1/10 to 1/20 of a recreational dose. For dried *P. cubensis* (3-5g recreational = 8.6-14.7 mg psilocin), this equates to **0.43-0.73 mg psilocin per dose**.
- **LSD microdose**: 10-20 microg (20 microg upper limit before perceptual changes in some).
- **Ibogaine HCl microdose**: ~25 mg. **DMT (smoked)**: ~6 mg.
- Dose categorization table provided: microdose < very low dose < low dose < medium dose < high dose.
- Regulatory definition of "microdose" (EMA/FDA/ICH) = 1% of pharmacologically active dose up to 100 microg; psychedelic microdosing (5-10 microg LSD) is actually 5-10% of active dose, lying between a full pharmacological dose and a pharmacological microdose.

### Dosing Schedules (Q7)
- Three main approaches identified:
  1. **Fadiman approach**: 2 dosing days + 2 non-dosing days (most popular)
  2. **Weekday dosing**: Monday-Friday, weekends off
  3. **Balanced approach**: every other day
- Dosing periods ranged from 1 week to 2 years.
- Half of survey respondents created their own schedule (Hutten et al., 2019).

### Existing Controlled Studies (Q1, Q3)
- **First placebo-controlled LSD microdosing study** (Yanakieva et al., 2018): Single doses of 5, 10, 20 microg LSD delayed time perception without self-rated perceptual/mentation/concentration effects.
- **Only psilocybin microdosing study** at time of writing (Prochazkova et al., 2018): Recreational setting, no placebo control, uncertain dose. Found improvements in convergent and divergent thinking.
- **Hasler et al. (2004)**: Very low dose psilocybin (45 microg/kg, ~2.3 mg psilocin) vs placebo: significant decrease in max heart rate at 6 hours, slight drowsiness, increased sensitivity, intensification of pre-existing mood.
- **Griffiths et al. (2011)**: 5 mg/70 kg psilocybin increased stimulation, distance from ordinary reality, and sense of peace. No dose was found to be completely without psychological effects.
- No study to date showed statistically significant effects of microdosing on creativity under placebo-controlled circumstances (Passie, 2019).

### Pharmacological Threshold (Q3, Q7)
- PET study (Madsen et al., 2019) found that doses producing <20% 5-HT2A receptor occupancy (~<0.028 mg/kg) were not detectable psychologically or physiologically, suggesting this as the **microdosing threshold**.
- Plasma psilocin producing 50% 5-HT2A occupancy: 1.95 microg/L (range 1.16-3.15).

### Safety Concerns (Q3)
- **Cardiac valvulopathy risk**: Psilocin binds 5-HT2B with high affinity (Ki = 4.6 nM). Repeated microdosing could stimulate 5-HT2B enough to cause tissue overgrowth, as seen with fenfluramine and methysergide.
- **Preclinical LSD data**: Repeated low-dose LSD in rodents produced persistent negative behavioral changes (aggression, anhedonia, hyper-reactivity) lasting weeks-months after discontinuation, with gene expression changes enriched for schizophrenia and bipolar depression (Marona-Lewicka et al., 2011; Martin et al., 2014).
- hERG channel blockade risk is low (no effect at 100 microM; plasma levels from 25 mg dose only reach ~160 nM).
- Behavioral tolerance develops with repeated psychedelic dosing, associated with reduced cortical 5-HT2A receptor binding.

### Biological Plausibility (Q1)
- Psychedelics promote synaptic plasticity via 5-HT2A-mediated BDNF expression and mTOR pathway activation (Ly et al., 2018).
- Potent anti-inflammatory effects at doses 30x below behavioral threshold (DOI in rodent models of asthma and inflammatory bowel disease).
- 5-HT2A receptor downregulation from repeated dosing might be therapeutic (e.g., in OCD, characterized by increased 5-HT2A binding).

### User Reports (Q1)
- Reported benefits: improved energy, mood, cognition, concentration, stress management, creativity, spiritual awareness, productivity, relationships.
- Reported drawbacks: illegality, stigma, physical discomfort, anxiety, overstimulation, cognitive interference, emotional difficulty, uncertainty of effect (Anderson et al., 2018).
- All reports confounded by dose uncertainty, lack of placebo control, and expectation/placebo effects.

## Methodology

- Narrative review structured as 11 sequential research questions.
- Literature search for microdosing protocols included books, online forums, and surveys.
- Focus predominantly on psilocybin due to clinical development proximity and short pharmacokinetics.
- Includes detailed pharmacological tables for receptor binding affinities (Ki values) and dose categories.
- Key limitation: as a 2019 review, the controlled evidence base was extremely thin (only 1-2 placebo-controlled studies).

## Limitations

- Published in 2019; the field has expanded significantly since then with multiple RCTs now completed.
- Only two preclinical microdosing studies existed at the time.
- Could not provide definitive answers to most questions posed due to lack of evidence.
- Some authors had conflicts of interest (COMPASS Pathways affiliations).

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Human evidence on microdosing)**: Directly relevant. Reviews all controlled human evidence available as of 2019 and catalogues anecdotal reports. Identifies the gap between anecdotal claims and controlled evidence. Highlights that no placebo-controlled study had demonstrated creativity enhancement.
- **Q3 (Methodological considerations)**: Highly relevant. Identifies critical methodological issues: lack of placebo controls, dose uncertainty, expectation effects, set and setting confounds. Recommends Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) framework for cognitive testing. Discusses need for dedicated dose-finding studies and PK/PD characterization.
- **Q7 (Dosing protocols and guidelines)**: Directly relevant. Provides dose definitions and categorization tables. Documents three main dosing schedules. Identifies the 5-HT2A occupancy threshold (~20%) as a potential floor for microdosing. Discusses regulatory requirements for dose-finding studies.

## Claims to Verify

- The assertion that "no study to date has revealed statistically significant effects of microdosing on creativity under placebo-controlled circumstances" (line 129-131) -- this was accurate as of 2019 but may no longer be current.
- The 5-HT2A receptor occupancy threshold of <20% corresponding to <0.028 mg/kg (lines 787-791) -- derived from Madsen et al. (2019), should be cross-referenced with the primary source.
- Psilocybin content ranges for different mushroom species (lines 193-210) -- derived from Tsujikawa et al. (2003) and Christiansen et al. (1981).
