# src-025: Cavanna et al. (2022) - Double-blind placebo-controlled microdosing study

**Citation:** Cavanna, F., Muller, S., de la Fuente, L.A., et al. (2022). Microdosing with psilocybin mushrooms: a double-blind placebo-controlled study. *Translational Psychiatry*, 12:307. DOI: 10.1038/s41398-022-02039-0

**Venue:** Translational Psychiatry (Nature portfolio, peer-reviewed)
**Citations:** 112
**Quality:** ok -- full paper text available

---

## Core Findings

A rigorous double-blind placebo-controlled within-subjects study (N=34) found that 0.5g dried *Psilocybe cubensis* mushrooms (~0.9 mg psilocybin) produced no significant positive effects on creativity, cognition, mood, well-being, or physical activity, despite producing measurable subjective effects and reduced EEG theta power. Acute subjective effects were driven by participants who correctly identified their condition, implicating expectation rather than pharmacological action. Small trends toward cognitive impairment were observed in attentional blink and Stroop tasks.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind placebo-controlled, within-subjects (crossover)
- **Sample:** 34 participants (11 female; mean age 31.26 +/- 4.41 years; 74 +/- 17 kg)
- **Dose:** 0.5g dried ground *Psilocybe cubensis* in gel capsule (placebo = same weight of edible mushroom)
- **Schedule:** Two dosing days per condition week (Wednesday and Friday), two conditions separated by 1-week washout. Order randomized by third party.
- **Estimated psilocybin:** ~0.9 mg per dose (LC-MS confirmed: 640.2 ug/g psilocybin, 950.7 ug/g psilocin)
- **Recruitment:** Word-of-mouth, social media, workshops; participants were planning to start microdosing on their own
- **Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05160220
- **Ethics:** Approved by Committee for Research Ethics, Universidad Abierta Interamericana

## Key Results

### Null Findings (Primary)
- **Creativity:** No significant differences in convergent thinking (RAT) or divergent thinking (AUT, WK) between psilocybin and placebo. AUT fluency (p=0.03) and WK elaboration (p=0.04) were nominally significant but did not survive Bonferroni correction. Most BF10 values < 1/3 (supporting null).
- **Self-reported scales:** No significant differences for STAI-S, PANAS (positive/negative affect), PSS (stress), TAS (absorption), BIEPS (well-being), MWQ (mind wandering), FSS (flow), CPS (creative personality), TECA (empathy), CFS (cognitive flexibility). Most BF10 < 1/3.
- **Perception:** No effect on binocular rivalry dominance durations, backward masking accuracy, or Local-Global ERP measures.
- **Physical activity:** No differences in step count, distance traveled, resting time, or activity time (Fitbit monitoring).

### Positive/Active Findings
- **Acute subjective effects (VAS):** Significantly higher total scores for psilocybin vs. placebo, BUT only for participants who correctly identified their condition ("unblinded" subset). No significant difference in the "blinded" subset. BF10 > 30 for unblinded group; inconclusive for blinded group.
- **EEG theta power:** Reduced power in theta band (4-8 Hz) under psilocybin during eyes-closed resting state (p < 0.05, Bonferroni corrected). Lempel-Ziv complexity was preserved (unlike higher doses where entropy increases).
- **Small cognitive trends toward impairment:** Decreased T2 visibility at 300ms lag in attentional blink (p<0.05 uncorrected), increased RT in Stroop (p<0.05 uncorrected), increased time for TMT Part A (p<0.05 uncorrected).

### Unblinding
- **75% overall unblinding rate** (49/64 weeks). 73.5% for active dose, ~70% for placebo.
- Blind was maintained in week 1 [Chi2(1)=0.09, p=0.76] but broken in week 2 [Chi2(1)=11.53, p=0.0006].
- The authors note this unblinding rate is consistent with Van Elk et al. (>60%) and Szigeti et al.

## Methodology Strengths

1. **Gold-standard design:** Double-blind placebo-controlled within-subjects crossover
2. **Ecological validity:** Recruited individuals already planning to microdose; used actual mushroom material rather than synthetic psilocybin
3. **Comprehensive assessment battery:** Covered creativity (3 tests), cognition (6 computer tasks), mood/well-being (10+ scales), EEG (resting state + ERP), and physical activity (Fitbit)
4. **Bayesian analysis:** Complemented frequentist tests with Bayes factors, providing evidence for null hypothesis (not just failure to reject)
5. **Blinding integrity assessment:** Systematically tested whether participants broke blind and analyzed "blinded" and "unblinded" subsets separately
6. **Chemical characterization:** LC-MS analysis of mushroom samples with quantification of psilocybin, psilocin, baeocystin, norbaeocystin
7. **Pre-registered:** ClinicalTrials.gov registration before study start
8. **Open data:** Available at OSF (https://osf.io/hnxq6/)

## Methodology Limitations

1. **Small sample size (N=34):** No a priori power analysis conducted. Bayesian analysis showed insufficient sample for the blinded subset specifically.
2. **Short duration:** Only 2 doses per condition (4 total over 2 weeks), cannot assess cumulative or long-term effects of microdosing schedules
3. **High unblinding rate (75%):** Severely compromises the placebo-control aspect, though the authors analyzed blinded/unblinded subsets separately
4. **Variable mushroom composition:** Three different mushroom sources; possible potency loss during storage (up to 50% per cited literature)
5. **Dose not weight-adjusted:** 0.5g fixed dose across participants weighing 74 +/- 17 kg
6. **Healthy participants only:** Ceiling effects could mask potential benefits in clinical populations
7. **Single dose level:** Cannot establish dose-response relationships
8. **Non-parametric statistics only:** Wilcoxon signed-rank tests; no parametric mixed-effects modeling

## Claims to Verify

- **N=34:** Consistent across abstract and methods. All 34 completed both conditions. Appears reliable.
- **~0.9 mg psilocybin per 0.5g dose:** Calculated from LC-MS data (640.2 ug/g psilocybin + 950.7 ug/g psilocin = ~1591 ug/g total; x 0.5g = ~0.8 mg). The 0.9 mg figure likely includes both psilocybin and psilocin contributions. Reasonable but the exact calculation is not shown in detail.
- **75% unblinding rate:** 49/64 = 76.6%. The abstract rounds to 75%. Minor rounding discrepancy.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Human clinical evidence on cognition and mood):** HIGH. One of the most rigorous and comprehensive placebo-controlled studies of psilocybin microdosing effects on cognition, mood, creativity, and brain activity. Directly addresses whether microdosing improves these domains (it does not, in this study).
- **Q3 (Methodological limitations):** HIGH. Exemplifies and explicitly discusses key methodological challenges: unblinding, expectation effects, dose variability, short study duration, sample size limitations. Provides quantitative data on unblinding rates.
- **Q5 (Evidence challenging the benefit narrative):** HIGH. A landmark null-finding study. Uses both frequentist and Bayesian methods to show evidence for the null hypothesis across multiple domains. Explicitly concludes that "expectation underlies at least some of the anecdotal benefits attributed to microdosing."
