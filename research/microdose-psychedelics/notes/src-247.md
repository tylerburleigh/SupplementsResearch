# src-247: Molecular design of a therapeutic LSD analogue with reduced hallucinogenic potential

**Citation:** Tuck JR, Dunlap LE, et al. PNAS 2025, Vol. 122, No. 16, e2416106122. Published April 14, 2025.
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2416106122

## Core Findings

JRT is a rationally designed constitutional isomer of LSD created by transposing only two atoms (moving the N,N-dimethylaminoethyl substituent from C3 to N1 of the indole). This minimal structural modification eliminates the indole N-H hydrogen bond with S242(5.46) in the 5-HT2A receptor, producing a compound with dramatically reduced hallucinogenic potential while retaining -- and in some measures exceeding -- LSD's neuroplasticity-promoting properties. A single dose produced a 46% increase in dendritic spine density in vivo, rescued chronic stress-induced cortical atrophy, and demonstrated potent antidepressant-like and procognitive effects.

## Compound Description

- **Name:** (+)-JRT (indolonaphthyridine constitutional isomer of LSD)
- **Design rationale:** Based on prior work showing that transposing the N,N-dimethylaminoethyl substituent from C3 to N1 in DMT reduced hallucinogenic potential (isoDMT analogues). Applied same logic to LSD's ergoline scaffold.
- **Synthesis:** De novo total synthesis in 12 steps, 11% overall yield. Could not be produced via semisynthesis from lysergic acid.
- **Mechanism:** Eliminates hydrogen bond between indole N-H and S242(5.46)/G238(5.42) in 5-HT2AR, accelerating ligand dissociation ~10-fold faster than LSD and disrupting beta-arrestin 2 recruitment while preserving Gq signaling.

## Receptor Pharmacology

- **Highly selective for serotonin receptors** across 55 CNS targets; no affinity for dopamine, histamine, or adrenergic receptors (unlike LSD).
- **5-HT2A:** Partial agonist (Emax = 81% in traditional assay; 33% in BRET-based Gq assay vs. 63% for LSD)
- **5-HT2B:** Partial agonist (Emax = 48%) -- potential cardiac valvulopathy risk noted
- **5-HT2C:** Near-full agonist (Emax = 89%) -- likely mediates antipsychotic-like effects
- **Ki range at 5-HT2 receptors:** 2-184 nM
- **Faster dissociation from 5-HT2A:** ~10x faster than LSD
- **Biased signaling:** Does not recruit beta-arrestin through 5-HT2A activation; G protein biased

## Neuroplasticity Findings (Q2)

### In vitro
- **DIV6 cortical neurons:** (+)-JRT showed comparable or greater efficacy than LSD or clozapine for promoting dendritic growth. Only (+)-JRT showed statistically significant increase in Sholl plot AUC vs. vehicle.
- **DIV19 cortical neurons:** (+)-JRT promoted dendritic spine growth vs. vehicle (P < 0.0001) and clozapine (P = 0.0005).
- **5-HT2R antagonist ketanserin** blocked (+)-JRT-induced spinogenesis, confirming serotonin receptor dependence.

### In vivo
- **Single dose (1 mg/kg, IP):** 46% increase in dendritic spine density in mPFC layer 1 at 24h, measured by S3EM (n = 3 female animals).
- **18% increase in synapse density** in mPFC; synapse size unchanged.
- **Rescue of chronic stress atrophy:** 20 days of corticosterone caused dramatic spine loss in mPFC layer 2/3; a single (+)-JRT dose completely rescued spine density to baseline levels.

## Therapeutic Behavioral Effects (Q4)

### Antidepressant-like effects
- **Forced swim test (rats):** (+)-JRT at least 100x more potent than ketamine; decreased immobility at all doses tested 24h after administration.
- **Sucrose preference test (mice):** Two doses (1 mg/kg, 48h apart) rescued CORT-induced anhedonia (F(1,7) = 6.664; P = 0.0364).
- **Probabilistic reward task (rats):** Rescued stress-induced deficits in response bias persisting at least 3 days after single dose, even under continued stress.

### Procognitive effects
- **Reversal learning (mice):** Single dose (1 mg/kg) completely rescued unpredictable mild stress-induced deficits in cognitive flexibility.

### Safety profile
- **No head-twitch response** at any dose; completely blocked LSD-induced HTR as pretreatment.
- **No PPI deficits** (unlike LSD); did not exacerbate MK-801-induced PPI deficits.
- **No SCZ-related gene expression:** LSD DEGs enriched in SCZ TWAS genes (P = 0.018); (+)-JRT DEGs not enriched (P = 0.557).
- **No serotonin syndrome-like responses** at 1 mg/kg.
- **Good ADME properties;** no hERG channel inhibition.

## Implications for Microdosing Field

1. **Neuroplasticity without hallucinogenic effects:** This is the strongest evidence yet that the neuroplasticity-promoting and therapeutic properties of psychedelics can be pharmacologically separated from their hallucinogenic effects through targeted structural modifications.
2. **Proof of concept for non-hallucinogenic psychoplastogens:** (+)-JRT demonstrates that exceptionally small modifications (two-atom transposition) can fundamentally alter a compound's hallucinogenic potential while enhancing its therapeutic profile.
3. **Relevance to contraindicated populations:** Addresses a critical gap -- psychedelics are contraindicated for patients with SCZ, bipolar disorder, or family history of psychosis, yet these populations exhibit cortical atrophy that psychoplastogens could address.
4. **Clinical exclusion context:** Notes that recent psilocybin trials excluded ~95% of volunteers due to contraindications.
5. **Mechanistic insight:** Partial 5-HT2A agonism (below a threshold of Gq activation) appears sufficient for neuroplasticity but insufficient for hallucinogenic effects, supporting the therapeutic potential of sub-hallucinogenic serotonergic activation.
6. **Limitation:** 5-HT2B partial agonism raises cardiac valvulopathy concerns for chronic use.

## Methodology

- Species: Rats (FST, PRT), mice (HTR, SPT, reversal learning, PPI, in vivo imaging), in vitro rat cortical neuron cultures
- Key techniques: S3EM electron microscopy, psychLight biosensor, BRET-based GPCR assays, Sholl analysis, transcriptomics
- Randomized, blinded experiments; power analyses conducted
- N varies by experiment; in vivo S3EM: n=3 female animals

## Limitations

1. Small sample sizes for some in vivo experiments (n=3 for S3EM)
2. 5-HT2B partial agonism -- chronic stimulation risk for cardiac valvulopathy
3. Behavioral effects primarily in rodent models; human translation uncertain
4. Does not directly address microdosing regimens (single-dose studies)
5. Mechanism of neuroplasticity promotion not fully confirmed via 5-HT2AR knockout studies
6. Sex differences observed (amphetamine hyperlocomotion reduction only in females)

## Claims to Verify

- "at least 100-fold more potent than ketamine" in FST (line 485-486): This is an approximation based on comparing lowest effective dose to ketamine's known minimum effective dose. Exact dose-response curves would confirm.
- "46% increase in dendritic spine density" (line 374): Based on n=3 female animals only. Should be corroborated with larger samples.

## Relevance

- **Q2 (Neuroplasticity mechanisms):** Strong direct evidence. Demonstrates that neuroplasticity-promoting effects are achievable through 5-HT2A partial agonism without full hallucinogenic signaling, and that cortical atrophy from chronic stress can be rescued by a single dose.
- **Q4 (Translational gap / non-hallucinogenic analogs):** Core evidence. JRT is a prime example of rational design producing a non-hallucinogenic psychedelic analog with therapeutic potential, directly addressing the translational barrier of hallucinogenic side effects.
