## Core Findings

This comprehensive review synthesizes preclinical evidence that psychedelics produce profound, long-lasting changes in neuronal physiology through 5-HT2AR activation and downstream signaling cascades. The authors frame psychedelics as **psychoplastogens** -- small molecules that rapidly promote structural and functional neuroplasticity -- and propose that the neurobiological mechanisms underlying acute (hallucinogenic) and sustained (therapeutic) effects may be distinct, enabling the engineering of non-hallucinogenic therapeutics.

## Psychoplastogen Concept

- Psychoplastogens are defined as small molecules that produce therapeutic effects by rapidly promoting structural and functional neuroplasticity (ref 83 in source; Olson lab coinage).
- In cortical cultures, psychedelics increase dendritic growth cone size, promote new neurite growth, increase dendritic spine density, and alter spine morphology (refs 84-89).
- Only short stimulation periods are required to induce long-lasting changes in cortical neuron growth (ref 90).
- Psychedelic-induced spine growth is associated with synaptogenesis (increased colocalization of pre- and postsynaptic markers; ref 84).

## Molecular Mechanisms of Neuroplasticity

### Receptor-Level Mechanisms
- **5-HT2AR is essential**: 5-HT2R antagonists block plasticity in vitro; 5-HT2AR knockout abolishes spine growth in vivo.
- **Intracellular 5-HT2ARs are critical**: A large population of 5-HT2ARs is localized to intracellular compartments in cortical neurons. Location bias explains why membrane-impermeable agonists (serotonin) cannot promote growth while psychedelics can.
- **TrkB binding**: LSD and psilocin may serve as positive allosteric modulators of BDNF signaling by directly binding to TrkB (Moliner et al. 2023, Nat Neurosci).

### Downstream Signaling Cascade
Psychoplastogenic effects require a multi-node cascade:
1. **5-HT2AR activation** (Gq signaling, PLC, IP3/DAG, Ca2+ release)
2. **TrkB engagement** (BDNF-TrkB-mTOR pathway)
3. **mTOR activation** (confirmed in human brain organoids; required for prosocial effects of LSD)
4. **AMPA receptor signaling** (required for plasticity)
5. **MAPK and CAMKII** (mediate IEG upregulation: arc, bdnf, egr-2)

### Gene Expression and Epigenetics
- Single LSD dose regulates only a small number of genes, but many implicate plasticity processes.
- IEGs upregulated: c-Fos, arc, egr-1, egr-2 (5-HT2AR-dependent).
- DOI dose-dependently increases cortical bdnf mRNA; BDNF inducible KO abolishes arc upregulation.
- Epigenetic effects: DOI increases enhancer levels of plasticity genes persisting >=7 days; repeated LSD increases CpG methylation of neuronal development genes.

## Structural Plasticity - Key In Vivo Evidence

| Compound | Effect | Duration | Reference |
|----------|--------|----------|-----------|
| DMT (single dose) | Increased spine density in PFC | 24h | ref 84 |
| DOI (single dose) | Increased spine density in PFC | 24h | ref 32 |
| 5-MeO-DMT (single dose) | Increased spine density in PFC | 24h | ref 81 |
| Psilocybin (single dose) | Increased spine density in cortex | >=1 month | ref 93 |
| 5-MeO-DMT (single dose) | Increased spine density | long-lasting | ref 94 |
| LSD (7-day repeated) | Increased spine density in PFC | - | ref 91 |
| DMT (chronic intermittent) | Spine retraction in female rats only | - | ref 92 |

- Key finding: psychedelics increase rate of spine **formation** but have little effect on spine **elimination**.
- Female mice show greater magnitude of structural plasticity response.
- Chronic intermittent DMT led to homeostatic spine retraction in females, suggesting overstimulation can counteract growth.

## Functional Plasticity

- Single doses of DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, and psilocybin increase spontaneous EPSC frequency and amplitude in cortical pyramidal neurons 24h post-administration.
- These functional changes are absent in 5-HT2AR KO mice.
- Single DOI administration enhances cortical long-term potentiation (LTP) measured 24h later.
- Repeated LSD increases burst activity of PFC pyramidal neurons and potentiates response to DOI and AMPA agonists (mTOR-dependent).

## Neuronal Survival and Neurogenesis

- Prolonged DOI exposure prevents excitotoxic and oxidative insults via mitochondrial biogenesis and 5-HT2AR-dependent sirtuin-1 activation.
- DMT promotes cell survival via sigma-1 receptor in hypoxic/ischemic models.
- Neurogenesis findings are mixed: 5-MeO-DMT induces neurogenesis (sigma-1 dependent), while DOI/LSD show no effect and psilocybin/25I-NBOMe decrease hippocampal neurogenesis.

## Non-Hallucinogenic Psychoplastogens

- Multiple non-hallucinogenic 5-HT2AR agonists promote structural plasticity: tabernanthalog (TBG), lisuride, IHCH-7079, ariadne.
- TBG rescues stress-induced spine loss; >30% of TBG-induced spines formed near sites of stress-induced spine loss; ~30% survived 12 days.
- A single dose of fluoxetine (SSRI) produced no measurable spine changes -- highlighting the uniqueness of psychoplastogen mechanisms.

## Relevance to Q2: Neuroplasticity Mechanisms at Cellular/Molecular Level

This source is **directly and centrally relevant** to Q2. It provides:
1. Comprehensive mechanistic cascade: 5-HT2AR -> TrkB -> mTOR -> structural plasticity
2. Evidence for intracellular receptor mechanisms (location bias) distinguishing psychedelics from endogenous serotonin
3. Epigenetic persistence mechanisms (>=7 days)
4. In vivo evidence for structural plasticity lasting >=1 month after a single dose
5. Functional plasticity correlates (EPSC changes, LTP enhancement)
6. Critical evidence that psychoplastogenic and hallucinogenic effects may be mechanistically separable

### Implications for Microdosing
- The finding that short stimulation periods produce long-lasting changes supports the plausibility of microdose regimens producing cumulative plasticity effects.
- The intracellular 5-HT2AR mechanism suggests even sub-threshold doses could engage plasticity pathways if intracellular concentrations are sufficient.
- However, the dose-response relationship for plasticity is not fully characterized at sub-hallucinogenic doses.
- The sex differences observed (greater plasticity in females) raise questions about microdose efficacy varying by sex.
- Chronic intermittent DMT caused spine retraction in females, a cautionary finding for repeated-dose regimens.

## Limitations

- Most structural plasticity data come from rodent models; human in vivo evidence is limited.
- The specific dose thresholds for psychoplastogenic vs. hallucinogenic effects are not precisely defined.
- Sex-dependent effects are noted but not mechanistically explained.
- The relationship between plasticity changes at the cellular level and behavioral/therapeutic outcomes is not fully established.
- Circuit-specific mechanisms (which circuits mediate plasticity vs. hallucinations) remain unknown.
