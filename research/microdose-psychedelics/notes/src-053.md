# src-053: Multimodal Creativity Assessments Following Acute and Sustained Microdosing of LSD

**Citation:** Murphy RJ, Sumner RL, Godfrey K, et al. Psychopharmacology (2025) 242:337-351. DOI: 10.1007/s00213-024-06680-z
**Venue:** Psychopharmacology | **Year:** 2025 | **Citations:** 7 | **Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial found **no acute or durable effect of 10 mcg LSD microdosing on creativity** as measured by four different assessment modalities (AUT, RAT, CAT, EPSQ) in 80 healthy adult males over a 6-week every-third-day protocol. Despite the null objective findings, participants had previously reported feeling more creative on dose days in the same trial, suggesting a dissociation between subjective experience and measurable creative output that may be attributable to placebo effects, positive mood uplift, or limitations in laboratory creativity testing.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel-group trial (MDLSD trial)
- **Participants:** 80 healthy adult males (LSD n=40, placebo n=40); 75 completed full course
- **Compound:** LSD, 10 mcg sublingual
- **Dosing schedule:** Every third day for 6 weeks (14 total doses)
- **Assessment timepoints:**
  - Baseline (drug-free)
  - Treatment visit (240 min after first dose, during acute effects)
  - Final visit (drug-free, approximately 2 days after last dose)
- **Creativity measures:**
  - Alternate Uses Test (AUT) -- divergent thinking
  - Remote Associates Task (RAT) -- convergent thinking
  - Consensual Assessment Technique (CAT) -- expert-rated visual art creativity
  - Everyday Problem-Solving Questionnaire (EPSQ) -- bespoke self-rated measure
- **Analysis:** Linear mixed effects models with Group and Visit as fixed effects, Participants as random effect; vocabulary (NIH Toolbox Picture Vocabulary Test) included as covariate for AUT and RAT; intention-to-treat; effect sizes as partial Eta squared

## Key Evidence

### Null results across all creativity measures
- AUT: No Group x Visit interaction on fluency, flexibility, elaboration, or originality (all p > 0.3, all eta2 < 0.015)
- RAT: No Group x Visit interaction on number correct or attempted (all p > 0.3, eta2 <= 0.013)
- CAT: No Group x Visit interaction on creativity or technical goodness ratings by 16 expert art teacher raters (p > 0.07)
- EPSQ: No Group x Visit interaction on idea generation difficulty or idea effectiveness

### Vocabulary as a strong predictor
- Vocabulary significantly predicted all AUT measures (fluency F=5.99 p=0.017; flexibility F=20.42 p<0.001; elaboration F=9.78 p=0.002; originality F=11.63 p=0.001)
- Vocabulary significantly predicted RAT correct answers (F=45.04, p<0.001, eta2=0.355) but not items attempted
- This highlights the importance of controlling for linguistic ability in creativity tasks

### Practice/placebo effect on self-report
- EPSQ idea generation difficulty showed significant main effect of Visit (F=47.35, p<0.001, eta2=0.378) in both groups, with participants rating generation as easier at Final vs Baseline regardless of drug condition

### Subjective-objective dissociation
- Previously reported VAS data from same trial: participants reported feeling more creative on dose days
- However, this subjective effect was not observed on dose days where participants remained blind to condition
- Creativity had the highest baseline expectancy ratings of any domain

## Methodology Notes

- **Strengths:** First study to use multimodal creativity battery (covering divergent thinking, convergent thinking, expert assessment, and self-assessment); well-powered parallel design with n=40 per group; controlled for vocabulary; preregistered RCT
- **Limitations:**
  - Males only (no female participants)
  - Single dose level (10 mcg); some prior creativity studies used 20 mcg
  - Creativity tasks were secondary measures; administered at 240 min post-dose rather than at peak effects (~150 min)
  - Tasks not counterbalanced within battery (potential fatigue effects on later tasks)
  - COVID-19 restrictions caused some Baseline/Final AUT and RAT tasks to be administered at home rather than in-lab (follow-up showed no effect of study wave on outcomes)
  - No baseline control for visual art ability in the CAT
  - Test-retest reliability not established for this specific item combination at 3 timepoints

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (Human clinical evidence on cognition effects):** Highly relevant. This is a well-designed RCT providing strong evidence of null cognitive effects of LSD microdosing on creativity -- one of the most frequently claimed cognitive benefits. The dissociation between subjective and objective measures is important for interpreting self-report data. The vocabulary covariate finding is methodologically instructive for future studies.

**Q7 (Dosing protocols comparison):** Moderately relevant. Provides detailed dosing protocol (10 mcg LSD, every third day, 6 weeks, 14 doses sublingual) and notes that creativity tasks were administered at 240 min post-dose. Comparison with other protocols: Bershad used crossover with 13 and 26 mcg; this study used a lower fixed dose in a parallel design.
