## Core Findings

This review provides a comparative pharmacological overview of four classes of hallucinogenic/psychotropic compounds (LSD/psilocybin as classic psychedelics, ketamine as a dissociative anesthetic, and MDMA as an entactogen), summarizing preclinical and clinical evidence for their therapeutic potential in psychiatric disorders. The review underscores that the mechanisms of hallucinogens are more complex than previously thought, involving effects beyond 5-HT2A agonism (including dopaminergic, glutamatergic, and transcriptional mechanisms), and highlights significant translational challenges in moving from rodent models to human therapeutics.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Pharmacology of Different Compounds

1. **Classic psychedelics (LSD, psilocybin, DMT):** Primarily 5-HT2A agonists, but with pleiotropic activity at 5-HT1A, 5-HT2B, 5-HT2C, 5-HT6, 5-HT7, dopamine D1/D2/D4, and TAAR1 receptors (lines 93-137).
2. **LSD dose-dependent effects:** Low doses (up to 30 mcg/kg) decrease 5-HT neuron firing without affecting VTA dopamine firing; higher doses (40-60 mcg/kg) decrease dopaminergic VTA firing (lines 108-113).
3. **LSD behavioral phases:** Drug discrimination tasks in rodents show an initial 5-HT2A-mediated phase followed by a dopamine D2-mediated phase (lines 117-120).
4. **Ketamine:** Noncompetitive NMDA receptor antagonist and mTORC1 pathway activator. Its metabolite (2R,6R)-HNK exerts antidepressant effects with less NMDA receptor activity, suggesting NMDAR-independent mechanisms (lines 318-404).
5. **MDMA:** Releases supraphysiological levels of serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine via SERT, DAT, and NET. Also stimulates oxytocin, vasopressin, and cortisol release (lines 489-496).

### Neuroplasticity Mechanisms

1. **mTORC1 pathway (ketamine):** A single dose of ketamine and HNK induces transient increases in phospho-mTOR, phospho-p70S6 kinase, and phospho-4E-BP1 in PFC and hippocampus. Intracerebroventricular rapamycin pretreatment blocks ketamine-induced synaptic and behavioral effects in rodents (lines 375-398).
2. **Synaptic plasticity:** Ketamine induces both synaptic and structural plasticity in hippocampus, mPFC, and lateral habenula, involving local dendritic mRNA translation of AMPAR subunits GluA1 and GluA2 (lines 367-404).
3. **Signaling cascades (psychedelics):** Both hallucinogenic and nonhallucinogenic 5-HT2A agonists activate intracellular signaling cascades in cortical pyramidal neurons, modulating beta-arrestin, EGR1, and EGR2 (lines 101-107).
4. **Brain connectivity (human neuroimaging):** LSD and psilocybin increase thalamocortical connectivity to sensory-somatomotor regions while decreasing Default Mode Network integrity. Psilocybin decreases claustrum connectivity with DMN while increasing frontoparietal control network connectivity (lines 221-275).

### Translational Gap (Preclinical vs. Clinical)

1. **No gold-standard preclinical protocol:** Dosing strategy, animal model choice, behavioral measures, time intervals, and environmental context all confound psychedelic research in animals (lines 150-186).
2. **Timing of antidepressant effects:** Psychedelics are not reliably rapid antidepressants in rodents; measurable changes may require 4+ weeks post-administration, echoing clinical timelines (lines 179-184).
3. **mTORC1 translational contradiction:** In rodents, rapamycin (mTORC1 inhibitor) blocks ketamine's antidepressant effects. In humans, peripheral rapamycin coadministration enhanced ketamine's antidepressant effect -- a striking preclinical-clinical discrepancy (lines 447-454).
4. **NMDAR antagonism insufficient:** Other NMDAR antagonists (memantine, MK-801) lack ketamine's clinical antidepressant efficacy, suggesting ketamine's mechanism extends beyond NMDAR antagonism in ways not fully captured by animal models (lines 335-346).
5. **Set and setting in animals:** Wistar-Kyoto rats given psilocybin showed distinct behavioral responses depending on testing schedule and environmental context, paralleling human "set and setting" (lines 186-210).
6. **MDMA circuit-specific mechanisms:** Prosocial effects involve 5-HT release in nucleus accumbens; fear extinction effects involve 5-HT release in basolateral amygdala. Translating circuit-specific findings to clinical populations remains challenging (lines 532-556).

### Clinical Evidence Summary

- **Ketamine:** 50-70% response rate at 1 day after single infusion; repeated treatments sustain effect. Subanesthetic dose: 0.5 mg/kg over 40 min (lines 408-428).
- **MDMA:** Phase 2 trials show robust, durable PTSD symptom reduction lasting at least 1 year. Phase 3 trial underway. Breakthrough Therapy designation granted by FDA (lines 593-621).
- **Psilocybin:** Open-label and double-blind studies show effects in depression and anxiety, though meta-analysis suggests more studies needed (lines 658-670).
- **LSD:** Promising results in patients with depression and anxiety; clinical trials ongoing for MDD (lines 671-673).

## Methodology

This is a narrative review (Symposium/Mini-Symposium paper) synthesizing preclinical (rodent behavioral pharmacology, electrophysiology, molecular biology) and clinical (neuroimaging, controlled trials) evidence. It does not present original data. The review covers pharmacology, behavioral effects, neuroimaging findings, and clinical trial results across four compound classes.

## Limitations

1. Narrative review without systematic search methodology -- potential for selective citation.
2. Preclinical psychedelic literature is sparse and results often appear conflicting without being truly comparable due to methodological variability.
3. Clinical neuroimaging studies in psychiatric populations are still scarce.
4. The review does not deeply address microdosing specifically (though it references DMT microdosing by Cameron et al., 2019).
5. Published in 2021; clinical trial landscape has advanced significantly since then, particularly for MDMA Phase 3 and psilocybin.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q2 (Mechanisms of action - 5-HT2A, neuroplasticity):** Highly relevant. Provides detailed pharmacological profiles of all four compound classes with receptor-level specificity. Covers mTORC1-mediated neuroplasticity for ketamine, 5-HT2A signaling cascades for psychedelics, and SERT-mediated 5-HT release for MDMA. Discusses how both hallucinogenic and nonhallucinogenic 5-HT2A agonists activate similar intracellular cascades.

**Q4 (Translational gap):** Highly relevant. Explicitly addresses preclinical-clinical discrepancies including the mTORC1/rapamycin contradiction, the absence of standardized preclinical protocols, dosing strategy confounds, timing of antidepressant effects, and the challenge of translating circuit-specific MDMA findings to clinical populations. The review's structure (organizing preclinical and clinical evidence side-by-side for each compound) inherently highlights translational gaps.
