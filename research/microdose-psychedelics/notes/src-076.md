# src-076: Rootman et al. 2022 - Psilocybin Microdosing and Mood/Mental Health

**Citation:** Rootman JM, Kiraga M, Kryskow P, Harvey K, Stamets P, Santos-Brault E, Kuypers KPC, Walsh Z. (2022). *Scientific Reports*, 12:11091. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-14512-3

**Venue:** Scientific Reports (Nature, open access) | **Citations:** 36 | **Quality:** ok

---

## Core Findings

In the largest prospective observational study of psilocybin microdosing to date (n=953 microdosers, n=180 non-microdosing controls), microdosers showed small-to-medium improvements in mood and mental health over ~30 days relative to controls. Effects were consistent across age, gender, and mental health status. The Stamets stack (psilocybin + lion's mane + niacin) did NOT enhance mood/mental health outcomes beyond psilocybin alone, but showed psychomotor benefits specific to older adults (55+).

## Study Design

- **Design:** Naturalistic, observational, longitudinal with non-microdosing comparator group
- **Duration:** Baseline + 1-month follow-up (22-35 days)
- **Platform:** iOS app (Quantified Citizen)
- **Recruitment:** Nov 2019 - May 2021 via media and psychedelic education events
- **Hypotheses:** Not pre-registered
- **Analysis:** Mixed linear effects models with Microdose*Time interaction as primary test

## Sample

- **Total N:** 1,133 (953 microdosers, 180 controls)
- **Demographics:** Predominantly White (85.7%), male (69.7%), ages 25-54 (79.2%), heterosexual (88%), employed full-time (59.5%)
- **Mental health concerns:** 28.5% reported current psychological/mental health/addiction concerns
- **Group differences:** Microdosers more likely to be older (p<0.01), White (p=0.03), employed full-time (p<0.01)
- **Exclusivity:** iOS only (no Android); recruited through pro-psychedelic venues

## Measures

- **DASS-21:** Depression, anxiety, stress (7 items each, 4-point scale)
- **PANAS:** Positive and negative affect (20 items; 1 negative item dropped due to technical error, scores converted to percentages)
- **Finger Tap Test:** Psychomotor performance (10s alternating taps)
- **Spatial Span (Corsi):** Spatial memory
- **PASAT:** Processing speed

## Key Results

### Mood and Mental Health (Q1)

| Outcome | Microdose*Time F | p | Between-group d |
|---------|-----------------|---|----------------|
| DASS Depression | 17.91 | <0.01 | 0.39 |
| DASS Anxiety | 18.33 | <0.01 | 0.32 |
| DASS Stress | 15.60 | <0.01 | 0.26 |
| PANAS Positive | 59.98 | <0.01 | 0.66 |
| PANAS Negative | 33.76 | <0.01 | 0.40 |

- Effects robust to outlier removal (all p<0.01)
- Effects maintained in subset who had NOT microdosed prior to baseline (n=594 for DASS; n=479 for PANAS)
- Consistent across mental health status (no moderation by MHC)
- Consistent across age (no PANAS moderation by age)
- **Gender moderation:** Females showed stronger depression reduction than males (F=6.61, p=0.01)

### Clinical Significance

Among microdosers with mental health concerns:
- Depression: 18.85 -> 11.73 (moderate to mild per DASS cutoffs)
- Anxiety: 11.04 -> 7.46
- Stress: 19.93 -> 13.91

### Dosing Practices (Q7)

| Parameter | Distribution |
|-----------|-------------|
| Dose: Low (<=0.1g) | 16.8% |
| Dose: Medium (0.1-0.3g) | 72.6% |
| Dose: High (>=0.3g) | 10.6% |
| Days: <10/month | 33.9% |
| Days: 11-20/month | 53.4% |
| Days: 21+/month | 12.7% |

### Stacking / Stamets Stack Results (Q7)

| Stack Group | n | % |
|-------------|---|---|
| Psilocybin only | 385 | 40.4% |
| Psilocybin + HE (lion's mane) | 304 | 31.9% |
| Psilocybin + HE + B3 (niacin) | 264 | 27.7% |

**Mood/mental health:** No differences between stacking conditions on DASS or PANAS (all F<2.44, p>0.10). The Stamets stack did NOT enhance mood or mental health beyond psilocybin alone.

**Psychomotor (finger tap):** Among older adults (55+), psilocybin+HE+B3 showed greater improvement than psilocybin alone (3-way interaction F(1,732)=8.4, p=0.004). This effect was NOT seen in younger participants. Authors caution this finding may be anomalous due to small subgroup sizes.

### Cognition

- No microdosing effects on spatial memory or processing speed (both p>0.63)

## Limitations

1. **Observational design** - no randomization, no blinding, cannot establish causation
2. **Expectancy/placebo confound** - participants knew their condition; recruited from pro-psychedelic venues
3. **Self-selection bias** - favorable responders may be overrepresented
4. **iOS only** - excludes Android users, potential socioeconomic bias
5. **Dose not controlled** - potency/composition of mushrooms unverified
6. **No adverse effects assessed** - safety profile not examined
7. **PANAS technical error** - one negative affect item dropped; converted to percentages
8. **Small subgroups** for stacking analyses, especially older adults in specific stack conditions
9. **Competing interests:** Multiple authors have financial ties to psychedelic/mushroom companies. Paul Stamets has patents pending on the psilocybin+HE+B3 combination and sells lion's mane supplements.
10. **Hypotheses not pre-registered**
11. **Short follow-up** (only 1 month)

## Relevance

- **Q1 (Human evidence on mood effects):** Highly relevant. Largest prospective microdosing study with a comparator group. Shows consistent small-to-medium mood improvements. However, the observational design and lack of blinding limit causal inference. The robustness analyses (outlier removal, restricting to new microdosers) strengthen confidence somewhat.
- **Q7 (Dosing protocols):** Directly relevant. Provides detailed breakdown of real-world dosing practices. The null result for stacking on mood/mental health is important and counter to popular claims. The psychomotor finding for older adults with the Stamets stack is novel but preliminary.

## Claims to Verify

1. **DASS stress effect sizes in Table 2:** The within-group d for microdosers (0.63) appears large relative to the raw score change (15.75 -> 11.04). The SD at baseline is listed as 6.7, which seems low compared to typical DASS stress SDs and the non-microdoser SD of 9.86. This may be a typo or data entry issue.
2. **Sample sizes in robustness checks:** "479 participants who were microdosing at the time of study initiation" for PANAS appears to contradict the DASS robustness check which says "594 participants who did not report microdosing prior to baseline" - these are likely referring to different analyses (one excluding prior microdosers, one including only prior microdosers) but the wording could be clearer.
