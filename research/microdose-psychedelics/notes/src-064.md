## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive review covering the neurobiological mechanisms by which psychedelic compounds (ketamine, LSD, psilocybin, DMT/5-MeO-DMT/ayahuasca, MDMA, DOI) may exert therapeutic effects in psychiatry. It focuses on three pillars: (1) neuroplasticity via mTOR, BDNF, and early growth response pathways; (2) immunomodulation via HPA axis, NF-kB, and cytokines; and (3) modulation of serotonin, dopamine, glutamate, GABA, and norepinephrine systems. The review highlights safety concerns including cardiac 5-HT2B receptor overstimulation from repeated microdosing and serotonin syndrome risk.

## Neuroplasticity Mechanisms (Q2)

### Pathways Covered
- **mTOR pathway**: Psychedelics (especially ketamine) activate mTOR, a key intracellular signaling pathway altered in MDD, ASD, and SCZ, leading to BDNF-mediated stimulation of dendritic, synaptic, and neuronal plasticity (lines 2110-2124).
- **BDNF-TrkB (NTRK2) signaling**: Central to neuroplastic effects across all psychedelic compounds. BDNF enhances AMPA receptor delivery to synapses, which may be involved in psychedelic-induced neural plasticity (lines 2088-2109).
- **Synaptogenesis**: DOI (close LSD relative) rapidly increases spine density growth via kalirin-7-mediated synaptogenic pathway while increasing Egr-1 in mouse somatosensory cortex (lines 2463-2466).
- **Early growth response (EGR) transcription factors**: LSD increases cFOS, Egr-1, and Egr-2 in neuronal cultures; EGR family involved in synaptic plasticity and neurogenesis (lines 2413-2472).

### Compound-Specific Findings
- **Ketamine**: Increases BDNF in PFC and hippocampus (CA3, dentate gyrus); increases spine density and synaptic plasticity markers (synapsin 1, PSD-95, mGluR1); prevents stress-induced dendritic spine loss (lines 2125-2177).
- **DMT/5-MeO-DMT/Ayahuasca**: Single ayahuasca dose increases circulating BDNF in both healthy controls and TRD patients; repeated ayahuasca increases hippocampal BDNF in female rats; single 5-MeO-DMT dose increases neurogenesis in adult mouse dentate gyrus (lines 2178-2196).
- **LSD**: Microdoses increase circulating BDNF in healthy volunteers; increases DHEA (S1R agonist with neurogenic properties); upregulates CCAAT/enhancer-binding protein-beta in PFC affecting synaptic scaling (lines 2398-2425).
- **Psilocybin**: Modulates neurotrophic-related gene expression preferentially in PFC (CEBPB, c-Fos, Dusp1, Ikba); biphasic effect on hippocampal neurogenesis -- low dose trend toward increase, high doses (1-5 mg/kg) decrease neurogenesis (lines 2473-2501).

### Critical Caveats
- Repeated DMT may hinder synaptic plasticity in a sex-specific manner (females) (lines 2392-2397).
- Microdosing may induce anxiogenic (not anxiolytic) and neurotic effects, confusion, and potentially impact metabolism and hinder synaptic plasticity (lines 810-818).
- Psilocybin's biphasic neurogenesis effect raises dose-dependency concerns.

## Safety Profile (Q6)

### Cardiovascular Risks (PRIMARY CONCERN FOR MICRODOSING)
- **Cardiac 5-HT2B receptor overstimulation** is the main off-target concern with repeated microdosing of serotonergic psychedelics (lines 5703-5741).
- Can lead to valvular fibrosis, thickening, and regurgitation (analogous to fenfluramine-phentermine-induced valvulopathy, which was retracted from market) (lines 5715-5719).
- Ergot alkaloids (ergotamine, methysergide) -- structurally related to LSD -- caused valvular damage requiring replacement (lines 5727-5731).
- Pulmonary hypertension is another risk from repeated serotonergic psychedelic administration (lines 5733-5741).
- One case report of cardiopulmonary arrest after frequent ingestion of Psilocybe semilanceata for 1 month (lines 5742-5745).

### General Safety in Clinical Settings
- No deaths reported from medical-grade psychedelics in controlled settings (line 5321-5323).
- Mild to moderate acute physical side effects: headaches, nausea/vomiting, increased blood pressure, heart rate, body temperature (lines 5342-5358).
- Cardiovascular effects likely mediated by serotonergic action; should not be overlooked in patients with pre-existing cardiac conditions (lines 5355-5358).

### Serotonin Syndrome Risk
- Potential risk when combining serotonergic psychedelics with SSRIs/SNRIs or MAOIs (lines 5541-5566).
- No cases reported in clinical settings, but should be considered; SSRIs may need to be suspended before therapy (lines 5567-5574).

### Psychiatric Risks
- Risk of switch to mania in bipolar disorder patients (lines 5449-5475).
- Risk of psychosis, especially in patients with SCZ, BD, or personality disorder history (lines 5476-5498).
- Hallucinogen-persisting perception disorder (HPPD) -- low incidence but reported (lines 5682-5700).
- Preliminary data: ~1 in 5 patients experience physical discomfort and ~1 in 10 experience anxiety when microdosing (lines 827-831).

### Abuse Potential
- Weak abuse potential profile overall (lines 5644-5681).
- Animal studies show acute LSD, psilocybin, mescaline fail to produce reliable evidence of abuse; repeated LSD may actually attenuate rewarding effects of methamphetamine (lines 5657-5665).

## Full-Dose vs. Microdose Comparison

The review contains a dedicated section (I.G, "Full Dosing versus Microdosing," lines 726-866) that discusses:
- Microdosing defined as "1% of the pharmacologically active dose, up to maximum 100 mg" (line 737).
- LSD doses <20 mcg considered microdoses, 21-30 mcg minidoses, >30 mcg psychedelic doses (line 802-805).
- Microdosing is sought for cognitive/emotional enhancement and by patients dissatisfied with conventional treatments.
- Strong biological effects reported at very low doses, especially anti-inflammatory effects of DOI (5-HT2A agonist).
- Whether microdosing elicits the full spectrum of therapeutic improvement as full doses remains unascertained.
- Microdosing may have anxiogenic effects (not anxiolytic), induce confusion, and potentially hinder synaptic plasticity -- contrasting the popular belief.
- Key concern: repeated cardiac 5-HT2B stimulation from chronic microdosing regimens.

## Immunomodulation (Brief)
- Psychedelics modulate HPA axis, NF-kB, and cytokine production (TNF-a, IL-1, IL-6, IL-10).
- 5-MeO-DMT upregulates NMDAR, ERK1/2, CREB, CAMK2; downregulates NF-kB, NFAT, TLR, mGluR5, and multiple cytokines.
- Anti-inflammatory effects potent even at very low doses (DOI).
- Microdosing might help alleviate symptoms partly via anti-inflammatory 5-HT2A agonism.

## Limitations
- Review article, not primary data (but synthesizes extensive primary evidence).
- Many cited preclinical findings lack replication in humans.
- Microdosing evidence is largely preliminary and based on limited studies.
- Neuroplasticity claims for microdosing are primarily inferred from full-dose data and animal models.

---

## Claims to Verify
1. **Psilocybin biphasic neurogenesis**: The claim that 0.1 mg/kg showed "non-significant trend" while 1-5 mg/kg decreased neurogenesis (Catlow et al., 2013). The statistical non-significance of the low-dose increase should be confirmed in the original paper.
2. **1 in 5 physical discomfort from microdosing**: Attributed to Anderson et al., 2019 -- this is from a survey study, not an RCT, which may overestimate or underestimate incidence.
3. **LSD microdose BDNF increase**: Attributed to Hutten et al., 2020 -- verify whether this was a controlled study and the magnitude of the BDNF change.
