# src-072: Associations Between Polymorphisms in Genes Related to Oxidative Stress and DNA Repair, Interactions With Serum Antioxidants, and Prostate Cancer Risk

**Citation:** Gong et al. (2022), Frontiers in Oncology, 11 citations, DOI: 10.3389/fonc.2021.808715

## Core Findings

This nested case-control study within the Prostate Cancer Prevention Trial (PCPT) examined whether genetic polymorphisms in oxidative stress and DNA repair pathways interact with serum antioxidant levels (including lycopene) to modify prostate cancer risk. Among 1,598 cases and 1,706 controls, there was **no main effect of serum lycopene on prostate cancer risk** in the placebo arm. However, several gene-lycopene interactions were observed: in the placebo arm, APEX1-rs1760944 was associated with increased prostate cancer risk among men with higher serum lycopene (p-interaction=0.002), while NQO1-rs1800566 was associated with decreased risk among men with higher lycopene (p-interaction=0.04). In the finasteride arm, NOS3-rs1799983 showed a differential association by lycopene level: reduced risk with lower lycopene, increased risk with higher lycopene (p-interaction=0.03).

## Key Evidence and Data Points

- **Study population:** 1,598 prostate cancer cases and 1,706 frequency-matched controls from PCPT (n=18,882 total trial); primarily White (86%)
- **Design:** Nested case-control within a double-blind RCT (finasteride vs. placebo); all participants had biopsy-determined outcomes
- **No main effect of serum lycopene on prostate cancer risk** (previously reported by the same group, ref 14)
- **Gene-lycopene interactions:**
  - Placebo arm: APEX1-rs1760944 TT genotype OR=1.80 (95% CI: 1.18-2.75) among high-lycopene men; p-interaction=0.002
  - Placebo arm: NQO1-rs1800566 CT genotype OR=0.69 (95% CI: 0.50-0.94) among high-lycopene men; p-interaction=0.04
  - Finasteride arm: NOS3-rs1799983 TT genotype OR=0.43 (95% CI: 0.25-0.73) among low-lycopene men; p-interaction=0.03
- **Median serum lycopene:** ~0.35-0.37 ug/mL (no significant difference between cases and controls)
- **Lycopene cutoff for stratification:** <0.38 ug/mL (low) vs. >=0.38 ug/mL (high), based on control median

## Methodology

- Nested case-control within PCPT, a 7-year randomized placebo-controlled trial of finasteride
- Serum antioxidants (lycopene, alpha-carotene, beta-carotene, beta-cryptoxanthin, retinol) measured by HPLC from pre-diagnostic blood samples
- 16 SNPs genotyped across 12 genes in oxidative stress and DNA repair pathways
- Multivariable logistic regression adjusted for age, race, education, BMI, family history, diabetes, smoking, physical activity, alcohol
- Effect modification tested via genotype x antioxidant interaction term (likelihood ratio test)
- Separate analyses for placebo and finasteride arms, and for low-grade vs. high-grade cancer

## Limitations

1. Multiple testing not systematically corrected (hypothesis-driven candidate SNP approach)
2. Sample size limited when stratifying by tumor grade and antioxidant levels -- may be underpowered for small effect sizes or interactions
3. Overall associations for main effects were moderate
4. Only carotenoids and retinol examined; other antioxidants not included
5. Primarily White population (86%) -- limited generalizability
6. Interactions, while statistically significant, involve small cell sizes in some strata

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1 (Prospective cohort/RCT evidence on lycopene and prostate cancer)
**Moderately relevant.** This study comes from a large RCT (PCPT) with prospective serum antioxidant measurement. Critically, it reaffirms the previously published finding from the same PCPT dataset that there was **no main effect of serum lycopene on prostate cancer risk**. The gene-antioxidant interaction findings suggest lycopene's role may depend on genetic background.

### Q5 (Evidence contradicting the popular narrative that lycopene reduces prostate cancer risk)
**Relevant.** The finding of no main effect of serum lycopene on prostate cancer risk in the PCPT cohort contradicts the popular narrative. Moreover, the APEX1-lycopene interaction showed increased risk among men with higher lycopene carrying certain genotypes, which is the opposite of what the protective narrative would predict.

### Q6 (Mechanisms of action and alignment with clinical outcomes)
**Relevant.** This study directly examines lycopene's hypothesized mechanism (oxidative stress reduction) at the genetic level. The complex gene-antioxidant interactions suggest that lycopene's mechanistic role is not straightforward and depends on genetic polymorphisms in oxidative stress pathways. The findings suggest that lycopene interacts with DNA repair gene variants, but not uniformly in a protective direction.

## Claims to Verify

- "No main effect of serum lycopene" -- this is stated as a previously published finding (ref 14) rather than directly demonstrated in this paper's results. The original PCPT lycopene analysis should be consulted for the primary data.
- The interaction p-values (e.g., p=0.002 for APEX1-lycopene) are notable, but no multiple testing correction was applied across the many SNP-antioxidant combinations tested, so these should be interpreted cautiously.
- Sample sizes in stratified cells are sometimes small (e.g., NOS3 TT genotype in low-lycopene finasteride arm: 28 cases/53 controls), which can produce unstable estimates.
