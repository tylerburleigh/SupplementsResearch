# src-041: Cochrane Systematic Review -- Lycopene for Prevention of Prostate Cancer

**Source:** Ilic D, et al. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2011. PMID: 22071840.
**Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (Cochrane)
**Quality:** Peer-reviewed, Cochrane gold-standard methodology
**Metadata note:** Metadata JSON fields (title, authors, year, abstract, venue) were empty; content was extracted from PubMed landing page abstract.

## Core Findings

This Cochrane systematic review found **insufficient evidence to either support or refute the use of lycopene for the prevention of prostate cancer**. Only three RCTs met inclusion criteria, totaling just 154 participants. Meta-analysis showed no statistically significant difference in PSA levels between lycopene and control groups (MD -0.34, 95% CI -2.01 to 1.32). Two of the three included studies had a high risk of bias.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

- **Search scope:** MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane CENTRAL; 64 citations screened, 3 RCTs included.
- **Total participants across all included RCTs: 154** (very small sample).
- **PSA levels (meta-analysis of 3 RCTs):** Mean difference -0.34 ng/mL (95% CI -2.01 to 1.32) -- not statistically significant.
- **Serum lycopene levels:** MD 0.39 ug/mL (95% CI -0.19 to 0.98) -- not statistically significant.
- **Prostate cancer incidence:** Only one study reported this outcome (10% lycopene group vs. 30% control) -- cannot be meta-analyzed and comes from a high-risk-of-bias study.
- **Prostate cancer mortality:** No studies reported this outcome.
- **Risk of bias:** Two of three included studies rated as "high" risk of bias.
- **Clinical heterogeneity:** All three studies differed in design, participant characteristics, and lycopene allocation, limiting meta-analysis value.

### Included Studies
1. **Bunker 2007** -- RCT of lycopene supplementation in Tobago men with high prostate cancer risk.
2. **Mohanty 2005** -- Lycopene as chemopreventive agent in high-grade prostate intraepithelial neoplasia.
3. **Schwarz 2008** -- Lycopene in benign prostate hyperplasia progression.

## Methodology

- Standard Cochrane methodology: systematic search, independent selection, data extraction, risk of bias assessment (Cochrane tool), meta-analysis using mean difference with random effects.
- Search conducted August 2011; no language restrictions.
- Inclusion limited to RCTs investigating lycopene for prostate cancer prevention.

## Limitations

- Only 3 RCTs with 154 total participants -- severely underpowered.
- High risk of bias in 2 of 3 studies.
- Clinical heterogeneity precluded meaningful pooling for most outcomes.
- No data on prostate cancer mortality at all.
- Search date is 2011; does not capture any trials published after that date.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (large prospective cohort studies and RCTs):** Directly relevant. This is the most authoritative synthesis of RCT evidence on lycopene and prostate cancer prevention as of 2011. Concludes there is no robust RCT evidence supporting lycopene for prostate cancer prevention. However, the review is now dated (2011) and may not reflect newer trials.

**Q5 (evidence contradicting popular narrative):** Strongly relevant. This Cochrane review is a key piece of contradictory evidence -- it found no statistically significant benefit of lycopene on PSA levels or prostate cancer incidence from RCTs, and explicitly states evidence is "insufficient to either support or refute" lycopene use. The tiny sample (154 participants) and high risk of bias in most studies undermines the popular claim that lycopene prevents prostate cancer.

**Q4 (tradeoffs between supplements and whole-food sources):** Tangentially relevant -- the included studies used various lycopene preparations, but the review does not directly address supplement vs. whole-food comparisons.

## Claims to Verify

- **154 total participants:** This number appears in the abstract. The three individual studies (Bunker 2007, Mohanty 2005, Schwarz 2008) would need to be checked individually to confirm the sum adds to 154. This is a small number and seems plausible for pilot-scale RCTs.
- **10% vs. 30% prostate cancer incidence:** Reported from a single study (likely Mohanty 2005, given its focus on high-grade PIN). The small sample size makes this estimate very imprecise.
