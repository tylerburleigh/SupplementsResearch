# src-031: Lycopene intake and prostate cancer risk in men at high cardiovascular risk

**Citation:** Lopez-Solis et al., BMC Medicine 23:627 (2025). DOI: 10.1186/s12916-025-04440-0
**Study type:** Prospective cohort (secondary analysis nested within PREDIMED RCT)
**Quality:** ok -- full paper text available via open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0)

## Core Findings

In 2,970 men aged 55-80 at high cardiovascular risk from the PREDIMED trial, those in the highest quartile of cumulative lycopene intake (mean 6.1 mg/day) had a 54% lower risk of prostate cancer compared to the lowest quartile (mean 1.7 mg/day) over a mean 5.8-year follow-up (HR: 0.46; 95% CI: 0.23-0.95; p-trend=0.035, fully adjusted model). A nonlinear dose-response was observed with significant protection emerging above 4.9 mg/day (HR: 0.36; 95% CI: 0.13-0.98; p-nonlinearity=0.0307). Only 104 prostate cancer cases were identified, limiting statistical power.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **Population:** N=2,970 men, aged 55-80, all at high cardiovascular risk (type 2 diabetes or >=3 major CV risk factors), recruited 2003-2009 in Spain. From original 3,165 men, exclusions for implausible energy intake (n=94), baseline cancer (n=93), no follow-up (n=3), prostate cancer as second malignancy (n=5).
- **Dietary assessment:** 137-item FFQ administered annually by trained dietitians. Lycopene intake estimated using USDA FoodData Central carotenoid database. Cumulative weighted averages used for analysis.
- **Primary sources of lycopene:** Tomatoes and tomato products (69.6%: raw tomatoes 55.3%, gazpacho 9.6%, tomato sauce 4.7%), watermelon (30.1%).
- **Lycopene intake by quartile:** Q1 mean 1.7 mg/day (SD 0.6); Q2+Q3 mean 3.3 mg/day (SD 0.5); Q4 mean 6.1 mg/day (SD 1.9).
- **Prostate cancer cases:** 104 total (32 in Q1, 57 in Q2+Q3, 15 in Q4) over mean 5.8-year follow-up.
- **Main result (Model 3, fully adjusted):** Q4 vs Q1 HR 0.46 (95% CI 0.23-0.95), p-trend=0.035. Adjusted for age, intervention group, education, BMI, physical activity, total energy intake, alcohol, smoking, family cancer history, cumulative fruit/vegetable/dairy consumption. Stratified by recruitment center.
- **Dose-response:** Restricted cubic splines showed nonlinear relationship (p=0.0307). Threshold at 4.9 mg/day, equivalent to ~175 g tomato or ~110 g watermelon.
- **Sensitivity analyses:** Robust across exclusions of <2-year follow-up, extreme intake values, inclusion of second malignancy cases.
- **Stratified analyses:** No statistically significant interactions by age, smoking, MedDiet adherence, diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, or other carotenoids.

## Methodology

- Prospective cohort analysis nested within PREDIMED trial (ISRCTN35739639).
- Time-dependent Cox proportional hazards models, stratified by recruitment center.
- Three sequential adjustment models with progressive confounder control.
- Restricted cubic spline regression for dose-response (knots at 25th, 50th, 75th percentiles: 2.5, 3.3, 4.3 mg/day).
- Outcome adjudication by blinded Clinical Event Committee; all cases confirmed adenocarcinomas via prostate biopsy.
- Proportional hazards assumption tested using Schoenfeld residuals.

## Limitations

1. **Small number of cases (n=104)** limits statistical power, especially in stratified analyses and prevents analysis by cancer subtypes/staging.
2. **Observational design** despite being nested in an RCT; residual confounding possible. Men with higher lycopene intake likely healthier overall.
3. **Prostate cancer was a secondary outcome** of PREDIMED, not the primary endpoint.
4. **~6-year follow-up** may not capture longer latency periods for prostate cancer.
5. **No data on cancer severity or staging**, providing incomplete picture.
6. **Generalizability limited** to older Mediterranean adults at high CV risk; findings may not extend to younger, healthier populations.
7. **Self-reported dietary data** despite annual FFQs with trained interviewers.
8. **Wide confidence intervals** on key estimates (e.g., HR 0.46 with 95% CI 0.23-0.95), indicating imprecision.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (prospective cohort evidence on lycopene and prostate cancer):** Directly relevant. This is a 2025 prospective cohort study from the PREDIMED trial showing significant inverse association between lycopene intake and prostate cancer risk in high-CV-risk men. Adds to evidence base with repeated dietary measurements and cumulative averages.
- **Q2 (bioavailability supplements vs whole food):** Marginally relevant. Notes that primary lycopene sources were fresh foods (tomatoes, watermelon) in Mediterranean context rather than processed sources, and suggests Mediterranean cooking methods and olive oil may modify bioavailability. No direct comparison of supplements vs whole foods.
- **Q4 (tradeoffs between supplementation and whole-food sources):** Indirectly relevant. The protective effect was observed with dietary lycopene from whole foods in a Mediterranean dietary pattern, not supplements. Cannot speak to supplement efficacy.
- **Q5 (evidence contradicting popular narrative):** This study SUPPORTS the protective narrative, not contradicts it. However, the authors acknowledge mixed findings in the literature (citing studies showing no association and one Japanese study showing detrimental effect), and note limitations including small case numbers.
- **Q6 (mechanisms of action):** Briefly discusses preclinical mechanisms (antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, apoptosis promotion, gene expression modulation, sex steroid hormone inhibition, mitochondrial effects) but provides no new mechanistic data. References review articles for mechanism discussion.
