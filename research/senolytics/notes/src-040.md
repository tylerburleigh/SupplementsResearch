# src-040: NIA Workshop on Repurposing Drugs/Dietary Supplements for Senolytic or Senomorphic Effects

**Citation:** Romashkan S, Chang H, Hadley EC. J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med Sci. 2021;76(6):1144-1152. doi:10.1093/gerona/glab028. PMCID: PMC8521777.

**Source type:** Workshop report / narrative review (NIA-convened, August 2019)

**Quality:** High -- authored by NIA Division of Geriatrics staff, synthesizes input from 17+ expert speakers including Campisi, Kirkland, Tchkonia, van Deursen, Robbins, Niedernhofer, and others.

---

## Core Findings

This NIA workshop report provides a comprehensive overview of candidate senolytic and senomorphic drugs being considered for repurposing, with detailed discussion of preclinical evidence, human safety data, pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic considerations, and clinical trial design challenges. It represents expert consensus (with acknowledged areas of uncertainty) from leading academic, industry, and government scientists on the state of the field as of 2019.

The report emphasizes that intermittent "hit-and-run" dosing is a key advantage of senolytics over senomorphics (which require continuous administration), and that the combination of dasatinib plus quercetin targets broader senescent cell populations than either agent alone. Critical gaps include the lack of tissue-specific senescence biomarkers, limited human pharmacokinetic data for flavonoids, and high risk of drug interactions in the polypharmacy-prone older adult target population.

## Specific Compounds Discussed

### Flavonoids (Quercetin and Fisetin)
- Fisetin is more potent than quercetin; significantly reduced SA-beta-gal and SASP factors in animal and human tissues
- Fisetin targets senescent cells by inhibiting p16 and p21; quercetin affects PI3K and other kinases
- Both can be senolytic AND senomorphic
- Short half-lives (3 hours for fisetin); rapidly conjugated, but deconjugation at inflammation sites may enable localized activity
- Fisetin: no adverse effects in lab animals at doses up to 3 g/kg/day for one month
- Both reversibly inhibit CYP2C8, affecting bioavailability of other drugs
- In very old mice, fisetin increased median and maximum lifespan by ~15%
- Over 60 clinical trials of flavonoids underway (mostly quercetin for neuroprotection)

### Dasatinib + Quercetin (D+Q)
- Combination rationale: dasatinib kills senescent preadipocytes; quercetin kills senescent vascular endothelial cells -- together broader coverage
- Mouse data: cleared senescent preadipocytes, decreased frailty, improved memory, increased healthspan and lifespan
- Human IPF study (n=14, open-label): 3-week intermittent D+Q; improved gait speed, walking distance, SPPB; one serious AE deemed unrelated
- Human diabetic kidney disease study (n=9, mean age 69, open-label): 3-day course; reduced p16, p21, SA-beta-gal in adipose tissue; no serious AEs
- Dasatinib toxicities in cancer use: myelosuppression, pleural effusion (older patients higher risk), cardiac events; median onset of cytopenia ~27 days, pleural effusion ~114 weeks of continuous use -- intermittent dosing for senolytic use may mitigate

### BCL-2/BCL-xL Inhibitors (Navitoclax, UBX0101)
- Navitoclax: prevented neurodegeneration and inhibited atherosclerotic plaque in animal models
- UBX0101: Phase I intra-articular injection (n=48, knee osteoarthritis); well tolerated; high doses (1-4 mg) reduced pain and senescence markers
- Dose-limiting thrombocytopenia is the key toxicity; manageable with platelet transfusions or intermittent dosing
- "Apoptotic priming" with Bruton's tyrosine kinase inhibitors (e.g., ibrutinib) may lower required BCL inhibitor doses

### HSP-90 Inhibitors
- Geldanamycin and 17-DMAG showed senolytic activity in cell culture and animal models
- Clinical development limited by hepatotoxicity, ocular toxicity, nephrotoxicity, diarrhea, fatigue
- PU-H71: well tolerated in NCI Phase I but trial stopped for lack of drug supply

### Rapamycin (Senomorphic)
- Inhibits cellular senescence via Nrf2-dependent (p16, p21 reduction) and Nrf2-independent (beta-gal, SASP reduction) pathways
- Pilot trial in 13 cardiac rehab patients (mean age 74): 0.5-2.0 mg/day; 62% developed diarrhea
- Placebo-controlled trial in 28 older adults (70-95): 1 mg for 8-16 weeks; small decreases in hemoglobin, hematocrit, RBC, weight; increased TNF-alpha; no significant effects on circulating SASP components
- No human data on in vivo effects on cellular senescence phenotypes

### JAK Inhibitors (Ruxolitinib)
- Reduced SASP cytokines (IL-6, MCP-1, MCP-3, GM-CSF) in senescent human preadipocytes and vascular endothelial cells
- In old mice: decreased inflammatory markers, improved bone phenotypes, metabolic function, physical function
- FDA-approved for myelofibrosis; AEs include anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
- Unclear whether benefits are mediated by effects on senescent vs. nonsenescent cells

## Clinical Trial Design Considerations

1. **Intermittent dosing** -- "hit-and-run" approach compatible with senolytic mechanism; senescent phenotypes mature over 2-6 weeks
2. **Target population selection** -- need individuals with high senescent cell burden; conditions with accelerated aging (post-cancer, HIV, obesity, progeroid syndromes) are attractive initial targets
3. **Biomarker development** -- no single marker is senescence-specific; combination panels recommended; need circulating indicators for tissues that cannot be biopsied
4. **Standardization** -- shared protocols, assays, outcome measures across trials to enable data pooling
5. **Multi-outcome assessment** -- trials should measure effects on multiple aging-related changes given senescent cells' systemic presence

---

## Key Challenges for Translation

- **Biomarkers:** No individual marker completely specific to senescent cells; p16, SA-beta-gal used but not specific
- **Drug interactions:** High polypharmacy prevalence in older adults; dasatinib, ruxolitinib, navitoclax, rapamycin all metabolized by CYP3A4; limited DDI data for flavonoids
- **Pharmacodynamic interactions:** Dasatinib-induced thrombocytopenia increases bleeding risk with anticoagulants; rapamycin hematologic effects concerning with anticoagulant use; NSAIDs worsen dasatinib fluid retention
- **Specificity:** Candidate drugs' specificity for killing senescent vs. nonsenescent cells must be assessed; off-target effects may confound mechanism attribution
- **Safety in older adults:** Limited clinical experience with candidate drugs in the actual target population (older adults with multimorbidity)

---

## Expert Consensus and Disagreements

- Broad agreement on intermittent dosing rationale for senolytics
- Agreement that D+Q combination is more effective than either alone for broad senescent cell clearance
- Acknowledged uncertainty about whether JAK inhibitor benefits are senescence-mediated
- Debate around balance between senolytic specificity and cytotoxicity for repurposed cancer drugs
- Agreement that standardized biomarkers and outcome measures are urgently needed
- Recognition that cancer drug databases (TCGA) could be mined for senescence-relevant data

---

## Limitations

- Workshop report, not a systematic review or meta-analysis
- No new primary data presented; synthesizes existing evidence
- Published 2021 based on 2019 workshop -- does not cover more recent clinical trial results
- Most clinical evidence cited is from small open-label uncontrolled studies
- No quantitative meta-analysis of efficacy data
