**Source:** Jinfiniti Precision Medicine blog (jinfiniti.com), published January 27, 2025
**Author:** Unnamed; medically reviewed by Prof. Jin-Xiong She, PhD
**Type:** Commercial blog article with product promotion (Jinfiniti sells supplements and testing)

## Core Findings

This article surveys nine OTC/natural compounds with reported senolytic or senescence-modulating properties: quercetin, fisetin, EGCG, piperlongumine, curcumin, apigenin, resveratrol, oleuropein, and hydroxytyrosol. It provides an accessible overview of mechanisms and food sources but is not a peer-reviewed source. It cites 20+ external references (PubMed, Nature, Frontiers) but does not present original data. The article is primarily a marketing vehicle for Jinfiniti's SenoAid supplement and AgingSOS biomarker panel.

## Proposed Mechanisms

- Senescent ("zombie") cells accumulate with age, releasing SASP inflammatory signals (inflammageing)
- Natural senolytics selectively induce apoptosis in senescent cells without harming normal cells
- Different compounds target different pathways: oxidative stress (piperlongumine), anti-apoptotic protein suppression (EGCG), sirtuin activation (resveratrol), inflammatory signaling blockade (apigenin)
- Synergy noted between quercetin and fisetin

## Dosing Info

- No specific dosing recommendations provided for any compound
- Article notes "normal dosages are well-established as safe" for apigenin
- Emphasizes moderation for EGCG, piperlongumine, and resveratrol due to dose-dependent toxicity
- States that achieving therapeutic doses through food alone is "challenging" due to variable concentrations and bioavailability

## Evidence Quality

- **Overall: Low to moderate.** This is a commercial blog post, not peer-reviewed research.
- Cites 20+ external references (some to real PubMed/Nature/Frontiers papers) but does not present original data or systematic review methodology
- No effect sizes, sample sizes, or quantitative results from cited studies are reported
- Claims are general and not supported by specific data within this article
- Claims about piperine increasing curcumin absorption "by up to 2000%" appear but are sourced to external literature
- Medical review by a PhD (not MD) is noted

## Safety Notes

- EGCG: can strain the liver at high doses
- Piperlongumine: excessive doses could affect healthy cells via same oxidative stress mechanism
- Resveratrol: becomes toxic to cells at high concentrations
- General advice to consult healthcare professional before starting supplements
- No specific contraindication or drug interaction information provided

## Limitations

- **Strong commercial bias:** Article is a marketing vehicle for Jinfiniti products and testing services
- **No original data:** Summarizes published literature without presenting new findings
- **No dosing guidance:** Despite recommending supplements, provides no specific dosage information
- **No clinical trial data:** Does not reference any specific clinical trial results with participant counts or effect sizes
- **Referenced studies not directly evaluated:** Citations provided but the article's characterization of those studies cannot be verified from this source alone
- **One-sided presentation:** Emphasizes benefits, downplays risks, and does not discuss negative or null findings

This source is relevant to questions about OTC/natural senolytic compounds, their proposed mechanisms, and the commercial supplement landscape.
