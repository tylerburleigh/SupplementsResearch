# Source src-061: PhD Thesis (2025) - Sophia Mahoney, University of Colorado

**Title:** The Role of Cellular Senescence and the Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype in Vascular Aging: Amelioration by the Natural Senolytic Fisetin

**Type:** PhD Dissertation, Department of Integrative Physiology, University of Colorado Boulder (2025)

**Supervisor:** Distinguished Professor Douglas R. Seals

**Content Quality:** Excellent -- full-text thesis with three substantive chapters: a review article (Chapter II, published J Cardiovascular Aging, March 2025), a preclinical intervention study (Chapter III, published Aging Cell, December 2023), and a mechanistic study (Chapter IV, manuscript in preparation for Nature Aging). Rich quantitative data throughout.

---

## Core Findings

This thesis establishes cellular senescence and the SASP as causal contributors to age-related vascular dysfunction and demonstrates that intermittent oral fisetin supplementation reverses vascular endothelial dysfunction and large elastic artery stiffness in old mice via suppression of excess senescent cells. A key mechanistic contribution is the identification of CXCL12 as a novel SASP factor that mediates endothelial dysfunction with aging and can be modulated by senolytic intervention. A clinical trial (NCT06133634) testing fisetin in mid-life/older adults is currently underway based on these preclinical findings.

## Chapter III: Intermittent Fisetin Intervention Study (Published Aging Cell, Dec 2023)

### Fisetin Dosing Protocol
- **Dose:** 100 mg/kg/day
- **Route:** Oral gavage
- **Vehicle:** 10% EtOH, 30% PEG400, 60% Phosal 50 PG
- **Intermittent schedule:** 1 week on daily dosing, 2 weeks off, 1 week on daily dosing
- **Rationale for intermittent dosing:** Remove excess senescent cells without chronically inhibiting cellular senescence (which would interfere with homeostatic functions like wound healing); distinguish senolytic effects from off-target/acute effects
- **Sacrifice timing:** 1-2 weeks after final dose (long after compound clearance; terminal half-life ~3.1 hours in plasma)
- **Dose selection rationale:** Peak plasma levels reach ~1 uM following oral ingestion at 100 mg/kg/day in mice, matching the in vitro senolytic concentration

### Mouse Models and Sample Sizes
- Old (27-month) male C57BL/6N wildtype: vehicle n=17, fisetin n=19
- Old (27-month) male and female p16-3MR transgenic: vehicle n=22, fisetin n=29
- p16-3MR model carries trimodal fusion protein under p16INK4A promoter allowing selective genetic clearance of p16INK4A-positive senescent cells via ganciclovir (GCV)
- No sex differences observed in p16-3MR mice; male and female data combined

### In Vitro Results
- 1 uM fisetin reduced viability of senescent HUVECs (replicative exhaustion model) without affecting non-senescent cells (viability at 1 uM: control 97% vs senescent 91%, P=0.003)
- IC50: control HUVECs 7.0 uM vs senescent HUVECs 3.4 uM
- 1 uM fisetin lowered Cdkn2a expression by 71% in senescent HAECs (P<0.0001), preferentially targeting p16INK4A pathway over p21
- SA-beta-gal signal reduced in concentration-dependent manner up to 1 uM

### In Vivo Vascular Senescence Results
- Aortic Cdkn2a expression: -85% (P=0.010)
- Aortic Cdkn1a expression: -48% (P=0.006)
- Aortic Serpine1 expression: -81% (P=0.011)
- Aortic p16INK4A protein: -36% (P=0.007)
- SASP factors reduced: Mmp3 (P=0.045), Plat (P=0.037), Ccl2 (P=0.032), with trends for Tnfa, Cxcl2, Vegf

### Endothelial Function Results
- Peak EDD: vehicle 81 +/- 3% vs fisetin 97 +/- 1% (P<0.001)
- Using p16-3MR pharmaco-dissection: GCV improved EDD in vehicle-treated mice (76% to 90%, P=0.005) but NOT in fisetin-treated mice (93% vs 92%, P=0.71), demonstrating fisetin's effects are mediated by senolysis
- NO bioavailability: peak NO-mediated dilation 43% (vehicle) vs 60% (fisetin), P<0.001; L-NAME abolished group differences
- No change in endothelium-independent dilation (SNP response), confirming endothelium-specific improvement

### Oxidative Stress Results
- Aortic whole-cell ROS: 1.7-fold lower in fisetin group (8173 vs 4703 AU, P=0.028)
- NADPH oxidase abundance: -30% (P=0.027)
- Aortic mitochondrial ROS: 2.6-fold lower (6603 vs 2527 AU, P=0.011)
- Phosphorylated p66SHC: ~40% lower (P=0.034)
- MnSOD abundance: ~100% higher (P=0.046)
- TEMPOL and MitoQ increased EDD in vehicle but not fisetin mice, confirming fisetin ameliorated tonic ROS suppression of EDD

### Arterial Stiffness Results
- Aortic PWV reduced ~20% with fisetin (425 to 335 cm/s, P<0.001); no change in vehicle
- Elastic modulus: ~20% lower (3342 vs 2736 kPa, P=0.012)
- p16-3MR pharmaco-dissection: GCV lowered elastic modulus in vehicle mice (4653 to 3036 kPa, P=0.009) but not fisetin mice, confirming senolysis-mediated mechanism
- ABT-263 (synthetic senolytic) confirmed same pattern
- AGEs: 36% lower (P=0.030), localized to adventitial region
- Collagen-1: trend toward reduction (P=0.070)
- No change in alpha-elastin

### Limitations (Chapter III)
- Possible off-target effects of fisetin beyond senolysis (antioxidant, anti-inflammatory)
- Measurements made 1 week after final dose to minimize acute effects
- p16-3MR model limitations: eliminates non-senescent p16INK4A positive cells; limited ability to target senescent cells not expressing p16INK4A

## Chapter IV: CXCL12 Mechanistic Study (Manuscript in preparation, Nature Aging)

### Single-Cell RNA Sequencing Findings
- 29 clusters representing 11 distinct aortic cell types identified from young (6mo) and old (27mo) mice
- Endothelial cells comprised largest group (46%); fibroblasts (22%), B cells (10%), macrophages (7%), T cells (6%), VSMC (4%)
- Endothelial cell proportion increased with age (P<0.001); T cells decreased (P=0.002)
- Cellular senescence was the most highly modulated pathway with aging (Reactome analysis)
- Senescent endothelial cells identified using EndoSen signature, concentrated in subcluster 10
- Subcluster 10 upregulated with age, downregulated back to young levels with fisetin

### CXCL12 as Novel SASP Factor
- Cxcl12 gene was the most highly upregulated SASP factor in senescent endothelial cells with aging
- CXCL12 protein levels elevated in plasma of old mice and restored to young levels by fisetin supplementation
- PAI1 also elevated with age and ameliorated by fisetin (validated senescent cell clearance)

### Circulating SASP Milieu Experiments
- Old vehicle plasma impaired EDD vs young plasma (76% vs 92%, P<0.0001)
- Old fisetin plasma preserved EDD (87% vs 76%, P=0.001)
- Exogenous CXCL12 added to young plasma impaired EDD (-12%, P=0.002)
- CXCL12 added to old fisetin plasma reduced EDD (-22%, P<0.0001)
- CXCL12 inhibition (LIT-927) improved function in old vehicle plasma
- ~7-fold more SA-beta-gal positive cells in aortas exposed to old vs young plasma; ~6-fold lower with old fisetin plasma
- Old vehicle plasma: 45-fold increase in Cdkn1a expression in HAECs; 7.5-fold decrease with old fisetin plasma
- Exogenous CXCL12 induced cellular senescence, reduced NO bioactivity, and increased mitochondrial superoxide

### EndoMT Findings
- Senescent endothelial cells showed reduced endothelial markers (Cdh5, Pecam1, Vwf, Nos3) and increased mesenchymal markers (Tgfb1, Acta2, Vim, Col1a1)
- CXCL12 promoted EndoMT; fisetin supplementation prevented circulating SASP- and CXCL12-induced EndoMT
- CXCL12 inhibition with LIT-927 reversed EndoMT markers

### Chapter IV Sample Sizes
- Young vehicle: 12 mice (6M/6F); Young fisetin: 12 mice (6M/6F)
- Old vehicle: 8 mice (3M/5F); Old fisetin: 10 mice (4M/6F)
- Same intermittent fisetin dosing paradigm as Chapter III

## Chapter II: Review Article (Published J Cardiovascular Aging, March 2025)
- Comprehensive review of mechanisms by which cellular senescence induces vascular aging
- Established mechanisms: mitochondrial dysfunction, telomere shortening/dysfunction, shear stress, ECM remodeling, circulating milieu, dysregulated immune responses, cell-cell contact, oxygen partial pressure
- Novel/putative mechanisms: circadian misalignment, glycolytic byproducts (MGO/GLO-1 pathway), membrane contact sites (MERCs), endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition
- Clinical groups with high senescent cell burden identified: older adults, cancer survivors, chronic kidney disease, HIV, diabetes, Alzheimer's disease
- Table 2.1: Comprehensive listing of senotherapeutic agents tested in vascular studies

## Human Data and Clinical Translation

- No original human data in this thesis; all experiments were preclinical (mouse models and cell culture)
- Fisetin is noted as commercially available as a dietary supplement, reported safe for human consumption
- Fisetin has been tested in a double-blind RCT for stroke (Wang et al. 2019)
- **Active clinical trial: NCT06133634** -- randomized, placebo-controlled trial of fisetin supplementation in healthy mid-life and older adults, being conducted by Dr. Matthew Rossman at University of Colorado Boulder
- Epidemiological data cited: flavonoid intake associated with lower all-cause and CVD mortality in multiple cohort studies

---

## Mechanism of Action Summary

1. **Senolysis:** Fisetin selectively eliminates senescent endothelial cells via targeting p16INK4A-expressing cells; demonstrated using genetic clearance models (p16-3MR + GCV) and pharmacological senolysis (ABT-263)
2. **NO restoration:** Improves NO bioavailability by reducing ROS-mediated NO scavenging
3. **Oxidative stress reduction:** Lowers whole-cell ROS (via reduced NADPH oxidase) and mitochondrial ROS (via reduced p-p66SHC and increased MnSOD)
4. **Arterial remodeling:** Reduces AGEs and collagen-1 in the adventitial wall
5. **SASP modulation:** Reduces multiple SASP factors; specifically targets CXCL12, a novel SASP factor that induces endothelial dysfunction, paracrine senescence, and EndoMT

## Claims to Verify

1. **Sample sizes:** Wildtype: vehicle n=17, fisetin n=19 (started with 18/19, 1 attrition); p16-3MR: vehicle n=22, fisetin n=29 (started with 27/35, 11 attrition). High attrition in p16-3MR group is noted as expected age-related mortality at 27 months.
2. **IC50 values:** Fisetin IC50 on senescent HUVECs reported as 3.4 +/- 0.3 uM; on control HUVECs 7.0 +/- 0.4 uM. These are reasonable and consistent with selective senolytic action.
3. **Clinical trial NCT06133634:** Stated as actively recruiting; should be verified on clinicaltrials.gov for current status.

## Relevance to Research Questions

This source is highly relevant to questions about:
- **Fisetin dosing and protocols:** Provides the most detailed published protocol for intermittent fisetin supplementation in vascular aging (100 mg/kg/day, 1 week on/2 weeks off/1 week on)
- **Arterial function outcomes:** Comprehensive data on endothelial function (EDD), arterial stiffness (PWV, elastic modulus), NO bioavailability, and oxidative stress
- **Mechanism of action:** Single-cell RNA sequencing identifies CXCL12 as a novel mediator; pharmaco-dissection with p16-3MR and ABT-263 confirms senolysis-dependent mechanism
- **Human translation:** Active clinical trial (NCT06133634) in progress; no published human data yet
- **Intermittent supplementation rationale:** Clear justification for intermittent over continuous dosing to preserve homeostatic functions of senescence
