# src-095: Justice et al. (2019) - Senolytics in IPF: First-in-Human Pilot Study

**Citation:** Justice JN, Nambiar AM, Tchkonia T, et al. "Senolytics in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: Results from a first-in-human, open-label, pilot study." eBioMedicine. 2019;40:554-563. doi:10.1016/j.ebiom.2018.12.052. PMCID: PMC6412088.

**Quality Note:** Metadata flagged as "degraded" (paywall_page EUR), but the source file contains the complete full-text article from PMC with all sections (Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion). Content is authoritative and complete.

## Core Findings

This was the first clinical trial of senolytic drugs in humans. Fourteen older adults with stable IPF received intermittent dasatinib plus quercetin (DQ) for 3 weeks. The study demonstrated feasibility (100% retention, no discontinuation) and showed statistically significant, clinically meaningful improvements in physical function (6MWD +21.5m, gait speed, chair-stands, SPPB), despite no change in pulmonary function or patient-reported outcomes. The results provided initial proof-of-concept that senolytics may alleviate physical dysfunction in senescence-associated diseases.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Two-center, single-arm, open-label pilot study
- **Sites:** Wake Forest School of Medicine (n=2) and University of Texas Health Science Center San Antonio (n=12)
- **ClinicalTrials.gov:** NCT02874989
- **Primary endpoints:** Retention rates and completion rates for planned clinical assessments
- **Secondary endpoints:** Safety/tolerability, change in functional and reported health measures
- **Exploratory:** Associations between function change and circulating SASP factor changes

## Patient Population

- **N=14** (2 women, 12 non-Hispanic white, 2 Hispanic white)
- **Mean age:** 70.8 +/- 7.9 years (range 55-84)
- **IPF severity:** 4 mild (FVC >80%), 8 moderate (FVC 50-80%), 2 severe (FVC <50%)
- **Medications:** 7 on nintedanib, 5 on pirfenidone, 2 on no antifibrotic therapy
- **Comorbidities:** Hypertension (8), depression/anxiety (7), allergies (6), hypothyroid (6), insomnia (5)
- Inclusion: confirmed IPF by consensus guidelines, stable therapy >=3 months, BMI 19-35, MoCA >21, postmenopausal women
- Exclusion: lung transplant, pulmonary hypertension, MI/stroke within 6 months, QTc prolongation, GFR <30, significant liver disease

## Dosing Regimen

- **Dasatinib:** 100 mg/day (Sprycel, Bristol Myers Squibb)
- **Quercetin:** 1250 mg/day (5 x 250 mg capsules, Thorne Research quercetin phytosome)
- **Schedule:** 3 consecutive days of dosing per week, repeated for 3 consecutive weeks = 9 total dosing days
- Doses separated by 4-day no-drug periods
- Nintedanib withheld on dasatinib dosing days to avoid drug-drug interaction (both tyrosine kinase inhibitors); pirfenidone maintained
- First dose administered onsite; subsequent doses self-administered with weekly adherence checks
- Adherence: 13/14 achieved perfect adherence; 1 participant took 1 extra dose

## Primary Outcomes

- **Retention:** 100% (14/14 completed)
- **Assessment completion:** 13/14 completed all planned assessments (1 omitted SPPB at follow-up due to hospitalization)
- Drug discontinuation: 0

## Physical Function Results (all p < 0.05, within-subjects paired t-test)

| Measure | Baseline | Follow-up | Change | p-value |
|---------|----------|-----------|--------|---------|
| 6MWD (m) | 447 +/- 83 | 468 +/- 81 | +21.5 +/- 28 | 0.012 |
| 4-m gait speed (m/s) | 1.1 +/- 0.2 | 1.2 +/- 0.2 | +0.12 +/- 0.2 | 0.024 |
| Chair-stands (s) | 14.8 +/- 3 | 12.6 +/- 2 | -2.2 +/- 3 | 0.013 |
| SPPB score | 10 +/- 1 | 11 +/- 0.9 | +0.9 +/- 1 | 0.003 |

- 21.5m 6MWD increase is consistent with clinically important improvement in IPF
- Majority of participants showed >=5% physical function gains
- 12 UTHSCSA participants had prior regular clinical 6MWD testing, minimizing learning effect concern

## Measures Unchanged After DQ

- Pulmonary function: FEV1 (p=0.38), FVC (p=0.53), FEV1:FVC ratio (p=0.78)
- Grip strength (p=0.314)
- Frailty index FI-LAB (p=0.24; 8/14 improved by >=5% but not significant as group)
- Patient-reported respiratory health (SGRQ, mMRC, UCSD-SoBQ), fatigue scales
- Body weight, vitals, clinical chemistries

## Safety Data

- **67 non-serious AEs:** 37 mild, 31 moderate, 0 severe
- **1 serious AE:** Possible bacterial multifocal pneumonia and pulmonary edema superimposed on IPF, requiring temporary hospitalization with complete resolution (occurred after DQ completion)
- **0 fatal AEs, 0 discontinuations**
- Most frequent AEs: respiratory symptoms (16 events), skin irritation/bruising (14, mostly biopsy-related), GI discomfort (14), general health (14), pain (7)
- No evidence of hepatic or renal toxicity, cell lysis syndrome, or QTc prolongation
- AE profile generally consistent with placebo arms of Phase III IPF trials

## Senescence Marker (SASP) Changes

- DQ effects on circulating SASP factors were inconclusive
- Baseline circulating SASP levels were not highly elevated in these patients
- Select SASP proteins (IL-6, MMP7, TIMP2) showed potential reduction (>=8 participants with decreased concentrations) but ELISA did not confirm IL-6 and MMP7 changes
- Moderate correlations (r >= 0.50) observed between function change and 23/48 SASP-related markers
  - 6MWD gain associated with lowered miR34c
  - Gait speed improvement with MMP8, MMP9, lowered RANTES
  - Faster chair-stands with decreased eotaxin, GRO-alpha, IL-18
- Skin biopsy for tissue senescence markers was planned but omitted due to technical complications

## Key Limitations

1. **Single-arm, open-label design** - no placebo or active control; functional improvements must be interpreted cautiously
2. **Small sample size** (n=14) - pilot/feasibility study, not powered for efficacy
3. **Short follow-up period** - assessments repeated only 5-7 days after last DQ dose
4. **No direct tissue senescence measurement** - skin biopsy abandoned; lung biopsy not feasible
5. **Inconclusive SASP biomarker data** - low baseline levels, small sample
6. **Predominantly white male population** - limited generalizability
7. **Not designed to evaluate efficacy** - primary purpose was feasibility

## Claims to Verify

- The 6MWD baseline of 447m seems relatively high for an IPF population with 2 severe and 8 moderate cases. This likely reflects the inclusion of only "stable" IPF patients. Cross-check against other IPF trial baselines.
- The abstract mentions "n=16 total events" for respiratory symptoms, but the results table shows 16 events for "IPF & respiratory" combined (Table 2, line 733-734). Confirmed consistent.

## Relevance to Senolytics Research

This is a landmark study as the first clinical trial of senolytic drugs in humans. It established feasibility of intermittent DQ dosing in a patient population with a senescence-associated disease (IPF). The statistically significant improvements in physical function (6MWD, gait speed, chair-stands, SPPB) despite no change in pulmonary function suggest senolytics may alleviate systemic consequences of cellular senescence rather than directly reversing organ fibrosis in the short term. The "hit-and-run" intermittent dosing concept was validated clinically. The study directly led to calls for larger randomized controlled trials and influenced the design of subsequent senolytic trials. The inconclusive SASP biomarker findings highlight an ongoing challenge in the field: lack of validated circulating biomarkers of tissue senescence burden for clinical trials.
