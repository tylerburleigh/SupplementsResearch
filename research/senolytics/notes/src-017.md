# src-017: Kirkland & Tchkonia (2020) "Senolytic drugs: from discovery to translation"

**Source:** Mayo Clinic Robert and Arlene Kogod Center on Aging, Rochester, MN, USA.

**Quality assessment:** Full text available from PMC. Substantive review article with detailed methods, preclinical data, and clinical trial summaries. Authoritative -- Kirkland and Tchkonia are the founders of the senolytics field.

## Core Findings

This is the landmark review from the originators of senolytic therapy. The paper traces the field from the hypothesis-driven discovery of the first senolytic drugs through preclinical validation to early human trials. The central thesis is that targeting senescent cells -- which accumulate with age and at causal sites of multiple chronic diseases -- can simultaneously alleviate dozens of age-related conditions, consistent with the Geroscience Hypothesis and the authors' proposed "Unitary Theory of Fundamental Aging Processes."

The paper establishes that senolytics work by transiently disabling Senescent Cell Anti-Apoptotic Pathways (SCAPs), causing apoptosis specifically of D-senescent cells (those with a tissue-destructive SASP), while sparing H-senescent cells and normal cells. Because senescent cells take weeks to reaccumulate, intermittent "hit-and-run" dosing is sufficient, reducing side-effect risk.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### SCAP-based drug discovery mechanism
- Senescent cells resist apoptosis via upregulated anti-apoptotic SCAP networks, despite producing their own pro-apoptotic SASP
- siRNA screens identified which SCAP nodes were critical per cell type; BCL-xL was key for endothelial cells but not adipocyte progenitors
- Dasatinib (a tyrosine kinase inhibitor) targets senescent adipocyte progenitors via dependence receptor/ephrin/Src kinase pathways
- Quercetin and Fisetin (flavonoids) target senescent endothelial cells via BCL-2 family inhibition and HIF-1alpha
- D+Q combination required for some cell types (e.g., mouse embryonic fibroblasts) due to SCAP redundancy

### First-generation senolytic compounds listed
- Dasatinib, Quercetin, Fisetin, Luteolin, Curcumin (and analog EF24), Navitoclax (ABT263), A1331852, A1155463, Geldanamycin, Tanespimycin, Alvespimycin, Piperlongumine, FOXO4-related peptide, Nutlin3a (caveat: also causes senescence), Cardiac glycosides (Ouabain, Proscillaridin A, Digoxin)

### Second-generation approaches
- High-throughput screens with senescent + nonsenescent cells in same wells (overcomes control cell problem)
- Galacto-oligosaccharide-coated nanoparticles with toxic cargos opened by SA-beta-gal
- Senescent cell vaccines, immunomodulators

### D-senescent vs. H-senescent cells
- D-senescent cells: have tissue-destructive SASP, upregulate SCAPs, are cleared by senolytics
- H-senescent cells: no SASP, promote stem/progenitor cell differentiation, NOT cleared by senolytics
- Each type can comprise 30-70% of total senescent cells

### Threshold effect and senescence spreading
- Transplanting as few as 1/10,000 senescent cells into middle-aged mice causes frailty and accelerated mortality
- 500,000 senescent cells was insufficient in lean middle-aged mice but sufficient in old or obese mice
- Senescent cells spread senescence to normal cells systemically, even at a distance

### Preclinical efficacy of D+Q
- Cardiovascular: partial reversal of age-related cardiac ejection fraction decline, reduced vessel calcification in old mice
- Diabetes/obesity: enhanced insulin sensitivity, reduced peripheral insulin resistance, decreased adipose tissue inflammation and monocyte homing
- Alzheimer's: reduced neurofibrillary tangle density, neuronal loss, neuroinflammation; increased cortical volume and neurogenesis in Tau+ mice; reduced amyloid load and improved cognition in Abeta+ mice
- IPF: improved treadmill endurance, reduced weight loss, enhanced lung compliance in bleomycin model
- Osteoporosis: increased femoral cortical thickness and bone strength; decreased bone resorption without coupled decrease in bone formation (unlike existing antiresorptives)
- Lifespan: D+Q extended remaining lifespan by up to 35%, Fisetin by up to 17% in old mice
- Over 40 conditions alleviated in preclinical models

### D+Q human tissue data
- In human diabetic adipose tissue organ culture, D+Q decreased senescent cells by 70% within 2 days (confirmed by TAFs and cleaved caspase-3)
- SASP factors (IL-6, IL-8, MCP-1, PAI-1, GM-CSF) decreased significantly
- Fisetin also reduced senescent cell numbers in adipose organ culture

### Clinical trials reported
1. **IPF pilot (first-in-human):** N=14, open-label, intermittent D+Q (3 days/week over 3 weeks). Significant improvement in 6-minute walk distance, 4m gait speed, chair-stands time (P<0.05). 23/48 SASP markers correlated with functional change (r>=0.50).
2. **Diabetic CKD pilot:** 3 days oral D+Q. 11 days post-dose, adipose tissue p16INK4a+ and SA-beta-gal+ cells decreased, macrophage crown-like structures reduced, composite blood SASP score decreased.

### Clinical trials underway or planned (as of 2020)
- D+Q: IPF (recruiting), diabetic CKD (recruiting), Alzheimer's (recruiting), age-related osteoporosis, BMT survivor frailty, childhood cancer survivor frailty, first-degree relatives of type 2 diabetics
- Fisetin: age-related frailty (recruiting), osteoarthritis (recruiting), age-related osteoporosis, COVID-19 cytokine storm prevention (FDA-approved, about to start)
- UBX0101: osteoarthritis (Phase 1 completed, Phase 2 in process)
- Navitoclax/UBX1967/UBX1325: macular degeneration (planned)

### Safety considerations
- Agents targeting single SCAP nodes (Navitoclax, Nutlin3a) are more "panolytic" -- cause off-target apoptosis of platelets, immune cells, and osteoblasts
- Multi-node agents (D+Q) have flatter side-effect profiles
- Intermittent dosing further reduces risk
- Authors emphasize senolytics should NOT be used outside clinical trials until safety is established

## Methodology

- Review article synthesizing the authors' own primary research and the broader senolytics literature
- Preclinical evidence from multiple mouse models: naturally aged, progeroid Ercc1-/Delta, DIO, ApoE-/-, Tau+, Abeta+, bleomycin-induced PF
- Modified Koch's postulates framework for proving true senolytic effects (8 criteria listed)
- Human tissue: ex vivo adipose tissue organ culture from diabetic patients
- Clinical trials: open-label pilot studies, progressing to RCTs

## Limitations

- Clinical trial data as of 2020 is limited to small open-label pilot studies (N=14 for IPF; unspecified N for diabetic CKD)
- No randomized controlled trial results reported yet
- Animal-to-human translation remains uncertain
- Authors hold patents on senolytic drugs and have financial conflicts of interest (acknowledged)
- Specific dosing regimens for humans not yet optimized
- No long-term safety data in humans

## Claims to Verify

- **"D+Q extended remaining lifespan in old mice by up to 35%"**: This is a frequently cited figure. The qualifier "up to" and "remaining" lifespan should be noted -- this is not total lifespan extension. Original data from Zhu et al. 2015 and Xu et al. 2018.
- **"D+Q and F eliminated 30 to 70% of transplanted or endogenous senescent cells"** in mice (line ~662-666): This range is broad; verify original source studies for exact numbers and methodology.
