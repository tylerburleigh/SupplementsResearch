# src-243: Saliev & Singh (2025) - Targeting Senescence: Senolytics and Senomorphics in Anti-Aging

**Citation:** Saliev, T.; Singh, P.B. (2025). "Targeting Senescence: A Review of Senolytics and Senomorphics in Anti-Aging Interventions." *Biomolecules*, 15, 860. DOI: 10.3390/biom15060860

**Type:** Comprehensive narrative review (19 pages, ~100 references)
**Quality:** Full text available, substantive content. Peer-reviewed, open access (CC BY).

---

## Core Findings

This review provides a broad survey of the senolytic and senomorphic landscape as of mid-2025, covering molecular mechanisms, compound classes, clinical trial status, and translational challenges. The central thesis is that senolytics (cell elimination) and senomorphics (SASP modulation) are complementary strategies requiring improved tissue specificity, better biomarkers, and context-sensitive deployment. The field is transitioning from blunt cell-elimination approaches toward precision geroscience integrating multi-omics, AI-guided drug discovery, and targeted delivery systems.

## Senolytic Compounds Reviewed (Table 1)

| Class | Agents | Targets | Key Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tyrosine kinase inhibitors | Dasatinib | Src family kinases, Eph receptors | Cell-type specificity; systemic toxicity |
| Flavonoid polyphenols | Quercetin, Fisetin | PI3K/AKT, NF-kB, ROS pathways | Variable potency; poor bioavailability in vivo |
| BCL-2 family inhibitors | Navitoclax (ABT-263), ABT-737 | BCL-2, BCL-xL, BCL-w | Thrombocytopenia (BCL-xL in platelets) |
| FOXO4-p53 disruptors | FOXO4-DRI peptide | FOXO4-p53 complex | Peptide delivery limitations; preclinical only |
| HSP90 inhibitors | 17-DMAG, Geldanamycin derivatives | HSP90 | General cytotoxicity; lacks SnC specificity |
| CDK/p53 modulators | UBX0101, Nutlin-3 | MDM2-p53, CDK4/6 | Short half-life; mixed clinical results |
| Natural plant-derived | Piperlongumine, Curcumin analogues | ROS, NF-kB, SASP factors | Low potency; unclear pharmacokinetics |

**D+Q (Dasatinib + Quercetin):** First-generation senolytic combination. Intermittent dosing in aged mice improved physical function, reduced senescent cell burden, and extended lifespan. Early human data: improvements in physical performance and reduced senescence biomarkers in IPF and diabetic kidney disease patients.

**Fisetin:** Highlighted as especially notable among natural senolytics -- broad efficacy across senescent cell types, found in strawberries and apples, reduces systemic inflammation, extends healthspan in mice, currently in human trials.

**Navitoclax/ABT-737:** Potent but dose-limited by thrombocytopenia. ABT-737 shares similar hematologic toxicity (thrombocytopenia + neutropenia). Prompts development of selective delivery systems (nanoparticles, senescence-targeted ligands).

| Glucocorticoids (Dexamethasone) | GR/NF-kB | Clinically approved; repurposing debated |
| Metformin | AMPK/NF-kB/mTOR | TAME trial (NCT04245771) ongoing |
| Resveratrol | SIRT1/NF-kB | Nutraceutical; limited clinical trials |
| HDAC inhibitors | Histone deacetylases | Preclinical/repurposing from oncology |

**Key point:** Senomorphics offer potentially safer long-term strategy vs. senolytics, especially for chronic dosing in older adults, since they avoid risks of cell ablation and regeneration imbalance. However, they present risks of pleiotropic effects, SASP rebound upon cessation, and continued accumulation of dysfunctional cells.

## SASP Temporal Dynamics

The SASP evolves over time:
- **Early stages:** Pro-fibrotic and immunosuppressive (TGF-beta, VEGF, ECM proteins) -- promotes tissue integrity, dampens excessive immune activation
- **Chronic stages:** Pro-inflammatory (IL-6, IL-8, IL-1beta, MMPs) -- immune recruitment, stem cell dysfunction, tissue degeneration, paracrine senescence propagation

This temporal heterogeneity has direct therapeutic implications: interventions must consider not just which SASP components to target but *when* during the senescence timeline.

## Clinical Trial Status (as of 2025)

- Multiple senolytic compounds in early-phase clinical trials for: Alzheimer's disease, chronic kidney disease, frailty, osteoarthritis
- UBX0101: Mixed clinical results in osteoarthritis (targeted intra-articular application)
- Rapamycin: Multiple aging trials (NCT02432287)
- Metformin: TAME trial (NCT04245771) ongoing for aging
- JAK inhibitors (ruxolitinib/baricitinib): Phase 2 for frailty and inflammation
- D+Q: Early human results in IPF and diabetic kidney disease

## Safety Profiles

- **BCL-2 inhibitors:** Dose-dependent thrombocytopenia (navitoclax, ABT-737); also neutropenia
- **Broad kinase inhibitors:** Systemic toxicity risk, especially with chronic use
- **Senomorphics:** Pleiotropic effects, potential immunosuppression, SASP rebound on cessation
- **General concern:** Aged/frail populations are primary targets but most vulnerable to adverse events due to multimorbidity and polypharmacy
- **Mitigation strategies:** Intermittent "hit-and-run" dosing, tissue-targeted prodrugs, ADCs, nanoparticles, combination regimens

---

## Translational Challenges

1. **Heterogeneity:** Senescent cells vary by tissue, trigger, age context -- no universal senolytic likely
2. **Biomarkers:** p16INK4a, p21CIP1, SA-beta-gal, gammaH2AX not exclusive to senescence; no consensus on systemic senescence load quantification
3. **Context-dependent role:** Senescence is beneficial in wound healing, tumor suppression -- indiscriminate elimination may impair regeneration
4. **Regulatory gap:** Aging not recognized as medical indication; no standardized endpoints for aging outcomes
5. **Economic/logistical:** Population-wide demand; healthcare system capacity, affordability, equitable access

---

## Future Directions

- **Next-gen prodrugs:** Activated only in senescent cells (metabolic signatures, pH, enzymatic profiles)
- **ADCs targeting:** DPP4, uPAR, B2M surface markers
- **Nanoparticle delivery:** Especially for brain, bone marrow
- **Multi-omics senescence atlases:** Single-cell transcriptomics, epigenomics, proteomics, metabolomics
- **AI/ML integration:** Drug discovery, biomarker identification, predictive modeling
- **Adaptive senescence modulation:** Temporal tuning rather than blanket suppression
- **Personalized senotherapy:** Guided by individual aging trajectories, molecular profiling
- **Niche-aware strategies:** Targeting senescent cells within stem cell niches (neural, hematopoietic)
- **Combinatorial approaches:** Senolytics + senomorphics + regenerative medicine + lifestyle interventions
- **Regulatory innovation:** FDA/EMA collaboration on aging-related surrogate endpoints; adaptive trial designs

---

## Claims to Verify

- No specific quantitative data (sample sizes, effect sizes, p-values) are presented in this review -- it is a narrative synthesis. All claims about compound efficacy reference other primary studies.
- The assertion that D+Q "extended lifespan" in some animal models is attributed to reference [32] (Tang et al. 2025, Oral Diseases). This should be cross-checked, as lifespan extension claims in D+Q literature are often based on specific mouse models and may not generalize.
- The paper mentions "multiple clinical trials now underway" but does not enumerate them beyond the specific NCT numbers cited for rapamycin and metformin. The actual number and scope of senolytic trials should be verified against clinicaltrials.gov.

## Relevance Assessment

This source is highly relevant as a comprehensive 2025 review providing a structured overview of the entire senolytic and senomorphic landscape. It is most useful for:
- Cataloging compound classes, mechanisms, and limitations
- Clinical trial status snapshot as of mid-2025
- Framework for translational challenges and future directions
- SASP temporal dynamics and therapeutic implications

Limitations: As a narrative review, it does not provide new primary data. Some claims about clinical efficacy are based on early-phase studies with small sample sizes. The review does not include systematic meta-analysis of trial outcomes.
