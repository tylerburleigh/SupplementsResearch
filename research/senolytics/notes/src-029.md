# src-029: Q-CABG Trial Protocol (Dagher et al., 2021)

## Core Findings

Q-CABG is a phase II, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial designed to evaluate whether perioperative quercetin supplementation (500 mg twice daily, total 1000 mg/day) can reduce systemic inflammation, myocardial injury, and senescence burden in 100 patients undergoing CABG following acute coronary syndrome. The protocol hypothesizes that quercetin's senolytic and anti-inflammatory properties will attenuate the vigorous inflammatory response triggered by cardiopulmonary bypass, improve endothelial function, and reduce postoperative complications. As a study protocol (not a results paper), it presents no outcome data -- only the rationale, design, and methodology.

## Trial Design

- **Design**: Phase II, single-center, prospective, randomized, double-blind, allocation-concealed, placebo-controlled
- **Registration**: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04907253
- **Center**: Montreal Heart Institute (MHI), Montreal, Quebec, Canada (~2,000 heart surgeries/year)
- **Allocation**: 1:1 ratio, computer-generated randomization with blocks of size 2, 4, and 6
- **Analysis**: Intention-to-treat principle
- **Recruitment started**: June 2021; estimated completion April 2022

## Population

- **N**: 100 total (50 per arm)
- **Inclusion**: Adults 18+ with ACS (unstable angina, NSTEMI, or STEMI) diagnosed within 30 days, scheduled for inpatient isolated CABG at MHI, treated per national guidelines
- **Key exclusions**: Combined heart surgery, severe kidney failure (eGFR < 30), severe liver disease (ALT/AST > 2x ULN), immunosuppression, systemic auto-immune disorder, cardiogenic shock, emergency CABG, current quercetin supplement use, allergy to quercetin/flavonoids, pregnancy/lactation, fluoroquinolone use, history of estrogen-dependent cancer
- **Off-pump cases not excluded** but tracked as they have different inflammatory profiles

## Intervention

- **Treatment arm**: Quercetin 500 mg PO bid (92% quercetin, 5% microcrystalline cellulose, 3% sodium stearyl fumarate), extracted from Dimorphandra mollis flowers
- **Placebo arm**: Matching capsules with microcrystalline cellulose (97% Arbocel A300)
- **Capsule covers**: Green for both arms (ensures concealment)
- **Dispensing**: Opaque bottles for nursing administration
- **Manufacturer**: Advanced Orthomolecular Research (AOR) Inc., Calgary, Alberta (provided free, no role in trial design/conduct)
- **Timing**: Starts 2-3 days before planned surgery (4-6 preoperative doses), continues until hospital discharge or maximum POD 7
- **Safety**: Stopped for allergic reaction, adverse effects, or fluoroquinolone need (competitive DNA gyrase binding)

## Primary Endpoint

Temporal evolution of:
1. **Inflammation**: High-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) -- the most important parameter
2. **Myocardial injury**: High-sensitivity troponin T (hs-cTnT)
3. **Senescence**: Angiopoietin-like 2 (ANGPTL2), measured by ELISA

**Blood draw timepoints**: Baseline (pre-drug), POD 1, POD 4, and hospital discharge (max POD 7)
- Timepoints chosen based on prior data (Noly et al. 2019) showing inflammatory markers peak at 4 days in this population

## Secondary Endpoint

**Endothelial function** of the internal mammary artery (IMA):
1. **Ex vivo vascular reactivity**: 2-mm IMA segment mounted in wire myograph; pre-constricted with U46619; cumulative ACh doses to assess endothelium-dependent vasorelaxation; measures ACh-EC50 (potency) and Emax (efficacy)
2. **Transcriptomic signature**:
   - Single-nuclei RNA-seq: 12 samples (6 quercetin vs. 6 placebo), 10X Chromium, analyzed with Seurat toolkit
   - Bulk RNA-seq: 12 different samples (6 quercetin vs. 6 placebo), pathway analysis via GO and KEGG databases

## Statistical Analysis

- **Sample size**: Powered to detect 50% relative reduction in hs-CRP change (quercetin vs. placebo) at 80% power, alpha = 0.05, two-tailed
  - Based on prior data: SD of log-transformed hs-CRP ratio = 1.215
  - Detectable difference on log scale = 0.693 (corresponding to 50% reduction)
- **Primary analysis**: Repeated measures ANCOVA with terms for group, time, group-by-time interaction, adjusted for baseline value
- **Main comparison**: hs-CRP at POD 4 between groups
- **Secondary analyses**: Student's t-tests for ACh EC50 and Emax; DEGs at p < 0.05 and |log2(FC)| > 1; pathway analysis FDR < 0.05
- **Software**: SAS version 9.4+

## Rationale and Background Evidence

- Prior study by Noly et al. (2019) of 47 cardiac surgery patients showed ANGPTL2 remained elevated post-surgery in older hypertensive patients (while hs-CRP decreased), suggesting surgery does not address senescence burden
- Quercetin selected over other senolytics (e.g., dasatinib) due to absence of clinically significant side effects
- Quercetin has documented cardioprotective effects and anti-inflammatory properties (e.g., reduces IkBalpha expression in coronary artery disease mononuclear cells)
- Dose of 1000 mg/day chosen as balance between safety and efficacy (Canadian max recommended: 1200 mg/day)

## Limitations

1. **Low bioavailability**: Quercetin has relatively poor absorption with substantial interindividual variation, which may explain inconsistent results across clinical studies
2. **Non-specific mechanism**: Quercetin is multifaceted, not purely anti-inflammatory
3. **Early discharge**: ERAS protocols may send patients home by POD 3, potentially precluding the POD 4 blood draw
4. **Surgical variability**: IMA harvesting technique (skeletonized vs. non-skeletonized) and electrosurgical heat damage may affect endothelial function measurements
5. **RNA integrity**: mRNA degrades rapidly if IMA tissue not snap-frozen promptly
6. **No clinical endpoints**: Primary outcomes are biomarkers only; clinical outcomes (complications) collected at 6-12 week follow-up but not formally powered

## Relevance to Senolytics Research

This protocol is directly relevant as one of the first randomized controlled trials testing a senolytic agent (quercetin monotherapy) in a surgical patient population. It specifically targets the senescence-inflammation axis in a clinically vulnerable population (post-ACS CABG patients). The inclusion of ANGPTL2 as a senescence biomarker and IMA endothelial transcriptomics provides mechanistic depth. However, it uses quercetin alone rather than the more commonly studied dasatinib + quercetin combination, and results were not yet available at time of publication.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample size calculation of n=100 relies on SD estimate of 1.215 from Noly et al. 2019 (n=47) -- this is a relatively small pilot to derive a variance estimate, which could be unstable.
- The 50% relative reduction in hs-CRP assumed as clinically significant is not justified by a formal minimally important difference threshold.
- Quercetin described as a "senolytic drug" but evidence for quercetin monotherapy as a senolytic in vivo is less robust than for dasatinib + quercetin combination; most preclinical senolytic data use the combination.
