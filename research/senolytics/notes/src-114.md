# src-114: Farr et al. (2024) -- D+Q Phase 2 RCT in Postmenopausal Women

**Citation:** Farr JN, Atkinson EJ, Achenbach SJ, et al. Effects of intermittent senolytic therapy on bone metabolism in postmenopausal women: a phase 2 randomized controlled trial. *Nature Medicine*. 2024;30(9):2605-2612. doi:10.1038/s41591-024-03096-2

**PMID:** 38956156 | **PMCID:** PMC11705617 | **ClinicalTrials.gov:** NCT04313634

## Core Findings

Intermittent D+Q (dasatinib 100 mg/day x 2 days + quercetin 1000 mg/day x 3 days, monthly for 5 cycles) did **not** reduce the primary endpoint of bone resorption (serum CTx at 20 weeks) in 60 postmenopausal women aged 62-88 years. However, D+Q significantly increased the bone formation marker P1NP at 2 and 4 weeks (+16% vs control, P<0.025), an effect that waned by 20 weeks. In exploratory analyses, women with the highest senescent cell burden (top tertile of T cell p16_variant 5 mRNA) showed robust skeletal responses: increased P1NP (+34%), decreased CTx (-11%) at 2 weeks, and increased radius BMD (+2.7%) at 20 weeks.

## Study Design

- **Type:** Phase 2, single-site, open-label, randomized controlled trial (Mayo Clinic)
- **N:** 60 postmenopausal women (30 per group), screened from 177 candidates
- **Population:** Women aged 60-90 years with T cell p16 mRNA above the 95th percentile of young controls (22-31 years). All vitamin D sufficient. Age range 62-88 years.
- **Duration:** 20 weeks
- **Arms:** (1) Control vs (2) D+Q
  - Dasatinib: 100 mg/day for 2 consecutive days (oral, Sprycel)
  - Quercetin: 250 mg x 4/day (1000 mg/day) for 3 consecutive days (oral, Quercetin Phytosome by Thorne Research)
  - Intermittent: repeated every 28 days (5 total dosing cycles)
- **Originally planned:** Three-arm study including fisetin, but fisetin arm dropped due to COVID and cost
- **Blinding:** Open-label (over-encapsulation was cost-prohibitive)
- **Randomization:** Block randomization by study statistician
- **First enrollment:** June 9, 2020; Last enrollment: November 15, 2022

## Dosing Regimen Details

- First dose of each cycle administered under supervision in Clinical Research and Trials Unit
- Subsequent doses taken at home with logging
- All participants received 1000 IU vitamin D daily and counseled for ~1200 mg/day calcium
- Dasatinib half-life: 3-4 hours; Quercetin half-life: ~3.5 hours

## Primary Endpoint

**Percentage change in serum CTx (C-terminal telopeptide of type 1 collagen) at 20 weeks.**

Result: **Not met.**
- D+Q: -4.1% (IQR: -13.2, 2.6)
- Control: -7.7% (IQR: -20.1, 14.3)
- P = 0.611

## Secondary Endpoints

### Bone Formation Marker (P1NP)
- **2 weeks:** D+Q +16% relative to control, P = 0.020 (significant)
- **4 weeks:** D+Q +16% relative to control, P = 0.024 (significant)
- **20 weeks:** D+Q -9% relative to control, P = 0.149 (not significant)

### CTx at Intermediate Time Points
- 2 weeks and 4 weeks: No difference between groups

### Bone Mineral Density (DXA)
- Radius, femur neck, lumbar spine: No differences between groups at 20 weeks in overall analysis

## Senescence Biomarker: T Cell p16 mRNA

The study evaluated two assays:
1. **p16_variant 1+5** (standard p16Ink4a/CDKN2A assay): Substantial spread even in young women; correlated with age in younger women but not well in the study participants
2. **p16_variant 5** (novel assay targeting a specific splice variant): Extremely low in young women; correlated strongly with age in the study cohort (r=0.51, P<0.001)

The p16_variant 5 assay was used as the primary stratification tool and proved superior for identifying D+Q responders.

## Exploratory Subgroup Analysis (by p16 Tertile)

### T3 (Highest p16_variant 5, ~10 per arm):
- P1NP at 2 weeks: +34% relative to control, P = 0.035
- P1NP at 4 weeks: +38% relative to control, P = 0.160 (trend, greater variability)
- CTx at 2 weeks: -11% relative to control, P = 0.049
- CTx at 4 weeks: -18% relative to control, P = 0.053 (trend)
- Radius BMD at 20 weeks: +2.7% relative to control, P = 0.004
- Femur neck and spine BMD: No significant differences

### T1/T2 (Lower p16_variant 5):
- No beneficial skeletal response to D+Q at any time point
- P1NP actually lower in D+Q group at 20 weeks

### SASP Factors
- Baseline SASP factors generally higher in T3 vs T1/T2 (sclerostin, Fas, MMP2, PARC significant)
- No differences in circulating SASP changes between D+Q and control in T3 group at 2 weeks
- Authors suggest circulating SASP may not reflect tissue-level effects; tissue biopsies may be needed

## Safety Data

- **No serious or severe adverse events** related to D+Q
- Most common AEs in D+Q group: headache (53%), diarrhea (23%), nausea (17%), constipation (10%), GI reflux (7%), vertigo (7%), fatigue (13%), body pain (7%)
- Overall AE reporting: 77% D+Q vs 17% control (expected given open-label design)
- **One withdrawal** for QTc prolongation after dasatinib (lost to follow-up after week 4)
- **One withdrawal** at week 9 for fatigue and nonspecific side effects
- **One withdrawal** after week 2 for COVID concerns
- Two control participants terminated early

## Key Mechanistic Insight

The time course of P1NP/CTx changes in T3 D+Q responders closely resembles romosozumab (sclerostin inhibitor) treatment. Preclinical data show Sost mRNA is reduced in osteocytes after senescent cell clearance, and SOST has been identified as a SASP factor. This suggests D+Q may partially act through sclerostin reduction, though circulating sclerostin was not reduced at 2 weeks (possibly because sclerostin functions as a paracrine factor).

## Limitations

1. **Open-label design** -- AE reporting biased toward D+Q group
2. **Women only** -- no data on men
3. **Primary endpoint not met** -- negative for main outcome
4. **Exploratory subgroup analyses** -- ~10 per arm in T3, underpowered
5. **No follow-up p16 measurements** post-treatment (due to cost/logistics)
6. **Circulating SASP not responsive** to treatment
7. **Short duration** (20 weeks) for BMD endpoints
8. **Cost-prohibitive blinding** -- could not over-encapsulate medications
9. The attenuation of P1NP response by 20 weeks mirrors romosozumab but raises question about durable efficacy

## Implications for OTC Senolytics

- This is the **largest RCT of sustained intermittent D+Q** in generally healthy older individuals (n=60)
- Quercetin at 1000 mg/day x 3 days per month showed acceptable safety (no serious AEs) but the dosing involved pharmaceutical-grade quercetin phytosome (Thorne Research), not standard OTC supplements
- The **overall null primary endpoint** underscores that senolytics do not universally produce skeletal benefits -- senescent cell burden appears to be a critical mediator of response
- The hit-and-run mechanism (short half-lives, monthly dosing) supports feasibility of intermittent OTC use, but efficacy data for OTC senolytics alone remain limited
- The study notes that quercetin and fisetin are "already being widely used on an empiric basis" despite limited safety/efficacy data in healthy older adults
- Suggests potential for even less frequent dosing (every 5-6 months) but this is speculative

## Power Calculation

Based on previous data, 30 per group provided 90% power to detect a 19.8% difference in CTx between groups (allowing for 10% withdrawal rate).

## Statistical Methods

- Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables
- Chi-squared for categorical
- Spearman correlations for p16-age relationships
- All analyses intention-to-treat
- Sensitivity analyses using imputed values and ANCOVA
- P < 0.05 considered significant
