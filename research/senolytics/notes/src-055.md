# src-055: Fisetin is a senotherapeutic that extends health and lifespan

**Citation:** Yousefzadeh MJ, Zhu Y, McGowan SJ, et al. *eBioMedicine*. 2018;36:18-28. doi:10.1016/j.ebiom.2018.09.015
**PMCID:** PMC6197652 | **PMID:** 30279143
**Venue:** eBioMedicine (open access)
**Quality:** Full text available, substantive methods/results/discussion

---

## Core Findings

Fisetin, a flavonoid polyphenol found in strawberries and other fruits/vegetables, was identified as the most potent senolytic among 10 flavonoids screened, demonstrating the ability to selectively reduce senescent cells in vitro (murine and human fibroblasts), in vivo (progeroid and aged wild-type mice), and ex vivo (human adipose tissue explants). Late-life dietary intervention with fisetin in aged mice extended both median and maximum lifespan, reduced age-related tissue pathology across multiple organs, and improved biochemical markers of tissue homeostasis.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### In Vitro Screening (Section 3, Fig. 1)
- **10 flavonoids screened** at 5 uM: fisetin, luteolin, curcumin, resveratrol, rutin, EGCG, apigenin, catechin, quercetin, pirfenidone, myricetin
- **Fisetin most potent** at reducing SA-beta-gal+ cells in Ercc1-/- MEFs (oxidative stress-induced senescence) and IMR90 human fibroblasts (etoposide-induced senescence)
- Dose-dependent reduction of senescence markers across 1-15 uM range
- Cell-type-specific mechanism: fisetin induces caspase 3/7-mediated apoptosis in senescent HUVECs but suppresses senescence markers in MEFs without killing
- Luteolin and curcumin showed weak senolytic activity at 5 uM; quercetin was ineffective at this dose

### In Vivo - Progeroid Mice (Section 3, Fig. 2-3)
- **Model:** Ercc1-/-;p16-Ink4a-luciferase reporter mice
- **Dosing:** 500 ppm fisetin diet (~60 mg/kg/day), intermittent (2 weeks on/off)
- **Key result:** Luciferase signal (p16 expression) suppressed during fisetin treatment and remained suppressed during 4-week washout period -- consistent with hit-and-run senolytic mechanism
- Fisetin reduced p16Ink4a, p21Cip1, and SASP markers (IL-1beta, IL-6, IL-10, TNF-alpha, CXCL2, MCP-1, PAI-1) in fat, spleen, liver, kidney, and peripheral CD3+ T cells
- Reduced hepatic oxidative stress (lower 4-HNE adducts, higher GSH:GSSG ratio)

### In Vivo - Aged Wild-Type Mice, Acute Treatment (Section 3, Fig. 4)
- **Model:** 22-24 month old C57BL/6 and INK-ATTAC mice
- **Dosing:** 100 mg/kg fisetin by oral gavage for 5 consecutive days
- CyTOF analysis of subcutaneous fat identified senescent cell types:
  - **Cleared by fisetin:** c-Kit+ mesenchymal stem/progenitor cells, CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, NK-1.1+ NK cells, CD146+CD31+ endothelial cells
  - **NOT cleared:** Macrophages and dendritic cells with high p16 or CENP-B
- First demonstration that a senolytic differentially affects senescent cells of different lineages in vivo
- CENP-B (centromeric marker of senescence) confirmed results; p21 was not significantly elevated in these populations

### Human Ex Vivo Data (Section 3, Fig. 4E-F)
- **Subjects:** n=3 females, ages 55-66 (2 lean BMI 25.5/26.2; 1 obese BMI 45.6)
- **Tissue:** Greater omental adipose explants treated with 20 uM fisetin for 48h
- **Results:** Significant reduction in SA-beta-gal+ cells and SASP factors IL-6, IL-8, MCP-1
- **Limitation:** Very small sample size (n=3), no healthy vs disease comparison

### Lifespan Study (Section 3, Fig. 5)
- **Model:** WT f1 C57BL/6:FVB mice, started fisetin at 85 weeks of age
- **Dosing:** 500 ppm fisetin diet continuously until death
- **n = 8-9 mice per group** (small sample for lifespan study)
- **Result:** Extension of both median and maximum lifespan (Log-rank Mantel-Cox test)
- Exact percentage extension not reported in the text; data presented in Fig. 5A-B

### Healthspan Markers (Fig. 5)
- Reduced serum ALT and amylase (improved liver and pancreatic homeostasis)
- Reduced age-related histopathology in brain, kidney, liver, lung, forepaw (Geropathology Grading Platform)
- Reduced senescence/SASP markers in multiple tissues of aged WT mice
- Reduced circulating MCP-1
- Reduced hepatic oxidative stress (lower HNE, higher GSH:GSSG)

## Mechanism of Action

Multiple mechanisms likely contribute:
1. **Senolytic (primary):** Targets PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway (a known SCAP); disrupts senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways; induces apoptosis selectively in senescent cells
2. **Senomorphic:** May also suppress SASP without killing in some cell types
3. **Antioxidant:** Direct free radical scavenging; upregulates glutathione synthesis
4. **Anti-inflammatory:** Inhibits NF-kB, TNF-alpha, IL-6
5. **Other:** Topoisomerase inhibitor; activates hSIRT1

Key mechanistic evidence: Effects persist days after fisetin is cleared (rapid half-life 0.09h, terminal 3.1h), consistent with cell elimination rather than ongoing receptor/drug activity.

## Limitations

1. **Small lifespan study sample:** Only 8-9 mice per group for lifespan endpoint, which limits statistical power
2. **Human data limited to n=3 ex vivo:** No in vivo human data; SENECA trial mentioned as underway but no results reported
3. **Exact lifespan extension percentage not reported** in the text; only shown graphically
4. **Cell-type specificity not fully explained:** Why macrophages/dendritic cells are spared is unclear
5. **Multiple mechanisms confound interpretation:** Fisetin has antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and other activities that could independently contribute to health/lifespan benefits beyond senolysis
6. **Fisetin bioavailability is poor:** Short half-life noted (0.09h rapid, 3.1h terminal)
7. **Single tissue (subcutaneous fat) for CyTOF analysis:** Other tissues not analyzed at single-cell resolution
8. **Conflict of interest:** Authors hold patents on senolytic drugs through Mayo Clinic

## Relevance to Research Questions

This is the foundational paper establishing fisetin as a senolytic. It provides:
- Direct evidence that an OTC-available flavonoid has senolytic activity comparable to or exceeding D+Q in vitro
- First demonstration that fisetin extends lifespan in vertebrate animals
- Mechanistic evidence for hit-and-run senolytic activity supporting intermittent dosing
- Human ex vivo data supporting translational feasibility
- Dosing framework for human clinical trials (SENECA trial cited as starting)

## Claims to Verify

- **Lifespan extension percentage:** The paper states "extension of median as well as maximal lifespan" but does not provide a percentage in the text. The data in Fig. 5A-B would need to be inspected for exact values. This is a critical quantitative gap.
- **Sample sizes for lifespan:** n=8-9 per group is notably small for a lifespan study; the log-rank test result should be interpreted cautiously.
- **"No adverse effects" claim:** The paper states "no adverse effects of fisetin have been reported, even when given at high doses" citing reference 45, but this should be verified against current safety data as this claim predates the more recent clinical trials.
