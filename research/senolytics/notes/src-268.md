# src-268: Yu et al. (2021) Quercetin Reverses Cardiac Systolic Dysfunction in HFD Mice

## Core Findings

Quercetin (50 mg/kg, oral gavage, 5-day biweekly for 10 weeks) improved left ventricular ejection fraction in high-fat diet-fed C57BL/6J mice from 56.20% to 67.42% (P=0.026 vs HFD vehicle), approaching control levels (73.38%). Critically, cardiac senescence was **not detected** in any group, meaning quercetin's cardioprotective effects were independent of senolytic activity and instead attributed to direct proangiogenic, antioxidant, and lipid-lowering mechanisms. This is a negative finding for the senolytic hypothesis in the heart specifically, though senolysis may operate in other organs.

## Study Design

- **Model:** Male C57BL/6J mice, 11 weeks old, 4 groups (n=6-8 each): control+vehicle, control+quercetin, HFD+vehicle, HFD+quercetin
- **Diet:** HFD group received 60% kcal from fat (Research Diets D12492) for 6 months before treatment began
- **Quercetin dosing:** 50 mg/kg by oral gavage, 5 consecutive days every 2 weeks (biweekly), for 10 weeks total
- **Cardiac function:** Assessed by 16.4T MRI (high-field small-animal scanner)
- **Senescence assessment:** SA-beta-Gal (colorimetric + fluorescent SPiDER-beta-Gal), qPCR for p16, p21, p53, activin-A
- **Other endpoints:** CD31 microvascular density, 8-OHdG oxidative stress, Oil-Red-O fat deposition, trichrome fibrosis, cardiomyocyte cross-sectional area
- **In vitro:** HUVEC tube formation assay with ox-LDL (100 ug/ml) +/- quercetin (10, 20 uM)

## Key Evidence

### Cardiac Function
| Parameter | CV | CQ | HV | HQ |
|-----------|-----|-----|------|------|
| LVEF (%) | 73.38 +/- 5.04 | 66.71 +/- 3.64 | 56.20 +/- 15.80* | 67.42 +/- 7.50 |
| Stroke volume (ml) | 40.93 +/- 6.10 | 40.02 +/- 5.13 | 37.40 +/- 11.38 | 49.66 +/- 9.66 |
| Cardiac output (cm3/min) | 20.83 +/- 2.46 | 20.04 +/- 3.19 | 17.35 +/- 6.04 | 23.77 +/- 4.44 |

*P<0.05 vs CV; HQ P=0.026 vs HV for LVEF

### Metabolic Effects
- Quercetin normalized heart weight (P=0.016 vs HV) and plasma triglycerides (P=0.027 vs HV)
- No significant effect on body weight, blood pressure, total cholesterol, or insulin resistance
- HOMA-IR showed trend toward increase in HFD groups but did not reach significance (P=0.188 HV, P=0.06 HQ)

### Senescence Findings (Key Negative Result)
- SA-beta-Gal staining (both colorimetric and fluorescent) showed no differences among groups
- Senescence gene expression (p16, p21, p53, activin-A) did not differ between CV and HV
- Paradoxically, p16 and p53 were **upregulated** in HQ (HFD+quercetin) compared to all other groups
- Authors speculate p53 upregulation may promote mesenchymal-to-endothelial transition; p16 upregulation may arrest fibroblast proliferation

### Angiogenesis Mechanism
- HFD decreased CD31+ microvascular density; quercetin blunted this rarefaction
- Quercetin upregulated angiopoietin and VEGF gene expression in HFD hearts
- In vitro: quercetin (10, 20 uM) reversed ox-LDL-impaired HUVEC tube formation
- Authors conclude cardioprotection was mediated by direct proangiogenic activity, not senolysis

### Other Cardiac Benefits
- Reduced interstitial and perivascular fibrosis
- Improved cardiomyocyte hypertrophy
- Decreased myocardial oxidative stress (8-OHdG)
- Decreased intramyocardial fat deposition (Oil-Red-O)

## Limitations

1. **Small sample sizes in some subgroups** -- HV had n=5 and HQ had n=4 for systemic parameters; this limits statistical power
2. **Non-fasting blood samples** for glucose and insulin; HOMA-IR is a crude estimate
3. **Cannot distinguish prevention vs reversal** -- Q treatment started after 6 months of HFD but effects could be partially preventive
4. **In vitro concentrations (10-20 uM) not directly comparable** to in vivo dosing (50 mg/kg)
5. **Male mice only** -- results may not generalize to females
6. **No dose-response** for in vivo quercetin (single dose tested)

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Quercetin Dosing and OTC Supplements
- Dose: 50 mg/kg oral gavage, intermittent schedule (5 days on / 9 days off, biweekly)
- This dose is substantially higher than typical OTC supplement doses (500-1000 mg/day for a human, roughly 7-14 mg/kg)
- Allometric scaling would suggest a human-equivalent dose of approximately 4 mg/kg or ~280 mg for a 70 kg person, which is within OTC supplement range
- Intermittent dosing schedule (not daily) aligns with senolytic protocols used in clinical trials (D+Q given 3 consecutive days every 2 weeks)
- The study rationale for intermittent dosing was explicitly senolytic: senescent cells take ~6 weeks to develop, so intermittent administration targets antiapoptotic pathways

### Senolytic vs Non-Senolytic Mechanisms
- This paper is important because it shows quercetin's cardioprotective effects can occur **independently of senolysis** in the heart
- The absence of cardiac senescence in HFD mice contrasts with the same group's prior finding of renal senescence in HFD (Kim et al., 2019, reference [7] in this paper)
- Suggests tissue-specific senescence responses to metabolic stress: kidney may develop senescence while heart does not
- Quercetin may still exert senolytic effects in other organs (fat, kidney, liver), indirectly benefiting the heart

## Claims to Verify

1. **Sample size discrepancy**: The methods state n=6-8 per group, but Figure 2 legend shows HV n=5 and HQ n=4 for systemic parameters, and Table 2 shows CQ n=7 and HV n=5 for MRI. These are small numbers that affect statistical reliability, particularly with the high variance in HV LVEF (SD=15.80).
2. **P-values**: LVEF improvement P=0.026 is reported in the text but should be cross-checked against the table. The large SD in the HV group (15.80) raises questions about whether a few outliers drive the group difference.
