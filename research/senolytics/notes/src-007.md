# src-007: Top 6 Natural Approaches to Aging: Defining Senolytic Supplements + More

**Source:** iHerb Wellness Hub blog article by Dr. Candace Mathers, N.D. (October 27, 2023)
**URL:** https://www.iherb.com/blog/top-natural-approaches-to-aging-senolytic-supplements/1809
**Type:** Commercial supplement retailer blog (non-peer-reviewed)
**Quality Assessment:** Low -- popular science article on a supplement retailer's website. Cites peer-reviewed studies but does not present original research. Evidence is filtered through a commercial lens.

## Core Findings

The article reviews six natural compounds with reported senolytic activity: fisetin, apigenin, quercetin, curcumin, milk thistle, and EGCG. Evidence overwhelmingly comes from in-vitro and animal studies, with only one human clinical pilot study (dasatinib + quercetin, n=9) cited. The article serves as a consumer-facing overview, not a rigorous scientific review.

## Compounds Discussed

1. **Fisetin** -- flavonoid found in strawberries, apples, tomatoes, cucumbers. In-vitro senolytic activity in human adipose tissue; extended lifespan in aged mice. Anti-inflammatory (suppresses cytokines via PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway).

2. **Apigenin** -- flavonoid in parsley, celery, artichokes, chamomile. Suppresses SASP (senescence-associated secretory phenotype) in vitro. Upregulates glutathione production.

3. **Quercetin** -- flavonoid in onions, broccoli, apples, cherries. Only compound with human clinical data cited: open-label phase I pilot (n=9, ages 68-71, diabetic kidney disease) using dasatinib + quercetin for 3 days, reducing senescent cells in adipose tissue at 11-day follow-up. Note: the senolytic effect here is from the combination with dasatinib, not quercetin alone.

4. **Curcumin** -- active compound in turmeric. Animal data only: ~40% average lifespan increase in Wistar rats, ~20% maximum lifespan increase in C. elegans. Proposed mechanism: telomere length support via enzyme activation.

5. **Milk Thistle (Silybum marianum)** -- in-vitro data showing elimination of senescent human skin cells and increased proliferation of younger cells. Also noted for antioxidant, metabolic, and blood pressure support.

6. **EGCG** -- flavonoid from green tea. In-vitro: promotes mitochondrial biogenesis, increases longevity-promoting protein synthesis via reduced oxidative stress.

## Mechanisms Covered

- **Senescent cell clearance (senolysis):** primary mechanism for fisetin, quercetin, milk thistle
- **SASP suppression:** apigenin, quercetin
- **Anti-inflammatory:** fisetin (PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway), quercetin
- **Antioxidant:** apigenin (glutathione), curcumin, milk thistle, EGCG
- **Mitochondrial support:** EGCG (new mitochondrial growth)
- **Telomere support:** curcumin (enzyme-mediated telomere length maintenance)

## Dosing

No specific dosing information is provided for any compound. The article does not discuss dosage, frequency, or duration of supplementation.

## Evidence Quality

- **Human clinical data:** Only one study cited -- dasatinib + quercetin pilot (n=9). This is the Hickson et al. 2019 EBioMedicine study. Notable limitation: effect is from combination therapy, not quercetin alone.
- **Animal data:** Fisetin (aged mice lifespan), curcumin (Wistar rats, C. elegans), EGCG (C. elegans implied).
- **In-vitro data:** Fisetin (human adipose tissue), apigenin (SASP suppression), milk thistle (human skin cells), EGCG (mitochondrial function).
- **No human RCTs** for any single natural compound as a senolytic are cited.
- References are legitimate peer-reviewed publications, but the article selectively presents positive findings.

## Safety

No safety information, side effects, contraindications, or drug interactions are discussed for any compound. The article includes only the standard FDA disclaimer that statements have not been evaluated and products are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent disease.

## Limitations

1. Commercial bias: published on iHerb, a supplement retailer, creating inherent conflict of interest
2. No original research -- synthesis of selected published studies
3. No dosing guidance provided
4. No safety or contraindication information
5. Selective citation of positive findings without discussing null results or replication failures
6. Animal and in-vitro evidence presented without adequate caveats about translational relevance
7. The one human study (dasatinib + quercetin) is for combination therapy, not the natural compound alone

## Relevance

Provides a consumer-level overview of six natural senolytic compounds with citations to legitimate primary research. Useful for identifying candidate compounds and locating primary references, but not suitable as an authoritative source for claims about efficacy, dosing, or safety in humans. The primary research citations (especially Yousefzadeh 2018 on fisetin, Hickson 2019 on dasatinib+quercetin, Perrott 2017 on apigenin) are more valuable than the article itself.

## Claims to Verify

- "curcumin supplementation was associated with an increase in the average life span of Wistar rats by almost 40%" -- this is a very large effect size for lifespan extension and should be verified in the original study (cited as Zia et al. 2021 or Pan et al. 2017)
- "curcumin supplementation increased the maximum lifespan [of C. elegans] by over 20%" -- verify original source
- Phase I pilot study details: n=9 participants, ages 68-71, 3-day treatment, 11-day follow-up -- these specifics match the Hickson et al. 2019 paper but should be cross-checked
