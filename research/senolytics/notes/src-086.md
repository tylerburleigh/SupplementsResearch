# src-086: SToMP-AD -- Senolytic Therapy in Mild Alzheimer's Disease (Phase 1)

**Citation:** Gonzales MM, Garbarino VR, Kautz TF, et al. "Senolytic therapy in mild Alzheimer's disease: a phase 1 feasibility trial." *Nature Medicine* 29, 2481-2488 (2023). DOI: 10.1038/s41591-023-02543-w

**Source quality:** Abstract + extended data descriptions from Nature Medicine landing page. Full text behind paywall. Abstract contains substantial quantitative detail.

**Citations:** 216 (as of fetch date)

---

## Core Findings

This first-in-human phase 1 open-label trial demonstrated that oral dasatinib plus quercetin (D+Q) senolytic therapy is feasible, safe, and well-tolerated in patients with mild Alzheimer's disease. Dasatinib crossed the blood-brain barrier (detected in CSF of 4/5 participants), while quercetin did not reach detectable CSF levels. Biomarker changes showed increased CSF IL-6 and GFAP (significant), trending decreases in senescence-related cytokines, and a non-significant trend toward higher Aβ42 levels.

## Study Design

- **Trial name:** SToMP-AD (Senolytic Therapy to Modulate the Progression of Alzheimer's Disease)
- **Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04063124
- **Phase:** 1 (open-label, proof-of-concept, feasibility)
- **Duration:** 12 weeks
- **Drug:** Oral dasatinib + quercetin (D+Q)
- **Population:** 5 participants with early-stage symptomatic AD
  - Mean age: 76 +/- 5 years
  - 40% female (2/5)
  - All completed the study (no early discontinuation)
- **Primary endpoints:** CNS penetrance, safety, feasibility
- **Secondary endpoints:** Cognitive and neuroimaging measures

## CNS Penetrance Data (Key Finding)

- **Blood dasatinib:** 12.7-73.5 ng/ml (increased in all participants)
- **Blood quercetin:** 3.29-26.3 ng/ml (increased in all participants)
- **CSF dasatinib:** Detected in 4/5 participants (80%), ranging 0.281-0.536 ng/ml
- **CSF-to-plasma ratio for dasatinib:** 0.422-0.919%
- **CSF quercetin:** Not detected in any participant

This is the first demonstration that dasatinib crosses the BBB in AD patients, though at low CSF-to-plasma ratios. Quercetin's failure to reach detectable CSF levels raises questions about its direct CNS contribution in the D+Q combination.

## Biomarker Changes

### Significant changes (paired t-tests, n=5)
- **CSF IL-6:** Increased, t(4) = 3.913, P = 0.008
- **CSF GFAP:** Increased, t(4) = 3.354, P = 0.028

### Trends (not statistically significant)
- **CSF Aβ42:** Trend toward increase, t(4) = -2.338, P = 0.079
- **Senescence-related cytokines/chemokines:** Trending decreases
- No correction for multiple comparisons (due to small n)

### Extended data (Simoa platform)
- CSF GFAP mean change: 2560 [95% CI: 440.9 to 4679]
- CSF Aβ42 mean change: -3.110 [95% CI: -96.96 to 90.74]
- CSF pTau-181 mean change: -12.04 [95% CI: -34.01 to 9.930]
- CSF NfL mean change: -23.20 [95% CI: -695.6 to 649.2]
- CSF pTau-231 mean change: -1.304 [95% CI: -25.19 to 22.58]
- Plasma biomarkers (pTau-181, NfL, GFAP, Aβ40, Aβ42) showed no significant changes

**Interpretation of IL-6/GFAP increases:** The authors suggest these increases may reflect senescent cell clearance releasing intracellular contents, consistent with senolytic mechanism of action. However, elevated IL-6 and GFAP are typically associated with worse AD pathology, so this interpretation requires confirmation in placebo-controlled trials.

## Safety and Tolerability

- Treatment well-tolerated with no early discontinuation
- No significant changes in cognitive or neuroimaging endpoints (supporting safety)
- Cognitive assessments included: WMS-IV, UDS neuropsychological battery, verbal fluency (FAS/animal naming), Boston Naming Test, Hopkins Verbal Learning Test-Revised, MoCA, Lawton IADL
- Neuroimaging: MRI-based brain volume analyses

## Limitations

1. **Sample size of 5** -- severely limits statistical power and generalizability
2. **Open-label, no placebo control** -- cannot attribute biomarker changes to treatment vs. natural history or procedural effects (lumbar puncture itself)
3. **No dosing details available** from abstract (specific D and Q doses not reported in accessible content)
4. **Quercetin undetectable in CSF** -- raises questions about its role in CNS effects
5. **IL-6 and GFAP increases** are ambiguous -- could reflect adverse neuroinflammation rather than therapeutic senescent cell clearance
6. **No multiple comparison correction** applied due to small sample size
7. **Short duration (12 weeks)** -- may be insufficient to detect meaningful cognitive or neuroimaging changes
8. **Homogeneous population** -- single-site study at UT Health San Antonio

## Claims to Verify

- **Dosing regimen:** The specific doses of dasatinib and quercetin used are not reported in the abstract or extended data descriptions available. The published protocol (Gonzales et al., J Prev Alzheimers Dis 2022, ref 29 in the paper) likely contains this information.
- **Blood D/Q levels:** "12.7-73.5 ng/ml for D and 3.29-26.3 ng/ml for Q" -- these ranges suggest substantial inter-individual variability. Verify whether these are peak or trough levels and the timing of blood draws relative to dosing.

## Implications for Senolytics Research

- **Proof of concept for CNS application:** Demonstrates that at least one component of the D+Q combination (dasatinib) reaches the CNS in AD patients, a prerequisite for direct CNS senolytic effects.
- **Justifies larger trials:** The safety and feasibility data support moving to placebo-controlled Phase 2 trials.
- **Mechanistic ambiguity:** The IL-6 and GFAP increases could indicate either senescent cell clearance (positive) or neuroinflammatory exacerbation (negative), necessitating controlled trials with appropriate comparators.
- **Quercetin limitation:** If quercetin does not cross the BBB, its contribution to CNS effects would be peripheral (e.g., reducing systemic SASP). Alternative senolytics with better BBB penetration may warrant investigation.
- **Patent and commercial interests:** Authors Kirkland and Tchkonia hold patents on senolytic therapy with royalties paid by Unity Biotechnologies; Orr et al. have pending patents on neuronal senescence biosignatures.

## Relevance

This trial is directly relevant to research questions about senolytic clinical trial outcomes, D+Q safety and efficacy in humans, CNS penetrance of senolytic drugs, and biomarker responses to senolytic therapy in neurodegenerative disease. It is one of the first human trials testing senolytics in AD and provides foundational pharmacokinetic and safety data for the field.
