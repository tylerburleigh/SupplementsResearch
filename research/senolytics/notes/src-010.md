## Core Findings

This is a general-audience overview of senolytic supplements aimed at consumers. It covers the concept of cellular senescence, SASP, senescence markers, natural and synthetic senolytic compounds, commercial products, safety concerns, and regulatory gaps. It does not present any original research or clinical data.

## Compounds Discussed

**Natural senolytics:**
- **Fisetin** -- found in strawberries and apples; noted for antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties
- **Quercetin** -- found in onions, apples, green tea; supports immune function
- **Resveratrol** -- from red grape skins, 70+ plant species; Howitz and Sinclair (2003) found lifespan extension in yeast; later studies in worms, fruit flies, fish (2006), and mice
- **Pterostilbene** -- from blueberries
- **Curcumin** -- from turmeric; uncertain classification as senolytic; neuroprotective; anti-inflammatory
- **EGCG** -- from green tea; supports cellular health
- **Glucosamine** -- mentioned repeatedly as having slow but steady anti-aging benefits

**Synthetic/pharmaceutical:**
- **Dasatinib** -- often combined with quercetin (D+Q regimen); Mayo Clinic mouse studies showed improved memory and learning
- **ABT-263 (Navitoclax)** -- senolytic but linked to negative effects on ovarian health in older females; potential to accelerate ovarian aging and promote infertility

## Mechanisms Described

1. Senolytics identify senescent cells and block protective proteins (e.g., Bcl-2 family)
2. Without anti-apoptotic protection, senescent cells undergo programmed cell death
3. Healthy cells replace the cleared senescent cells
4. SASP (Senescence Associated Secretory Phenotype) described as pro-inflammatory secretions from senescent cells that influence neighboring tissues
5. Senescence markers discussed: telomere length, p16INK4a, SA-beta-gal, DNA methylation patterns

## Safety Concerns

- ABT-263: negative effects on ovarian health in older females; potential infertility risk
- High doses of fisetin: may impair immune cell functions, complicating infection responses
- General side effects: nausea, headaches possible; severe reactions in those with pre-existing conditions
- Long-term immunological consequences of senolytic use are poorly understood
- Experts caution about nuanced effects on immune surveillance and infection recovery

## Regulatory Concerns

- Senolytic supplements classified as dietary supplements in the US
- FDA does NOT evaluate for safety or effectiveness before market entry
- Manufacturers cannot guarantee ingredients, quality, or compound amounts
- Third-party testing recommended but not required
- Product labels/websites carry "informational purposes only" disclaimers
