# src-199: Islam et al. (2023) — D+Q Attenuates Adipose Tissue Inflammation and Ameliorates Metabolic Function in Old Age

**Citation:** Islam MT, Tuday E, Allen S, Kim J, Trott DW, Holland WL, Donato AJ, Lesniewski LA. Aging Cell. 2023;22(2):e13767. doi:10.1111/acel.13767. PMCID: PMC9924942.

## Core Findings

Intermittent oral administration of dasatinib (5 mg/kg) and quercetin (50 mg/kg) — three consecutive days every two weeks for three months — to 21-month-old male C57BL/6 mice reduced senescence markers (SA-beta-gal, p16, p21, P16 protein) and pro-inflammatory SASP gene expression specifically in perigonadal white adipose tissue (pgWAT), with no robust effects in liver or skeletal muscle. D+Q also reduced T cell and macrophage infiltration in pgWAT, lowered fasting glucose, improved glucose tolerance via suppressed hepatic gluconeogenesis (not via improved insulin sensitivity), improved insulin-stimulated NEFA suppression, reduced plasma triglycerides, and improved lipid tolerance.

## Study Design

- **Model:** Male C57BL/6 mice from NIA colony (Charles River). Young (~3-4 mo) and old (21 mo at treatment start).
- **Groups:** Young control (n=8), Old vehicle control (n=15), Old D+Q (n=17).
- **Dosing:** Dasatinib 5 mg/kg + Quercetin 50 mg/kg, oral gavage, 3 consecutive days every 2 weeks for 3 months. Vehicle: 10% PEG 4000 solution.
- **Metabolic tests performed:** GTT (2 g/kg ip), ITT (1 U/kg ip), PTT (2 g/kg ip), LTT (15 ul/g oral gavage intralipid), glucose-stimulated insulin secretion, insulin-stimulated NEFA suppression, plasma triglycerides (fed and fasted), plasma cholesterol panel.
- **Tissue analyses:** SA-beta-gal histology (pgWAT), qPCR (senescence and SASP genes), Western blot (P16, p-Akt, p-CREB), flow cytometry (T cells CD3+, macrophages CD64+), liver H&E and picrosirius red.
- **Statistics:** One-way ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc for group comparisons; repeated measures ANOVA for tolerance tests. Alpha = 0.05.

## Key Evidence

### Senescence markers (pgWAT)
- SA-beta-gal+ cells and crown-like structures increased with age, reduced by D+Q (all p <= 0.001).
- p16 and p21 gene expression increased with age, reduced by D+Q (all p <= 0.04).
- P16 protein increased with age, reduced by D+Q (both p <= 0.001).
- SASP genes (mcp1, tnf-alpha, il-1alpha, il-1beta, il-6, cxcl2, cxcl10) all reduced by D+Q (all p <= 0.03).

### Immune cell infiltration (pgWAT)
- T cells (CD3+) increased with age, reduced by D+Q (p <= 0.04).
- CD3e and FoxP3 gene expression reduced by D+Q (all p <= 0.02).
- Total, M1, and M2 macrophages increased with age, all reduced by D+Q (all p <= 0.01).

### Tissue specificity
- Liver: p16 gene reduced by D+Q but p16 protein unchanged; only mcp1 reduced among SASP markers. p21 unchanged.
- Gastrocnemius muscle: No effects of D+Q on any senescence or inflammatory markers.

### Metabolic outcomes
- **Fasting blood glucose:** Reduced by D+Q (p = 0.002).
- **Glucose tolerance:** Improved (interaction p < 0.0001, treatment p < 0.0001). AUC lower (p = 0.0004).
- **Plasma insulin during GTT:** No difference between old groups (p >= 0.69).
- **Insulin tolerance:** Baseline difference in glucose but no improvement in insulin sensitivity per se.
- **Hepatic gluconeogenesis (PTT):** Improved by D+Q (treatment p = 0.004). Reduced pck1, pck2, fbp2, g6pc expression (all p <= 0.04). Lower p-CREB/CREB ratio (p = 0.047).
- **Insulin-stimulated NEFA suppression:** Improved by D+Q (p = 0.01). Higher irs-1 and cpt-1alpha in pgWAT.
- **Plasma triglycerides:** Fed and fasted both reduced (p <= 0.04).
- **Lipid tolerance:** Improved (treatment p = 0.006).
- **Cholesterol panel:** No changes in total cholesterol, LDL/VLDL, or HDL.
- **Liver collagen (PSR):** Age-related increase reduced by D+Q (p = 0.007).
- **Body composition:** D+Q reduced body mass and pgWAT mass, increased scWAT mass (all p <= 0.05).

## Methodology Notes

- Mouse-only study; male sex only.
- D+Q dosing matches standard preclinical protocol (Zhu et al., 2015; Xu et al., 2018).
- The authors note that C57BL/6 mice on standard chow do not recapitulate all aspects of human age-related metabolic dysfunction (no age-related glucose intolerance was observed between young and old controls), which is a recognized limitation of this model.
- Flow cytometry used FMO gating; histology scored by blinded examiner.

## Limitations

1. **Male mice only** — sex-specific effects not assessed.
2. **No age-related metabolic impairment in controls** — young and old mice showed similar GTT, PTT, and LTT, limiting interpretability of "reversal" claims. D+Q improved old mice beyond baseline, but this is improvement rather than reversal of an age-related deficit.
3. **No direct human translational data** — dosing, metabolism, and cholesterol handling differ between mice and humans. Quercetin bioavailability in humans is substantially lower than suggested by oral gavage in mice.
4. **No dose-response or alternative regimens tested.**
5. **Tissue-specific effects** (pgWAT but not liver/muscle at the protein level) may limit generalizability to whole-body senolysis claims.
6. **Sample sizes modest** (5-17 per group depending on assay).

## Relevance to Human OTC Use of Senolytics

- This study uses the standard preclinical D+Q cocktail at established doses, providing mechanistic support for the rationale behind human D+Q trials.
- The tissue-specific effects (adipose > liver > muscle) suggest that human OTC quercetin alone would not replicate these findings, as the synergy with dasatinib (a prescription tyrosine kinase inhibitor) is central to the senolytic effect.
- The absence of age-related metabolic impairment in the mouse model means the clinical significance of the D+Q-induced improvements is uncertain for otherwise healthy older adults.
- Plasma cholesterol was unchanged, which may be relevant for OTC users expecting lipid benefits.
- The dosing regimen (intermittent, not daily) aligns with the senolytic paradigm but differs from typical OTC quercetin supplementation patterns.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample sizes stated as Y Ctrl = 8, O Ctrl = 15, O D&Q = 17 in Table 1 note (line 651). These are plausible for a mouse aging study but uneven group sizes should be confirmed against the Methods section (which does not explicitly state randomization or allocation).
- The claim of "no difference" in glucose tolerance between young and old (p = 0.21 interaction) is unusual for C57BL/6 mice and may reflect specific cohort characteristics.
- p-CREB/CREB ratio p = 0.047 (line 934) is borderline; the significance of this single finding should be interpreted cautiously.
