What Are Natural Senolytics?

Senolytics are compounds that target and clear out senescent cells, also called “zombie cells
2
” that build up as we age and send out inflammatory signals. By selectively eliminating these cells, senolytics may help reduce inflammation, improve tissue function, and promote healthier aging

Senolytics come in both natural and synthetic forms. According to a 2019 study published in EbioMedicine, pharmaceutical senolytics like Dasatinib have shown strong effects in lab studies but can come with side effects
3
. On the other hand, natural senolytics like Quercetin are plant-derived compounds found in everyday foods and herbs.

The concept of senolytics emerged around 2015. According to Science Daily, researchers from the Mayo Clinic and Scripps Institute were the first to find compounds capable of selectively eliminating senescent cells
4
. Since then, studies in animals and early human trials have shown promising benefits, particularly for age-related inflammation and tissue repair.

How Senolytics Combat Cellular Senescence

As we age, some of our cells enter a state called senescence, where they stop dividing but don’t die off like they should. These so-called zombie cells linger in tissues, releasing harmful molecules that trigger chronic inflammation and damage surrounding healthy cells
5
. This ongoing inflammation, known as inflammageing, contributes to many age-related diseases, including arthritis and heart disease
6
.

Senolytics target and eliminate these dysfunctional cells, allowing the body to function more efficiently. In fact, compounds like quercetin and fisetin can selectively induce apoptosis (programmed cell death) in senescent cells without harming normal ones
7
.

With their ability to clear out these nefarious cells, senolytics help reduce systemic inflammation, improve tissue regeneration, and support overall longevity.

The 9 Best Natural-Source Senolytic Supplements

Research has identified several plant-derived compounds with senolytic activity that help clear senescent cells that accumulate over time, reduce overall inflammation, and support healthy aging.

Quercetin and Fisetin

Quercetin and fisetin are two of the most well-researched natural senolytics known for their ability to help clear senescent cells and reduce inflammation. Found in fruits and vegetables like apples, onions (quercetin), and strawberries (fisetin), these flavonoids have been shown to promote cellular health and longevity
8
.

A study published in the International Journal of Molecular Sciences found that fisetin, in particular, may be one of the most potent natural senolytics. This senolytic selectively eliminates senescent cells without harming healthy ones.
9

Moreover, research suggests that quercetin and fisetin work even better together
10
, enhancing their senolytic effects.

Epigallocatechin Gallate (EGCG)

Epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) is a major polyphenolic compound found in green tea that has been studied for its potential senolytic properties, which involve the selective elimination of senescent cells.

The senolytic properties of EGCG are highlighted by its ability to induce cell death in senescent cells. EGCG suppresses the accumulation of anti-apoptotic proteins and modulates key signaling pathways, facilitating the removal of these dysfunctional cells, which could be beneficial in developing anti-aging therapies
11
.

Green tea is the richest natural source of EGCG, making it a simple addition to any longevity-focused routine. However, taking too much can strain the liver in some people
12
, so it’s important to consume it in moderation.

Piperlongumine

Piperlongumine is a bioactive compound found in the fruits and roots of long pepper. According to a study published in Scientific Reports, Piperlongumine selectively targets and eliminates senescent cells by increasing oxidative stress within them while sparing healthy cells.
13
This makes it an effective natural senolytic in reducing age-related inflammation and improving tissue function.

Since Piperlongumine works by increasing oxidative stress to target senescent cells, taking too much could also affect healthy cells
14
if not used properly. While more research is needed to know its long-term safety, early studies suggest it could be a helpful tool for supporting healthy aging.

Curcumin

Curcumin is the active compound in turmeric. It has long been studied for its anti-inflammatory and senolytic effects. In a study published in Frontiers, researchers found that curcumin can clear senescent cells by targeting inflammation and oxidative stress.
15
Curcumin helps improve tissue health and function, making it a powerful addition to any anti-aging regimen.

Apigenin

Apigenin is a natural flavonoid found in chamomile. It has long been known for its ability to fight inflammation, protect against cell damage, and stop the growth of abnormal cells.

The flavonoid works by blocking certain signals in the body that cause low-grade inflammation
16
, which causes many age-related diseases (ARDs), such as arthritis, renal dysfunction, and neurodegenerative diseases.

The research indicates that normal dosages are well-established as safe and effective. While the long-term effects of high doses require further study, this should not deter use. Chamomile, whether consumed as a tea or supplement, is a compound that may significantly benefit cellular health.

Resveratrol

Resveratrol is a natural compound found in grapes, berries, red wine, peanuts, and cocoa. It helps activate
sirtuins
, proteins that manage cellular health by controlling aging, inflammation, and metabolism. Resveratrol also modulates cell cycles and multiple pathways involved in cell growth, apoptosis, and senescence.
17

The Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype (SASP) is a hallmark of cellular senescence. Resveratrol has been shown to significantly decrease the expression of SASP-related factors
18
, which may help mitigate the inflammatory and tissue-damaging effects associated with senescent cells.

However, as with any supplement, moderation is important. At high concentrations, resveratrol can become toxic to cells
19
, potentially causing damage rather than providing benefits.

Oleuropein and Hydroxytyrosol

Oleuropein (OLE) and hydroxytyrosol (HT) are two powerful compounds found in olive oil. These natural antioxidants help to protect cells from oxidative stress and inflammation
20
, both of which contribute to aging and chronic diseases.

Oleuropein has been found to support the elimination of senescent cells
21
while hydroxytyrosol can help enhance mitochondrial function
22
, contributing to healthier, more resilient cells.

OLE and HT are easy for the body to absorb, but how well you process them can vary based on what else you’re eating and your overall health picture. While olive oil is an excellent daily staple, supplements may help you reach optimal levels.

FAQs

What exactly are senescent cells and why do they matter for aging?

Senescent cells are aged cells that have stopped dividing but remain metabolically active, secreting inflammatory compounds that can harm surrounding tissues. These “zombie cells” significantly impact the aging process and overall tissue function.

Can senolytics reverse aging?

While senolytics cannot “reverse” aging, research shows they can help optimize cellular health by removing damaged cells that contribute to aging processes.

What are the risks of senolytics?

Natural senolytics generally demonstrate strong safety profiles when used appropriately. However, timing and dosing matter. Because senolytics affect cellular processes, it’s important to follow evidence-based protocols and monitor your response. You should also consult with a healthcare professional before starting any new supplement regime.

What’s the most powerful senolytic?

Natural compounds like fisetin and quercetin demonstrate significant effectiveness in studies, particularly when combined strategically. The most effective approach typically involves using multiple compounds that work through complementary pathways.

What foods are senolytics?

Several foods contain natural senolytic compounds. These include apples and onions (quercetin), strawberries (fisetin), green tea (EGCG), and turmeric (curcumin). However, achieving therapeutic doses through food alone can be challenging, as the concentrations and bioavailability of these compounds vary significantly.

How do natural senolytics support healthy aging and longevity?

Natural senolytic compounds selectively target and remove senescent cells from tissues. This process helps maintain cellular health, reduces inflammation, and supports optimal organ function as we age.
