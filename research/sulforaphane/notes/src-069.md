## Study Design

- **Design:** 12-week randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial
- **N = 291** participants
- **Population:** Residents of He-He Township, Qidong, in the Yangtze River delta region of China (rural area with substantial airborne pollutant exposure)
- **Demographics:** Adults (MeSH terms include Adult, Middle Aged, Aged, Young Adult; both Male and Female)
- **Intervention:** Broccoli sprout-derived beverage providing daily doses of **600 umol glucoraphanin + 40 umol sulforaphane**
- **Control:** Placebo beverage
- **Duration:** 12 weeks (84 days) of daily dosing; measurements at days 0, 1, 7, 14, 28, 42, 56, 70, and 84
- **Primary endpoints:** Urinary excretion of mercapturic acids of benzene, acrolein, and crotonaldehyde (measured by LC-MS/MS)
- **Secondary measures:** Urinary sulforaphane metabolites (sulforaphane-cysteine, sulforaphane-mercapturic acid) via isotope dilution mass spectrometry; GSTM1/GSTT1 genotype stratification

## Key Findings

1. **Benzene detoxication:** 61% increase in urinary benzene-derived mercapturic acid excretion in broccoli sprout beverage group vs. placebo (P <= 0.01). Rapid and sustained over the full 12-week period.

2. **Acrolein detoxication:** 23% increase in acrolein-derived mercapturic acid excretion (P <= 0.01).

3. **Crotonaldehyde:** No significant increase in excretion.

4. **Bioavailability sustained:** Urinary sulforaphane metabolite levels did not decline over the 12-week daily dosing period (measured at days 1, 42, and 84), indicating no tolerance or adaptation effect.

5. **GSTT1 genotype effect:** Excretion of benzene-derived mercapturic acid was higher in GSTT1-positive participants vs. null genotype, irrespective of study arm assignment.

6. **All increases were statistically significant at P <= 0.01** for benzene and acrolein.

## Methodology

- Randomized controlled trial with placebo arm
- Biomarker measurement: urinary mercapturic acids via liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS)
- Geometric means used for reporting; left-censoring accounted for using PROC NLMIXED in SAS 9.2
- Air quality (PM10) concurrently monitored in Shanghai and Qidong during study period
- Genotyping for GSTM1 and GSTT1 polymorphisms to assess pharmacogenomic modulation

## Limitations

- Only abstract available (PubMed landing page); full methods, exact p-values, confidence intervals, and adverse events not extractable
- Population limited to rural Chinese adults with high air pollution exposure -- generalizability to other populations uncertain
- Crotonaldehyde showed no significant response, indicating selectivity of effect
- The beverage format (broccoli sprout-derived) may differ from commercial supplements in bioavailability
- No clinical disease endpoints; biomarkers of detoxication serve as surrogates

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1 (Large RCT with biomarker endpoints)
**Directly relevant.** This is a 291-participant RCT -- one of the largest human sulforaphane intervention trials -- with objective biomarker endpoints (urinary mercapturic acids). Published as a randomized controlled trial with NIH extramural support.

### Q3 (Effect sizes)
**Strong evidence.** Provides precise quantitative effect sizes: 61% increase in benzene detoxication, 23% increase in acrolein detoxication, sustained over 12 weeks. These are among the most concrete, clinically meaningful effect sizes reported in the sulforaphane literature.

### Q5 (Broccoli sprout beverage vs. supplement)
**Directly relevant.** The intervention was specifically a broccoli sprout-derived beverage, not an isolated supplement. This speaks to whole-food matrix delivery and the practical/frugal intervention model the authors emphasize.

### Q7 (Dosing: 600 umol glucoraphanin + 40 umol SFN daily)
**Directly relevant.** The exact dosing regimen (600 umol glucoraphanin + 40 umol sulforaphane daily) is reported. This combination of precursor (glucoraphanin) plus direct sulforaphane is notable and may differ from supplement-only dosing strategies.
