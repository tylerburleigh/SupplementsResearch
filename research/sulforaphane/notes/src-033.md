## Scope

Systematic review of human intervention studies using broccoli, glucoraphanin (GRA), and sulforaphane (SFN). Searched PubMed and Virtual Health Library databases. Included only randomized clinical trials in humans published 2003-2013 in English or Portuguese. Outcomes of interest were clinical and molecular parameters of cell damage.

## Methods

- Two independent researchers performed the search
- Descriptors: "broccoli" or "glucoraphanin" or "sulforaphane" (required in title or abstract)
- Inclusion: RCTs in humans, published 2003-2013, English or Portuguese, clinical/molecular parameters of cell damage as outcomes
- 17 studies selected

## Key Findings

1. **Predominant intervention type:** Broccoli sprouts (most common across included studies)
2. **Strongest evidence -- blood glucose and lipid profile:** Most consistent results showed improvement in these clinical parameters after intervention
3. **Strong evidence -- oxidative stress:** Molecular parameters of oxidative stress showed improvement after intervention
4. **Weaker evidence -- inflammation:** Less solid evidence for decreased inflammation
5. **Weaker evidence -- H. pylori:** Less solid evidence for decreased Helicobacter pylori colonization
6. **Weaker evidence -- cancer protection:** Less solid evidence for protection against cancer
7. **Overall conclusion:** Evidence for use of broccoli, GRA and SFN in humans is relevant but limited; further intervention studies needed

## Relevance Assessment

### Q1: Evidence hierarchy across multiple RCTs
- **Directly relevant.** This is a systematic review of 17 RCTs spanning 2003-2013. It synthesizes evidence across human trials and identifies which outcome domains have stronger vs. weaker evidence. The finding that glucose, lipid profile, and oxidative stress show the most consistent improvements across RCTs is directly responsive to Q1.

### Q2: Bioavailability
- **Weakly relevant.** The abstract does not discuss bioavailability pharmacokinetics. However, the predominance of broccoli sprouts as the intervention vehicle may be tangentially relevant (sprouts have higher GRA content).

### Q7: Dosing
- **Weakly relevant from abstract alone.** The abstract does not report specific doses, but a full-text review would likely contain a table of dosing regimens across the 17 included RCTs. The predominance of broccoli sprout interventions suggests the dosing context is primarily whole-food/sprout-based rather than purified SFN.

## Limitations

- **Paywall: full text not accessible.** Only abstract available. A full systematic review would contain a detailed table of all 17 included studies with dosing, duration, population, and outcomes -- none of which can be extracted from the abstract alone.
- **Date range limitation:** Only includes studies from 2003-2013, so misses more recent RCTs (2014-present)
- **Small pool:** Only 17 RCTs met inclusion criteria over a decade, underscoring the limited human evidence base
- **Language restriction:** English and Portuguese only; may have excluded relevant trials in other languages
