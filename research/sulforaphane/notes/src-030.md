# src-030: Sulforaphane RCT in Former Smokers -- Ki-67 and Bronchial Histopathology

**Source URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40041932
**Publication type:** Randomized Controlled Trial, Phase II
**Venue:** American Association for Cancer Research (2025)
**Trial registration:** NCT03232138
**Funding:** R01 CA213123, T32 CA186873 (NCI)
**Content quality:** Abstract only (PubMed landing page); full text behind paywall at Silverchair. Abstract is detailed and substantive.

## Core Findings

Oral sulforaphane supplementation (95 umol/day for 12 months) in former smokers significantly reduced the bronchial tissue Ki-67 proliferation index (a surrogate biomarker for lung cancer risk) but did not improve bronchial histopathology scores. The Ki-67 reduction was 20% in the treatment group versus a 65% increase in placebo (P = 0.014), with even stronger effects for high-density (3+) Ki-67 positivity: 44% decrease vs. 71% increase (P = 0.004). No effects were observed on apoptotic markers (caspase-3, TUNEL).

## Study Design and Methods

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase II clinical trial
- **Population:** Former smokers
- **Sample size:** 43 randomized; 37 completed (17 sulforaphane, 20 placebo)
- **Intervention:** Sulforaphane 95 umol/day orally for 12 months
- **Primary endpoints:** Changes in histopathology scores and Ki-67, caspase-3, TUNEL indices in pre- vs. post-treatment bronchial biopsies
- **Safety:** No severe adverse events observed

## Key Evidence and Data Points

| Endpoint | Sulforaphane Group | Placebo Group | P-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ki-67 index | 20% decrease | 65% increase | 0.014 |
| High-density Ki-67 (3+) | 44% decrease | 71% increase | 0.004 |
| Bronchial histopathology | No significant change | -- | NS |
| Caspase-3 index | No significant change | -- | NS |
| TUNEL index | No significant change | -- | NS |

- Higher bioavailability of sulforaphane was correlated with greater reduction of Ki-67 index (P for trend = 0.019), suggesting a dose-response relationship.
- Attrition: 6 of 43 participants (14%) did not complete the study.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: RCT with surrogate biomarkers
**Directly relevant.** This is a Phase II RCT using Ki-67 as a surrogate endpoint biomarker for lung cancer risk. It demonstrates that sulforaphane can modulate a proliferation biomarker in bronchial tissue in humans, providing translational evidence from preclinical models. However, the study is small (n=37 completers) and uses a surrogate rather than a clinical endpoint.

### Q6: Null results on histopathology
**Directly relevant.** The study found no significant effect of sulforaphane on bronchial histopathology scores after 12 months of supplementation. This is a clear null finding on a tissue-level endpoint, contrasting with the positive Ki-67 result. Also null on caspase-3 and TUNEL apoptotic markers.

### Q7: Dosing (95 umol/day for 12 months)
**Directly relevant.** The study used exactly 95 umol/day sulforaphane orally for 12 months -- one of the longest intervention durations reported in sulforaphane RCTs. The dose is moderate (roughly equivalent to 1-2 servings of broccoli sprouts). The bioavailability-correlation finding (P for trend = 0.019) suggests interindividual variation in sulforaphane absorption influenced the Ki-67 response.

## Methodology Notes

- Bronchial biopsies were obtained pre- and post-treatment, allowing within-subject comparison.
- The abstract does not specify the sulforaphane formulation (broccoli sprout extract vs. purified SFN), the specific randomization method, or details of the blinding procedure.
- The Ki-67 measure included both overall index and high-density (3+) positivity, adding granularity.

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size:** Only 37 completers (17 treatment, 20 placebo). Limited statistical power, especially for histopathology endpoints.
2. **Abstract only:** Full text not available; details on randomization, allocation concealment, adherence measures, and adverse event specifics are unknown.
3. **Surrogate endpoints only:** Ki-67 is a surrogate biomarker, not a clinical endpoint. No data on actual lung cancer incidence.
4. **Population restricted to former smokers:** Generalizability to never-smokers, current smokers, or other at-risk populations is unknown.
5. **No dose-ranging:** Single dose tested (95 umol/day); optimal dose is not established.

## Claims to Verify

- **N = 43 randomized, 37 completed:** Standard attrition for a 12-month study (14%). Numbers are not suspiciously round.
- **95 umol/day dose:** Verify from full text whether this was actual delivered dose or intended dose (some participants may have had lower adherence).
- **P = 0.014 for Ki-67 and P = 0.004 for high-density Ki-67:** These are plausible for a small trial with a moderate effect size but should be verified against the reported test statistic and adjustment method.
- **"No severe adverse event":** Needs verification -- does the full text report mild/moderate adverse events?
