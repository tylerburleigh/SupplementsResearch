# src-047: Systematic Review of SFN in Cancer Therapy (RCTs)

**Citation:** ElKhalifa D, Al-Ziftawi N, Awaisu A, Alali F, Khalil A (2023). *Frontiers in Oncology*, 13:1251895. doi:10.3389/fonc.2023.1251895
**Type:** Systematic review of RCTs (PROSPERO: CRD42022323788)
**Quality rating:** ok (full text, peer-reviewed, 76K chars, 640 sentences)
**Citation count:** 12

---

## Core Findings (2-3 sentences)

This systematic review identified only 8 RCTs (total N across studies ~403) investigating sulforaphane as a therapeutic intervention in cancer patients, covering prostate cancer (n=4 studies), breast cancer (n=2), pancreatic cancer (n=1), and melanoma (n=1). While SFN showed some favorable signals on gene expression biomarkers and one trial reported a non-significant improvement in overall survival for pancreatic cancer, no study demonstrated statistically significant benefits on hard clinical endpoints, and 75% of included trials had high risk of bias. SFN was well-tolerated with no significant adverse event differences vs. placebo across studies.

---

## Study Design

- **Scope:** Systematic review following Cochrane guidelines and PRISMA 2020 statement
- **Databases searched:** PubMed, Cochrane, Embase, Web of Science, Google Scholar, Natural Medicines, ProQuest, ClinicalTrials.gov, ICTRP (up to May 12, 2023)
- **Eligibility:** Human RCTs, confirmed cancer diagnosis, SFN as primary intervention (any formulation/dose/duration)
- **Excluded:** Preclinical studies, non-randomized studies, cancer prevention studies in healthy individuals
- **Quality assessment:** Cochrane RoB2 tool
- **Total records identified:** 2,070; after deduplication and screening, 8 RCTs included
- **Meta-analysis:** NOT performed due to high methodological and clinical heterogeneity
- **GRADE assessment:** NOT performed due to limited numbers of results and patients per outcome

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Overall Survival
- Only one RCT (Lozanovski 2020, pancreatic cancer) assessed overall survival
- Lower death rate at day 30 (0% vs 18%), day 90 (0% vs 25%), day 180 (25% vs 43%)
- NOT statistically significant (P=0.291 at day 180)
- High dropout rate: 72% treatment group, 55% placebo group at 1 year

### PSA Outcomes (Prostate Cancer)
- Zhang 2020: No PSA difference after 4-8 weeks
- Traka 2008: No consistent PSA changes after 6 or 12 months
- Cipolla 2015: Mean PSA change significantly lower in SFN vs placebo (+0.099 +/- 0.341 vs +0.620 +/- 1.417 ng/mL; P=0.0433); PSA doubling time 86% longer (28.9 vs 15.5 months)

### Gene Expression and Biomarkers
- Zhang 2020: ARLNC1 gene 4.3-fold lower (P=0.0281); AMACR mRNA 7-fold lower (P<0.0001); but no significant differences in H3K18ac, HDAC3, HDAC6, Ki-67, p21
- Traka 2019: High-dose glucoraphanin soup (492 umol) suppressed EMT and inflammatory responses, enhanced xenobiotic metabolism and ROS pathways; no Nrf2-regulated gene changes
- Traka 2008: Significant TGFb1 (P=0.001), EGF (P=0.068), and insulin signaling (P=0.035) changes in GSTM1-positive patients
- Atwell 2015: Significant HDAC activity change between groups (P=0.04); significant in non-NSAID users (P=0.04) but not NSAID users (P=0.30)
- Visvanathan 2018: NQO1 upregulated, AKR1C1 downregulated; Ki67% change not significant (P=0.32)
- Tahata 2018: No significant cytokine correlation by dose group; pooled data showed significant reductions in MCP-1, IP-10, MIG, MIP-1b

### Safety / Adverse Events
- No study reported significant differences between SFN and controls for adverse events
- Main side effects: GI symptoms (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, bloating, flatulence, constipation), taste alteration, headache
- Cipolla 2015: 44.4% reported at least one AE; 89% were Grade 1, 11% Grade 2, no Grade 3/4; only 1 patient withdrew due to bowel discomfort
- Atwell 2015: AEs in 29.6% SFN vs 33.3% placebo (not significant); compliance equivalent (P=0.88)
- Lozanovski 2020: Increased digestive problems in treatment group (no statistical significance reported)
- Zhang 2020: Compliance high and similar (84% vs 85%, P=0.44)

---

## Risk of Bias

- 75% of studies (6/8) rated "high risk of bias"
- Only 2 studies rated "some concern" (Atwell 2015, Cipolla 2015)
- No study achieved "low risk" overall
- Key bias domains: missing outcome data (37.5%), deviations from intended interventions (37.5%), randomization process (25%), measurement of outcomes (25%)
- Only one study had low risk in randomization process (Cipolla 2015)

---

## Methodology Strengths

- Registered protocol (PROSPERO) with no changes made during conduct
- Followed Cochrane Handbook and PRISMA 2020 guidelines
- Comprehensive search across 9+ databases including gray literature
- Independent dual reviewers for screening, extraction, and risk of bias
- Contacted authors for missing information
- Included gray literature (ClinicalTrials.gov posting) to reduce publication bias

## Methodology Limitations

- Could not perform meta-analysis due to heterogeneity
- Could not apply GRADE due to limited data
- Only 4 cancer types represented; many cancer types unstudied
- Highly variable formulations, doses, and durations across studies
- Most studies small (N range: 17-98 per study)
- Short intervention durations in several studies (2-8 weeks)
- English language restriction only
- Publication bias cannot be fully ruled out
- Most trials had high risk of bias
- No important oncological endpoints assessed (PFS, response rate, complete response, pathological complete response)
- Only one study assessed overall survival

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: Evidence Hierarchy (Types of Endpoints in RCTs)
- **Highly relevant.** This review catalogs exactly what endpoints were measured in the 8 available RCTs. The evidence hierarchy is primarily at the biomarker/surrogate level: gene expression (AMACR, ARLNC1, TGFb1, EGF, HDAC), histological markers (Ki-67, H3K18ac), and tumor markers (PSA). Only ONE trial measured overall survival. No trial measured PFS, response rate, or pathological complete response. The reviewers explicitly note this gap -- the current evidence base lacks hard clinical endpoints.

### Q4: Risks/Adverse Effects
- **Highly relevant.** Safety was a secondary outcome across all studies. Consistent finding: no significant AE differences between SFN and placebo. Predominantly mild GI symptoms. The most comprehensive safety data comes from Cipolla 2015 (6-month, 78-patient trial): 89% Grade 1, 11% Grade 2, no Grade 3/4 events. Only one dropout due to AEs across all studies. However, the authors note that larger well-designed trials are needed to confirm the safety profile.

### Q6: Null Results
- **Highly relevant.** Multiple null findings documented:
  - PSA: No significant change in 2 of 3 PCa studies (Zhang 2020, Traka 2008)
  - Overall survival: Non-significant in pancreatic cancer (P=0.291)
  - Ki-67: No significant change in breast cancer (P=0.32, Visvanathan 2018)
  - Tissue biomarkers: No significant differences for H3K18ac, HDAC3, HDAC6, Ki-67, p21 in PCa (Zhang 2020)
  - Nrf2-regulated genes: No changes after 1-year treatment (Traka 2019)
  - HDAC activity: No significant change in breast tissue (Atwell 2015, though PBMC HDAC was significant)
  - Cytokines: No significant correlation by individual dose group in melanoma (Tahata 2018)
  - The review explicitly states: "None of the studies reported significant differences between SFN and comparative controls in terms of adverse events"

### Q7: Dosing Protocols
- **Highly relevant.** Detailed dosing information extracted for all 8 RCTs:
  - **Capsules (BSE):** 50-200 umol SFN/day (melanoma); 100 umol BID (PCa); 90 mg SFN + 180 mg glucoraphanin/day (pancreatic)
  - **Tablets (stabilized SFN):** 60 mg/day as 20 mg TID (PCa)
  - **Broccoli soup:** 214 or 492 umol glucoraphanin/week (PCa)
  - **Frozen broccoli:** 400g/week (PCa)
  - **Beverage:** 100 umol SFN in mango juice/day (breast)
  - Duration ranged from 14 days to 12 months
  - Reviewers note: "variable and inconsistent" regimens; impossible to recommend a specific effective dose
  - Longer durations (6-12 months) were more likely to show efficacy signals than short durations (2-8 weeks)

## Claims to Verify

1. **Total sample size ~403:** Summing N across all 8 studies (98+61+78+21+34+54+17+40 = 403). However, some participants overlap or were excluded from analyses. The actual number of analyzed participants may be lower.
2. **"75% high risk of bias":** This is 6/8 = 75%. Verified from the text.
3. **Cipolla PSA change: +0.099 +/- 0.341 vs +0.620 +/- 1.417 ng/mL, P=0.0433:** Reported directly from the Cipolla et al. 2015 study. The large SD relative to mean change in the placebo arm suggests high variability.
4. **AMACR 7-fold reduction, P<0.0001:** Reported in Zhang 2020. Remarkably strong effect size; worth confirming in the original study.

---

## Key Takeaway

This is the most comprehensive systematic review of SFN cancer therapeutic RCTs to date (as of May 2023). Its central message is sobering: despite promising preclinical data, the human RCT evidence base is thin (only 8 small trials), methodologically weak (75% high risk of bias), and has not demonstrated efficacy on hard clinical endpoints. The strongest signal is on gene expression biomarkers in prostate cancer, but the clinical significance of these changes remains unproven.
