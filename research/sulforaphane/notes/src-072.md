# src-072: Topical Broccoli Sprout Extract for Epidermolysis Bullosa Simplex

**Source:** PubMed PMID 27889290 (likely: Kerns et al., J Am Acad Dermatol, 2016)
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27889290
**Type:** Randomized Controlled Trial (split-body, single-blinded, placebo-controlled)
**Content Quality:** PubMed abstract page with figure descriptions; full text behind paywall. Abstract provides substantive study detail.

---

## Study Design

- **Design:** 1-week, randomized, split-body, single-blinded, placebo-controlled trial
- **Sample size:** 5 subjects enrolled; 1 excluded a posteriori (age 71, poor tissue quality); **4 analyzed**
- **Population:** Adults aged 34-71 years; mixed sex; phototypes II and V; healthy volunteers (not EBS patients)
- **Intervention:** Topical broccoli sprout extract (BSE) enriched for sulforaphane at **500 nmol/mL**, applied to inner aspect of the arm daily for 1 week
- **Control:** Vehicle alone applied to contralateral arm (split-body design)
- **Assessments:** RT-PCR and indirect immunofluorescence for keratin expression, Nrf2, and other markers

## Key Findings

1. **Nrf2 activation:** Topical BSE activated Nrf2 in human epidermis in all analyzed subjects, as shown by immunofluorescence (Fig 3).
2. **Keratin 17 upregulation:** BSE upregulated K17 in the epidermis of all subjects. K17 is a homologous keratin that can functionally compensate for defective K14 in EBS.
3. **Variable effects on K6 and K16:** Expression changes were inconsistent across subjects.
4. **No alteration of K5 or K14:** BSE did not change expression of the native keratins that are mutated in EBS -- an important safety/ specificity signal.
5. **Statistical significance:** K17 upregulation reached P < .01 (unpaired 2-tailed Student t-test, Fig 1D).

## Methodology

- Split-body design (each subject serves as own control) strengthens internal validity despite tiny sample
- Each data point = mean of 3 technical replicates per patient
- Fluorescence quantitation normalized across epidermal thickness (basal lamina to stratum corneum edge)
- Nrf2 quantitation: n = 6 image fields (likely pooled across the 4 subjects)

## Limitations

- Very small sample (n = 4 analyzed after 1 exclusion)
- Healthy volunteers, not EBS patients -- therapeutic effect on blistering not tested
- 1-week duration only
- Single-blinded (subjects may have known which arm received treatment, though split-body design mitigates bias)
- Authors explicitly acknowledge small sample size as a limitation

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1 (Clinical RCTs in humans)
- **Highly relevant.** This is a human RCT testing topical sulforaphane (as broccoli sprout extract), though extremely small (n = 4 analyzed) and in healthy volunteers, not patients. It represents proof-of-concept for topical SF delivery to human skin and measurable Nrf2 activation and keratin modulation. However, it is not a therapeutic efficacy trial.

### Q4 (Safety of topical application)
- **Moderately relevant.** The study reports no adverse events (implicit -- no safety concerns mentioned). The fact that BSE did not alter K5/K14 expression is a favorable safety signal for keratin integrity. However, safety was not a primary endpoint and systematic adverse event reporting is not described in the abstract.

### Q7 (Dosing)
- **Highly relevant.** Specific dose reported: **500 nmol sulforaphane per mL** of BSE, applied topically daily for 1 week to inner arm. This is a concrete topical dosing regimen with demonstrated biological activity (Nrf2 activation, K17 upregulation) in human skin.

## Claims to Verify

1. **Sample size of 5 (4 analyzed):** Stated in abstract. Consistent across text. Plausible for a pilot split-body study. No concern.
2. **Dose of 500 nmol/mL:** Single report in abstract. Should be cross-checked against full text if available to confirm this is glucoraphanin content vs. actual sulforaphane concentration after myrosinase conversion.
3. **P < .01 for K17:** Reported in figure legend. Unclear whether this is per-subject or pooled analysis; full text would clarify.
