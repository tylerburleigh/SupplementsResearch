# src-035: Sulforaphane for Negative Symptoms of Schizophrenia (Huang et al., 2025)

## Core Findings

High-dose sulforaphane (1,700 mg Avmacol Extra Strength daily, providing ~136 umol SFN/day) added to antipsychotics significantly reduced negative symptoms in schizophrenia patients over 24 weeks, with a large effect size at the 24-week endpoint (d = 0.83-0.86, P <= .001). The effect was specific to negative symptoms and not mediated by changes in depression, cognition, or amisulpride treatment.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (2:1 SFN:placebo)
- **Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04521868
- **Duration:** 24 weeks with assessments at baseline, week 12, and week 24
- **Site:** Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University, Hunan, China
- **Recruitment:** August 2020 to August 2022
- **Population:** 77 analyzed (53 SFN, 24 placebo) -- adults 18-50 with DSM-5 schizophrenia, illness duration <=10 years, PANSS negative score >=20 with at least 1 item >3, stable antipsychotic regimen
- **Sample breakdown:** 70% first-episode, 30% multi-episode; mean age ~24; mean illness duration ~44 months
- **Randomized:** 90 total (60 SFN, 30 placebo); analyzed those with at least 1 post-intervention evaluation
- **Completers at 24 weeks:** 47 SFN, 20 placebo

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Primary Outcomes (PANSS Negative Symptoms)

| Measure | SFN (n=53) Baseline | Placebo (n=24) Baseline | 24-week change SFN | 24-week change Placebo | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PANSS negative sum | 24.70 +/- 0.58 | 24.42 +/- 0.82 | -9.35 +/- 0.57 | -5.82 +/- 0.81 | d = 0.86, T = -3.54, P < .001 |
| PANSS 5-factor negative | 19.13 +/- 0.49 | 18.67 +/- 0.92 | -7.84 +/- 0.49 | -4.92 +/- 0.70 | d = 0.83, T = -3.39, P = .001 |

- Overall treatment x time effect on negative sum: F = 4.69, df = 2,59, P = .013
- Overall treatment effect on negative sum change: F = 6.78, df = 1,58, P = .01 (met Benjamini-Hochberg correction)
- Overall treatment effect on 5-factor negative change: F = 6.22, df = 1,58, P = .02 (met BH correction)
- Effect was NOT significant at 12 weeks; became prominent only at 24 weeks
- ~3-point greater decrease in SFN vs placebo at 24 weeks

### PANSS Other Domains

- PANSS total: No significant group difference (F = 0.28, P = .60)
- PANSS positive: Trend for placebo to decrease more (F = 3.06, P = .09); significant at 24 weeks (P = .027) favoring placebo -- placebo had higher baseline positive symptoms (16.33 vs 12.06, P = .003)
- PANSS general: No significant difference (F = 0.55, P = .46)
- CGI: No significant improvement in SFN group

### Individual Negative Symptom Items (Significant at 24 weeks, P < .05)

Blunted affect, emotional withdrawal, passive apathetic social withdrawal, lack of spontaneity in conversation showed significant SFN effects. Poor rapport, abstract thinking, stereotyped thinking did not.

### Mediation Analysis

- Depression factor did NOT mediate negative symptom improvement (ACME P > .57)
- Cognitive factor did NOT mediate negative symptom improvement (ACME P > .57)
- Correlation between negative and cognitive factor decreases: r = 0.40, P < .01

### Amisulpride Confound Analysis

- 17 SFN patients were on amisulpride vs only 1 in placebo -- substantial imbalance
- Mediation analysis showed amisulpride did NOT mediate SFN effect (ACME = 0.56, P = .30 for negative sum; ACME = 0.19, P = .68 for negative factor)
- SFN patients NOT on amisulpride actually had greater negative symptom decrease than those on amisulpride (-9.59 vs -6.18, P = .01)
- Authors acknowledge the imbalance "may create problems in interpreting the statistical analyses and make our conclusions less certain"

## Dosing Protocol (Q7)

- **Product:** Avmacol Extra Strength sulforaphane tablets (Nutramax)
- **Dose:** 1,700 mg (2 tablets) once daily
- **Glucoraphanin content:** >=30 mg per tablet; total >=60 mg glucoraphanin/day
- **SFN potential:** ~24 mg (~136 umol) SFN per day
- **Duration:** 24 weeks
- **Administration:** Add-on to stable antipsychotic regimen (unchanged during trial)
- **Also contains:** Moringa leaves and beta-glucans (noted as confound)

## Safety / Adverse Effects (Q4)

- SFN was well tolerated; TESS scale showed no major differences vs placebo
- Extrapyramidal symptoms (akathisia, tremor, motor symptoms): No significant change vs placebo
- Drowsiness: SFN group showed less decrease in drowsiness from baseline (difference score P = .011, S > P)
- Stuffiness: Placebo had higher scores at some time points (P = .003 at week 24)
- Abnormal EKG: SFN showed no change; placebo had higher abnormality at baseline and decreased
- No significant differences in most metabolic lab values (glucose, HDL, LDL, triglycerides, cholesterol) between groups at 24 weeks
- Cholesterol change at 24 weeks: SFN +0.05 vs placebo +0.52, P = .03 (placebo increased more)
- LDL change at 24 weeks approached significance: SFN +0.05 vs placebo +0.35, P = .05
- Weight gain/loss: No significant differences

## Methodology Strengths

1. Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design with 2:1 allocation
2. Pre-registered (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04521868)
3. Specific enrollment criteria targeting negative symptoms (PANSS negative >= 20)
4. Mixed model analysis handling missing data from dropouts
5. Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple comparisons on primary outcomes
6. Mediation analyses to rule out confounding from depression, cognition, and amisulpride
7. Published in peer-reviewed journal (J Clin Psychiatry) with 11 citations

## Methodology Limitations

1. **Small sample size:** Only 77 analyzed (53 SFN, 24 placebo); underpowered for some secondary analyses
2. **Unbalanced amisulpride use:** 17/53 SFN patients on amisulpride vs 1/24 placebo -- major potential confound despite mediation analysis
3. **Baseline imbalance in positive symptoms:** Placebo had significantly higher positive symptoms (16.33 vs 12.06, P = .003)
4. **No blinding effectiveness assessment** -- did not ask participants to guess treatment
5. **Only 3 assessment time points** (baseline, 12, 24 weeks) -- may miss symptom fluctuations
6. **No formal EPS measures** (Simpson-Angus, Barnes Akathisia) -- relied on TESS only
7. **No functional outcome measures** (e.g., PSP) -- cannot assess clinical meaningfulness
8. **Tablet contained additional ingredients** (Moringa leaves, beta-glucans) -- cannot attribute effects solely to SFN
9. **No plasma/blood SFN concentration measured**
10. **No dietary control** for broccoli/SFN intake
11. **Placebo group showed greater decrease in positive symptoms** -- raises questions about overall clinical benefit
12. **No CGI improvement in SFN group** despite negative symptom reduction

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample size:** 90 randomized (60 SFN, 30 placebo); 77 analyzed (53 SFN, 24 placebo). Flowchart shows 117 assessed for eligibility. Numbers are internally consistent.
- **Effect sizes d = 0.83-0.86:** These are reported for the 24-week time point only. The overall treatment effects were more modest (d = 0.22-0.27). The large effect sizes at a single time point should be contextualized.
- **PANSS negative sum decrease of ~3 points more in SFN:** This is the estimated marginal mean difference. Clinical significance is uncertain as noted by the authors.
- **Comparison to cariprazine (1.46-point difference):** The authors compare their 3-point difference favorably to cariprazine, but this is an indirect comparison across very different study populations.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: Evidence hierarchy (RCT with what endpoints?)
**Highly relevant.** This is a well-designed phase 2 RCT with PANSS negative symptoms as primary endpoint, published in J Clin Psychiatry (2025). It provides Level 1b evidence for SFN as an adjunctive treatment for negative symptoms in schizophrenia.

### Q3: Effect size significance
**Highly relevant.** Large effect sizes at 24 weeks (d = 0.83-0.86) with statistically significant differences (P <= .001). However, the overall treatment effects were more modest, and no clinically meaningful improvement was seen on global measures (CGI). The 3-point PANSS difference exceeds that reported for cariprazine but its clinical significance remains uncertain.

### Q4: Risks/adverse effects
**Relevant.** Good safety profile. No increase in EPS, metabolic parameters, or most side effects vs placebo. Minor signal of less drowsiness reduction in SFN group. Well-tolerated over 24 weeks.

### Q7: Dosing protocols
**Highly relevant.** Specific dosing: 1,700 mg Avmacol Extra Strength tablets (2 tablets/day, providing ~136 umol SFN/day = ~24 mg SFN). Duration: 24 weeks. Effect only emerged at 24 weeks (not at 12 weeks), suggesting prolonged treatment may be necessary. This dose is notably higher than in prior negative studies (~66-99 umol/day).
