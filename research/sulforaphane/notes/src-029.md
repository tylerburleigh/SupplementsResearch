# src-029: BSE and Gut Microbiota on Fasting Blood Glucose in Prediabetes (RCT)

**Citation:** Dwibedi et al. (2025). *Nature Microbiology*, 10, 681-693. doi:10.1038/s41564-025-01932-w
**Venue:** Nature Microbiology (high-impact, peer-reviewed)
**Citations:** 15
**Quality:** ok (full paper text, raw PDF extraction with minor OCR artifacts)

---

## Core Findings (2-3 sentences)

In a 12-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=74 analyzed; BSE n=35, placebo n=39), broccoli sprout extract (BSE) delivering 150 micromol sulforaphane daily reduced fasting blood glucose by 0.2 mmol/L vs. placebo (95% CI -0.44 to -0.01; P=0.04), failing to meet the prespecified primary outcome of 0.3 mmol/L reduction. Exploratory subgroup analysis revealed that individuals with mild age-related diabetes (MARD)-like characteristics -- low BMI, low insulin resistance, reduced insulin secretion -- had a pronounced 0.4 mmol/L reduction (95% CI -0.6 to -0.1; P=0.008), and this response was further modulated by gut microbiota composition, specifically the abundance of the BT2160 transcriptional regulator (encoded by Bacteroides) required for converting inactive glucoraphanin to bioactive sulforaphane.

---

## Study Design, Sample Size, and Population

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-arm trial
- **Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03763240
- **Location:** Gothenburg, Sweden (Sahlgrenska University Hospital)
- **Duration:** 12 weeks
- **Screening:** 450 individuals screened; 89 met inclusion criteria and were randomized (44 BSE, 45 placebo)
- **Analysis set:** 74 participants (35 BSE, 39 placebo) after 15 lost to follow-up (9 BSE, 6 placebo), mainly due to GI side effects
- **Population:** Drug-naive individuals with impaired fasting glucose (6.1-6.9 mmol/L), age 35-75, BMI 27-45 kg/m2
- **Demographics:** Mean age 63 +/- 9 years; 64% male; mean fasting glucose 6.4 +/- 0.2 mmol/L; mean BMI 32 +/- 4 kg/m2
- **Power calculation:** 80% power to detect 0.3 mmol/L treatment effect at alpha 0.05; required >= 74 participants (SD of change in fasting glucose = 0.63 mmol/L)

## Key Findings with Effect Sizes

### Primary Outcome (Full Cohort)
- **Fasting glucose change:** -0.2 mmol/L (95% CI -0.44 to -0.01; P=0.04, linear model adjusted for BMI and HOMA-IR; P=0.045 by ANCOVA)
- **Did NOT meet prespecified primary outcome** of 0.3 mmol/L reduction
- No significant changes in secondary outcomes (HbA1c, BMI, HOMA-IR, HOMA-B, fatty liver index, lipids, physical activity, dietary patterns)

### Exploratory Subgroup: MARD-like Cluster (n=44; 24 BSE, 20 placebo)
- **Fasting glucose change:** -0.4 mmol/L (95% CI -0.6 to -0.1; P=0.008)
- **HOMA-B (insulin secretion) improved:** 95% CI 3.3-26.0; P=0.02
- Significant treatment-by-cluster interaction (P=0.008)
- No significant effects in SIRD-like or MOD-like clusters

### Responder Analysis (post hoc, top quartile > 0.3 mmol/L reduction; n=13)
- 11/13 pronounced responders had MARD-like characteristics
- Responders had different baseline gut microbiota composition (P=0.001)
- Higher abundance of Desulfovibrio sp., Anaerostipes caccae, Bacteroides M10, Bacteroides D2
- Higher butyrate kinase abundance (P=0.047)

### BT2160 Operon and Sulforaphane Serum Levels
- Bacteroides-encoded BT2160 transcriptional regulator (part of BT2156-BT2160 operon) converts inactive glucosinolate to bioactive sulforaphane
- Pronounced responders had higher BT2160 abundance at baseline (P=0.02)
- Serum sulforaphane showed bimodal distribution: ~0.2 nmol/mL (low) vs. ~1.3 nmol/mL (high)
- High serum sulforaphane group had higher BT2160 abundance (P=0.046)
- In MARD-like participants, BT2160 correlated with glycemic response (Spearman r=-0.47; P=0.026)
- Participants with BT2160 above median + MARD characteristics: 0.7 mmol/L reduction (95% CI 0.20-1.21; P=0.009)

### Remission Analysis (post hoc)
- 11 participants achieved fasting glucose < 6.1 mmol/L (remission) after BSE
- 10/11 were MARD-like
- Regression model: BT2160 abundance + GGT predicted remission (R2=0.64; BT2160 P=0.02, GGT P=0.01)

### Safety
- GI side effects more common in BSE group (loose stools, nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, reflux)
- No severe adverse events
- 15 discontinued (9 BSE, 6 placebo), primarily due to GI effects

## Methodology Strengths

1. **Gold-standard design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled with concealed allocation
2. **Pre-registered primary outcome** with power calculation
3. **Independent monitoring** by external monitor
4. **Gut microbiota analysis:** Shotgun metagenomic sequencing with rigorous bioinformatics (Kraken2, Bracken, DESeq2)
5. **Replication cohort** (n=164) for cluster distribution stability
6. **Machine learning validation** (XGBoost + SHAP) for identifying response predictors
7. **Serum sulforaphane measurement** via cyclocondensation reaction for dithiocarbamate levels
8. **Cluster analysis** based on well-validated ANDIS methodology (>27,000 patients)
9. Published in *Nature Microbiology* with full methods and data availability

## Methodology Limitations

1. **Primary outcome NOT met** -- 0.2 mmol/L fell short of the prespecified 0.3 mmol/L threshold
2. **Subgroup analyses are exploratory/post hoc** -- not powered for subgroup comparisons; require confirmation in future trials
3. **Small sample size** for subgroup analyses (e.g., MARD cluster n=44; pronounced responders n=13)
4. **Short follow-up** (12 weeks) -- no long-term glycemic control or diabetes incidence data
5. **High attrition** (17%; 15/89) primarily due to GI side effects, potentially introducing bias
6. **CIs not adjusted for multiple comparisons**
7. **Self-selection bias** in recruitment (volunteers may be more motivated)
8. **Homogeneity within clusters assumed** but may not hold
9. **GI side effects notable** -- BSE caused significantly more GI symptoms than placebo
10. **Competing interests:** Authors hold patents on sulforaphane for glucose treatment; one owns stock in Optisan AB; Lantmannen AB (BSE provider) co-financed study

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: Evidence Hierarchy (RCT with What Endpoints?)
**Highly relevant.** This is a well-designed Phase 2 RCT (double-blind, placebo-controlled) with a pre-registered primary endpoint (fasting blood glucose reduction). It represents high-quality clinical evidence, though it did not meet its primary outcome. The exploratory subgroup findings are hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

### Q2: Bioavailability/Pharmacokinetics
**Relevant.** Serum sulforaphane was measured showing bimodal distribution (~0.2 vs. ~1.3 nmol/mL), suggesting substantial inter-individual variation in bioavailability. The BT2160 operon (Bacteroides-encoded) was shown to be required for converting glucoraphanin to bioactive sulforaphane, and its abundance correlated with serum sulforaphane levels. This provides a mechanistic link between gut microbiota and sulforaphane bioavailability.

### Q3: Effect Size Significance
**Highly relevant.** Overall effect: -0.2 mmol/L (95% CI -0.44 to -0.01; P=0.04) -- statistically significant but did not meet prespecified target. MARD subgroup: -0.4 mmol/L (95% CI -0.6 to -0.1; P=0.008). The 0.2 mmol/L reduction is comparable to metformin's effect in the Diabetes Prevention Program (~0.2 mmol/L, reducing diabetes incidence by 31% over 3 years). A reduction from 6.4 to 6.0 mmol/L (as in MARD responders) would correspond to ~50% reduction in diabetes hazard ratio per epidemiological meta-analysis.

### Q5: Supplement vs. Dietary Comparison
**Relevant.** The intervention used broccoli sprout extract (BSE) -- a standardized, non-pharmaceutical food extract providing 150 micromol sulforaphane per dose. The paper notes BSE improved glucose tolerance to the same extent as pure (99% reagent-grade) sulforaphane in prior work (ref 12). BSE is described as available at "very low cost per dose" and could be provided as a "functional food."

### Q7: Dosing Protocols
**Highly relevant.** Dose: 150 micromol sulforaphane delivered via BSE once daily in the morning, suspended in approximately 1 dl water. Sulforaphane content verified by reverse-phase HPLC. Prior safety data cited at doses up to 400 micromol sulforaphane. The BSE was provided by Lantmannen R&D as a dried powder of aqueous broccoli sprout extract with maltodextrin as bulking agent.

## Claims to Verify

1. **Sample size claim:** 450 screened, 89 randomized, 74 analyzed. These numbers are clearly documented in the CONSORT diagram and Table 1. Consistent across the paper.
2. **Effect sizes:** 0.2 mmol/L overall, 0.4 mmol/L in MARD, 0.7 mmol/L in high-BT2160 MARD -- all reported with CIs and P-values. Appear consistent across results, table, and discussion sections.
3. **Serum sulforaphane bimodal distribution** (~0.2 vs 1.3 nmol/mL): Described qualitatively rather than with exact cutoff; reported in Supplementary Fig. 8 (not available in this extraction). The factor-of-6 difference is large but plausible given known inter-individual variation.
4. **Comparison to metformin** (~0.2 mmol/L in DPP): This is a secondary literature comparison in the Discussion, not a head-to-head trial. The DPP reference (Knowler et al., NEJM 2002) is correctly cited but the specific 0.2 mmol/L figure for metformin would need verification in the original source.

---

## Key Takeaway

This is a well-conducted RCT showing that BSE (150 micromol sulforaphane/day) produces a modest but statistically significant reduction in fasting glucose in prediabetes, with substantially larger effects in individuals with specific pathophysiological (MARD-like) and gut microbiota (high BT2160) profiles. The primary endpoint was not met, and the most interesting findings are from exploratory analyses requiring confirmation.
