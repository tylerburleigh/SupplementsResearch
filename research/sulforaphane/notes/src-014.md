## Key Claims and Practical Recommendations

1. **Sulforaphane activates the NRF2 pathway**, regulating 200+ genes related to antioxidant defense, anti-inflammation, detoxification, heavy metal removal, and tumor suppression (p53 stabilization).
2. **Dose target:** 40-60 mg of sulforaphane daily, based on clinical studies. This equates to 100-140 grams of fresh broccoli sprouts.
3. **Conversion assumption:** 1 gram of fresh broccoli sprouts yields approximately 0.425 mg of sulforaphane.
4. **Rhonda Patrick's personal practice:** Eats up to 4 ounces (~113g) of broccoli sprouts multiple times per week; currently takes supplements due to time constraints.
5. **Sprouting method:** Mason jars, harvest every 3 days (young sprouts have more glucoraphanin). Stores in fridge, not freezer (freezing caused stomach discomfort).
6. **Avoid juicing** sprouts -- removes prebiotic fiber. Add to smoothies instead.
7. **Avoid heating sprouts** -- Rhonda stopped warming sprouts after discussion with Jed Fahey, because overheating destroys myrosinase enzyme.
8. **Supplement contamination risk:** Many commercial supplements do not contain what they claim (per Jed Fahey's lab testing).
9. **Cancer prevention mechanism:** Sulforaphane reduces DNA adducts, deactivates carcinogens, enhances excretion, and stabilizes tumor suppressor protein p53.
10. **Additional source -- Moringa:** Contains moringin (isothiocyanate similar to sulforaphane). Jed Fahey recommends at least 1 tsp moringa powder daily, prepared as cold-brewed tea (1:100 powder-to-water ratio). Kuli Kuli brand found most effective in his research. No human dosing studies yet.

## Evidence Cited

- **Christine Houghton study** -- referenced for NRF2 pathway details (no DOI provided).
- **Jed Fahey's lab** -- tested multiple sulforaphane supplements over decades; found many mislabeled or ineffective.
- **Influenza study** -- sulforaphane from broccoli sprouts improved immune response against influenza virus; enhanced natural killer cell enzyme synthesis (no specific citation).
- **Three clinically-tested supplements identified:**
  - **Prostaphane** (Nutrinov, France) -- pure sulforaphane, highest bioavailability. Only available in France. 2 pills/day recommended by Rhonda.
  - **BROQ** (Superhuman Health, US) -- partnered with Prostaphane manufacturers, nearly identical product for US market.
  - **Avmacol** (Nutramax Laboratories) -- glucoraphanin + myrosinase. 15 mg (regular) or 30 mg (extra strength) sulforaphane glucosinolate per pill. Bioavailability ~35%, yielding ~5.25 mg (regular) or ~10.5 mg (extra) sulforaphane per pill.
  - **Crucera-SGS** (Thorne) -- glucoraphanin only, no added myrosinase. Relies on gut bacteria conversion. Least predictable bioavailability.

## Supplement vs. Dietary Comparison (Q5 Relevance)

- **Sprouts preferred** by Rhonda Patrick when time permits (cheaper, more natural)
- **Supplements more practical** for travel, busy schedules, or immunocompromised individuals (sprouts carry bacterial contamination risk)
- **Sprout disadvantage:** Requires 3-day grow cycle, sterilization effort; risk of bacterial contamination (especially for pregnant women or immunocompromised)
- **Supplement advantage:** No bacterial contamination risk; clinically verified doses; convenient
- **Supplement disadvantage:** More expensive; many products are mislabeled or ineffective; only 3 products (Prostaphane, BROQ, Avmacol) verified in clinical trials

## Bioavailability (Q2 Relevance)

- **Pure sulforaphane supplements** (Prostaphane/BROQ) have highest bioavailability
- **Glucoraphanin + myrosinase** (Avmacol) has ~35% bioavailability
- **Glucoraphanin-only** (Crucera-SGS) relies on gut bacteria for conversion; least predictable
- **Heating sprouts destroys myrosinase** -- Rhonda stopped warming sprouts after Jed Fahey advised against it
- **Moringa myrosinase** is heat-sensitive; cold-brew preparation recommended (not hot tea)
- **Gut bacteria** can convert glucoraphanin to sulforaphane, but inter-individual variability makes dosing unpredictable
