# src-048: Oral GR and CUR Induce Cytoprotective Genes and Reduce Inflammatory Cytokines in Human Skin In Vivo

**Source type:** PubMed abstract (MDPI open-access journal, likely Nutrients or similar)
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40559384
**Quality:** Abstract-only content from PubMed page; full text available via PMC/MDPI

---

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, open-label, mechanism-based clinical trial with before/after comparison
- **Sample size:** 18 healthy volunteers (9 males, 9 females, aged 18-69)
- **Arms:** 3 groups of n=6 each:
  1. Glucoraphanin (GR) 450 mg (1 mmol) daily
  2. Curcumin (CUR) 1000 mg (2.7 mmol) daily
  3. GR 450 mg + CUR 1000 mg daily
- **Duration:** 8-day low-phytochemical diet run-in, then 10-day intervention phase
- **Tissue sampling:** Skin punch biopsies (buttock, non-UV and UV-irradiated), blood (PBMC), urine
- **UVB challenge:** 2x MED UVB on randomized buttock spots; biopsies at 1 and 3 days post-UVB
- **Erythema measurement:** Chromameter daily for 3 days post-UVB

## Key Findings

### NQO1 Induction
- GR alone: **3.1-fold** increase in NQO1 mRNA in non-UVB skin biopsies vs. baseline (n=6)
- CUR alone: **3.3-fold** increase
- GR + CUR combination: **3.6-fold** increase
- Each treatment arm showed statistically meaningful NQO1 upregulation

### Inflammatory Marker Changes
- Across all treatments (n=18), pro-inflammatory cytokines were reduced:
  - **IL-1beta** and **TNF-alpha**: reduced
  - **IL-6, IL-17, STING, CYR61**: reduced, though less robustly
- Modulation persisted but was **less pronounced** in UVB-exposed biopsies
- No significant changes measured in PBMCs or dyskeratotic keratinocyte counts
- No significant effect on erythema post-UVB

### Bioavailability to Skin (SF Detection)
- **SF and its metabolites were confirmed in skin** post-treatment in 6 of 12 subjects who ingested GR
- DTC (dithiocarbamate) measured in skin and urine as a biomarker of SF delivery
- Urinary excretion data collected for all subjects (percent of GR dose excreted)

## Methodology Notes
- Each subject served as their own control (pre/post design within subjects)
- Biopsies compared to control biopsy from same subject on day 8 of non-intervention phase
- Gene expression reported as fold-change with box plots (25th-75th percentile)
- Compliance assessed via urinary biomarkers
- Supplements well tolerated with excellent compliance

## Limitations
- Small sample size (n=6 per arm)
- No placebo control arm (before/after design)
- No correction for multiple comparisons reported in abstract
- Erythema endpoint was not significant despite biomarker changes
- Abstract-only content reviewed; full text may contain additional detail

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1 (RCT with biomarker endpoints)
**Directly relevant.** This is a randomized clinical trial with mechanistic biomarker endpoints (NQO1 mRNA, inflammatory cytokine gene expression) in human skin tissue. Demonstrates that oral GR reaches skin and activates the Nrf2-ARE pathway.

### Q2 (Bioavailability - SF detected in skin)
**Directly relevant.** Confirms SF and metabolites detected in human skin post oral GR ingestion (6 of 12 subjects tested). DTC measured in both skin biopsies and urine. This is key evidence for systemic bioavailability of SF to skin tissue.

### Q7 (Dosing: 450mg GR = 1mmol)
**Directly relevant.** Study explicitly uses 450 mg glucoraphanin (= 1 mmol) as the dosing regimen. Demonstrates this dose achieves measurable NQO1 induction (3.1-fold) and skin bioavailability in humans. Supports 450mg GR as a clinically studied dose.

## Claims to Verify
- **"n=6 per arm" with "18 healthy volunteers":** 3 arms x 6 = 18; consistent.
- **"6 of 12 subjects who ingested GR":** 6 in GR-only arm + 6 in GR+CUR arm = 12 total receiving GR; 6 tested for skin SF; confirmed in figure description.
- **Fold-change values (3.1, 3.3, 3.6):** These are from the abstract results; should verify in full text Table 2 if available.
- **No significant erythema effect despite biomarker changes:** This disconnect is notable and may relate to study power (n=6 per arm).
