- **Author:** Dr. Adrian Blackwell (board-certified obesity/emergency medicine; CEO of PonteVita Rx)
- **Date:** October 24, 2025
- **Peer review:** None. This is a non-peer-reviewed health blog post.
- **Quality:** Moderate. The article cites legitimate peer-reviewed studies with DOIs, but presents findings in a promotional/simplified tone. No new primary data. The author runs a telehealth company, creating potential commercial bias. Effect sizes and caveats are sometimes glossed over.
- **Metadata quality field:** "ok" -- content is readable and substantive, though the article itself is not a primary research source.

## Key Claims About Sulforaphane and Longevity

1. Sulforaphane extends lifespan and healthspan in C. elegans via DAF-2/DAF-16 (insulin/IGF-1) and TRA-1/GLI signaling pathways.
2. Sulforaphane-treated worms appeared ~20% "biologically younger" by transcriptional aging clock measures (citing Sedore et al. 2025 bioRxiv preprint).
3. Red flour beetles on lyophilized broccoli showed ~30% lifespan increase, even under heat stress.
4. In aged mice, sulforaphane improved skin collagen, dermal thickness, and reduced MMP9 via Keap1-Nrf2 activation (3-month treatment).
5. In humans, a 12-week RCT (n=144 older adults) showed improved processing speed and mood, but no change in oxidative/inflammatory biomarkers.
6. Observational data from >12,000 U.S. adults: broccoli intake 1-2x/week associated with 32-43% lower all-cause mortality.
7. Sulforaphane is more bioavailable than many polyphenols; supplements with active myrosinase can replicate clinical doses.

## Methodology

This is a narrative review/blog post. It synthesizes findings from multiple animal studies, cell culture experiments, clinical trials, and epidemiological data. No systematic search strategy is described. Selection of studies appears purposive rather than comprehensive.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: Evidence Hierarchy (What evidence does it cite?)
- **Tier 1 (RCTs):** Nouchi et al. 2022 -- 144 older adults, 12-week RCT, processing speed/mood outcomes.
- **Tier 2 (Observational):** Large cohort (n>12,000) linking broccoli intake to 32-43% lower all-cause mortality (uncited).
- **Tier 3 (Animal models):** C. elegans lifespan studies (Qi et al., Ji et al., Sedore et al.), aged mouse skin study (Petkovic et al.), diabetic rodent models.
- **Tier 4 (In vitro/Mechanistic):** Aging lens cell studies (Kubo et al.).
- **Tier 5 (Reviews):** Santin-Marquez et al. 2019, Alves et al. 2025.
- Note: The Sedore et al. 2025 paper is a bioRxiv preprint, not yet peer-reviewed.

### Q3: Effect Size Significance
- C. elegans lifespan extension: effect sizes not quantified in this article (original papers would need checking).
- Transcriptional aging clock: ~20% reduction in biological age (from preprint).
- Beetle lifespan: ~30% increase (uncited).
- Human RCT: Processing speed improved, mood improved, but no significant change in oxidative/inflammatory biomarkers.
- Observational: 32-43% lower all-cause mortality for broccoli 1-2x/week. This is a large effect and should be treated cautiously -- likely confounded by healthy-user bias, and the specific study is not cited.
- **Caveat:** The 32-43% mortality reduction claim lacks a citation and is unusually large for a dietary observational study.

### Q7: Dosing Protocols
- Article explicitly states: "Best dose and timing for humans are still unclear."
- Notes that sulforaphane only extended lifespan in animals when supplementation began early.
- Cooking destroys myrosinase; raw or lightly steamed (1-3 min) preserves sulforaphane.
- Supplements with active myrosinase can replicate clinical trial doses.
- No specific human dose recommendations are made.
- Food sources listed: broccoli sprouts (highest), broccoli, cauliflower, kale, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, bok choy, watercress.

## Limitations

1. Not peer-reviewed; commercial health blog with potential bias.
2. Several key claims lack specific citations (beetle study, large cohort study, diabetes study).
3. Effect sizes from observational data are presented without adequate discussion of confounding.
4. No systematic methodology for study selection.
5. Simplified presentation may overstate strength of evidence.
6. The Sedore et al. 2025 paper is a preprint.

## Claims to Verify

1. **32-43% lower all-cause mortality from broccoli 1-2x/week (n>12,000):** No citation provided. This is a large effect size for an observational study and likely subject to healthy-user confounding. The specific study should be identified and the adjusted effect sizes checked.
2. **30% lifespan increase in red flour beetles:** No citation provided. Needs verification.
3. **~20% reduction in biological age (transcriptional clock):** From a bioRxiv preprint (Sedore et al. 2025). Not yet peer-reviewed.
4. **Pancreatic cancer survival difference:** Described as "not statistically significant" but still cited as evidence. The framing is misleading.
5. **RCT n=144:** The article says 144 older adults in the Nouchi et al. 2022 trial. This should be cross-checked against the original paper.
