# src-324: Role of Nrf2 in Oxidative Stress and Toxicity

**Author:** Qiang Ma (CDC/NIOSH, West Virginia University)
**Venue:** Annual Review of Pharmacology and Toxicology, 2013, 53:401-426
**DOI:** 10.1146/annurev-pharmtox-011112-140320
**Type:** Comprehensive narrative review (peer-reviewed)
**Content Quality:** Full text available, high-quality extraction from PDF

---

## Core Findings

This review comprehensively covers the Nrf2-Keap1 signaling pathway, its molecular mechanism of activation through cysteine thiol modification, its role in regulating antioxidant defense genes, and the dual-edged nature of Nrf2 in cancer (protective against carcinogenesis in normal cells, but hijacked by tumors for prosurvival advantage). Sulforaphane is discussed as a key Nrf2 inducer and chemopreventive agent.

---

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Nrf2-Keap1 Mechanism ("Dedepression" Model)
- Nrf2 is suppressed under basal conditions by Keap1/Cul3-dependent ubiquitination-proteasomal degradation (half-life ~20 min)
- Activation occurs via modification of critical cysteine thiols of Keap1 (C151, C273, C288) and Nrf2 by oxidants and electrophiles
- "Hinge-and-latch" model: ETGE motif (high affinity) and DLG motif (lower affinity) bind to two Keap1 DC domains in a 1:2 ratio
- Disruption of DC-DLG binding (by inducers like tBHQ) prevents ubiquitination without disrupting the stronger DC-ETGE binding
- Newly synthesized Nrf2 bypasses Keap1 and enters nucleus to activate ARE-dependent genes

### Sulforaphane-Specific Mentions
- Listed among key Nrf2 inducers along with CDDO, oltipraz, auranofin, and endogenous chemicals
- Potency metric: Sulforaphane has a Dm value of 290 nM for NQO1 induction; the most potent synthetic inducer TP-225 has 0.27 nM (1,074x more potent)
- Chemoprevention: Protected against Bap-induced forestomach cancer in mice (Ref 148)
- Skin cancer: Inhibited DBA/TPA-induced skin tumors in mice (Ref 150)
- Human trial in Qidong, China: Broccoli sprout glucosinolate (sulforaphane precursor) consumption reduced aflatoxin-DNA adduct excretion, though "large individual variability in bioavailability" was noted (Ref 155)
- Oltipraz (not sulforaphane) showed measurable pharmacokinetic effects in the same Qidong trials

### Nrf2 Dual Role ("Double-Edged Sword")
- **Protective**: Nrf2 KO mice are significantly more susceptible to carcinogen-induced tumors; chemopreventive agents require Nrf2 for efficacy
- **Cancer-promoting**: Some tumors have persistently elevated Nrf2/ARE gene expression via:
  - Somatic mutations in NRF2 (ETGE 57%, DLG 43% motifs) disrupting Keap1 binding
  - KEAP1 mutations (DC 65%, IVR 29%, BTB 3%, NTR 3%)
  - KEAP1 promoter methylation (lung, prostate tumors)
  - Oncogenic Kras, Braf, Myc stimulating Nrf2 transcription
  - DJ-1 oncoprotein stabilizing Nrf2 by interfering with Keap1 binding
  - p62 accumulation in autophagy deficiency creating positive feedback loop
- Keap1 KO mice did NOT develop spontaneous cancer over 2 years, suggesting Nrf2 activation alone is insufficient for tumor initiation
- Nrf2 redirects glucose and glutamine into anabolic pathways to support aggressive cancer proliferation

### Nrf2 Target Gene Categories
1. Drug-metabolizing enzymes/transporters (CYP2A5, ALDH3A1, NQO1, AKRs, UGTs, GSTs, MRP2/3)
2. Antioxidant enzymes/proteins (SOD, Prx, GPx, GSR, TrxR, Srx, GCLC, GCLM, G6PDH, HO-1, xCT, MTs)
3. Oxidant signaling proteins (p62, DJ-1 with positive feedback loops)

### Evolutionary Conservation
- Nrf2-Keap1 signaling conserved across vertebrates and invertebrates (C. elegans SKN-1, Drosophila CncC)
- Cysteine codes for alkenal sensing (C288) have ancient bilaterian origin; NO sensing (C151) evolved more recently with NOS gene family

### Nrf2 and Disease (Select Examples)
- **Acetaminophen hepatotoxicity**: Nrf2 KO mice showed severe liver injury at normally nontoxic doses; Keap1 knockout protected
- **COPD**: Nrf2 KO mice developed earlier/more extensive emphysema from cigarette smoke; CDDO-Im protected via Nrf2-dependent mechanisms
- **Neurodegeneration**: Nrf2 overexpression delayed ALS onset; Nrf2 KO increased sensitivity to MPTP (Parkinson's model)
- **Atherogenesis**: Complex role -- Nrf2 was required for cholesterol crystal-induced inflammasome response, potentially promoting IL-1beta-mediated vascular inflammation

## Methodology

This is a narrative review synthesizing primary literature through ~2012. It draws on evidence from:
- Nrf2 KO and Keap1 KO mouse models
- Cell culture studies with various inducers
- Structural biology (crystallography of Keap1 DGR at 1.85-Angstrom resolution)
- Human chemoprevention trials (Qidong, China)
- Comparative genomics across species

---

## Limitations

- Pre-2013 review; does not cover subsequent clinical trials or more recent mechanistic findings
- Narrative review rather than systematic review or meta-analysis
- Human clinical evidence for sulforaphane is limited to one trial mentioned (Qidong), with noted high variability in bioavailability
- No quantitative effect sizes or meta-analytic pooling of data across studies
- The dual-role discussion of Nrf2 in cancer is largely based on mouse models and cell culture data rather than human clinical outcomes

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: Evidence Hierarchy (Mechanistic vs. Clinical Evidence for Nrf2 Activation)
**Strongly relevant.** This review provides the definitive mechanistic framework for Nrf2-Keap1 signaling, including the dedepression model, cysteine code hypothesis, and hinge-and-latch structural model. It establishes the mechanistic basis for how sulforaphane activates Nrf2. However, clinical evidence is limited: the Qidong trial showed reduced aflatoxin-DNA adducts but with high inter-individual variability, highlighting a gap between robust mechanistic evidence and uncertain clinical translation.

### Q3: Effect Size Significance
**Moderately relevant.** Provides potency comparisons: sulforaphane Dm = 290 nM for NQO1 induction vs. TP-225 at 0.27 nM. Notes that chemopreventive effects in mice require intact Nrf2. However, this review does not provide quantitative clinical effect sizes for sulforaphane supplementation.

### Q4: Risks of Nrf2 Overactivation (Friend-or-Foe)
**Strongly relevant.** This is one of the key papers establishing the "double-edged sword" concept for Nrf2 in cancer. Documents multiple mechanisms by which cancer cells hijack Nrf2 (somatic mutations, epigenetic silencing of KEAP1, oncogene-driven transcription, p62/DJ-1 positive feedback). Critically notes that Keap1 KO mice did NOT develop spontaneous cancer over 2 years, suggesting Nrf2 overactivation alone is not tumorigenic but may promote established tumors. Also documents Nrf2's complex role in atherogenesis where it may promote IL-1beta-mediated inflammation.

## Claims to Verify

1. **Nrf2 half-life ~20 min**: Stated without specific citation in the text; derived from multiple references (46-48). Reasonable but should be confirmed from primary sources.
2. **Sulforaphane Dm = 290 nM for NQO1 induction**: From Ref 156 (likely Prochaska et al.); this is a well-established value but worth confirming.
3. **TP-225 Dm = 0.27 nM, 1,074x more potent than sulforaphane**: The ratio 290/0.27 = 1,074 is arithmetically correct. From Ref 156.
4. **NRF2 mutations in squamous cell carcinoma: ETGE 57%, DLG 43%**: From Refs 159-160. Distribution depends on sample size of the study.
5. **KEAP1 mutation distribution: DC 65%, IVR 29%, BTB 3%, NTR 3%**: From Refs 161-164. Again, dependent on study sample sizes.
6. **Keap1 KO mice did not develop spontaneous cancer over 2 years**: From Ref 167. This is an important negative finding for the "Nrf2 causes cancer" concern.
7. **Broccoli sprout glucosinolate reduced aflatoxin-DNA adduct excretion in Qidong trial**: From Ref 155 (likely Kensler et al.). The high individual variability note is important -- this may indicate modest or inconsistent effects.
