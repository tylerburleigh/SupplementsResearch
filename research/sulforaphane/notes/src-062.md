# src-062: BSE Supplementation RCT in Men Scheduled for Prostate Biopsy

**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31155953
**Trial Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01265953
**Publication Type:** Randomized Controlled Trial, NIH Extramural Research Support
**Content Quality:** Abstract + figure captions only (PubMed landing page; full text not available)

## Study Design

- **Design:** Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial
- **Population:** 98 men scheduled for prostate biopsy (aged/middle-aged males)
- **Intervention:** Broccoli sprout extract (BSE) providing 200 umol SFN daily vs. placebo
- **Duration:** Not specified in available abstract (likely pre-biopsy supplementation period)
- **Outcomes assessed:**
  - Blood histone deacetylase (HDAC) activity
  - Prostate RNA gene expression (RNA-Seq)
  - Tissue biomarkers via immunohistochemistry: H3K18ac, HDAC3, HDAC6, Ki67, p21
  - Urine and plasma SFN isothiocyanates and metabolites (compliance/absorption confirmation)

## Key Findings

### Positive/Significant Results
- **Compliance confirmed:** Urine and plasma SFN isothiocyanates and individual SFN metabolites were statistically significantly higher in the treatment group, confirming bioavailability.
- **Gene expression changes:** RNA-Seq identified **40 differentially expressed genes** correlated with BSE treatment in prostate biopsy tissue. These included downregulation of two genes previously implicated in prostate cancer development: **AMACR** (alpha-methylacyl-CoA racemase) and **ARLNC1** (androgen receptor long noncoding RNA 1).
- Expression of AMACR and ARLNC1 was confirmed by qPCR (n = 7-18 per group).
- A cluster of six genes was identified in the heatmap analysis, with AMACR and ARLNC1 being the most notable.

### Null/Negative Results
- **No significant difference in blood HDAC activity** between BSE and placebo groups.
- **No significant difference in prostate tissue biomarkers** (H3K18ac, HDAC3, HDAC6, Ki67, p21) between groups.
- The authors concluded that BSE supplementation correlates with changes in gene expression but NOT with several other prostate cancer biomarkers.

## Methodology

- Nonparametric tests used to evaluate differences in blood HDAC activity and tissue IHC biomarkers between groups.
- RNA-Seq analysis performed on prostate biopsies; differential expression analyzed with DESeq2 software package.
- qPCR confirmation on two key genes (AMACR, ARLNC1) with n = 7-18 samples per group.
- Heatmap visualization with hierarchical clustering based on Euclidean distance.

## Limitations

- Sample size of 98 is modest; qPCR confirmation had very small subgroups (n = 7-18).
- Only abstract-level content available for this review; full methods, statistical details, and effect sizes are not accessible.
- Duration of supplementation not stated in abstract.
- Gene expression findings (40 genes) may not survive multiple testing correction at stringent thresholds; the abstract uses the language "correlated with" rather than definitive causal claims.
- Null results on HDAC activity and tissue biomarkers suggest that SFN at this dose does not produce measurable epigenetic or proliferative changes in prostate tissue, despite bioavailability confirmation.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: RCT with biomarker endpoints
This is a direct RCT with biomarker endpoints in a high-risk population (men scheduled for prostate biopsy). It provides evidence that SFN at 200 umol/day reaches target tissue (confirmed by plasma/urine metabolites) and produces gene expression changes in prostate tissue, but the biomarker results are largely null (no HDAC, no tissue IHC changes).

### Q6: Null results
This study is a strong example of a trial with mostly null outcomes. Despite confirmed bioavailability, the primary hypothesized mechanisms (HDAC inhibition, changes in proliferation markers Ki67/p21, histone acetylation changes) were not observed. Only gene expression showed differences, and the clinical significance of 40 differentially expressed genes is unclear.

### Q7: Dosing (200 umol/day)
This study used 200 umol/day SFN from broccoli sprout extract, providing direct human data at this dose level. The dose was sufficient to produce measurable plasma and urine metabolites, demonstrating bioavailability, but insufficient to produce the hypothesized HDAC or tissue biomarker changes.

## Claims to Verify

- **98 participants:** Cannot be verified from abstract alone; would need full text to confirm randomization details and dropout rates.
- **40 differentially expressed genes:** The statistical threshold and correction method are not stated in the abstract. The n = 7-18 per group for qPCR is small.
- **"200 umol daily":** The exact preparation and standardization of the BSE supplement should be confirmed from the full text.

## Summary

This double-blind RCT in 98 men scheduled for prostate biopsy tested broccoli sprout extract (200 umol SFN/day) vs placebo. Despite confirmed bioavailability (elevated urine/plasma SFN metabolites), there were no significant changes in blood HDAC activity or prostate tissue biomarkers (H3K18ac, HDAC3, HDAC6, Ki67, p21). RNA-Seq identified 40 differentially expressed genes including downregulation of prostate cancer-associated genes AMACR and ARLNC1, but the clinical significance of these gene expression changes is uncertain. The study is notable as a well-designed human RCT with mostly null biomarker outcomes, suggesting that SFN at this dose does not produce the hypothesized epigenetic effects in prostate tissue.
