## Core Findings
This is a comprehensive review evaluating the relationships between formulation (plants, sprouts, beverages, supplements), bioavailability, and efficacy of sulforaphane, with attention to doses used in pre-clinical and clinical studies. It covers over 3000 publications and over 50 clinical trials. The review highlights challenges in translating animal model doses to human clinical studies using allometric scaling.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Formulation and Bioavailability (Q2)
- Glucoraphanin is the inert precursor present in broccoli plants; sulforaphane is formed via myrosinase hydrolysis
- Glucoraphanin is most abundant in broccoli seeds on a weight basis (Figure 1)
- NQO1 induction capacity follows the same curve as glucoraphanin content across plant parts
- Multiple formulations evaluated: hot-water extracts of 3-day-old broccoli sprouts (lyophilized), reconstituted beverages, commercial dietary supplement tablets

### Specific Doses from Clinical Studies (Q7)
- **GR 800:** Beverage from hot-water extract of 3-day-old broccoli sprouts, lyophilized, reconstituted in mango juice/water; delivers 800 micromol glucoraphanin
- **SF 150:** Hot-water extract cooled, treated with daikon (myrosinase source), lyophilized; delivers 150 micromol sulforaphane
- **GR600 + SF40:** Combined preparation delivering 600 micromol glucoraphanin + 40 micromol sulforaphane
- **Commercial supplement tablets:** Lyophilized broccoli sprouts + milled broccoli seeds; 75 and 150 micromol glucoraphanin (with myrosinase present)
- Studies conducted in Qidong, China with urinary excretion measured by isotope dilution mass spectrometry

### Dose-Response and Allometric Scaling (Q5, Q7)
- Mouse oral doses: varied by route (gavage vs. in diet) and efficacy outcome (Figure 2)
- Mouse intraperitoneal doses also catalogued (Figure 2, bottom panel)
- Dose extrapolation assumptions: 25 g body weight, 4 g food/mouse/day
- Mechanism studies in mice: Nrf2-dependent, anti-inflammation, and other mechanisms (Figure 3)
- Allometric scaling correction factors: 0.081 for murine doses, 0.162 for rat doses (Figure 5)
- Human doses estimated based on 70 kg body weight

### Supplement vs. Dietary (Q5)
- Seeds have highest glucoraphanin content but require myrosinase for conversion
- Microbiome can provide myrosinase, but plant-derived myrosinase gives more reliable conversion
- Commercial supplements use combination of lyophilized sprouts and milled seeds
- Daikon used as exogenous myrosinase source in some preparations

## Methodology
- Review article synthesizing >3000 publications and >50 clinical trials
- Systematic evaluation of doses across pre-clinical (mouse, rat) and clinical studies
- Allometric scaling used to compare animal and human doses
- Urinary metabolite excretion (sulforaphane + sulforaphane-cysteine + sulforaphane-N-acetylcysteine) used as "internal dose" biomarker
- Isotope dilution mass spectrometry for metabolite quantification
