# src-009: Chowdhury et al. (2014) - Vitamin D and Risk of Cause Specific Death

**Citation:** Chowdhury R, Kunutsor S, Vitezova A, et al. Vitamin D and risk of cause specific death: systematic review and meta-analysis of observational cohort and randomised intervention studies. BMJ 2014;348:g1903.

**Source URL:** https://www.bmj.com/content/348/bmj.g1903

**Quality Assessment:** Full text available (BMJ open access). Peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analysis. High methodological quality following PRISMA/MOOSE guidelines.

---

## Core Findings

1. **Observational evidence strongly links low 25(OH)D to increased mortality.** Across 73 cohort studies (849,412 participants), bottom vs top third of 25(OH)D distribution was associated with 35% higher all-cause mortality (RR 1.35, 95% CI 1.22-1.49), 35% higher CVD mortality (1.13-1.61), and 14% higher cancer mortality (1.01-1.29). Each 10 ng/mL decline in 25(OH)D was associated with 16% (CI 8%-23%) higher all-cause mortality.

2. **Vitamin D3 supplementation (but not D2) significantly reduced all-cause mortality in RCTs.** In 22 RCTs (30,716 participants, mostly older adults), D3 reduced all-cause mortality by 11% (RR 0.89, 95% CI 0.80-0.99), while D2 showed no benefit (RR 1.04, 0.97-1.11). The D3 effect was consistent across subgroups (dose, duration, location, sex).

3. **D3 and D2 have meaningfully different effects on mortality.** D3 supplementation showed stable effects across all subgroups examined. D2 showed increased mortality risk in trials with lower doses and shorter intervention periods, consistent with D2 being less potent at maintaining circulating 25(OH)D.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **Total sample:** 880,128 participants across 95 study reports from 26 nations; 71,625 mortality outcomes (10,777 CVD deaths, 6,911 cancer deaths)
- **Observational cohorts:** 73 studies, 849,412 participants, 66,511 events; median baseline 25(OH)D = 20.7 ng/mL (IQR 17.5-24.3)
- **RCTs:** 22 trials, 30,716 participants; 14 tested D3, 8 tested D2; average follow-up 0.38-6.8 years
- **D3 dose range in trials:** 10-6,000 IU/day; D2 dose range: 208-4,500 IU/day
- **Insufficiency prevalence (<30 ng/mL):** 69.5% US, 86.4% Europe; severe deficiency (<10 ng/mL): 15% US, 4% Europe
- **Population attributable risk:** 12.8% of US deaths and 9.4% of European deaths attributable to vitamin D deficiency
- **Cause-specific observational findings:** Significant inverse associations for coronary disease, lymphoma, upper digestive tract cancer, and respiratory disease mortality

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of both observational cohort studies and RCTs
- **Search:** Medline, Embase, Cochrane Library to August 2013; no language restrictions
- **Inclusion for RCTs:** Only studies administering vitamin D alone (excluding combined calcium/vitamin D), vs placebo/no treatment
- **Statistical approach:** Random effects models; standardized to bottom vs top third comparisons for observational data; Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for cohort quality; Cochrane tool for RCT quality
- **Key methodological strength:** Separated D3 from D2 trials and excluded mixed interventions (calcium + D), which had confounded prior meta-analyses

## Limitations

1. **Observational data subject to confounding.** Low 25(OH)D may reflect poor health, obesity, low socioeconomic status, or inadequate sun exposure rather than causing mortality directly. Residual confounding remains a concern despite extensive adjustment.
2. **Reverse causality plausible.** Stronger associations in studies with <5 years follow-up suggest underlying disease may lower 25(OH)D.
3. **RCTs were in older adults only.** Average age 56-85; results may not generalize to younger populations.
4. **Insufficient power for cause-specific mortality in RCTs.** Could not reliably assess effects on CVD, cancer, or other specific causes.
5. **No serial 25(OH)D measurements.** Observational studies lacked within-person variability assessment, risking regression dilution.
6. **Small number of trials per supplement type** (14 D3, 8 D2), limiting subgroup precision.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Largest meta-analyses on D3 and all-cause mortality):** Highly relevant. One of the largest and most cited meta-analyses. 22 RCTs with 30,716 participants; D3 showed significant 11% mortality reduction.
- **Q2 (D3 on CVD, cancer):** Moderately relevant. Observational data show inverse associations with CVD and cancer mortality. RCT data insufficient for cause-specific outcomes.
- **Q3 (Optimal levels and doses):** Relevant. Shows dose-response (16% increase per 10 ng/mL decline). Insufficiency prevalence data. Notes threshold effect up to ~112 nmol/L achievable with 600 IU/day D3. But does not establish optimal dose from RCTs.
- **Q4 (Effects by baseline deficiency):** Relevant. Subgroup analyses showed stronger associations in populations with low baseline supplement use (proxy for lower baseline 25(OH)D).
- **Q5 (D3 vs D2):** Highly relevant. Key finding of differential effects: D3 reduced mortality, D2 did not. Discusses mechanisms (D2 less potent at maintaining 25(OH)D).
- **Q6 (Expert disagreement):** Partially relevant. Concludes D3 reduces mortality but calls for further studies before widespread supplementation. Highlights gaps in optimal dose/duration.
- **Q7 (Evidence challenging popular narrative):** Partially relevant. Notes that observational associations may be confounded; overall vitamin D supplementation (pooled D2+D3) did NOT significantly reduce mortality; only the D3 subgroup showed benefit.

## Claims to Verify

- Population attributable risk figures (9.4% Europe, 12.8% US) are derived estimates based on prevalence assumptions and pooled RRs -- these are modeled, not directly observed.
- The 11% mortality reduction with D3 (RR 0.89) has a confidence interval that barely excludes 1.0 (0.80-0.99), making this a borderline finding susceptible to publication bias or inclusion criteria changes.
- The claim of 880,128 unique participants may double-count if some participants appeared in multiple study reports; authors state they selected the most up-to-date publication when multiple existed.
