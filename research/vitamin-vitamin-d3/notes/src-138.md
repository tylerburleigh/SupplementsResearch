**Type:** Clinical Practice Guideline (GRADE methodology, systematic reviews by Mayo EPC)

**Population Scope:** Generally healthy individuals without established indications for vitamin D treatment or 25(OH)D testing. Does NOT apply to those with malabsorption, nephrotic syndrome, CKD, or other conditions substantially altering vitamin D physiology.

## Core Findings

This is a landmark 2024 guideline from the Endocrine Society, updating their 2011 guideline. It addresses 14 clinical questions using GRADE methodology with systematic reviews commissioned from the Mayo Evidence-Based Practice Center. All recommendations are conditional (strength 2), reflecting moderate-to-low certainty of evidence in most cases. The key departure from the 2011 guideline is: **no more endorsement of a 30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L) target for 25(OH)D**, and **no more routine 25(OH)D screening** in any population considered.

## Key Recommendations (Summary of All 14)

### Populations FOR empiric supplementation:
1. **Children 1-18 years** (Rec 1): Suggest empiric vitamin D to prevent nutritional rickets and potentially lower respiratory tract infections. Doses in trials: 300-2000 IU/day, weighted avg ~1200 IU/day. Evidence: low certainty (GRADE 2, O+O).
2. **Adults >=75 years** (Rec 6): Suggest empiric vitamin D for potential to lower mortality. RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.93-1.00), 25 trials, 49,879 participants, HIGH certainty. Weighted avg dose ~900 IU/day. Daily low-dose preferred over intermittent high-dose. Evidence: conditional, moderate certainty.
3. **Pregnancy** (Rec 8): Suggest empiric vitamin D for potential to lower preeclampsia, intra-uterine mortality, preterm birth, SGA birth, neonatal mortality. Point estimates favor benefit for all outcomes but 95% CIs include null. Weighted avg dose ~2500 IU/day. Evidence: low certainty.
4. **High-risk prediabetes** (Rec 10): Suggest empiric vitamin D to reduce progression to diabetes. RR 0.90 (95% CI 0.81-1.00), 10 trials. IPD-MA HR 0.85 (95% CI 0.75-0.96). Weighted avg dose ~3500 IU/day. Evidence: moderate certainty. Lifestyle modification must be concurrent.

### Populations AGAINST empiric supplementation (above DRI):
5. **Adults <50 years** (Rec 2): Suggest against. Follow IOM RDA of 600 IU/day. Very low certainty evidence.
6. **Adults 50-74 years** (Rec 4): Suggest against. High-certainty evidence of no benefit for fractures (RR 0.97), cancer (RR 1.00), CVD (RR 1.00), mortality (RR 1.07). Follow IOM RDA (600-800 IU/day).

### 25(OH)D Testing:
- **Rec 3, 5, 7, 9, 12, 13, 14**: Suggest against routine 25(OH)D testing in ALL populations considered -- healthy adults, adults 50-74, adults >=75, pregnancy, dark complexion, obesity. No trial evidence defines outcome-specific threshold levels.

### Dosing:
- **Rec 11**: For adults >=50 with indications for vitamin D, suggest daily lower-dose over intermittent high-dose. Intermittent high-dose (>100,000 IU) may increase fracture risk (RR 1.14, 95% CI 1.02-1.27).

## Critical Guideline Changes from 2011

1. **No longer endorses 30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L) target** for 25(OH)D
2. **No longer defines specific 25(OH)D levels** for sufficiency/insufficiency/deficiency
3. **Recommends against routine 25(OH)D screening** -- a major shift from 2011
4. Does NOT replace current IOM DRIs; recommendations are for supplementation ABOVE DRI levels

## Key Evidence Details

### Mortality (Adults >=75)
- 25 trials, 49,879 participants
- RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.93-1.00), absolute: 6 fewer deaths per 1000
- Community-based studies: RR 0.95 (95% CI 0.90-0.99)
- Effect consistent across subgroups (risk of bias, gender, calcium co-admin, dose, setting)
- Weighted average dose: ~909 IU/day

### Fractures (Adults 50-74)
- 13 RCTs, 86,311 participants
- RR 0.97 (95% CI 0.91-1.03)
- Median baseline 25(OH)D: 24 ng/mL (60 nmol/L) -- most participants were NOT deficient

### Mortality signal in 50-74 age group concern
- RR 1.07 (95% CI 0.95-1.20) overall
- With calcium: RR 0.90 (0.79-1.01); vitamin D alone: RR 1.12 (1.01-1.24)
- High-dose vitamin D: RR 1.22 (1.06-1.39) vs standard: RR 0.95 (0.86-1.04) -- potential harm signal

### Prediabetes
- 10 RCTs, 5316 participants
- Aggregate RR 0.90 (0.81-1.00)
- IPD-MA of 3 diabetes-prevention trials: HR 0.85 (0.75-0.96), 4190 participants
- Effect more pronounced: age >62 (HR 0.81), baseline 25(OH)D <12 ng/mL (HR 0.58), BMI <30 (HR 0.79)
- Absolute 3-year risk reduction: 24 fewer per 1000 (systematic review) or 33 fewer per 1000 (IPD-MA)
- Compared to metformin in DPP: 70 fewer per 1000

### Pregnancy
- 10 RCTs, 2979 participants (many from South Asia with very low baseline 25(OH)D)
- Preeclampsia: RR 0.73 (0.46-1.15), 23 fewer per 1000
- Intra-uterine mortality: RR 0.70 (0.34-1.46), 6 fewer per 1000
- Preterm birth: RR 0.73 (0.39-1.36), 28 fewer per 1000
- SGA: RR 0.78 (0.50-1.20), 41 fewer per 1000
- Neonatal mortality: RR 0.57 (0.22-1.49), 8 fewer per 1000
- WHO does NOT recommend vitamin D in pregnancy (2016/2020 guidance) -- this Endocrine Society guideline differs

### Respiratory infections (children)
- 12 RCTs, 12,951 participants (mostly in Asia)
- Any RTI: RR 0.94 (0.87-1.02), 43 fewer per 1000
- Low-bias studies: RR 0.99 (no effect); some-concern bias: RR 0.75
- Lower RTI: RR 0.93 (0.83-1.04)
- Rate ratio for total infections: 0.64 (0.51-0.82) -- significant

### Dark Complexion / Obesity Screening
- No trials examined screening in these populations
- Subgroup analyses by self-identified race (Black) showed no clear benefit for fractures, mortality, CVD, or cancer
- Panel noted self-identified race is inaccurate proxy for skin complexion
- Obesity subgroup analyses: no clear benefit for fractures, mortality, cancer; one signal from FIND trial for CVD reduction with high-dose vitamin D in obese (HR 0.19, 0.04-0.82)

## Methodology

- GRADE methodology with Evidence-to-Decision (EtD) framework
- Systematic reviews by Mayo Evidence-Based Practice Center
- Searches conducted February 2022, updated December 2023
- 14 clinical questions, 4-6 outcomes per question
- Prioritized RCTs; excluded Mendelian randomization studies
- Panel: 14 members + patient representative + methodologist
- Co-sponsored by AACE, ESE, PES, ASBMR, ASN, and others
- External peer review completed

## Key Limitations (per guideline)

1. Most large trials had baseline 25(OH)D in "adequate" range (~24-31 ng/mL) -- null results don't prove vitamin D is ineffective for those with low levels
2. Control participants still received vitamin D through sun/diet/supplements
3. No trials designed/powered for subgroup analysis by baseline 25(OH)D
4. Many trials too short for chronic disease outcomes
5. Various doses and schedules prevented specific dose recommendations
6. Study populations largely European ancestry / non-Hispanic White
7. Many older-adult trials combined vitamin D with calcium, making isolation of vitamin D effects difficult
8. Ecological fallacy in study-level subgroup analyses by baseline 25(OH)D

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (deficiency thresholds):** Directly relevant -- guideline explicitly declines to endorse any threshold for sufficiency/insufficiency/deficiency, departing from 2011 position
- **Q2 (dose-response):** Relevant -- provides dose ranges from trials but cannot specify optimal doses; daily low-dose preferred over intermittent high-dose
- **Q3 (supplementation benefits):** Centrally relevant -- comprehensive review of RCT evidence across all populations; identifies benefits only for children, elderly >=75, pregnancy, high-risk prediabetes
- **Q4 (special populations):** Relevant -- addresses dark complexion, obesity, pregnancy, children explicitly
- **Q5 (interaction with calcium):** Relevant -- documents calcium co-administration effects in mortality and fracture subgroup analyses
- **Q6 (screening and testing):** Centrally relevant -- recommends against routine 25(OH)D testing in ALL populations, noting no evidence for threshold-based management
- **Q7 (adverse effects):** Relevant -- reviews nephrolithiasis, hypercalcemia, kidney disease across all questions; notes kidney stone signal in 50-74 group (RR 1.10); potential harm from high-dose intermittent vitamin D

## Claims to Verify

1. **IPD-MA prediabetes result (HR 0.85)** -- This is cited from reference 101 (D2d and related trials). The claim that baseline 25(OH)D <12 ng/mL yields HR 0.58 is based on IPD subgroup analysis with likely small sample; verify the n in that subgroup.
2. **FIND trial CVD in obesity (HR 0.19, 95% CI 0.04-0.82)** -- Very wide CI suggests very few events; this should not be overinterpreted.
3. **High-dose vitamin D mortality harm signal in 50-74 (RR 1.22, 95% CI 1.06-1.39)** -- This is statistically significant and concerning. Verify whether this was a pre-specified subgroup analysis.
