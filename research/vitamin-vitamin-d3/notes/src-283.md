# src-283: Vitamin D Supplementation and ARTI Risk in Healthy Pediatrics (Meta-Analysis)

## Citation
Fang Q, Wu Y, Lu J, Zheng H (2023). "A meta-analysis of the association between vitamin D supplementation and the risk of acute respiratory tract infection in the healthy pediatric group." *Frontiers in Nutrition*, 10:1188958. DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2023.1188958

## Core Findings (2-3 sentences)

This is the first meta-analysis specifically examining vitamin D supplementation and acute respiratory tract infection (ARTI) risk in healthy pediatric populations (0-18 years). Across 8 RCTs, vitamin D supplementation showed **no significant benefit** in reducing ARTI rates compared to placebo (OR=0.98), and high-dose regimens did not outperform standard doses (400 IU/d) for overall ARTI prevention (OR=0.85). The only significant finding was a reduction in **Influenza A specifically** with high-dose vs. low-dose vitamin D (OR=0.39, P<0.001).

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Primary outcome: ARTI rates (Vitamin D vs. Placebo)
- **OR = 0.98** (95% CI 0.90-1.08, P=0.61), I-squared=32%
- 4 studies, 13,367 individuals
- No significant benefit of vitamin D over placebo

### Secondary outcome: High-dose vs. standard-dose vitamin D for ARTIs
- **OR = 0.85** (95% CI 0.64-1.12, P=0.32), I-squared=37%
- 2 studies, 1,596 individuals
- No significant dose-response effect

### Influenza A sub-analysis (high-dose vs. low-dose)
- **OR = 0.39** (95% CI 0.26-0.59, P<0.001), I-squared=0%
- Only significant finding; no heterogeneity
- Caveat: only based on studies that reported this specific outcome

### Safety
- Only 2 of 8 studies (8,972 patients) reported side effects
- One symptomatic hypercalcemia in vitamin D group
- Three cases of hypervitaminosis D (asymptomatic)
- No renal stones in either group
- Overall acceptable safety profile

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA guidelines
- **Databases:** 7 databases searched on May 27, 2022
- **Eligibility:** RCTs in healthy pediatric patients (0-18 years) assessing vitamin D supplementation vs. placebo or comparing doses for ARTI outcomes
- **Analysis:** R software v4.0.2; fixed or random effects models based on heterogeneity (I-squared >50% or P<0.05 triggers random effects)
- **Included studies:** 8 RCTs from 326 screened records
- **Geographic diversity:** Canada (2), China, Mongolia, Finland, India, Vietnam, Afghanistan
- **Sample sizes:** Ranged from 49 (Brett-2018) to 8,851 (Ganmaa-2020)
- **Follow-up:** 4 months to 3 years
- **Risk of bias:** 5 studies low risk, 3 high risk (Cochrane tool)
- **Dose threshold:** 400 IU/d defined as standard; >400 IU/d as high dose

## Limitations

1. **Different treatment arms across studies** prevented pooling all studies in the same analysis
2. **Infection-type stratification was infrequent**, preventing collective evidence per infection type (only Influenza A had enough data for meta-analysis)
3. **Seasonal variation and sun exposure** could not be studied due to data scarcity
4. **Safety profile assessed in only 2 of 8 studies** -- incomplete safety evidence
5. **Small number of included RCTs (8)** limits statistical power for subgroup analyses
6. Some included studies had **high risk of bias** (3 of 8)
7. Population was restricted to healthy children -- results may not generalize to deficient or comorbid populations

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q2 (immune function):** Directly relevant. Shows null effect of vitamin D supplementation on ARTIs in healthy children, though with a notable Influenza A exception at high doses. Challenges the immune-benefit narrative for vitamin D in well-nourished pediatric populations.
- **Q4 (baseline deficiency status):** The authors note that prior trials found benefit in children with baseline deficiency/insufficiency, suggesting effect modification by baseline status, though this meta-analysis did not directly stratify by baseline 25(OH)D.
- **Q7 (challenging popular narrative):** Strongly relevant. First meta-analysis in healthy pediatrics finding no benefit of vitamin D for ARTI prevention, counter to popular supplementation narratives. The overall null result despite biological plausibility is notable.

## Claims to Verify

- The pooled sample of 13,367 for the placebo comparison across 4 studies is consistent with the individual study sample sizes (Loeb: 1300, Mandlik: 244, Holland: 3046, Ganmaa: 8851 = 13,441; close to stated 13,367, minor discrepancy possibly due to exclusions).
- The dose classification of 400 IU/d as "standard" is a reasonable but author-defined threshold; different cut-points could change results.
- The Influenza A finding (OR=0.39) is compelling but comes from a limited number of study arms; this should be interpreted cautiously given it is a sub-analysis.
