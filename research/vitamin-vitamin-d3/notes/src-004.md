# src-004: Umbrella Review of Vitamin D and All-Cause Mortality (116 RCTs)

**Citation:** Cao M, He C, Gong M, Wu S, He J. "The effects of vitamin D on all-cause mortality in different diseases: an evidence-map and umbrella review of 116 randomized controlled trials." *Frontiers in Nutrition*. 2023;10:1132528. DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2023.1132528. PMCID: PMC10325578.

**Study Design:** Umbrella review of 27 meta-analyses and 19 updated RCTs, encompassing 116 total RCTs and 149,865 participants. PROSPERO registered (CRD42021252921). Databases searched: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar (inception through April 25, 2022).

## Core Findings

1. **No mortality benefit in the general population.** Vitamin D showed no significant effect on all-cause mortality in the general population (RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.96-1.03), based on 43 RCTs. This is a strong null finding with narrow confidence intervals.

2. **Benefits limited to specific disease subgroups.** Statistically significant mortality reductions were found only in: respiratory cancer patients (respiratory cancer mortality: RR 0.56, 95% CI 0.33-0.96), COVID-19 patients (all-cause mortality: RR 0.54, 95% CI 0.33-0.88), and liver disease patients (all-cause mortality: RR 0.64, 95% CI 0.50-0.81, especially cirrhosis: RR 0.63, 95% CI 0.50-0.81). However, the liver disease and COVID-19 findings rest on very few RCTs (3 and ~4 respectively).

3. **No benefit across most disease categories.** Null results for cancer overall, CKD (24 RCTs), critical illness (13 RCTs), CVD (8 RCTs), musculoskeletal diseases (6 RCTs), sepsis (4 RCTs), and T2DM (3 RCTs).

### Dose and Baseline Level Variation

- Baseline serum 25(OH)D ranged from 16.4 +/- 5.2 nmol/L to 128.7 +/- 47.3 nmol/L across included RCTs
- Daily supplement doses ranged from 800 IU to 600,000 IU (likely a bolus/Loading dose, not truly daily)
- Authors note protective concentration estimated at 75-110 nmol/L, requiring ~1,800-4,000 IU/day orally
- Serum vitamin D level of at least 20 ng/ml (50 nmol/L) generally recommended as minimum

## Methodology

- **Design:** Umbrella review (highest level of evidence synthesis) following PRISMA guidelines
- **Quality Assessment:** AMSTAR (16 high, 9 moderate, 2 low), GRADE (13 high, 7 moderate, 4 low, 3 very low), Cochrane ROB for updated RCTs
- **Statistical Analysis:** Fixed-effects model; random-effects if I2 > 50%. Effect measure: risk ratio. RevMan 5.4.
- **Heterogeneity:** Assessed via I2; subgroup analyses conducted for disease subtypes
- **Publication bias:** Funnel plots if >10 studies; Egger regression test

## Limitations

1. **Inherent limitations of included studies** -- secondary nature of umbrella review inherits biases from source meta-analyses and RCTs
2. **Small RCT counts for some conditions** -- only 3 RCTs for liver disease, 3 for T2DM, 4 for sepsis; limits statistical power and generalizability
3. **Wide dosing range** -- no restriction on vitamin D type (D2 vs D3), dose, route, or combination use; introduces heterogeneity
4. **No analysis by baseline deficiency status** -- did not stratify results by baseline vitamin D levels or VDR polymorphisms across races
5. **Incomplete disease coverage** -- excluded Parkinson's and AIDS data due to statistical criteria
6. **Fixed-dose vs fixed-level problem** -- most RCTs used fixed doses rather than targeting specific serum levels, potentially diluting effects in deficient populations

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Largest meta-analyses/RCTs):** Highly relevant. This is one of the largest umbrella reviews covering 116 RCTs and ~150K participants. Strong null finding for general population mortality.
- **Q2 (Disease-specific evidence):** Directly addresses cancer, CVD, musculoskeletal, and immune-related conditions. Null for CVD, overall cancer, and musculoskeletal diseases. Positive signals only for respiratory cancer mortality and COVID-19.
- **Q3 (Optimal levels/doses):** Discusses dose variation across studies but does not conduct dose-response analysis. Notes the fixed-dose vs fixed-level problem as a key limitation.
- **Q4 (Baseline deficiency):** Does NOT stratify by baseline deficiency status -- explicitly noted as a limitation.
- **Q5 (D3 vs D2):** Does not distinguish between vitamin D2 and D3 -- pooled all vitamin D interventions.
- **Q6 (Expert disagreement):** Documents the evolution of controversy (2014 Bjelakovic finding benefit, 2019 Zhang finding cancer mortality benefit, 2022 Neale overturning cancer benefit). Directly relevant.
- **Q7 (Challenging popular narrative):** Highly relevant. The broad null finding across most populations challenges the popular narrative of universal vitamin D supplementation for mortality reduction.

## Claims to Verify

1. **Participant count (149,865):** Stated in abstract and results. Appears consistent across sections. However, this is the total across all 116 RCTs pooled from 27 meta-analyses plus 19 updated RCTs -- some participants may be double-counted across overlapping meta-analyses. The paper states they deduplicated RCTs, but the extent of overlap verification is unclear.
2. **Dose range (800 IU to 600,000 IU):** The 600,000 IU figure seems like a loading/bolus dose rather than daily. The text says "daily supplement doses varying from 600,000 IU to 800IU" which may be a reporting error -- should verify whether this refers to cumulative or single doses.
3. **Harmful effects in 2 general-population RCTs:** RR 3.70 [95% CI 1.06-12.92] is based on very small event counts (wide CI), and the other (RR 1.20 [95% CI 1.04-1.37]) appears more robust. These are individual study outliers, not pooled findings.
