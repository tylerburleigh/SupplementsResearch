# src-001: Vitamin D Supplementation and Mortality — AAFP/theNNT.com Meta-Analysis Summary

**Source URL:** https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/0701/od1.html
**Type:** Narrative summary of a meta-analysis, published in American Family Physician (via theNNT.com)
**Date:** 2020 (reviewing meta-analysis of RCTs published before Dec 26, 2018)
**Quality Assessment:** ok — substantive content with quantitative results from a large meta-analysis

---

## Core Findings

1. **No all-cause mortality benefit.** A meta-analysis of 52 RCTs (75,454 participants) found no statistically significant reduction in all-cause mortality with vitamin D supplementation (RR 0.98; 95% CI 0.95-1.02). This updates earlier meta-analyses that had suggested small benefits; newer trials with larger samples have nullified those findings.

2. **Cancer mortality reduction detected.** Vitamin D supplementation was associated with a significant reduction in cancer mortality (RR 0.84; 95% CI 0.74-0.95; NNT = 250 over 1.2 years). However, the absolute benefit is small (ARR = 0.004).

3. **No cardiovascular benefit.** No significant effects on cardiovascular mortality, cerebrovascular mortality, ischemic heart disease mortality, or noncancer/noncardiovascular mortality.

4. **D3 superior to D2 in subgroup analysis.** All-cause mortality was significantly lower with D3 vs D2 (P for interaction = 0.04). D3 also reduced cancer mortality while D2 did not (P for interaction = 0.11). However, the source cautions that subgroup analyses are observational and not randomized comparisons.

5. **Longer follow-up may matter.** All-cause mortality was significantly lower in trials with follow-up exceeding 3 years, suggesting supplementation duration could influence results.

---

## Methodology

- **Design:** Meta-analysis of 52 RCTs
- **Participants:** 75,454 total adults taking vitamin D supplements
- **Search period:** Through December 26, 2018
- **Analysis:** Intention-to-treat
- **Subgroup analyses:** Dose (<2000 vs >=2000 IU/day), vitamin D type (D2 vs D3), dosing schedule (daily vs intermittent), baseline 25-OH-D (<20 vs >=20 ng/mL), age (<70 vs >=70), follow-up duration (<3 vs >=3 years), publication year (before/after 2014), sex, residential status (community vs institution), bolus vs non-bolus, vitamin D alone vs with calcium, latitude
- **Publication bias:** Assessed; no evidence of publication bias or small-study effects

## Limitations

- This is a secondary summary (AAFP/theNNT.com) of the original meta-analysis, not the primary publication. Some details (exact study identification, individual trial quality ratings) are not included.
- Subgroup findings (D3 vs D2, follow-up duration) are observational within the meta-analysis and require confirmation in dedicated RCTs.
- The original meta-analysis search cut-off is Dec 2018, meaning important large trials published after that date (e.g., VITAL 2019 sub-analyses, D-Health) are not included.
- The source does not report the specific baseline 25-OH-D results by subgroup, limiting assessment of Q4 (effects by baseline deficiency status).

## Claims to Verify

- **NNT = 274 (headline) vs NNT = 250 (narrative body):** The benefits table states "1 in 274 did not die from cancer over 1.2 years" but the narrative states NNT = 250. These should be reconciled against the original meta-analysis. The evidence unit uses 250 as reported in the detailed results section.
- **75,454 participants:** Cannot cross-check against original Methods from this summary; verify against the source meta-analysis.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (mortality meta-analyses):** Highly relevant. This is a direct summary of a large meta-analysis (52 RCTs) on all-cause and cause-specific mortality.
- **Q5 (D3 vs D2):** Relevant. Subgroup analysis comparing D3 and D2 for mortality outcomes, with interaction p-values.
- **Q7 (challenging supplementation narrative):** Highly relevant. Key finding is no all-cause mortality benefit despite widespread supplementation; earlier positive findings overturned by larger trials.
- **Q4 (baseline deficiency):** Partially relevant. Subgroup by baseline 25-OH-D was performed (threshold 20 ng/mL) but results not reported in this summary.
- **Q2 (bone health, immune, CVD, cancer, cognitive):** Partially relevant for CVD and cancer mortality endpoints only; no data on bone health, immune function, or cognitive decline.
