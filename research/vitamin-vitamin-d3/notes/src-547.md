# src-547: Ross et al. 2011 -- IOM Report on DRIs for Calcium and Vitamin D (JCEM)

## Citation
- **Authors:** A. Catharine Ross (Chair), JoAnn E. Manson, Steven A. Abrams, John F. Aloia, Patsy M. Brannon, Steven K. Clinton, Ramon A. Durazo-Arvizu, J. Christopher Gallagher, Richard L. Gallo, Glenville Jones, Christopher S. Kovacs, Susan T. Mayne, Clifford J. Rosen, Sue A. Shapses
- **Venue:** Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism (JCEM), 2011, 96(1):53-58. doi: 10.1210/jc.2010-2704
- **PMID:** 21118827 | **PMCID:** PMC3046611
- **Open Access:** Yes (CC BY-NC)

## Core Findings (2-3 sentences)

This is the official IOM Committee summary of the 2011 Dietary Reference Intakes for calcium and vitamin D, the most authoritative guideline document in North America. The Committee concluded that bone health is the only outcome with sufficient evidence for causality and dose-response to set DRIs; for extraskeletal outcomes (cancer, CVD, diabetes, autoimmune disorders), evidence was inconsistent, inconclusive, and insufficient. The Committee set vitamin D RDAs at 600 IU/d (ages 1-70) and 800 IU/d (ages 71+), corresponding to serum 25OHD of at least 20 ng/ml (50 nmol/L), and found that the prevalence of vitamin D inadequacy has been overestimated.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Vitamin D RDAs (Table 1)
- Ages 0-6 months: AI = 400 IU/d; UL = 1000 IU/d
- Ages 6-12 months: AI = 400 IU/d; UL = 1500 IU/d
- Ages 1-70: RDA = 600 IU/d; UL = 2500-4000 IU/d (varies by age)
- Ages 71+: RDA = 800 IU/d; UL = 4000 IU/d
- EAR for vitamin D: 400 IU/d for all life-stage groups
- Target serum 25OHD: 20 ng/ml (50 nmol/L) covers >=97.5% of population

### Calcium RDAs
- Range from 700 mg/d (ages 1-3) to 1300 mg/d (ages 9-18)
- Ages 51-70 women and 71+: 1200 mg/d
- ULs range from 1000-3000 mg/d

### 25OHD Thresholds
- 16 ng/ml (40 nmol/L) = EAR (meets needs of ~50% of population)
- 20 ng/ml (50 nmol/L) = RDA (meets needs of >=97.5% of population)
- 30 ng/ml (75 nmol/L) = not consistently associated with greater benefit
- 50 ng/ml (125 nmol/L) = level above which adverse effects are a concern

### Extraskeletal Outcomes
- Cancer, CVD, diabetes, falls, physical performance, autoimmune disorders: evidence inconsistent, inconclusive as to causality, insufficient for DRI development
- RCT evidence was sparse; few trials with extraskeletal outcomes as primary prespecified endpoints
- U-shaped associations observed for CVD, vascular calcification, falls, frailty, pancreatic cancer, all-cause mortality (increased risk at both low and high 25OHD levels)

### Safety
- UL for vitamin D: 4000 IU/d for ages 9+ (up from 2000 IU/d in 1997)
- Starting point for UL was 10,000 IU/d (no hypercalcemia below this), but corrected for uncertainty based on chronic disease outcomes and all-cause mortality
- Serum 25OHD >50 ng/ml should raise clinical concerns about adverse effects

### Population Intake Assessment
- Most North American groups have adequate calcium intake (above EAR), except girls aged 9-18
- Average vitamin D intake from foods is <400 IU/d, but mean 25OHD levels in representative samples exceed 20 ng/ml
- Subgroups at risk: those with poor nutrition, northerly latitudes, institutionalized, dark skin pigmentation, 25OHD <16 ng/ml

## Methodology

- IOM Committee of 14 scientists, active March 2009 - November 2010
- Eight in-person meetings, open public workshop, two open sessions for scientist input
- Comprehensive review of existing evidence including two AHRQ systematic reviews (2007, 2009) plus Committee's own literature review
- Used risk assessment framework: identified health outcomes causally linked to nutrients, estimated EARs (median population needs), then RDAs (EAR + 2 SD)
- Vitamin D DRIs based on assumption of minimal or no sun exposure (due to variability in cutaneous synthesis and skin cancer risks)
- 2011 DRIs represent a shift from AIs (1997) to EARs/RDAs based on much more evidence

## Limitations

- This is a guideline summary, not a systematic review itself -- the full IOM report contains the detailed evidence review
- Published in 2011; major RCTs (VITAL, ViDA, D-Health) published after this report
- The Committee noted extensive uncertainties and identified urgent need for large-scale RCTs
- Observational studies of 25OHD and health outcomes are subject to confounding (obesity, physical activity, race, reverse causation)
- Historical analogy: many micronutrients promising in observational studies (beta-carotene, vitamins C and E) failed in RCTs, some even showed harm

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q3 (Optimal dosing):** Primary source -- sets the baseline RDA of 600-800 IU/d targeting 25OHD >=20 ng/ml. Many subsequent studies and guidelines (Endocrine Society 2011) departed from these recommendations, making this a critical reference point for any discussion of optimal dosing.
- **Q5 (Safety/upper limits):** Sets UL at 4000 IU/d for ages 9+; identifies serum 25OHD >50 ng/ml as concerning. Notes U-shaped risk curves for multiple outcomes. Essential for framing the safety discussion.
- **Q6 (Bone vs. extraskeletal):** The Committee's central conclusion that only bone health has sufficient evidence for causality is a pivotal claim in the vitamin D literature. Many subsequent studies have challenged or supported this. The U-shaped associations noted for extraskeletal outcomes are particularly relevant.
- **Q1-Q2 (Mortality/healthspan):** Notes all-cause mortality U-shaped association with 25OHD, and that observational studies are subject to confounding. Relevant as a skeptical baseline.
- **Q4 (Form/bioavailability):** Not directly addressed; the report does not distinguish between D2 and D3 forms.

## Claims to Verify

- The U-shaped associations for CVD, vascular calcification, falls, frailty, pancreatic cancer, and all-cause mortality are cited from references 7-11 in the paper (Wang 2008, Stolzenberg-Solomon 2010, Melamed 2008, Toner 2010, Visser 2006). These should be verified against the original sources for effect sizes and strength of evidence, as this is a secondhand claim in a guideline document.
- The assertion that "nearly all individuals meet their needs" at the specified RDA/25OHD levels relies on national survey data. The exact proportion of the population with 25OHD below 20 ng/ml should be cross-checked with NHANES data.
