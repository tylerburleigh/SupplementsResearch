# src-012: High Dose Vitamin D3 Supplementation Is Not Associated With Lower Mortality in Critically Ill Patients

**Citation:** Gao Z, Xie J, Li C, Liu L, Yang Y (2022). *Frontiers in Nutrition*, 9:762316. doi: 10.3389/fnut.2022.762316

**Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs (PRISMA-compliant, PROSPERO CRD42020179195)

**Quality assessment:** Full paper text available. Peer-reviewed, open-access, Frontiers in Nutrition. GRADE evidence quality rated LOW for primary outcomes. Metadata was sparse (empty fields) but content is substantive.

---

## Core Findings

1. **No mortality benefit from high-dose vitamin D3 in critically ill patients.** Pooled analysis of 10 RCTs (n=2,058) showed no significant reduction in 28-day mortality (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.78-1.11, P=0.43) or 90-day mortality (RR 0.91, 95% CI 0.79-1.05, P=0.21).

2. **Significant reduction in ventilator days.** High-dose vitamin D3 reduced ventilator days (MD -9.38, 95% CI -13.44 to -5.31, P<0.001), but did not significantly reduce ICU or hospital length of stay.

3. **The VIOLET trial (n=1,059) dominates the pooled result.** Sensitivity analysis showed that removing the VIOLET trial changed the outcome to significant, explaining why a prior meta-analysis (Putzu et al. 2017) found a mortality benefit. The VIOLET trial's inclusion of less severely deficient patients (<20 ng/ml vs. <12 ng/ml) and lack of maintenance dosing may have biased toward null.

## Methodology

- **Search:** PubMed, Web of Science, EMBASE, Cochrane Central through Dec 4, 2020
- **Inclusion:** Adult ICU patients; high-dose vitamin D3 (300,000-540,000 IU single dose); placebo-controlled RCTs; mortality data required
- **10 RCTs included:** USA (4), Austria (2), Iran (3), China (1). Three multicenter. Sample sizes ranged from 20 to 1,059 (VIOLET trial was dominant).
- **Dose range:** 300,000 IU to 540,000 IU, administered enterally or intramuscularly
- **Analysis:** Mantel-Haenszel random effects, Cochrane risk of bias, GRADE, trial sequential analysis, univariate meta-regression, post-hoc subgroup analysis

## Limitations

1. Most included trials had small sample sizes; only one large multicenter RCT (VIOLET)
2. Only 2 of 10 RCTs used LC-MS/MS (the reference method) to measure 25(OH)D
3. Substituted other mortality timepoints for 28-day/90-day in some studies
4. Subgroup analyses based on small numbers -- should be interpreted cautiously
5. Meta-regression and subgroup analyses were post-hoc (hypothesis-generating only)
6. GRADE quality rated LOW for primary outcomes
7. Trial sequential analysis showed sample size was insufficient

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (mortality meta-analyses):** Directly relevant -- a 2022 meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (n=2,058) finding no mortality benefit from high-dose D3 in critically ill patients. Important counterweight to earlier positive meta-analyses.
- **Q4 (baseline deficiency status):** Highly relevant -- discusses how the VIOLET trial included patients with <20 ng/ml (not severely deficient) and suggests patients with <12 ng/ml may still benefit. Notes ongoing VITDALIZE trial targeting severe deficiency.
- **Q7 (evidence challenging popular supplementation narrative):** Strongly relevant -- shows no mortality benefit from high-dose vitamin D3 in a large pooled analysis, with the largest single RCT (VIOLET, n=1,059) showing no benefit. The prior positive meta-analysis finding was driven by smaller studies and was overturned when VIOLET was included.

## Claims to Verify

- **n=2,058 across 10 RCTs:** Confirmed in text at line 143 and in Table 1. The VIOLET trial alone contributed 1,059 patients (51% of the total), so results are heavily influenced by this single study.
- **Subgroup RR 0.47 for 300k IU:** This is a post-hoc subgroup based on only ~4 studies with small sample sizes. The identical RR (0.47) for both dose and route subgroups confirms they are the same studies (300k IU was always given IM). This finding should be treated as hypothesis-generating only.

## Notes for Synthesis

This paper provides a useful contrast with earlier meta-analyses that found mortality benefits. The key insight is that the VIOLET trial (2019, NEJM, n=1,059) -- the largest and highest-quality study -- showed no benefit, and when pooled with smaller studies, the overall result is null. The authors' discussion of why high-dose D3 may not work in critical illness (impaired activation, failure to restore status, lack of synergistic factors) is relevant to Q7.
