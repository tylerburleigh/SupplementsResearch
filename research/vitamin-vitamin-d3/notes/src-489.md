# src-489: USPSTF 2021 Final Recommendation Statement -- Vitamin D Deficiency Screening in Adults

**Source:** US Preventive Services Task Force, April 13, 2021
**URL:** https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/vitamin-d-deficiency-screening
**Type:** Guideline / Recommendation Statement
**Quality:** ok -- full text of the recommendation statement with detailed rationale, evidence review, and references

## Core Findings

The USPSTF reaffirms its 2014 I statement: there is **insufficient evidence** to assess the balance of benefits and harms of screening for vitamin D deficiency in asymptomatic, community-dwelling, nonpregnant adults. The task force found no direct evidence on benefits or harms of screening itself, and the treatment evidence from 26 RCTs shows no benefit for mortality, fractures, or diabetes incidence, with inadequate evidence for other outcomes. No major organization recommends population-based screening.

## Key Evidence

### Screening Recommendation
- **Grade: I statement** (insufficient evidence) -- applies to community-dwelling, nonpregnant, asymptomatic adults
- Does NOT apply to hospitalized or institutionalized persons
- Consistent with the 2014 recommendation -- no change after updated evidence review

### Treatment Effectiveness (from 26 RCTs + 1 nested case-control)
- **All-cause mortality:** 8 RCTs, n=2006, RR 1.13 (95% CI 0.39-3.28) -- no benefit
- **Fractures:** 6 RCTs, n=2186, RR 0.84 (95% CI 0.58-1.21) -- no benefit
- **Diabetes incidence:** 5 RCTs, n=3356, RR 0.96 (95% CI 0.80-1.15) -- no benefit
- **Falls:** Conflicting results. Fallers RR 0.90 (0.75-1.08, NS); falls IRR 0.76 (0.57-0.94, significant). High heterogeneity and possible selective outcome reporting
- **Cardiovascular events:** VITAL (n=2001 subgroup) and ViDA (n=1270 subgroup) -- no significant differences in subgroups with baseline 25(OH)D <20 ng/mL
- **Cancer incidence:** VITAL, ViDA post-hoc, WHI nested case-control -- no benefit in deficient subgroups
- **Depression:** VITAL-DEP (n=1328 with baseline <20 ng/mL) and 2 smaller trials -- no benefit
- Vitamin D deficiency was variably defined across studies (<20 to <31.2 ng/mL)

### Harms
- No direct evidence on harms of screening
- 36 studies on treatment harms: adverse event rates generally similar between treatment and control groups
- Vitamin D toxicity rare, associated with levels >150 ng/mL

### Defining Deficiency -- Key Controversies
- No single 25(OH)D cutpoint defines deficiency for all individuals
- NAM: 97.5% of population meets needs at 20 ng/mL; deficiency risk <12-20 ng/mL
- Known risk factors explain only 20-30% of variability in 25(OH)D levels
- Controversy about whether total 25(OH)D is the best measure across racial/ethnic groups
- Non-Hispanic Black persons have 2-10x higher "deficiency" prevalence but lower fracture rates
- 25(OH)D measurement is difficult; assays underestimate or overestimate; LC-MS/MS is reference standard but also subject to error

### Other Organizations' Positions
- **No organization** recommends population-based screening
- **ASCP** recommends against population screening
- **AAFP** supports USPSTF 2014 I statement
- **Endocrine Society** and **AACE** recommend screening only in at-risk individuals
- Endocrine Society explicitly does not recommend population screening for non-at-risk individuals

### Prevalence Data
- NHANES 2011-2014 (LC-MS/MS): 5% of population >=1 year had 25(OH)D <12 ng/mL; 18% had levels 12-19 ng/mL
- Obesity associated with 1.3-2x increased risk of vitamin D deficiency
- Vitamin D testing increased >80-fold in Medicare from 2000-2010

## Methodology

- Systematic review commissioned to update 2014 recommendation (Kahwati et al., 2021)
- Focused on asymptomatic, community-dwelling, nonpregnant adults >=18 years
- Reviewed benefits/harms of screening and early treatment
- Draft posted for public comment Sep 22 - Oct 19, 2020
- USPSTF explicitly did not review emerging COVID-19 evidence

## Limitations

- No direct screening evidence -- all evidence is about treatment effectiveness
- Treatment RCTs used varying definitions of vitamin D deficiency
- Many outcomes had few studies, small sample sizes, or short follow-up
- Falls evidence had high heterogeneity and possible selective reporting
- Cancer follow-up may be too short (ViDA only 3 years)
- Does not address institutionalized or hospitalized populations
- Does not address supplementation without screening (separate USPSTF recommendations exist)
