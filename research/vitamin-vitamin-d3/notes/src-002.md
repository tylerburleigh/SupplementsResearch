# src-002: Vitamin D Status and Mortality: A Systematic Review of Observational Studies

**Citation:** Heath AK, Kim IY, Hodge AM, English DR, Muller DC. *Int J Environ Res Public Health.* 2019;16(3):383. doi:10.3390/ijerph16030383. PMCID: PMC6388383, PMID: 30700025.

**Type:** Systematic review (narrative synthesis, no new meta-analysis)

**Quality assessment:** Full-text peer-reviewed open-access article. Substantive content with methods, results, discussion, and 108 references. Content quality is high.

---

## Core Findings

1. **Strong observational evidence for inverse association between 25(OH)D and all-cause mortality.** Among 84 articles (57 unique studies), the vast majority found higher mortality at lower 25(OH)D levels. The association appears non-linear, with mortality decreasing as 25(OH)D increases up to a threshold, then plateauing.

2. **D3 supplementation reduces mortality in meta-analyses of RCTs; D2 does not.** Cochrane review: D3 RR=0.94 (95% CI 0.91-0.98); D2 RR=1.02 (95% CI 0.96-1.08). Observational data from the Melbourne Collaborative Cohort Study corroborate this: 25(OH)D3 inversely associated with mortality (HR 0.85 per 25 nmol/L), while detectable 25(OH)D2 was associated with increased risk (HR 1.80).

3. **Cardiovascular mortality association in observational studies is likely confounded.** Mendelian randomization found the opposite direction of effect, and VITAL RCT found no benefit. This challenges a causal interpretation for CVD.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### All-Cause Mortality (Q1)
- 84 articles, 57 unique studies reviewed; most found inverse 25(OH)D-mortality association
- Only 7 studies found no association; these were relatively small (fewer than 800 deaths) and mostly in elderly populations
- Non-linear dose-response: threshold varies by study (50-87.5 nmol/L); individual participant data meta-analysis found lowest mortality at ~78 nmol/L, plateau between 50-125 nmol/L
- Mendelian randomization supports causality: OR for genetically-determined 25 nmol/L lower 25(OH)D = 1.39 (95% CI 1.06-1.81), similar to observational OR of 1.27

### VITAL Trial Results (Q1, Q6)
- n=25,871; 2000 IU D3/day; median 5.3 year follow-up
- All-cause mortality: HR=0.99 (95% CI 0.87-1.12) -- null
- Mean baseline 25(OH)D was 77 nmol/L; only 12.7% had levels <50 nmol/L
- Cancer mortality: HR=0.83 (95% CI 0.67-1.02); excluding first 2 years HR=0.75 (95% CI 0.59-0.96)
- CVD mortality: HR=1.11 (95% CI 0.88-1.40) -- null

### Optimal Blood Levels and Thresholds (Q3)
- Busselton Health Survey: threshold at 65-80 nmol/L
- ESTHER study: threshold at 75 nmol/L
- HUNT Study: inverse below 35 nmol/L, plateau 60-100 nmol/L
- NHANES 2001-2004: threshold at 52 nmol/L
- Southern Community Cohort: ~87.5 nmol/L
- Individual participant data meta-analysis: lowest risk at ~78 nmol/L, plateau 50-125 nmol/L
- Clalit cohort: optimal varied by BMI (66.5-73.0 nmol/L)

### Effects by Baseline Deficiency Status (Q4)
- ESTHER: 34% mortality reduction per 20 nmol/L increase in deficient individuals vs 10% overall
- Health ABC: HR per 14 nmol/L increment = 0.81 at 25 nmol/L baseline vs 0.94 at 75 nmol/L
- NHANES: HR per 25 nmol/L = 0.54 (CI 0.35-0.84) at <=52 nmol/L vs 0.83 (CI 0.63-1.11) above

### D3 vs D2 (Q5)
- Cochrane meta-analysis: D3 RR=0.94 (0.91-0.98); D2 RR=1.02 (0.96-1.08)
- Alternative meta-analysis: D3 RR=0.89 (0.80-0.99); D2 RR=1.04 (0.97-1.11)
- Melbourne Collaborative Cohort: 25(OH)D3 inversely associated with mortality; detectable 25(OH)D2 associated with increased risk (HR=1.80, CI 1.09-2.97)
- Caveat: D3 trials were generally larger and better quality; few participants had detectable 25(OH)D2

### Cardiovascular Mortality (Q2)
- Multiple observational studies found inverse associations
- RCT evidence null: Cochrane RR=0.98 (0.90-1.07); VITAL HR=1.11 (0.88-1.40)
- Mendelian randomization found OPPOSITE direction: OR=0.72 (CI 0.47-1.10) per 25 nmol/L genetically-lower 25(OH)D
- Suggests observational CVD association is due to confounding/reverse causation

### Cancer Mortality (Q2)
- Moderate evidence for inverse association
- Cochrane: D3 reduced cancer mortality RR=0.88 (0.78-0.98)
- Evidence suggests vitamin D affects cancer progression/survival rather than incidence (except colorectal)
- Meta-analysis: 25(OH)D associated with cancer mortality only in those with prior cancer diagnosis

### Respiratory Disease Mortality (Q2)
- 5 of 6 studies found increased risk for low vs high 25(OH)D
- Pooled RR for lowest vs highest third = 1.81 (95% CI 1.24-2.65)

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review with narrative synthesis (no new meta-analysis due to assay standardization issues)
- **Search:** PubMed and EMBASE, inception to August 2018; 722 unique records screened
- **Inclusion:** General population adults; measured circulating 25(OH)D; prospective cohort design
- **Exclusion:** Studies selecting participants based on pre-existing disease
- **Scope:** 84 articles, 57 unique studies, published 2006-2018
- **Conversions:** ng/mL converted to nmol/L (x2.496); different 25(OH)D metrics harmonized for comparability

---

## Limitations

1. Narrative synthesis rather than quantitative meta-analysis (deliberate choice due to assay standardization concerns)
2. Observational studies cannot determine causation; reverse causation remains possible despite adjustment
3. Most studies had only a single 25(OH)D measurement
4. Most participants were middle-aged or elderly; limited data for younger populations
5. Predominantly European and US populations; limited data from other regions
6. U-shaped associations in minority of studies may reflect supplement use by ill individuals
7. Review published 2019; search cutoff August 2018 -- does not capture more recent RCTs (e.g., D-Health, FIND)

---

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (meta-analyses and RCTs on mortality):** Highly relevant -- comprehensive review of 57 studies synthesizing observational evidence; cites key meta-analyses and VITAL
- **Q2 (bone, immune, CVD, cancer, cognitive):** Directly addresses CVD and cancer mortality; respiratory disease mortality (immune proxy); does not address bone health or cognitive decline
- **Q3 (optimal levels):** Extensive evidence on dose-response thresholds across multiple cohorts and meta-analyses; range of 50-87.5 nmol/L with ~78 nmol/L from IPD meta-analysis
- **Q4 (baseline deficiency):** Strong evidence that benefits are concentrated in deficient individuals; quantified effect modification
- **Q5 (D3 vs D2):** Dedicated section with RCT meta-analysis evidence and observational 25(OH)D2 vs D3 comparison
- **Q6 (expert disagreement):** Does not directly address guideline disagreements but provides evidence that RCT benefits are smaller than observational predictions
- **Q7 (challenging popular narrative):** Strong evidence that CVD observational associations are likely confounded (MR shows opposite); RCT benefits much smaller than expected from observational data; VITAL null for all-cause mortality in mostly replete population

## Claims to Verify

- "expected reduction in all-cause mortality for a 20 nmol/L increase in 25(OH)D was 34% for those with vitamin D deficiency versus 10% for all individuals" -- cited to Brenner et al. 2017 (ref 30), an estimation study, not directly observed
- "12.7% of participants had 25(OH)D < 50 nmol/L" in VITAL -- this is from the VITAL paper (Manson et al. 2019, ref 79), not independently verified
- Mendelian randomization OR=1.39 for all-cause mortality -- cited to Afzal et al. 2014 (ref 29); this is a single MR study, not replicated
