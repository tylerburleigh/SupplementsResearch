# src-075: Zhao et al. 2017 — JAMA Meta-analysis of Calcium/Vitamin D and Fracture

**Citation:** Zhao JG, Zeng XT, Wang J, Liu L. Association Between Calcium or Vitamin D Supplementation and Fracture Incidence in Community-Dwelling Older Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. JAMA. 2017;318(24):2466-2482. doi:10.1001/jama.2017.19344

**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5820727/
**PMCID:** PMC5820727 | **PMID:** 29279934
**Quality:** Full text available, substantive content (methods, results, tables, discussion).

## Core Findings

This meta-analysis of 33 RCTs (51,145 community-dwelling adults >50 years) found that supplementation with calcium alone, vitamin D alone, or combined calcium plus vitamin D was **not associated with lower fracture risk** at any site (hip, nonvertebral, vertebral, or total). The findings do not support routine use of these supplements for fracture prevention in community-dwelling older adults. Notably, a harm signal emerged for intermittent high-dose vitamin D (once yearly), which was significantly associated with increased hip fracture risk (RR 1.41, 95% CI 1.02-1.96).

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Vitamin D alone (17 RCTs)
- Hip fracture: RR 1.21 (95% CI 0.99-1.47); ARD 0.00 -- NOT significant
- Nonvertebral fracture: RR 1.10 (95% CI 1.00-1.21) -- borderline, not significant
- Vertebral fracture: RR 0.97 (95% CI 0.54-1.77)
- Total fracture: RR 1.01 (95% CI 0.87-1.17)

### Combined calcium + vitamin D (13 RCTs)
- Hip fracture: RR 1.09 (95% CI 0.85-1.39)
- Nonvertebral fracture: RR 0.88 (95% CI 0.75-1.03)
- Vertebral fracture: RR 0.63 (95% CI 0.29-1.40)
- Total fracture: RR 0.90 (95% CI 0.78-1.04)

### Calcium alone (14 RCTs)
- Hip fracture: RR 1.53 (95% CI 0.97-2.42) -- point estimate suggests possible harm
- Nonvertebral fracture: RR 0.95 (95% CI 0.82-1.11)
- Vertebral fracture: RR 0.83 (95% CI 0.66-1.05)
- Total fracture: RR 0.88 (95% CI 0.75-1.03)

### Harm signal: Intermittent high-dose vitamin D
- Once-yearly dosing: RR 1.41 for hip fracture (95% CI 1.02-1.96) -- **statistically significant increased risk**
- Other intermittent frequencies (quarterly, monthly, weekly): RR 0.84 (95% CI 0.48-1.50) -- not significant

### Baseline 25(OH)D subgroup
- Participants with baseline 25(OH)D >=20 ng/mL: RR 1.49 for hip fracture (95% CI 1.03-2.17) -- significant
- Interaction test with <20 ng/mL group: P = .30 (not significant), so this finding should be interpreted cautiously

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs; registered in PROSPERO (CRD42016053867)
- **Search:** PubMed, Cochrane, EMBASE from inception to December 2016, plus update search July 2012 to July 2017
- **Inclusion:** RCTs comparing calcium, vitamin D, or combined with placebo/no treatment in community-dwelling adults >50 years, reporting fracture outcomes
- **Exclusion:** Institutionalized populations, corticosteroid-induced osteoporosis, combined with anti-osteoporotic drugs, vitamin D analogues, dietary interventions
- **Analysis:** Mantel-Haenszel method with random-effects models; risk ratios, absolute risk differences, 95% CIs
- **Quality assessment:** Cochrane risk-of-bias criteria; 1 trial low quality, 6 high quality, rest moderate
- **Vitamin D form:** Vitamin D3 in 23 trials, vitamin D2 in 2 trials
- **Sensitivity analyses:** Excluding low-quality trials, trials with specific populations -- results unchanged

## Subgroup Findings

Subgroup analyses were performed by dose, sex, fracture history, dietary calcium intake, and baseline 25(OH)D. Results were generally consistent across all subgroups for all three supplement types (calcium alone, vitamin D alone, combined). No subgroup showed a statistically significant benefit for fracture reduction. The only significant finding was the harm signal for intermittent once-yearly vitamin D and for participants with baseline 25(OH)D >=20 ng/mL.

## Limitations

1. Search strategy may have omitted eligible trials
2. Many included trials did not test baseline 25(OH)D for all participants -- subgroup results may differ with complete data
3. Some RCTs had poor quality with unclear allocation concealment
4. Study quality classification was relatively lenient
5. Adherence was not analyzed (differing definitions across trials)
6. Excludes institutionalized populations -- results may not apply to nursing home residents
7. Few trials specifically enrolled participants with established osteoporosis

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q2 (Bone health/fracture prevention):** This is a major, directly relevant meta-analysis. The key takeaway for Q2 is that neither vitamin D alone nor combined with calcium reduces fracture risk in community-dwelling older adults. The finding that intermittent high-dose vitamin D may *increase* hip fracture risk is clinically important. The authors explicitly note that benefits may differ for institutionalized populations, who were excluded here.

**Broader context:** This paper contradicts some earlier meta-analyses (e.g., Bischoff-Ferrari et al., Tang et al.) that found benefits, but the authors explain the discrepancies: prior meta-analyses included institutionalized populations (Chapuy trial), did not adjust for cluster randomization, or did not exclude hormone therapy confounders in the WHI trial. The results are consistent with the newer, larger trials (ViDA, BEST-D) that showed neutral or harmful effects.
