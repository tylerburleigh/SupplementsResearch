# src-003: Zhang et al. (2019) BMJ Meta-Analysis -- Vitamin D Supplementation and Mortality

## Citation
Zhang Y, Fang F, Tang J, Jia L, Feng Y, Xu P, Faramand A. "Association between vitamin D supplementation and mortality: systematic review and meta-analysis." *BMJ* 2019;366:l4673. doi:10.1136/bmj.l4673

## Core Findings

1. **No association with all-cause mortality.** Across 50 RCTs totaling 74,655 participants with 7,993 all-cause deaths, vitamin D supplementation showed no significant effect on all-cause mortality (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.95-1.02, I^2=0%). GRADE quality of evidence: **high**.

2. **15% reduction in cancer mortality.** Vitamin D supplementation significantly reduced cancer death risk (RR 0.85, 95% CI 0.74-0.97, I^2=0%), based on 865 cancer deaths across included trials. This benefit was seen only with vitamin D3, not D2.

3. **No effect on cardiovascular mortality.** No significant differences for cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.98, 0.88-1.08), cerebrovascular disease mortality (RR 1.04, 0.84-1.29), or ischaemic heart disease mortality (RR 0.96, 0.81-1.15).

## Key Evidence and Data

| Outcome | Risk Ratio | 95% CI | I^2 | Deaths |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-cause mortality | 0.98 | 0.95-1.02 | 0% | 7,993 |
| Cancer mortality | 0.85 | 0.74-0.97 | 0% | 865 |
| Cardiovascular mortality | 0.98 | 0.88-1.08 | 0% | 1,331 |
| Non-cancer, non-CV mortality | 1.05 | 0.93-1.18 | 0% | 1,045 |

- **Vitamin D3 vs D2:** P for interaction = 0.04. D3 trended toward lower all-cause mortality (RR 0.95, 0.91-1.00, P=0.07) but did not reach statistical significance. D2 showed no signal.
- **Follow-up duration:** Meta-regression showed significantly lower all-cause mortality in trials with longer follow-up (P=0.04). Excluding trials with <1 year follow-up: RR 0.97 (0.93-1.00).
- **Trial sequential analysis:** Met optimal sample size for 10% and 7.5% relative risk reduction; futility not reached at 5% threshold.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs, PROSPERO registered (CRD42018117823), PRISMA compliant.
- **Search:** Medline, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL from inception to Dec 26, 2018. No language restrictions.
- **Inclusion:** Adults 18+, any health condition; vitamin D (any dose) vs placebo/no treatment; reported mortality.
- **Exclusion:** Vitamin D + calcium co-administration, hydroxylated vitamin D or analogues, pregnant/lactating women, critically ill patients, retracted trials.
- **Analysis:** Fixed-effects models (I^2 <50%); intention-to-treat. Cochrane risk of bias tool, GRADE assessment, trial sequential analysis, Egger/Begg/Harbord tests for small-study effects.
- **Risk of bias:** 20 trials low risk, 18 unclear, 12 high risk.
- **Recent trials:** 17 RCTs published after 2014 contributed 50.3% (37,568/74,655) of total participants.

## Differences from Prior Meta-Analyses

- **Cochrane 2014** (Bjelakovic, 56 trials, 95,286 participants): Found vitamin D reduced all-cause mortality (RR 0.97, P=0.02).
- **Bolland 2014** (40 trials, 81,173 participants): Found small effect (RR 0.96, P=0.04).
- This review excluded ~50,000 participants from vitamin D + calcium trials, 6 hydroxylated vitamin D/analogue trials, and 1 retracted trial -- which explains the more conservative conclusion compared to prior reviews.

## Limitations

1. **Selective reporting likely.** Most vitamin D trials did not report mortality; those that did may be a biased subset.
2. **Secondary outcome.** All-cause mortality was a secondary outcome in all included trials; data collection may differ from primary outcome collection.
3. **Control group contamination.** In VITAL, 42.5% of control participants used personal vitamin D supplementation (up to 800 IU/day), diluting treatment effects.
4. **Baseline sufficiency.** More than half of participants (50,466/66,546) had baseline 25(OH)D >50 nmol/L, potentially masking benefits in deficient populations.
5. **Heterogeneous dosing.** Varied regimens (daily, weekly, monthly, bolus) prevented determination of an effective daily dose.
6. **Subgroup analyses observational.** D3 vs D2 comparison is not based on randomized comparisons; results should be interpreted cautiously.

## Claims to Verify

- **Participant count discrepancy:** The abstract states "50 trials with a total of 74,655 participants" while the metadata/research brief mentions "52 RCTs, 75,454 participants." The paper itself consistently reports 50 trials and 74,655 participants. The research brief figures appear to be from a different source or an inaccurate citation. The paper's numbers are internally consistent.
- **Baseline 25(OH)D data:** Only 66,546 of 74,655 participants had baseline 25(OH)D reported (89.1%); the claim about >50% above 50 nmol/L applies to the subset with reported data.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Largest meta-analyses on D3 and all-cause mortality):** Directly relevant -- one of the largest meta-analyses (50 RCTs, 74,655 participants). Null finding contrasts with earlier Cochrane review.
- **Q2 (Evidence for D3 on CVD, cancer):** Strong evidence for null CVD effect; positive finding for cancer mortality reduction (15%).
- **Q4 (Effects by baseline deficiency status):** Important caveat -- majority of participants were not deficient at baseline (>50 nmol/L), limiting generalizability to deficient populations.
- **Q5 (D3 vs D2 differences):** Significant D3 vs D2 interaction for all-cause mortality (P=0.04); D3 showed cancer mortality benefit while D2 did not.
- **Q6 (Expert disagreement on routine supplementation):** Null all-cause mortality finding supports arguments against routine supplementation for mortality prevention.
- **Q7 (Evidence challenging popular supplementation narrative):** Cornerstone source. Despite enormous sample size, no mortality benefit found for vitamin D supplementation overall.
