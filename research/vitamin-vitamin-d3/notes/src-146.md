# src-146: Tripkovic et al. 2012 — Comparison of Vitamin D2 and D3 Supplementation (AJCN)

## Core Findings

This is the first systematic review and meta-analysis comparing vitamin D2 (ergocalciferol) and D3 (cholecalciferol) efficacy at raising serum 25(OH)D. The meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (out of 10 eligible studies, 1016 total participants) found that vitamin D3 is significantly more efficacious than D2 overall (weighted mean difference: 15.23 nmol/L, 95% CI: 6.12-24.34, P = 0.001). The superiority of D3 was strongly significant for bolus dosing (WMD: 34.10 nmol/L, P = 0.0002) but not statistically significant for daily supplementation (WMD: 4.83 nmol/L, P = 0.10).

## Key Evidence

### Primary meta-analysis (7 studies, random-effects model)
- Overall D3 vs D2: WMD 15.23 nmol/L (95% CI: 6.12, 24.34; Z = 3.28; P = 0.001)
- High heterogeneity: I-squared = 81%
- Could not perform meta-regression due to small number of studies

### Bolus dosing subgroup (3 studies)
- Doses: 50,000-300,000 IU, follow-up 4 weeks to 1 year
- D3 vs D2: WMD 34.10 nmol/L (95% CI: 16.38, 51.83; Z = 3.77; P = 0.0002)
- High heterogeneity: I-squared = 77%

### Daily dosing subgroup (6 studies)
- Doses: 1,000-4,000 IU/day
- D3 vs D2: WMD 4.83 nmol/L (95% CI: -0.98, 10.64; Z = 1.63; P = 0.10)
- Moderate heterogeneity: I-squared = 41%
- **Not statistically significant**

### Individual study highlights
- **Heaney et al (2011):** D3 was 87% more potent than D2 (weekly 50,000 IU for 12 weeks; P < 0.001; n = 33)
- **Armas et al (2004):** 28-day AUC significantly greater for D3 (P < 0.002; n = 30); D2 concentrations fell to baseline by day 14 while D3 remained elevated
- **Glendenning et al (2009):** D3 associated with 31% greater increase in 25(OH)D (P = 0.01; n = 70)
- **Holick et al (2008) and Biancuzzo et al (2010):** No significant difference between D2 and D3

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs
- **Search:** ISI Web of Knowledge (Jan 1966-Jul 2011), Cochrane Central Register, ISRCTN, clinicaltrials.gov
- **Inclusion:** Human adults, direct comparison of D2 vs D3, serum 25(OH)D as primary outcome
- **Analysis:** RevMan 5.1.4, random-effects model, weighted mean difference
- **Total participants:** 1,016 across 10 studies, aged 18-97 years
- **Geographic coverage:** US, Canada, UK, Australia, Denmark, Italy
- **Quality assessment:** Jadad score; only 3 of 10 studies were RCTs with double-blinding and placebo control

## Proposed Mechanism for D3 Superiority

The structural difference (D2 has an additional methyl group on carbon 24) affects:
1. **Hepatic 25-hydroxylation:** D3 is potentially the preferred substrate for hepatic 25-hydroxylase (CYP2R1)
2. **24-Hydroxylation (deactivation):** Once 1,24,25(OH)3D2 is formed, ergocalciferol is irreversibly deactivated. Cholecalciferol [1,24,25(OH)3D3] retains VDR-binding capacity and requires additional side-chain oxidation for deactivation, giving D3 a longer biological half-life
3. **Vitamin D binding protein affinity:** Differing affinities may affect bioavailability

## Limitations

1. **Small number of studies** (n = 10 total, 7 in meta-analysis) limits extrapolation
2. **High heterogeneity** (I-squared = 81%) due to diverse dosing strategies, frequencies, and routes of administration
3. **Underpowered individual studies** (sample sizes 19-89)
4. **Only 3 of 10 studies** were gold-standard (randomized, controlled, double-blind, parallel)
5. **All studies used doses above RDA** (600 IU), limiting applicability to typical supplementation levels
6. **Insufficient studies for publication bias assessment**
7. **Inconsistent 25(OH)D assay methods** across studies (HPLC, RIA, ELISA, chemiluminescence)
8. **No data by age, sex, or ethnicity** subgroups

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q5 (D3 vs D2):** This is a landmark source. Provides the first meta-analytic evidence that D3 is significantly more efficacious than D2 at raising 25(OH)D, with the effect most pronounced for bolus dosing. The daily dosing comparison was non-significant, which is an important nuance. The mechanistic discussion of 24-hydroxylation differences provides biological plausibility.
