# src-015: Vitamin D3 Supplementation and Cancer Mortality (Systematic Review & IPD Meta-Analysis)

**Source:** PMID 37004841 (PubMed abstract, full text behind Elsevier paywall)
**Type:** Systematic review and individual patient data (IPD) meta-analysis
**Year:** 2023
**Venue:** Elsevier journal (likely Ageing Research Reviews or similar based on NIH grant support)
**Quality Assessment:** Abstract only available (paywall); abstract contains rich quantitative data suitable for evidence extraction.

## Core Findings

Vitamin D3 supplementation did not significantly reduce cancer mortality in the primary analysis of 14 RCTs (104,727 participants, 2015 cancer deaths; RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.86-1.02). However, a significant subgroup interaction was found by dosing regimen: daily vitamin D3 reduced cancer mortality by 12% (RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.78-0.98) across 10 trials, while bolus dosing in 4 trials showed no benefit (RR 1.07, 95% CI 0.91-1.24; interaction p=0.042). Post-hoc analyses of daily-dosing trials suggested stronger benefits for adults aged 70+ and those initiating supplementation before cancer diagnosis.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

1. **Primary result:** 14 RCTs, 104,727 participants, 2015 cancer deaths. Non-significant 6% reduction (RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.86-1.02).
2. **Daily vs. bolus dosing:** 10 daily-dosing trials showed RR 0.88 (95% CI 0.78-0.98, significant); 4 bolus trials showed RR 1.07 (95% CI 0.91-1.24). Interaction p=0.042.
3. **IPD meta-analysis:** 7 RCTs covering 94,068 participants (90% of total). Confirmed main finding: RR 0.93 (95% CI 0.84-1.02).
4. **Age subgroup (post-hoc, daily only):** Adults 70+ years: RR 0.83 (95% CI 0.77-0.98).
5. **Timing subgroup (post-hoc, daily only):** Initiation before cancer diagnosis: RR 0.87 (95% CI 0.69-0.99).
6. **No effect modification** by age, sex, BMI, ethnicity, baseline 25(OH)D, adherence, or cancer-related factors in the overall analysis (all trials combined).
7. **Cancer-specific survival** in cancer patients was comparable to cancer mortality findings in the general population.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review with both aggregate meta-analysis and IPD meta-analysis.
- **Inclusion:** Randomised, placebo-controlled trials of vitamin D3 supplementation.
- **IPD availability:** 7 of 14 RCTs contributed IPD, covering 90% of all participants.
- **Subgroup analyses:** Duration, health status, region, dose, regimen, age, sex, BMI, ethnicity, baseline 25(OH)D, adherence, cancer-related factors.
- **Funding:** Multiple NIH grants (NHLBI, NCCIH, NCI, WHI) and UK MRC. No industry conflicts declared except one author (Julie E. Buring) with association to Pharmavite.

## Limitations

1. **Paywall restriction:** Full text unavailable; analysis based on abstract only. Cannot verify methods details, assess risk of bias, or examine supplementary tables.
2. **Sparse baseline 25(OH)D data:** Measurements were too infrequent across trials to draw conclusions about effect modification by baseline vitamin D status -- this is a critical gap for Q4 (effects by deficiency status).
3. **Ethnic diversity:** Inclusion of non-White participants was too sparse for conclusions.
4. **Post-hoc nature of daily-dosing age/timing subgroups:** These findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.
5. **Bolus vs. daily interaction:** While statistically significant (p=0.042), this emerged from subgroup analysis and should be confirmed prospectively.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (all-cause mortality meta-analyses):** Not directly relevant -- this study focuses exclusively on cancer mortality.
- **Q2 (cancer evidence):** Highly relevant. Largest IPD meta-analysis specifically on vitamin D3 and cancer mortality. Provides strong evidence that the dosing regimen matters critically (daily vs. bolus).
- **Q3 (optimal levels/doses):** Relevant to dosing regimen (daily vs. bolus) but does not address specific blood level targets.
- **Q4 (baseline deficiency):** Limited relevance. Baseline 25(OH)D measurements were too sparse for conclusions about effect modification by deficiency status.
- **Q5 (D3 vs D2):** Not addressed -- this study only examines vitamin D3.
- **Q6 (expert disagreement):** Relevant as evidence source for the debate. The null primary result supports skepticism about routine supplementation, while the daily-dosing subgroup supports targeted supplementation.
- **Q7 (challenging popular narrative):** Strongly relevant. The null primary finding challenges broad claims about vitamin D reducing cancer mortality. The dosing regimen distinction provides nuance.
