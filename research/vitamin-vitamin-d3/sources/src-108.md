Abstract

The saturation of aromatic scaffolds is valuable for the synthesis of complex rings. Herein, we demonstrate a process for photochemical dearomative reduction of quinolines. The process involves capture of a quinoline excited state with
γ
-terpinene. Importantly, the reaction is chemoselective as other easily reduced functionalities such as halogens or alkenes do not undergo reduction. The mechanism of the reaction has also been investigated. Finally, the generality of the approach towards other substrates is demonstrated.

The full or partial saturation of aromatic rings has received extensive investigation over the past several decades.
1
Common processes involve cycloaddition,
2
hydrogenation,
3

4
These reactions are important as widely available 2D aromatic compounds can be easily converted to 3D compounds. This fact, coupled with an increased interest in developing methods for the synthesis of complex saturated chemical structures, has fueled interest in this area.
5

Recently our lab, in collaboration with the Houk and Glorius groups, developed a dearomative cycloaddition reaction of quinolines and alkenes (
Scheme 1
).
6
−
8
Based on our previous work and Morofuji/Kano’s work, the reaction is proposed to operate by triplet energy transfer to a Lewis/Brønsted acid activated quinoline, followed by stepwise cycloadditions with an alkene.
6
The key intermediate in these reactions is proposed to be a triplet quinoline. Due to the novel reactivity toward alkenes, we questioned if other processes could be developed by intercepting the reactive intermediate with other trapping reagents. In particular, we were interested in capture of the excited state with H atom donors to result in the formation of reduced products.
9
Very recently, Ma demonstrated a related process that results in the reduction of the benzene ring of quinoline.
10
Herein, we demonstrate that photochemically induced hydrogenation of quinolines can be achieved with the use of widely available
γ
-terpinene. Furthermore, we demonstrate that photochemical hydrogenation with
γ-
terpinene is generalizable to two other classes of substrates.

Given that a photosensitzer can be used to promote the reaction, this suggests that a triplet state quinoline intermediate is involved (

Having demonstrated a photochemical reduction of quinoline using the concept of radical trapping using

Scope of Cyclopentenone and Imine Reduction
In conclusion, we developed a protocol for photochemical chemoselective reduction for quinolines using
γ
-terpinene. The reaction pathway is believed to be initiated by trapping of a radical intermediate in the presence of
γ
-terpinene through a stepwise HAT mechanism. Mechanistic experiments as well as a Rehm−Weller calculation have been performed to support the mechanism. The reduction is scalable using a continuous flow reactor, which shows the potential for broader applicability of the reaction. Finally, we demonstrate that the reaction is amenable to other classes of substrates.
