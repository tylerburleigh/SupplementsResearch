ABSTRACT
INTRODUCTION: Although a well-established compo -
nent of bone metabolism, the efficacy and safety of vita-
min D supplementation for the prevention of fractures 
in elderly healthy individuals is still unclear.
PURPOSE: To perform a meta-analysis comparing vita-
min D supplementation with placebo and its contribu -
tions on fracture incidence.
METHODS:  This meta-analysis was registered in the 
International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews 
(PROSPERO), under protocol CRD42023484979. 
We systematically searched PubMed, Embase, and 
Cochrane Central databases from inception to Novem -
ber 2023 for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) com -
paring vitamin D supplementation versus placebo in 
individuals with 60  years of age or more and without 
bone related medical conditions such as cancer and 
osteoporosis.
RESULTS:  Seven RCTs with 71,899 patients were 
included, of whom 36,822 (51.2%) were women. There 
was no significant difference in total fracture incidence 
(RR 1.03; 95% CI 0.93–1.14; p = 0.56; I2 = 58%) between 
groups or subgroups. However, women had an increased 
risk for hip fractures (164 vs. 121 events; RR 1.34; 95% 
CI 1.06–1.70; p = 0.01; I2 = 0%). There was no significant 
difference in non-vertebral fractures, osteoporotic frac-
tures development, or falls (RR 1.02; 95% CI 0.94–1.12; 
p = 0.6; I2 = 47%; RR 0.97; 95% CI 0.87–1.08; p = 0.63; 
I2 = 0%; RR 1.01; 95% CI 0.97–1.04; p = 0.66; I2 = 55%, 
respectively).
CONCLUSION:  Vitamin D supplementation does not 
reduce the total fracture development rate in the elderly 
healthy population, and it may increase the incidence of 
hip fractures among elderly healthy women. This finding 
suggests refraining from prescribing high intermittent 
doses of vitamin D, without calcium, to individuals aged 
60 or older with unknown vitamin D serum concentra -
tion or osteoporosis status and inadequate calcium 
intake.

INTRODUCTION
Vitamin D has a well-established role in bone health, as low 
concentrations are associated with impaired calcium absorp-
tion and a compensatory rise in parathyroid hormone (PTH), 
which results in excessive bone resorption. 1–4 Individuals 
with osteoporosis present low bone density, deterioration of 
bone tissue, disruptions of bone microarchitecture, and an 
increased risk for fractures.4 The association between hypo-
vitaminosis D and osteoporosis is well-studied as a risk fac-
tor for developing the condition.4
Routine assessments of 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D) 
levels are conducted in patients with osteoporosis, with the 
standard recommendation of vitamin D and calcium sup -
plementation as part of the treatment of osteoporosis. The 
recommendations of the Endocrine Society for prevention 
disease differ depending on age. In healthy individuals aged 
50 to 74 years, it suggests against routine 25(OH)D testing 
and vitamin D supplementation. However, for the popula -
tion aged 75 years and older, it suggests empiric vitamin D 
supplementation with 600 IU daily for individuals aged 50 
to 70 years, and 800 IU daily for those older than 70 years, 
for the potential decreased risk of mortality. 5,6 However, 
the impact of vitamin D supplementation alone in primary 
prevention of fracture in elderly individuals without osteo -
porosis and hypovitaminosis D is a subject of controversy. 
For instance, two previous meta-analyses observed positive 
outcomes with vitamin D supplements (≥  800 IU daily) in 
preventing hip and non-vertebral fractures in individuals 
aged 65 years and older, 7 as well as its combination with 
calcium demonstrated a reduction in overall fractures and 
hip fractures. 8 However, their conclusions may be influ -
enced by participants residing in institutional settings. 
Conversely, other published meta-analyses demonstrated a 
neutral impact of vitamin D supplementation and the inci -
dence of fractures. 9,10

Therefore, we sought to perform an updated systematic 
review and meta-analysis examining the impact of vitamin 
D supplementation alone, without calcium, compared to 
placebo on fracture outcomes in healthy participants aged 
60 years or more. We hypothesize that such supplementa -
tion does not prevent fractures in this population.
METHODS
This systematic review and meta-analysis were performed 
and reported in accordance with the Cochrane Collabora -
tion Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions 
and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews 
and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) Statement Guidelines. 11,12
Eligibility Criteria
Inclusion in this meta-analysis was restricted to studies that 
met all the following eligibility criteria: (1) randomized 
controlled trial; (2) comparing vitamin D in an average 
daily dose of 800 IU or higher with placebo (since that is 
the minimum dosage recommended for the main guidelines 
for that  population3,6); (3) enrolling patients with more than 
60 years old; (4) with follow-up with at least 12 months; and 
(5) included at least 500 participants, in order to minimize 
random error and publication bias. In addition, studies were 
included only if they reported fractures as the clinical out -
comes of interest. We excluded studies that had (1) calcium 
supplementation on the intervention; (2) participants living 
in nursing homes; and (3) participants with medical condi -
tions related with bone metabolism, such as cancer, osteopo-
rosis, nephrolithiasis, sarcoidosis, or parathyroid disorders.
Search Strategy and Data Extraction
We systematically searched PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane 
Central databases from inception to November 2023 with 
the main following search terms: “Vitamin D,” “supple -
mentation,” “elderly,” “fracture,” and “randomized trial.” 
Search strategies used in each databases are presented in 
the Appendix. The references of all included studies and 
previous meta-analyses were also searched manually for any 
additional studies. Study triage against eligibility criteria 
was performed by two authors (M.S and R.D.). Disagree -
ments were resolved through a consensus after discussing 
reasons for discrepancy.
Two authors (M.S. and V.D.) independently extracted the 
data following predefined search criteria and quality assess-
ment. Disagreements were resolved through a consensus 
after discussing reasons for discrepancy. The prospective 
meta-analysis protocol was registered in the International 
Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) 
on November  20th, 2023, under protocol CRD42023484979.
Endpoints and Subgroup Analyses
Outcomes included total fractures, hip fractures, osteoporo-
tic fractures, non-vertebral fractures, vertebral fractures, and 
falls.
Prespecified subgroups analyses included data stratified by 
(1) gender and (2) equivalent dose of vitamin D (≥ 1500 IU/
day and < 1500 IU/day).
Leave-one-out sensitivity analyses were performed in total 
fractures and hip fractures outcomes.
Sensitivity analyses were performed removing studies 
with a high risk of bias and removing the only trial that used 
daily vitamin D supplementation.
Quality Assessment
We evaluated the risk of bias for the included studies using ver-
sion 2 of the Cochrane Risk of Bias assessment tool (RoB 2).12 
Each trial received a score of high, low, or unclear risk of bias 
in five domains: selection, performance, detection, attrition, and 
reporting biases. Two independent authors completed the risk of 
bias assessment (M.S. and R.D.). Disagreements were resolved 
through a consensus after discussing reasons for discrepancy.
Small-study effect was investigated by funnel-plot analysis 
of point estimates in relation to study weights.
Statistical Analysis
Statistical analyses were performed according to the 
Cochrane Collaboration Handbook for Systematic Reviews 
of  Interventions12 and in line with the PRISMA statement.11 
Pooled risk ratio (RR) and hazard ratio (HR) with associated 
95% confidence intervals were used to compare treatment effects 
for binary endpoints with an intention-to-treat basis. Heterogene-
ity was evaluated with Cochran Q test and I2 statistics; p values 
inferior to 0.10 and I2 > 25% were considered significant for 
heterogeneity. We used DerSimonian and Laird random-effects 
model due to anticipated heterogeneity between studies in clini-
cal practice. Review Manager 5.4 (Nordic Cochrane Centre, The 
Cochrane Collaboration, Copenhagen, Denmark) was used for 
statistical analysis. R version 4.3 was used to perform funnel 
plots of study weights vs. point estimates and Egger’s test.

DISCUSSION
In this systematic review and meta-analysis of seven RCTs 
involving a total of 71,899 participants, we investigated the 
efficacy of vitamin D supplementation compared with pla -
cebo for fracture prevention in elderly healthy individuals. 
The main findings were as follows: (1) vitamin D increased 
the relative risk of hip fractures by 34% among women; (2) 
there was no significant difference between vitamin D or 
placebo and the incidence of total fractures, non-vertebral 
fractures, osteoporotic fractures, and falls for this population. 
To the best of our knowledge, our study stands out as the 
first to assess the consequences of vitamin D supplementa -
tion alone in a population without major diseases and only 
community-dwelling, and it is also the largest in terms of 
participant numbers ever conducted.
Previous meta-analyses have suggested that the benefits 
of vitamin D supplementation may be limited to individu -
als residing in institutional settings, as they are a popula -
tion more prone to develop osteoporosis and fractures due 
to factors such as immobility, reduced sun exposure, and 
poor diet. 7,8 In addition, several published meta-analyses 
have shown that calcium supplements in combination with 
vitamin D may have an important role in fracture preven -
tion.9,22, 23
From a pathophysiology perspective, when calcium 
intake is insufficient, a transient hypocalcemia occurs,

indicate an increased risk of hip fractures among women, 
although this finding did not reach statistical significance, 
likely due to reduced statistical power resulting from the 
exclusion of one of the largest trials.

CONCLUSION
Vitamin D supplementation, especially in high and inter -
mittent doses, and without adequate calcium intake, is 
not associated with fracture development prevention and 
might increase the incidence of hip fracture in older healthy 
women. This study provides evidence supporting the rec -
ommendation to refrain from prescribing high intermittent 
doses of vitamin D supplements, without calcium, to indi -
viduals aged 60 or older with unknown osteoporosis and 
inadequate calcium intake.
