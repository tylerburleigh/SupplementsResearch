Abstract

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide. Early detection of at-risk individuals is crucial for effective management. This systematic review aimed to evaluate the efficacy of advanced inflammatory markers, such as fibrinogen (FIB), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and galectin-3 (Gal-3), in predicting CVD outcomes, compared to traditional markers such as high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP). A systematic review was conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-analyses guidelines. Electronic databases, including PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar, were searched for relevant studies published between 2019 and 2023. Inclusion criteria were adults (≥18 years) with CVD; assessed the prediction of CVD using CRP, FIB, IL-6, and Gal-3 to achieve favorable cardiovascular outcomes; and randomized controlled trials, observational studies, or meta-analyses. Two independent reviewers performed data extraction and risk of bias assessment. From the initial 2181 records, seven studies were included, demonstrating the superior predictive power of advanced markers over traditional markers. IL-6 showed a stronger correlation with immediate cardiovascular events than hs-CRP. FIB levels provided additional insights into blood clotting, aiding in the prediction of cardiovascular events. Gal-3 has been identified as a crucial predictor of heart failure, outperforming the classical inflammatory markers. Advanced inflammatory markers, particularly IL-6, FIB, and Gal-3, exhibit higher predictive accuracy for CVD risk than traditional markers, such as hs-CRP. These markers enhance early detection and enable more targeted interventions, potentially improving patient outcomes and resource utilization in health care. Longitudinal and mechanistic research is expected to confirm the prognostic power of advanced inflammatory markers, and consequently, discover how biological innovations could be therapeutic targets.

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease (CVD), which is recognized as the world’s leading cause of mortality, costs more than 20.5 million lives in 2021.

Even with advancements in treatment and prevention, risk stratification and early intervention require new prediction indicators. Recent interest has focused on utilizing the inflammatory markers to predict CVD. This study examined the predictive validity of inflammatory markers in cardiovascular risk assessment through a systematic review. Although healthcare and public research has made considerable progress in treatment and prevention, early diagnosis of at-risk individuals remains a critical task for effective CVD management strategies. In recent years, there has been ongoing discussion on how inflammation contributes to CVD formation. Therefore, more interest has arisen in the benefits that inflammatory makers, such as biomarkers, offer for prediction. Traditional markers, including high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), a high-sensitivity parameter, have been extensively studied in most cases where CVD risk is assessed. Nevertheless, provisional investigations are already capable of revealing whether more advanced parameters of inflammation, such as fibrinogen (FIB), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and galectin-3 (Gal-3), can identify the additional risks of CVDs. These icons are markers associated with different stages of the inflammatory cascade that are known to contribute to CVDs. While some markers, including various inflammatory markers, have been explored for their predictive power in independent studies, there is a need for a broader compilation of clinical evidence, which should guide future practice. This systematic review endeavors to close this gap by assessing the efficacy of newly developed inflammatory markers in predicting cardiovascular risk, in contrast to the traditional ones used in the process. Novel routes, including inflammation and immunological dysregulation, as well as known risk factors such as smoking, dyslipidemia, and hypertension, contribute to CVD. In contrast, chronic inflammation, characterized by the high levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and acute-phase reactants, causes atherosclerosis, which is the primary cause of most CVD events.

Similarly, inflammation causes CVD through endothelial dysfunction because inflammatory mediators disrupt the vasoactive chemical equilibrium, decrease vasomotor function, and promote thrombosis. In contrast, inflammation destabilizes atherosclerotic plaques, making them more likely to rupture and thrombose, which could primarily lead to acute cardiovascular events, including myocardial infarction (MI) and stroke.

Despite abundant observational evidence linking inflammatory markers to CVD risk, their prognostic and therapeutic relevance is still debated, because some studies have linked elevated inflammatory markers to poor cardiovascular outcomes. However, others have found conflicting results or questioned whether adding them to risk-prediction algorithms will add value.

This systematic review fills this gap in the literature by assessing the methodological quality of relevant studies, investigating heterogeneity, and pooling estimates of the predictive accuracy of inflammatory markers for future cardiovascular events. Inflammatory markers are critical for predicting clinical states across a wide variety of disorders, from acute COVID-19 and liver cirrhosis to chronic CVD, as by reflecting pathophysiological processes, these biomarkers can reveal illness severity, progression, and medication response. Although numerous inflammatory biomarkers have been explored for their predictive capabilities in the literature, we specifically focused on select groups of advanced inflammatory markers such as FIB, IL-6, and Gal-3 due to their prominent roles in recent research and their potential to enhance clinical utility. These markers, which are integral to the inflammation cascade, have demonstrated superior predictive power in certain stages of CVD development compared with traditional markers such as hs-CRP. While the title of the article suggests a broader scope of biomarkers that contribute to inflammation-related pathophysiology, our systematic review was limited to these three advanced markers because of their established roles in predicting adverse cardiovascular events. Other relevant biomarkers were not included to maintain a clear focus on those with the strongest evidence for clinical utility. This approach allowed us to provide a more detailed analysis of how these markers can enhance early detection and improve CVD management strategies. The decision to limit the focus stems from practical considerations, such as the consistency and volume of data available for these specific markers as well as their clinical relevance. These markers have been well-studied in terms of their association with inflammation and CVD outcomes, providing a clearer framework for systematic analysis. Future studies may consider other emerging biomarkers, but for the purposes of this systematic review, IL-6, FIB, and Gal-3 were chosen to maintain methodological rigor, ensure meaningful conclusions, and highlight those most impactful in clinical practice today.

This systematic review aimed to analyze and compare the efficacy of advanced surrogate inflammatory markers, such as FIB, IL-6, and Gal-3, with traditional markers to predict CVD outcomes and risk. These markers are supposed to point to diverse inflammation engines and are more likely to be closely linked to pathological processes preceding CVD complications. They may provide a more precise prognostic framework than traditional markers such as hs-CRP. This review assessed the predictive accuracy of these markers, analyzed their clinical relevance in everyday healthcare, examined the methodological quality of recent studies, and explored their integration into existing CVD risk stratification models. By evaluating these advanced markers, this review sought to establish their potential to enhance early detection, reduce CVD susceptibility, and inform timely targeted interventions.

Methodology

Research design

This systematic review was written following the 2020 Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-analyses (PRISMA) checklist to conduct qualitative and quantitative analyses to identify the cardiovascular outcomes and predict CRP, FIB, IL-6, and Gal-3 in CVD. The authors opted for a qualitative systematic review instead of a meta-analysis because of the significant heterogeneity in the study designs, populations, and endpoints across the included studies. This qualitative approach allows for a comprehensive overview and synthesis of existing evidence, highlighting important insights and trends that might not be captured through a meta-analysis, thereby enriching the understanding of cardiovascular outcomes and prediction with advanced inflammatory markers. The protocol was created
a priori
and registered with the international prospective register of systematic reviews (PROSPERO CRD42024542335).

Eligibility criteria

Eligibility criteria included adults (≥18 years old), a previous diagnosis of CVD (irrespective of baseline severity), assessment of the prediction of CVD using CRP, FIB, IL-6, and Gal-3 to achieve favorable cardiovascular outcomes, and randomized controlled trials (RCTs), observational studies, or meta-analyses published between 2019 and 2024. Only studies published in English or peer-reviewed journals were considered. Eligible studies estimated the mortality records using advanced inflammatory markers involving more than 100 patients and rejected participants who were <100 and did not identify the mortality records. Studies that did not report the cardiovascular outcomes and those published outside the specified date range were excluded. The exclusion criteria were studies that fell beyond the specified time interval (before 2019 or after 2024), and CRP, FIB, IL-6, and Gal-3 studies unrelated to CVD prediction. In this systematic review, cases were defined as adults aged 18 years or older with a clinically or laboratory-confirmed diagnosis of CVD, including but not limited to coronary artery disease, MI, heart failure (HF), stroke, or peripheral artery disease. The selected studies evaluated the prediction of adverse cardiovascular outcomes through a combination of traditional (hs-CRP) and advanced (FIB, IL-6, and Gal-3) inflammatory biomarkers.

Source of data and search strategy

A systematic search of relevant articles was conducted using electronic databases, including Embase, PubMed, Google Scholar, Global Clinical Trials, Cochrane Library, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, and Medline. Clinical trials registered in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU Clinical Trials Registers, and the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform were manually searched for additional relevant RCTs. References and citations of published papers were also inspected for potential additional studies. In addition, search filters endorsed by the Cochrane Collaboration and in consultation with a librarian were used to identify the relevant RCTs. All retrieved papers were imported into EndNote 20, where the studies were initially deduplicated. Two investigators (MM and XBL) screened titles and abstracts, followed by a full-text review according to the aforementioned eligibility criteria. The open gray literature database was also analyzed to examine the gray literature. The open gray literature database was also analyzed to examine the gray literature.

Search strategies were formulated using the following keywords, their synonyms, abbreviations, and MeSH terms: (“cardiovascular diseases” OR “CVD”) AND (“Inflammatory Markers” OR “C-Reactive Protein” OR “Fibrinogen” OR “Interleukin-6” OR “Galectin-3”) AND (“Prognosis” OR “Prediction” OR “Outcome”). Cardiovascular-related diseases include coronary heart disease, MI, and HF.

Outcome

The primary outcomes of CVD have been identified, such as major adverse cardiovascular events and primary endpoints such as nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke, HF, peripheral artery disorder, and reduction in heart rate. Secondary outcomes included long-term and short-term tolerability and safety outcomes. Only data presented using the intention-to-treat principle were extracted for efficacy outcomes. Meanwhile, only the data presented using each study’s safety analysis set were extracted for safety outcomes.

Data extraction and risk of bias assessment

Data extraction and risk of bias (RoB) assessment were performed by at least two independent reviewers (MM and XBL). Before data extraction, a standardized Excel form was created to capture the following information: study characteristics (e.g., author, year of publication, and study design), inflammatory markers assessed (e.g., CRP and IL-6), CVD outcomes, and effect estimates. The RoB assessment was conducted using Cochrane’s RoB2 tool for RCTs and the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) [
Supplementary File
] for observational studies to ensure a comprehensive evaluation. The RoB2 tool graded studies as having “low risk,” “some concerns,” or “high risk” of bias, assessing the following components: (i) randomization process, (ii) deviations from intended interventions, (iii) missing outcome data, (iv) outcome measurement, (v) selection of the reported result, and (vi) overall RoB. NOS was used to assess observational studies, such as cohort studies, evaluating the aspects such as selection of participants, comparability of study groups, and outcome assessment. When there were discrepancies between the two reviewers, a third reviewer performed independent data extraction and RoB assessment and arbitrated the dispute.

Discussion

Determining inflammatory markers (FIB, IL-6, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, and Gal-3) subsequently facilitates the beneficial risk assessment and management of CVD. They function as crucial signs of pathophysiological processes. At the same time, these markers could help identify patients who are at risk in anticipation of better therapeutic approaches and prognoses. Our findings confirm the trend that many experts have stated: Focusing on inflammatory biomarkers is a valuable addition to routine clinical assessment of cardiovascular health.

In this systematic review, the results demonstrated the profound efficacy of new-generation inflammatory markers in augmenting accurate CVD risk prediction. Advanced inflammatory markers such as IL-6, FIB, and Gal-3 have a higher capability for predicting CVD than hs-CRP; consequently, using these markers in clinical practice can improve the possibility of more accurate and professional CVD management.

In addition, finding additional inflammatory markers other than CRP and FIB not only identifies new ways of doing things but also reveals the complicated relationship between inflammation and CVD, which should also be explored. Here, we discuss the use of indicators beyond hs-CRP, including IL-6, and Gal-3, and emerging markers of immune activation, endothelial dysfunction, and oxidative stress. IL-6 belongs to a class of pro-inflammatory cytokines and has diverse and exceptional functions in immunity and tissue repair.
[
8
]
A rise in the blood concentration of IL-6 has been found in people with CVD and has been linked to complications such as HF and MI. Some researchers have shown evidence that IL-6 signals are involved in CVD, implying that these signals could be targets for treatment. The anti-inflammatory effects and cardiovascular risk-reducing power of IL-6 inhibitors have clinched the deal in favor of additional research.

Gal-3, a class of β-galactosidebinding lectins, is undoubtedly a recently discovered biomarker for inflammation and fibrosis.
[
9
]
The data showed that the overexpression of Gal-3 was associated with deteriorated cardiac remodeling, ventricular dysfunction, and HF. Recent studies have begun to outline the mechanistic roles of Gal-3 in CVD development, including fibroblast activation and remodeling of the extracellular matrix. In addition, Gal-3 inhibitors and modulators have been shown to reduce cardiac fibrosis and enhance cardiac function. They represent potential therapeutic strategies for the treatment of CVD.

In addition to IL-6 and Gal-3, recent prognostic markers such as endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and the activation of immunity appear promising and can add to the risk assessment of CVD. Endothelial dysfunction, a specific condition of vasodilation defects and vascular permeability, is the beginning of atherosclerosis and CVD development. Some biomarkers, including endothelin-1, von Willebrand factor, and soluble intercellular adhesion molecule-1, have been identified and could be predictors of atherogenesis and atheroma formation, leading to CVD. Oxidative stress is a state of imbalance between reactive oxygen species production and antioxidant forces, which are significant determinants of CVD etiology.

Biomarkers of oxidative stress, including malondialdehyde, oxidized low-density lipoprotein, and isoprostanes, indicate increased oxidative damage to lipids, proteins, and DNA, which reflects a prognostic marker of CVD risk. Immune activation, often characterized by unrestrained type 1 immunity and chronic inflammation, influences atherosclerosis through the induction of plaque formation and susceptibility to disruption. Markers of inflammation such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and Monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 are examples of facilitators that showcase the inflammation that occurs, which provides insight into how CVDs arise and progress.
[
16
]
Employing these other inflammatory markers alongside existing comprehensive risk assessment algorithms helps enhance the accuracy of CVD risk prediction.
[
17
]
Moreover, this would facilitate the implementation of interventions for high-risk patients. Nevertheless, progress in this field includes the need for future investigations concerning clinical applications and clarification of pathophysiological pathways. By furthering our comprehension regarding inflammation as an underlying CVD mechanism, we can trickle down effective preventive measures, early detection, and management solutions to this health problem.

The current research is limited because most of the studies were carried out in different parts of the globe; therefore, one cannot generalize the findings. Similarly, research evidence indicates that adopting design studies is significant because they help to understand the value of these markers over many CVD stages. In addition, greater benchmarking across the board involving advanced and traditional markers in diverse cohorts should be performed to confirm the universal superiority of modern marker.
[
18
]

It is important to expand and conduct longitudinal cohort studies to pay more attention to temporal links such as cause and effect. Research involving different population types would enable the determination of validity markers for broader classification purposes.
[
19
]
In addition, it is necessary to pursue mechanistic studies to expand the knowledge of the underlying biological pathways that may affect CVD and to suggest new therapeutic targets.

Conclusion

The results of our systematic review provide strong evidence for the use of these advanced inflammatory markers to predict CVD risk, as they can outperform traditional markers, such as hs-CRP, regarding both specificity and effectiveness, as for the markers IL-6, FIB, and Gal-3 are, above all, associated with increased CVD risk. Specifically, they demonstrated their important role in early CVD detection. IL-6, especially, shows a strong positive correlation with cardiovascular events and is a promising therapy-related tool for evaluating the risk of such occurrences during the early stages. FIB is not only an inflammation medium; investigating its role in thrombogenesis offers new perspectives, and its GAL-3 connection with fibrosis opens new pathways for determining events that lead to HF. These advanced markers could become the latest game changers in CVD management by enabling more accurate early detection and allowing more precise and focused treatment options. In addition, it will result in better patient outcomes and more efficient use of resources within healthcare centers. Future research should continue to investigate the magnitude of these markers in more disadvantaged subpopulations for greater proof and clinical value. This ensures that newly emerging approaches are effective and reliable.
