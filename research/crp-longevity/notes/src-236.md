# src-236: Non-linear Mendelian randomization of vitamin D and C-Reactive Protein: an interrogation of methods

**Authors:** Leyden GM, Hamilton FW, Sanderson E, Davey Smith G
**Year:** 2025 (preprint, medRxiv, not peer-reviewed)
**DOI:** 10.1101/2025.09.10.25335520
**Quality:** ok -- full text available, substantive paper with methods, results, and discussion
**Affiliation:** MRC Integrative Epidemiology Unit, University of Bristol

## Core Findings

This paper is a **methodological critique** that re-examines the claim (from Zhou et al. 2023) of a non-linear causal effect of Vitamin D on CRP. Using UK Biobank data (N=336,839), the authors find **no evidence of a causal effect of Vitamin D on CRP** by any robust method. The apparent protective effect seen in prior non-linear MR analyses is attributed to bias in the stratification procedures, not a true causal relationship. The authors advocate for a pause in publishing non-linear MR results until bias-robust methods are developed.

## Key Evidence

### Overall (linear) MR: Null effect
- Standard 2SLS MR: Beta=-0.0002, SE=0.0005, P=0.72 (N=336,839 White British ancestry)
- F-statistic = 13,727 (strong instrument, no weak instrument bias concern)
- Vitamin D PRS explained ~3.9% of variance (21 variants at 4 loci: GC, DHCR7, CYP2R1, CYP24A1)
- CRP was log-transformed due to skewed distribution

### Non-linear MR (residual method): Biased apparent protective effect
- Showed protective effects in lowest strata (stratum 1: Beta=-0.008, P=0.001; stratum 2: Beta=-0.004, P=9.49e-9)
- Recapitulated Zhou et al. 2023 findings
- Attributed to violation of the "constant genetic effect" assumption, previously demonstrated for Vitamin D (Burgess 2023)

### Non-linear MR (doubly-ranked method): Null but inconsistent
- Supported null effect across strata
- Inconsistent estimates between strata suggest the method remains sensitive to bias

### Negative control evidence of bias
- Both non-linear MR methods showed non-null, stratum-specific effects of Vitamin D on **age** and **sex** -- outcomes Vitamin D cannot causally affect
- This proves bias is introduced by the stratification procedure itself

### Effect modification by ill-health
- Illness summary score showed strong interaction with Vitamin D PRS (interaction Beta=0.4, P=3.05e-3)
- Illness effect on Vitamin D: Beta=-0.043, P<1e-300
- Demonstrates that genetic effects on Vitamin D vary by health status, which is sufficient to induce bias in both non-linear MR methods

### Non-collider stratification (TDI): Null across all subgroups
- Stratified by TDI quartiles x season (N=38,757-44,818 per stratum)
- Null effects in ALL strata, including those with lowest Vitamin D
- Negative controls (age, sex) also null in all TDI strata -- no bias introduced by this stratification

### Assessment centre stratification: Null across all centres
- 20 centres, mean Vitamin D 39.2-56.8 nmol/L, N=1,613-31,090
- Null in all groups (Cochran's Q=8.1, df=19, P=0.99; meta-regression P=0.57)
- Further split by season: only one significant result (Edinburgh summer, P=0.004) but not replicated in Edinburgh winter (P=0.64) with lower mean Vitamin D and larger N

## Methodology

- **Design:** Individual-level MR analysis in UK Biobank, re-examining the Vitamin D-CRP relationship described by Zhou et al. 2023
- **Sample:** N=336,839 White British ancestry (from ~500,000 UKB participants); age 38-73; recruited 2006-2010
- **Instrument:** Focused PRS of 18 SNPs at 4 vitamin-D-related loci (GC, DHCR7, CYP2R1, CYP24A1); explained ~3.9% of variance
- **Covariates:** Sex, age, assessment centre, month of assessment, fasting time, genotype chip, first 10 genetic PCs
- **Non-linear MR methods:** Both residual and doubly-ranked methods via SUMnlmr R package, 10 strata
- **Sensitivity tests:** Negative controls (age, sex), non-collider stratification (TDI), assessment centre stratification with heterogeneity testing
- **Software:** ivreg R package (2SLS), SUMnlmr, PLINK v2.0, Metafor

## Methodological Concerns with Non-linear MR

1. **Constant genetic effect assumption (residual method):** Violated for Vitamin D -- genetic effects on Vitamin D levels differ across the distribution. This is sufficient to produce spurious non-linear findings.

2. **Rank preservation assumption (doubly-ranked method):** Weaker assumption but still violated in practice. Effect modification by unmeasured confounders (e.g., ill-health) induces unpredictable bias.

3. **Effect modification by ill-health:** Demonstrated empirically (interaction P=3.05e-3). Any variable that interacts with the genetic effect on the exposure can induce bias. Ill-health is just one of many plausible interactors.

4. **Negative controls reveal bias:** Non-null effects on age/sex prove stratification introduces bias regardless of method.

5. **Retractions of prior non-linear MR studies:** Two high-profile Vitamin D non-linear MR papers (Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol, Eur Heart J) were formally retracted due to spurious findings from these biases.

## Limitations

- Preprint (not peer-reviewed), though from a highly authoritative group (MRC IEU, Bristol)
- Focused on Vitamin D-CRP only; findings about non-linear MR bias may generalize to other exposure-outcome pairs
- Ill-health proxy is imperfect -- composite score of 7 health metrics rather than a direct measure
- Assessment centre analysis had reduced power in some sub-groups

## Relevance to Research Question

**Directly relevant to whether Vitamin D supplementation causally affects CRP.** The overall MR analysis finds no causal effect (Beta=-0.0002, P=0.72). Prior claims of a non-linear protective effect in deficient individuals are shown to be artifactual -- arising from bias in the non-linear MR methods rather than a true causal relationship. Every robust test (overall MR, non-collider stratification, assessment centre stratification, negative controls) converges on the null. This paper undermines the key prior study (Zhou et al. 2023) that had suggested a protective Vitamin D effect on CRP in deficient individuals.

## Claims to Verify

- N=336,839 stated as the analytical sample size. This is a derived number after exclusions from the White British subset (409,703). Reasonable but cannot be independently verified from this preprint alone.
- F-statistic of 13,727 is extremely large; this is plausible given the sample size and a PRS explaining 3.9% of variance, but worth noting it may reflect a combined F-statistic rather than a single-SNP statistic.
- The illness interaction Beta=0.4 with P=3.05e-3: the SE=0.135 is provided, and the z-score works out to ~2.96, consistent with the reported P-value.

## Key Takeaway

This paper argues strongly that there is **no causal effect of Vitamin D on CRP** -- the apparent non-linear protective effect seen in earlier studies (Zhou et al. 2023) is a methodological artifact. Vitamin D supplementation is unlikely to causally reduce CRP levels, even in deficient individuals. The non-linear MR methods themselves are unreliable in their current form.
