## Key Evidence and Data Points

### hs-CRP Assay and Biology
- hs-CRP detects CRP at low concentrations (0.1-10 mg/L) vs. standard CRP (>10 mg/L), enabling detection of chronic low-grade inflammation
- CRP is produced by hepatocytes in the liver, regulated primarily by IL-6 released from macrophages and adipocytes
- CRP binds dead/dying cells and some bacteria to activate the complement system

### Risk Thresholds (AHA/CDC guidelines referenced implicitly)
- Low risk: <1 mg/L
- Average risk: 1-3 mg/L
- High risk: >3 mg/L

### Disease Associations
- Cardiovascular disease: levels above 3 mg/L associated with higher heart attack/stroke risk
- JUPITER trial cited: hs-CRP above 2 mg/L linked to higher cardiovascular events even with normal cholesterol
- Diabetes: inflammation linked to insulin resistance
- Cancer: chronic inflammation linked to development/progression of some cancers
- Autoimmune disorders: elevated hs-CRP common in RA and lupus

### Longevity Connection
- "Inflammaging" concept: chronic low-grade inflammation accelerates aging
- JAMA study cited (no specific citation): older adults with lower hs-CRP had reduced 5-year mortality risk
- Lower hs-CRP levels associated with healthier aging and longer lifespan

### Case Studies (anecdotal)
- 50-year-old man: hs-CRP 4.5 mg/L dropped to 1.8 mg/L after lifestyle changes + statin therapy over 6 months
- 40-year-old woman with T2D: hs-CRP 5 mg/L dropped to 2 mg/L after low-carb diet + exercise over 1 year

### Lifestyle Interventions
- Weight loss of 5-10% body weight can lower hs-CRP
- Anti-inflammatory diet (omega-3s, fruits/vegetables, whole grains)
- 150 min/week moderate aerobic exercise + 2x/week strength training
- Stress management, quality sleep (7-9 hours), smoking cessation
- Supplements: omega-3 fish oil, curcumin, vitamin D
