**Citation:** Del Giudice, M., & Gangestad, S. W. (2018). Rethinking IL-6 and CRP: Why they are more than inflammatory biomarkers, and why it matters. *Brain, Behavior, and Immunity*, 70, 61-75. ~658 citations.

## Core Findings

The paper argues that IL-6 and CRP are routinely misinterpreted as unambiguous inflammatory markers in the behavioral sciences. In immunology, it is well-established that neither molecule is exclusively pro-inflammatory. The authors propose a "somatic maintenance" framework in which elevated IL-6 and CRP indicate investment in tissue protection, preservation, and repair -- not necessarily inflammation. This reframing has major implications for interpreting CRP's role in aging and longevity research.

## CRP Isoforms and Their Different Functions

### Pentameric CRP (pCRP)
- Secreted by the liver as five identical subunits arranged in a ring
- Highly soluble, diffuses freely in plasma
- Largely **anti-inflammatory** effects: induces M2 macrophage phenotype (tissue repair), recruits complement inhibitors
- Mainly involved in the **resolution phase** of inflammation
- Participates in wound healing, tissue repair, and clearance of damaged cells even in absence of inflammation
- **This is the isoform measured by standard CRP assays** used in virtually all behavioral and biomedical studies

### Monomeric CRP (mCRP)
- Produced when pCRP contacts cues of damage or inflammation (e.g., oxidized lipids from cell membranes) and the pentamer irreversibly dissociates
- Not soluble in plasma; remains localized in inflamed tissue
- **Highly pro-inflammatory**: stimulates pro-inflammatory cytokines, induces M1 macrophage phenotype, promotes reactive oxygen species release
- **Not detected by standard CRP assays** because it deposits on microparticles (membrane lipid vesicles)
- Later in inflammatory response, mCRP may help resolve inflammation by recruiting complement inhibitors

### Key implication
Moderate levels of circulating pCRP (the only isoform routinely measured) need not indicate chronic low-grade inflammation. pCRP is predominantly anti-inflammatory and repair-oriented.

## IL-6 Signaling Pathways

### Classic Signaling
- IL-6 binds **membrane-bound** IL-6R (found on limited cell types: hepatocytes, some epithelial cells, some leukocytes)
- The complex then binds gp130 to trigger signaling
- Bell-shaped dose-response curve (maximal at moderate, not extreme, IL-6 levels)
- Predominates in **non-inflammatory** states
- Effects: stimulates acute phase protein synthesis in liver (including CRP), promotes lipolysis, enhances insulin sensitivity, supports tissue regeneration
- Exercise-induced IL-6 (up to 100-fold increase) operates via classic signaling with no IL-1beta/TNF-alpha elevation

### Trans-Signaling
- IL-6 binds **soluble** IL-6R (sIL-6R), which is cleaved from cell membranes primarily by ADAM17 during inflammation
- The IL-6/sIL-6R complex can bind gp130 on nearly any cell type (gp130 is ubiquitously expressed)
- **Powerfully upregulated during inflammation** (triggered by IL-1beta, TNF-alpha, bacterial toxins, DAMPs)
- Mediates most deleterious health outcomes attributed to IL-6: heart dysfunction, cancers, Alzheimer's, brain degeneration, periodontal disease
- Contributes to sickness behavior and fever induction in the brain

### Key implication
When inflammation is absent, IL-6 still signals through classic pathways to regulate metabolism, tissue repair, and immune readiness. Elevated IL-6 without concurrent elevation of IL-1beta or TNF-alpha likely reflects non-inflammatory maintenance activity.

## CRP Has Net Anti-Inflammatory Effects

The paper presents multiple lines of evidence that CRP's net in vivo effect is **weakly anti-inflammatory**:

1. pCRP induces the anti-inflammatory M2 macrophage phenotype while activating complement cascade
2. pCRP recruits complement inhibition factors that limit indiscriminate tissue damage
3. CRP stimulates release of anti-inflammatory agents (IL-10, IL-1ra)
4. **Mendelian randomization studies**: Genetically elevated CRP levels fail to predict increased risk for cancer, cardiovascular disease, arthritis, psoriasis, or diabetes, despite observational associations between circulating CRP and disease rates
5. pCRP acts as a "sensor" of local inflammation but its own effects are predominantly anti-inflammatory; only upon conversion to mCRP (at sites of active damage) do pro-inflammatory effects emerge

## Somatic Maintenance Framework

The authors integrate immunological findings with life history theory to propose:

- **IL-6 and CRP are maintenance molecules**, not merely inflammatory markers
- Both participate in two overlapping aspects of somatic maintenance: immunity and tissue repair
- IL-6 promotes maintenance by mobilizing energy (lipolysis) and nutrients (proteolysis) toward repair processes
- IL-6 from adipose tissue functions as a **state-dependent allocation mechanism**: more stored fat leads to more IL-6, channeling energy toward ongoing maintenance
- IL-6 is functionally analogous to leptin (structurally related receptors, both indicate energetic status)
- Elevated IL-6 and CRP can indicate three distinct states:
  1. Acute inflammation (high IL-6, high IL-1beta/TNF-alpha, mCRP present)
  2. Chronic low-grade inflammation (moderate IL-6/CRP, elevated IL-1beta/TNF-alpha)
  3. **Upregulated tissue repair and immune readiness** (moderate IL-6/CRP, normal IL-1beta/TNF-alpha, pCRP only)

### Resistance vs. Tolerance
Within the maintenance framework:
- **Resistance** = pathogen limitation (inflammation, neutrophil attack, complement activation)
- **Tolerance** = damage limitation (negative feedback loops, tissue repair, antioxidant buffering)
- IL-6 and CRP participate in both: resistance early in inflammation, tolerance during resolution
- This dual role explains their mixed pro- and anti-inflammatory effects

## Methodological Details

- Review/synthesis paper, not an empirical study
- Draws on ~200 references from immunology, behavioral science, and life history theory
- Illustrates implications with three empirical domains: socioeconomic status, stress, and depression

## Limitations

- The somatic maintenance framework is presented as a theoretical proposal; direct experimental tests are needed
- Evidence from traditional populations on CRP patterns is mixed and based on small samples
- Some interpretations of SES and depression data are acknowledged as speculative
- The paper does not provide specific cutoffs or algorithms for distinguishing inflammatory vs. maintenance states from biomarker profiles

## Relevance to CRP and Longevity Research

This paper is highly relevant. It challenges the foundational assumption in much epidemiological longevity research that elevated CRP = chronic inflammation = pathological. The Mendelian randomization evidence that genetically elevated CRP does not cause disease is particularly important. If moderate CRP elevation reflects somatic maintenance (tissue repair, immune readiness) rather than damaging inflammation, the causal pathway from CRP to mortality is far more complex than typically assumed. The paper suggests that CRP elevation in some contexts may be protective rather than harmful, and that pharmacologically reducing CRP could worsen outcomes in individuals whose elevated CRP reflects repair activity rather than inflammation.
