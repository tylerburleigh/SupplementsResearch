# src-082: Interleukin-6 and C-reactive protein, successful aging, and mortality: the PolSenior study

**Citation:** Puzianowska-Kuznicka et al. (2016), *Immunity & Ageing*, 13:21. DOI: 10.1186/s12979-016-0076-x
**Citations:** 377 | **Quality:** ok | **Type:** Open Access, peer-reviewed

## Core Findings

1. Both IL-6 and CRP increase with age in elderly Eastern Europeans, including those aging "successfully" (free of major chronic diseases with preserved cognitive and physical function).
2. A single measurement of IL-6 or CRP is a significant predictor of all-cause mortality, even in successfully aging individuals who are free of CVD, diabetes, cancer, and cognitive/functional impairment -- and this association persists after multivariate adjustment.
3. The mortality-predictive effect of IL-6 is notably *stronger* in successfully aging individuals (unadjusted HR=1.163 per pg/mL) than in those with aging-related diseases (HR=1.063 per pg/mL), suggesting inflammation is an independent risk pathway even absent overt disease.

## Study Design

- **Cohort:** PolSenior study, 4,979 individuals aged 65+ recruited via stratified random draw from urban and rural municipalities in Poland (Eastern Europe). Response rate 42.6%.
- **Blood samples:** 4,101 provided blood; after excluding leukocytosis >10,000/uL and glucocorticoid users: IL-6 analyzed in n=3,496, CRP in n=3,632.
- **Follow-up:** Mean 4.3 years; 1,734 total deaths (1,318 among blood donors). Total person-years: 14,535.
- **Successful aging definition:** Never diagnosed with CVD, MI, stroke, T2D, or cancer; MMSE >=24; Katz ADL >=5. This subgroup comprised 35.4% of the study population (mean age 76.3 +/- 7.9 years).
- **IL-6 measurement:** ELISA (R&D Systems, sensitivity 0.04 pg/mL).
- **CRP measurement:** High-sensitivity immunoturbidimetric (Roche, sensitivity 0.11 mg/L).
- **Survival analysis:** Cox proportional hazards with time-dependent covariates (age and BMI violated proportional hazards assumption). Covariates: age, gender, BMI, HDL, LDL (IL-6 model only), eGFR, smoking status.

## Key Evidence

### Age-related increases in IL-6 and CRP
- IL-6 increased with age across all groups: entire cohort (p<0.001), successfully aging (p<0.001), diseased (p<0.001).
- CRP increased with age in the entire cohort (p=0.003) and successfully aging (p<0.001), but NOT in the diseased subgroup (p=0.249).
- Median IL-6: successfully aging 2.0 pg/mL vs. diseased 2.5 pg/mL (p<0.001).
- Median CRP: successfully aging 2.1 mg/L vs. diseased 2.5 mg/L (p<0.001).
- Difference between groups was only ~20%, suggesting phenotype arises from balance of pro-/anti-inflammatory factors, not absolute levels alone.

### IL-6 and CRP as predictors of mortality

**Univariate (unadjusted) hazard ratios:**

| Group | IL-6 HR per pg/mL (95% CI) | CRP HR per mg/L (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Entire cohort | 1.077 (1.068-1.086) | 1.025 (1.020-1.029) |
| Successfully aging | 1.163 (1.128-1.199) | 1.074 (1.047-1.100) |
| Diseased/disabled | 1.063 (1.052-1.074) | 1.020 (1.015-1.025) |

**Multivariate (adjusted) hazard ratios:**

| Group | IL-6 adj HR per pg/mL (95% CI) | CRP adj HR per mg/L (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Entire cohort | 1.042 (1.029-1.055) | 1.017 (1.012-1.023) |
| Successfully aging | 1.112 (1.069-1.155) | 1.035 (1.021-1.050) |
| Diseased/disabled | 1.031 (1.018-1.045) | 1.013 (1.007-1.019) |

All p-values < 0.001. The HR is per unit increase in raw concentration.

### 3-year mortality in successfully aging subgroup (dose-response)
- By IL-6 quartile: 2.9%, 6.9%, 14.0%, 25.3% (Q1 through Q4) -- roughly a 9-fold gradient.
- By CRP category: 7.3% (<=3 mg/L), 13.2% (3.1-10 mg/L), 27.2% (>10 mg/L) -- roughly a 4-fold gradient.

### Association with physical and cognitive performance
- Higher IL-6 and CRP associated with lower ADL and lower MMSE scores (all p<0.001 for entire group and both subgroups).
- After adjusting for age, gender, BMI, lipids, eGFR, smoking: ADL and MMSE remained significantly associated with IL-6 (p=0.006 and p=0.007) and CRP (p=0.019 and p<0.001) in the entire group.

### Other notable findings
- Low-dose aspirin did not affect IL-6 or CRP levels (IL-6 p=0.22, CRP p=0.73).
- IL-6 showed a U-shaped relationship with BMI (highest in underweight and obese).
- IL-6 negatively associated with total cholesterol, HDL, and LDL (all p<0.001).
- IL-6 and CRP were correlated (r=0.502-0.538 across subgroups).
- CRP did not increase with age in the diseased subgroup (p=0.249), possibly due to ceiling effect or liver function impairment.

## Limitations

1. **Single time-point measurement** of IL-6 and CRP; cannot establish causality or assess longitudinal trajectories.
2. **Not all participants provided blood** (4,101 of 4,979), though authors argue no systematic selection bias.
3. **No data on morbidity after baseline** -- cannot determine if mortality in successfully aging individuals was mediated by incident disease.
4. **HR per unit increase** can be hard to interpret clinically; the raw HR (e.g., 1.163 per pg/mL for IL-6) reflects the scale of measurement.
5. **Successful aging definition** excluded only major conditions; other conditions (e.g., arthritis, COPD) were not exclusion criteria.
6. **Population** was exclusively Eastern European Caucasian -- generalizability may be limited.
7. **Cox model**: age and BMI violated proportional hazards, requiring time-dependent covariates adjustment.

## Relevance to Research Question

This study provides strong, directly relevant evidence that:
- CRP and IL-6 are elevated in aging populations in an age-dependent manner, even in disease-free individuals.
- Both markers predict mortality in successfully aging individuals independently of traditional risk factors.
- The association is dose-responsive and robust across multiple analytical approaches.
- The effect sizes are notably *larger* in the successfully aging subgroup than in diseased individuals, suggesting inflammation may be a particularly informative marker in apparently healthy elderly.

## Claims to Verify

- **3-year mortality rates for successfully aging subgroup by IL-6 quartile**: The 2.9% to 25.3% gradient is stated in the Discussion but the raw figures should be cross-checked with the Kaplan-Meier curves, which report 1-year rates in figure legends. The 3-year figures appear reliable as they are explicitly stated in the text.
- **Successful aging subgroup size**: 35.4% of 3,750 = ~1,328; reported as n=1,258 for IL-6 and n=1,312 for CRP. Slight discrepancy likely reflects the subset with blood data passing quality filters.
