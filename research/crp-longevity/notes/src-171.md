# src-171: EVOO vs Refined Olive Oil on Lipid Profile and Inflammatory Biomarkers in Coronary Angiography Patients

**Citation:** Khandouzi N, Zahedmehr A, Nasrollahzadeh J. (2020). Effect of extra-virgin olive oil on lipid profile and inflammatory biomarkers in patients undergoing coronary angiography: a randomized, controlled, clinical trial. *Research Square preprint*. DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-31446/v1

**Quality:** ok (full text available from PDF extraction, 22 pages)

---

## Core Findings

Daily consumption of 25 mL polyphenol-rich extra-virgin olive oil (EVOO, 500 ppm phenolics) for 6 weeks significantly reduced plasma CRP by 24% compared to refined olive oil (ROO, <10 ppm phenolics) in coronary angiography patients already on statin therapy. EVOO also lowered LDL-C and increased anti-inflammatory IL-10 production, but did not affect IL-6, MDA, or other lipid ratios (TC/HDL-C, sd-LDL-C). The study did not track clinical outcomes -- CRP changes were not correlated with any cardiovascular events or mortality.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, controlled, parallel-arm clinical trial
- **Registration:** NCT03796780 (clinicaltrials.gov)
- **Population:** 40 patients (38 male) undergoing coronary angiography at Shahid Rajaei Cardiovascular Center, Tehran; mean age 54.83 years; all had at least one CV risk factor (hypertension, dyslipidemia, or diabetes)
- **Intervention:** 25 mL/day EVOO (500 ppm total phenolics) vs. ROO (<10 ppm) consumed raw with meals for 6 weeks
- **Randomization:** Stratified by BMI (normal/overweight) and statin use; computer-generated allocation
- **Blinding:** Not explicitly described (likely open-label given raw oil consumption)
- **Follow-up:** Weekly telephone check-ins; dietary recall at baseline and 6 weeks
- **Concomitant meds:** 95% on statins in both groups; majority on aspirin, clopidogrel, beta-blockers

## Key Results

### CRP (primary inflammatory biomarker)
- Baseline CRP: EVOO 1.65 mg/L (95% CI: 1.01-2.28) vs ROO 1.70 mg/L (0.98-2.42)
- 6-week CRP: EVOO 1.24 mg/L (0.72-1.76) vs ROO 1.70 mg/L (1.00-2.39)
- Change: EVOO -0.40 mg/L vs ROO +0.007 mg/L, **p = 0.01** (statistical power = 0.76)
- 24% relative reduction in CRP with EVOO vs ROO

### Lipids
- LDL-C: EVOO -9.52 mg/dL vs ROO +8.68 mg/dL, **p = 0.007** (power = 0.81)
- TC: 6% reduction with EVOO, **p = 0.007**
- No significant changes in HDL-C, TC/HDL-C, LDL-C/HDL-C, sd-LDL-C, or TG

### Cytokines (ex-vivo LPS-stimulated whole blood)
- IL-10: EVOO +12.13 pg/mL vs ROO -17.47 pg/mL, **p = 0.035** (power = 1.00)
- IL-6: No significant between-group difference
- MDA: No significant between-group difference (though within-group decrease in EVOO)

## CRP and Clinical Outcomes

**The study did not track any clinical outcomes** (no cardiovascular events, no mortality, no angiographic changes). CRP was measured solely as a biomarker endpoint. The authors interpret CRP reduction as evidence of "anti-inflammatory effects" but provide no data linking CRP changes to clinical benefit. The short 6-week duration and small sample make clinical event tracking infeasible.

## Methodology Notes

- CRP measured by turbidometric immunoassay (not hs-CRP specifically noted)
- Sample size powered for IL-6 change (primary outcome), not CRP; 18 subjects calculated for 80% power to detect 15% decrease in IL-6, inflated to 20/group for 10% attrition
- Randomization stratified by BMI and statin use -- good design choice given high statin use
- Both groups increased MUFA intake similarly; difference attributed to polyphenol content
- Parallel design chosen over crossover to avoid seasonal confounding of inflammatory biomarkers

## Limitations

1. **Very small sample** (n=20/group) -- limited power for subtle effects; CRP result had power of only 0.76
2. **Short duration** (6 weeks) -- cannot assess maintenance or clinical impact
3. **Open-label** -- no blinding described; participants knew which oil they consumed
4. **Predominantly male** (90-100%) -- limited generalizability to women
5. **No clinical endpoints** -- biomarker-only study; CRP reduction not linked to outcomes
6. **Preprint** -- published as Research Square preprint; peer review status uncertain
7. **Baseline imbalance in smoking** (10% EVOO vs 40% ROO, p=0.06) -- not adjusted for in analysis

## Relevance to Research Question

This study provides direct evidence that dietary EVOO (polyphenol-rich olive oil) can reduce CRP in a cardiovascular disease population over 6 weeks. However, it does not address whether CRP reduction translates to improved clinical outcomes or longevity. The small sample, short duration, and lack of clinical endpoints limit its contribution to questions about CRP as a causal risk factor vs. a bystander biomarker. The finding that CRP decreased while IL-6 did not change is noteworthy -- it suggests the CRP-lowering mechanism may not operate through classical IL-6 pathways.

## Claims to Verify

- CRP baseline values appear imbalanced: EVOO 1.65 vs ROO 1.70 mg/L -- the abstract states -0.40 +/- 0.52 vs 0.007 +/- 0.42; these SDs are large relative to the means, suggesting substantial individual variation.
- The 24% CRP reduction claim (line 166) -- calculated from mean changes, but the large SDs mean individual responses were highly variable.
- The TC values in Table 5 appear anomalous: ROO baseline TC 114.42 mg/dL seems very low for a CVD population, though 95% were on statins.
