## Core Findings

This Johns Hopkins Medicine page provides clinical guidance on using CRP (specifically hs-CRP) for cardiovascular risk assessment. It establishes three risk tiers based on CRP levels and emphasizes that CRP testing is most valuable for patients at **intermediate** cardiovascular risk, where it can inform treatment decisions. The page clarifies that CRP is a marker (not a causal driver) of atherosclerosis-related inflammation, and that confounders like age, smoking, and obesity can elevate hs-CRP independently.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

1. **CRP Risk Thresholds:**
   - Low risk: < 1 mg/L
   - Intermediate risk: 1-3 mg/L
   - High risk: >= 3 mg/L
   (Source line 32)

2. **Risk Group Utility:**
   - **Low-risk patients** (low cholesterol, minimal risk factors): Even elevated CRP unlikely to change management beyond lifestyle modifications.
   - **Intermediate-risk patients**: CRP testing most useful -- can provide "missing piece of information" for treatment decisions.
   - **High-risk patients**: CRP testing not essential; aggressive treatment already warranted.
   (Source lines 15-29)

3. **Statins lower CRP**, but the value of statin therapy for low-risk patients with borderline-high CRP is debated and under further study. (Source lines 43-45)

4. **CRP is not causal**: No evidence CRP molecule itself drives atherosclerosis; it signals low-grade vessel wall inflammation. (Source lines 38-39)

5. **Confounding factors**: hs-CRP rises with aging, smoking, and obesity, meaning elevated CRP may reflect these factors rather than additional independent cardiovascular risk. (Source lines 36-37)
