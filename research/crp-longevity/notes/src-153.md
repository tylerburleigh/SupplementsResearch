# src-153: Immunotherapy for Cardiovascular Disease

**Citation:** Lutgens E, Atzler D, Doring Y, Duchene J, Steffens S, Weber C. "Immunotherapy for cardiovascular disease." *European Heart Journal* 40(48):3937-3946, 2019. doi:10.1093/eurheartj/ehz283. 180 citations.

**Quality:** ok -- full text available with substantive content (methods, results, discussion, figures, references).

## Core Findings

This review comprehensively evaluates the state of immunotherapy for cardiovascular disease following the landmark CANTOS trial. CANTOS proved the inflammatory hypothesis of atherosclerosis by showing that anti-IL-1beta therapy (canakinumab) reduces cardiovascular events in patients with residual inflammation (hs-CRP >2 mg/dL). However, canakinumab did not improve overall or cardiovascular mortality and increased fatal infections. The authors argue that next-generation immunotherapeutics must be more specific to atherosclerosis-relevant pathways to avoid broad immunosuppression. They identify four promising target classes: chemokines and their receptors, immune checkpoints, immunometabolic pathways, and hormonal/lipid mediators.

## CANTOS Outcomes and Limitations

### Positive results
- Canakinumab reduced major cardiovascular events (MI, stroke, CV death) at one dose
- Greatest risk reduction in patients with largest reductions in IL-6 and hs-CRP
- Benefits were independent of LDL changes
- Sub-study showed reduced hospitalization for heart failure and HF-related mortality
- Provided proof-of-principle that inhibiting inflammation prevents atherosclerosis-related events

### Limitations
- Did not improve overall or cardiovascular mortality
- Caused leucopenia and a slight but significant increase in fatal infections
- Effective at only one dose level
- Novartis decided not to pursue canakinumab for CVD (shifted to oncology), reflecting unfavorable cost-efficacy and safety ratio

### CIRT trial contrast
- Low-dose methotrexate (broad-spectrum anti-inflammatory) failed to reduce CVD events or lower CRP/IL-1beta/IL-6
- Underscores need for specific pathway targeting rather than broad immunosuppression

## Novel Anti-inflammatory Targets Beyond Canakinumab

### 1. Chemokines and their receptors
- CCL2/CCR2 axis: CCR2-blocking antibody MLN1202 reduced CRP in phase 2 trial
- CCL5-CXCL4 heterodimers: Disruption with peptide MKEY reduced atherosclerosis and preserved heart function in mice
- CCR5: Maraviroc (FDA-approved HIV drug) reduced plaque burden in mice
- CXCR4-CXCL12: Complex role; CXCR4 blockade can worsen atherosclerosis via leucocytosis
- Promising approach: targeting chemokine heterodimer formation preserves normal immune function

### 2. Immune checkpoints
- CD28-CD80/86 axis: CTLA4-Ig fusion protein abatacept reduced atherosclerosis in mice; CD80 as imaging marker for vulnerable plaques
- CD40-CD40L-TRAF6 axis: Small molecule inhibitors (TRAF-STOPs, e.g., 6877002) selectively block CD40-TRAF6 without affecting CD40-TRAF2/3/5, preserving immune function while reducing atherosclerosis
- Nanomedicinal delivery: rHDL nanoparticles loaded with TRAF-STOPs targeted macrophages and reduced plaque formation in mice; safe in non-human primates

### 3. Immunometabolic pathways
- Glycolysis inhibition in macrophages reduces IL-6 and IL-1beta production
- Promoting fatty acid oxidation (via fibrates, thiazolidinediones) shifts macrophages toward anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype
- Tryptophan/arginine metabolism: IDO1 deficiency reduced plaque size; kynurenine promotes Treg differentiation
- Metabolic radiotracers for diagnostic imaging of vulnerable plaques

### 4. Hormones and lipid mediators
- Melanocortin receptor 1 (McR1) on plaque macrophages: regulates cholesterol efflux via ABCA1/ABCG1; McR1 deficiency worsened atherosclerosis in mice
- Endocannabinoid system: CB1 blockade improves atherogenic dyslipidemia; peripheral CB1 in vasculature promotes inflammation
- Neuroendocrine pathways: hypothalamic control of hepatic lipid metabolism via McR3/McR4 signaling
- Novel poly-agonists integrating gut hormones and steroids for hypothalamic targets

## Methodology

This is a comprehensive narrative review published in the European Heart Journal. It synthesizes evidence from CANTOS, CIRT, and other clinical trials alongside extensive preclinical data from mouse models of atherosclerosis (ApoE-/-, LDLR-/-). The authors are affiliated with Ludwig-Maximilians-University Munich and Amsterdam UMC, and lead the DFG CRC 1123 collaborative research center on atherosclerosis mechanisms.

## Limitations

- Review article; no original data presented
- Most novel therapeutic targets discussed have only preclinical (mouse) evidence
- Several proposed targets (e.g., CXCR4, IDO1) have conflicting or controversial evidence
- Authors disclose conflict of interest as shareholders of Cartesio Therapeutics Inc.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **CRP/inflammation and CVD outcomes:** Provides detailed analysis of the IL-1beta-IL-6-CRP axis as confirmed by CANTOS, including hs-CRP >2 mg/dL threshold and correlation between CRP reduction and event reduction
- **Therapeutic targets for cardiovascular inflammation:** Most directly relevant -- catalogs next-generation targets beyond canakinumab, with evidence levels for each
- **Inflammation and longevity:** Indirectly relevant through the lens of CVD mortality reduction and the failure of canakinumab to improve overall mortality

## Claims to Verify

- hs-CRP threshold stated as >2 mg/dL (line 107); should verify whether CANTOS used mg/dL or mg/L (CANTOS typically uses mg/L, so this may be a unit error in the review)
- "slight but significant increase in fatal infections" -- the magnitude and statistical significance should be cross-referenced with the primary CANTOS publication
