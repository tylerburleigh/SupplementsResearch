# src-126: High-sensitivity C-reactive Protein in ASCVD: To Measure or Not to Measure?

**Citation:** Mehta A, Blumenthal RS, Gluckman TJ, Feldman DI, Kohli P. US Cardiol. 2025;19:e06. doi:10.15420/usc.2024.25. PMCID: PMC11959579.

**Type:** Narrative review, cardiology journal (open access, CC BY-NC 4.0)

**Quality assessment:** Full-text available, substantive content with methods/data summaries from multiple landmark trials. Peer-reviewed. Quality is adequate for citation.

---

## Core Findings

This 2025 review from Johns Hopkins-affiliated authors comprehensively argues for hs-CRP measurement in both primary and secondary ASCVD prevention as a tool to identify "residual inflammatory risk" that persists after lipid-lowering therapy. The authors conclude that hsCRP >=2 mg/L is a validated risk enhancer endorsed by ACC/AHA 2019 primary prevention guidelines (class 2a, LOE: b-nr), while acknowledging that no formal guideline recommendation exists for secondary prevention. They recommend a tiered approach: first target inflammation upstream via lifestyle modification (weight loss, exercise, dietary changes, smoking cessation), and only then consider anti-inflammatory pharmacotherapy (colchicine) in select patients with residual inflammatory risk.

---

## Guideline Positions on hs-CRP

### Primary Prevention
- **ACC/AHA 2019 Guidelines (class 2a, LOE: b-nr):** hsCRP should be considered as a risk enhancer in intermediate-risk adults (10-year ASCVD risk >=7.5% to <20%). hsCRP >=2 mg/L supports statin initiation or dose escalation.
- **Reynolds Risk Score:** Incorporates hsCRP for improved risk stratification, particularly in intermediate-risk individuals.
- **Context vs. CAC:** MESA-JUPITER analysis showed CAC scoring provides superior risk reclassification vs. hsCRP alone. hsCRP was not associated with CHD/CVD after multivariable adjustment in the multiethnic MESA cohort. CAC advantages: direct measurement of disease; more stable across ethnicities. hsCRP advantages: lower cost, no radiation.

### Secondary Prevention
- **No formal guideline recommendation** for hsCRP measurement in secondary prevention. The authors argue for an individualized approach.
- Suggested use: identify residual inflammatory risk in patients with controlled LDL-C who continue to have recurrent CV events.
- **Residual inflammatory risk** defined as hsCRP >=2 mg/L with LDL-C <70 mg/dL.

---

## Key Evidence (Arguments FOR hs-CRP Testing)

1. **Physicians' Health Study** (n=1,086 healthy men): hsCRP >=2.11 mg/L associated with ~3x MI risk (p<0.001) and ~2x stroke risk (p=0.02) vs. lowest quartile, independent of traditional risk factors.

2. **Women's Health Study** (n=27,939, ~8-year follow-up): hsCRP was a stronger predictor of CV events than LDL-C, with 1.5x greater risk per quintile increase vs. comparable LDL-C increase.

3. **REGARDS study** (n=6,136): Low hsCRP strongly associated with reduced CHD, CHD death, and stroke risk. Low LDL-C did not confer as much protective benefit as expected.

4. **JUPITER trial** (n=17,802): Rosuvastatin 20 mg in patients with LDL-C <130 mg/dL and hsCRP >=2 mg/L reduced primary endpoint by 44% (HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.46-0.69, p<0.00001). hsCRP reduced 37% and LDL-C reduced 50% at 12 months. Trial stopped early after median 1.9 years.

5. **Collaborative analysis** (n=31,245 on statins): Highest vs. lowest hsCRP quartile: HR 1.31 for MACE (95% CI 1.20-1.43), HR 2.68 for CV mortality (95% CI 2.22-3.23), HR 2.42 for all-cause mortality (95% CI 2.12-2.77).

6. **CANTOS trial** (n=10,061 post-MI, hsCRP >=2 mg/L): Canakinumab (anti-IL-1beta) 150 mg: HR 0.85 for recurrent vascular events (95% CI 0.74-0.98, p=0.021). Greatest benefit in those achieving hsCRP <2 mg/L. Proved that targeting inflammation independently of lipids reduces CV events. Not FDA-approved for ASCVD due to fatal infection risk.

7. **PROVE-IT and IMPROVE-IT**: ~1/3 of ASCVD patients with hsCRP >=2 mg/L and LDL-C <70 mg/dL had increased recurrent event risk (3.1 per 100 person-years and 33.7%, respectively).

8. **FOURIER and SPIRE-1/SPIRE-2**: Residual inflammatory risk persisted even with statin + PCSK9 inhibitor. 13% relative MACE increase per doubling of hsCRP after adjustment for LDL-C. 62% relative risk increase with hsCRP >3 mg/L.

---

## Arguments AGAINST or LIMITING hs-CRP Testing

1. **Non-specificity:** hsCRP is an acute-phase reactant elevated by infection, trauma, medications, and non-CV inflammatory conditions (rheumatoid arthritis, liver disease). Not specific to cardiovascular inflammation.

2. **Interindividual variability:** Influenced by sex (higher in women), race (African-American > Hispanic > Caucasian > Asian), age, smoking, alcohol, obesity, and concurrent illness.

3. **Intraindividual variability:** Can be transiently elevated; two measurements >=2 weeks apart recommended to confirm. Levels >=10 mg/L suggest non-CV processes requiring further workup.

4. **MESA-JUPITER analysis:** hsCRP was NOT associated with CHD or CVD after multivariable adjustment, possibly due to multiethnic cohort composition.

5. **CAC superiority:** CAC provides superior discrimination and risk classification; it directly measures disease rather than a risk factor; more stable across ethnicities.

6. **Serial monitoring not recommended:** Limited cost-effectiveness, inconvenience, and lack of evidence-based guidelines for interventions based on serial measurements.

## Anti-inflammatory Treatment Options

### Colchicine
- First anti-inflammatory agent FDA-approved for secondary ASCVD prevention (2023).
- LoDoCo2: 31% MACE reduction in stable atherosclerosis. COLCOT: 23% MACE reduction post-MI.
- **CLEAR SYNERGY (OASIS 9)** (n=7,062 post-MI): No benefit vs. placebo (9.1% vs. 9.3%, HR 0.99, p=0.93), higher diarrhea incidence. This contradicts prior trials.
- 2023 AHA/ACC/Multisociety: class 2b, LOE: b. 2024 ESC: class 2a, LOE: a (but updated before CLEAR SYNERGY).
- Narrow therapeutic window: avoid in CKD (eGFR <60), hepatic dysfunction, with strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors.
- Authors recommend reserving colchicine for select patients with hsCRP >=2 mg/L despite statin therapy, normal renal function, and lower infection risk.

### IL-6 Inhibitors (Ziltivekimab)
- RESCUE trial (n=264, hsCRP >=2 mg/L, CKD stage 3-5): hsCRP reductions of 77-92% across dose groups vs. -4% placebo (all p<0.0001).
- ZEUS trial ongoing: 6,200 patients with stage 3-4 CKD and elevated hsCRP; primary endpoint MACE.

### Bempedoic Acid
- CLEAR-Outcomes: 21% LDL-C reduction, 22% hsCRP reduction. 13% relative reduction in first major CV event in statin-intolerant patients. hsCRP was a stronger predictor of future CV events/death than hyperlipidemia.

---

## Authors' Clinical Recommendations (Practical Framework)

1. **Primary prevention:** Measure hsCRP in intermediate-risk patients to guide statin decisions; use alongside CAC when available.
2. **Secondary prevention:** Consider hsCRP measurement in patients with recurrent events despite controlled LDL-C to identify residual inflammatory risk.
3. **First-line for elevated hsCRP:** Intensive lifestyle modification (diet, weight loss, exercise, smoking cessation, waist circumference reduction).
4. **Second-line:** Colchicine for select patients with hsCRP >=2 mg/L despite statin therapy, adequate renal function, and lower infection risk.
5. **Measurement protocol:** Two measurements >=2 weeks apart, irrespective of fasting status. Do not measure during acute infection/trauma. Levels >=10 mg/L warrant workup for other inflammatory conditions.
6. **Serial monitoring:** Not routinely recommended beyond confirming persistent elevation.

## Relevance to Research on hs-CRP and Longevity

This review is highly relevant as a comprehensive clinical perspective on hs-CRP's role in cardiovascular risk assessment and management. Key points for longevity research:
- hsCRP >=2 mg/L is a well-validated threshold for elevated CV risk across multiple large cohorts and RCTs.
- Residual inflammatory risk (hsCRP >=2 mg/L with controlled LDL-C) independently predicts MACE, CV mortality, and all-cause mortality.
- The association between hsCRP and "inflammaging" is noted as a contributor to subclinical atherosclerosis and MACE.
- Lifestyle modification is positioned as the first-line anti-inflammatory strategy, aligning with longevity medicine approaches.
- Emerging IL-6 inhibitors (ziltivekimab) may offer targeted anti-inflammatory therapy without renal contraindications of colchicine.

## Claims to Verify

1. **PROVE-IT/IMPROVE-IT residual risk numbers:** "3.1 per 100 person-years and 33.7%" -- these appear to come from a cited Ridker 2017 Circ Res commentary (ref 34) rather than the original trial publications. The exact derivation is unclear.

2. **FOURIER 62% relative risk increase with hsCRP >3 mg/L:** This comes from the SPIRE-1/SPIRE-2 trials specifically (ref 42), not FOURIER alone as the text structure might suggest. The sentence conflates two trials.

3. **Colchicine discontinuation rate in LoDoCo:** "15% discontinued in the run-in phase" -- verify from the original LoDoCo publication (ref 43) rather than this review.
