# src-346: Ridker et al. NEJM 2005 — CRP Levels and Outcomes After Statin Therapy (PROVE IT-TIMI 22)

**IMPORTANT NOTE:** This source is the PROVE IT-TIMI 22 CRP analysis by Ridker et al. (NEJM 2005), NOT the PRINCE trial as described in the directive. The PRINCE trial (Albert et al., JAMA 2001) is cited as reference 5 within this paper. The metadata quality field is "ok" and the content is substantive and complete.

## Core Findings

Among 3745 patients with acute coronary syndromes treated with statins, achieving CRP levels below 2 mg/L after 30 days of therapy was associated with significantly lower rates of recurrent myocardial infarction or coronary death, an effect that was independent of and comparable in magnitude to achieving LDL cholesterol targets. The achieved CRP and LDL levels were minimally correlated (r=0.16), confirming that statins reduce CRP through mechanisms largely separate from lipid lowering, and suggesting that monitoring both targets is necessary for optimal risk reduction.

Patients achieving both LDL <70 mg/dL and CRP <2 mg/L had the best outcomes (2.4 events/100 person-years), while those achieving both LDL <70 and CRP <1 mg/L had the lowest event rate of all subgroups (1.9/100 person-years). Only 15.9% of the population reached these dual aggressive targets, but 81.8% of this group was on intensive atorvastatin therapy.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Prospective secondary analysis of PROVE IT-TIMI 22, a randomized 2x2 factorial trial (statin intensity x gatifloxacin vs. placebo)
- **Population:** 3745 of 4162 enrolled patients with acute coronary syndromes (alive and event-free at day 30 with both LDL and CRP measurements); mean age 58, 22% women, 17% diabetic, 36% current smokers
- **Interventions:** Intensive statin (atorvastatin 80 mg/day) vs. moderate statin (pravastatin 40 mg/day)
- **Follow-up:** Mean 24 months; plasma samples at randomization, 30 days, 4 months, and end of study
- **Primary endpoint:** Recurrent myocardial infarction or death from coronary causes after day 30
- **Exposure definition:** Achieved LDL and CRP defined as values at 30-day follow-up visit
- **CRP assay:** High-sensitivity CRP (Denka Seiken)
- **Analysis:** Prespecified in protocol; quartile-based analyses, median split analyses (LDL at 70 mg/dL, CRP at 2 mg/L), Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for age, sex, smoking, BMI, diabetes, hypertension, and gatifloxacin assignment
- **Period:** November 2000 to February 2004; 349 sites in 8 countries

## Key Evidence

### CRP as an independent treatment target
- CRP <2 mg/L after statin therapy: 2.8 events/100 person-years vs. CRP >=2: 3.9 events/100 person-years (P=0.006)
- LDL <70 mg/dL: 2.7 events/100 person-years vs. LDL >=70: 4.0 events/100 person-years (P=0.008)
- The CRP effect size was virtually identical to the LDL effect size

### Independence of CRP and LDL reduction
- Spearman correlation between achieved CRP and achieved LDL: r=0.16 (P=0.001)
- Less than 3% of variance in achieved CRP explained by variance in achieved LDL
- This minimal correlation was also present in patients who had recurrent events (r=0.18)

### Dose-response relationship (quartile analysis)
- CRP quartiles: <0.9, 0.9-1.9, 2.0-4.2, >4.2 mg/L
- Fully adjusted relative risks for recurrent events (vs. lowest quartile): Q2=1.5 (P=0.06), Q3=1.3 (P=0.15), Q4=1.7 (P=0.01)
- Linear relationship between achieved CRP and risk, independent of LDL

### Four-group stratification (LDL and CRP above/below median)
- LDL >=70 + CRP >=2: 4.6 events/100 person-years (n=1086)
- LDL <70 + CRP >=2: 3.1 events/100 person-years (n=742)
- LDL >=70 + CRP <2: 3.2 events/100 person-years (n=899)
- LDL <70 + CRP <2: 2.4 events/100 person-years (n=1018)
- P for trend <0.001; hazard ratios 1.3, 1.4, 1.9 respectively vs. reference

### Atorvastatin vs. pravastatin effects on CRP
- Baseline CRP: similar (12.2 vs. 11.9 mg/L, P=0.60)
- 30-day CRP: 1.6 vs. 2.3 mg/L (P<0.001)
- 4-month CRP: 1.3 vs. 2.1 mg/L (P<0.001)
- End-of-study CRP: 1.3 vs. 2.1 mg/L (P<0.001)
- Target achievement: 57.5% atorvastatin patients had CRP <2 mg/L vs. 44.9% pravastatin (P<0.001)
- LDL target achievement: 72.3% atorvastatin had LDL <70 vs. 21.7% pravastatin (P<0.001)
- Once LDL and CRP targets achieved, no residual effect of statin type (HR=1.00, 95% CI 0.75-1.34, P=0.90)

### Aggressive dual targets (post hoc analysis)
- LDL <70 + CRP <1 mg/L: 1.9 events/100 person-years (lowest rate observed)
- Only 15.9% of population reached both targets; 81.8% were on atorvastatin

## Methodology Notes

- All main analyses were prespecified in the PROVE IT-TIMI 22 protocol
- CRP and LDL measured at 30 days to capture statin effect while eliminating residual ischemia effects
- All analyses adjusted for gatifloxacin vs. placebo assignment (which had no significant effect on CRP)
- Core laboratory used validated high-sensitivity CRP assay
- 90% of enrolled patients (3745/4162) had complete data for analysis

## Limitations

1. **Secondary prevention only:** Authors explicitly state findings should not be generalized beyond secondary prevention
2. **Observational analysis of achieved levels:** Despite being a randomized trial, the CRP/LDL analysis compares achieved levels, not randomized groups — potential for residual confounding
3. **Post hoc elements:** The CRP <1 mg/L analysis and the dual aggressive target analysis were post hoc
4. **Acute coronary syndrome population:** All patients had recent ACS; different inflammatory dynamics than stable populations
5. **Short follow-up:** Mean 24 months; long-term implications unknown
6. **Not the PRINCE trial:** The directive identified this as PRINCE but it is PROVE IT-TIMI 22; PRINCE is cited as reference 5

## Relevance to Research Questions

This is one of the most influential papers establishing CRP as a treatment target (not just a risk marker) in cardiovascular disease. It demonstrates that CRP reduction with statins is:
1. Largely independent of lipid lowering (<3% shared variance)
2. Clinically meaningful (comparable effect size to LDL reduction)
3. Additive to LDL lowering for risk reduction
4. Achievable with more intensive statin regimens (atorvastatin > pravastatin)

The paper directly foreshadows the JUPITER trial (reference 26-27) and supports the concept that reducing inflammation/CRP may have therapeutic value in cardiovascular disease.
