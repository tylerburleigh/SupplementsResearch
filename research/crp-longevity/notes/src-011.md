# src-011: hs-CRP and All-Cause Mortality Among Oldest-Old in Chinese Longevity Areas

**Citation:** Chen P-L, Li Z-H, Yang H-L, et al. (2022). "Associations Between High-Sensitivity C-Reactive Protein and All-Cause Mortality Among Oldest-Old in Chinese Longevity Areas: A Community-Based Cohort Study." *Frontiers in Public Health*, 10:824783. doi: 10.3389/fpubh.2022.824783

## Core Findings

Elevated hsCRP concentrations were significantly and positively associated with increased all-cause mortality risk among Chinese oldest-old adults (aged 80+), with a fully adjusted HR of 1.49 (95% CI: 1.20-1.87) for the highest vs. lowest hsCRP quartile. The association persisted across sensitivity analyses and was robust to extensive covariate adjustment including demographics, lifestyle, physical exam, disease history, and biochemical markers. Smoking status was the only significant effect modifier: the association held in non-smokers but not current smokers.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Prospective, community-based cohort study
- **Setting:** Eight Chinese longevity areas (selected for high density of oldest-old adults), recruited 2012 and 2014
- **Participants:** 2,206 adults aged 80+ years with available CRP test results (1,506 from 2012 wave, 704 from 2014 wave); 269 lost to follow-up
- **Follow-up:** Median 3.1 years (IQR: 1.6-3.9 years); follow-up surveys in 2014 and 2017
- **Outcome:** All-cause mortality (1,106 deaths verified from family/caregiver reports)

## Key Results

### hsCRP Quartiles and All-Cause Mortality (Fully Adjusted Model 3)

| hsCRP Quartile | Deaths/N | HR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 (<0.46 mg/L) | 263/491 | 1.00 (ref) |
| Q2 (0.47-1.13) | 264/493 | 1.17 (0.94, 1.46) |
| Q3 (1.14-2.92) | 271/483 | 1.28 (1.01, 1.61) |
| Q4 (>2.93 mg/L) | 308/470 | 1.49 (1.20, 1.87) |
| P for trend | | <0.001 |

### AHA/CDC Risk Categories (Fully Adjusted)

| Category | Deaths/N | HR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| <1.0 mg/L (low risk) | 491/911 | 1.00 (ref) |
| 1.0-3.0 mg/L (average) | 312/565 | 1.17 (0.96, 1.42) |
| >3.0 mg/L (high risk) | 303/461 | 1.39 (1.14, 1.70) |
| P for trend | | 0.001 |

### Effect Modification by Smoking

- P for interaction = 0.011
- Non-current smokers: HR 1.17 (95% CI: 1.07-1.28) per 10 mg/L increase in hsCRP
- Current smokers: HR 0.83 (95% CI: 0.66-1.18) -- not significant
- No significant interactions by age, sex, BMI, frailty, education, or other lifestyle factors

## Methodology Details

- **hsCRP measurement:** High-sensitivity immunoturbidimetry assay on fasting venous blood; central lab at Capital Medical University; detection limit 0.11 mg/L
- **Covariate adjustment (Model 3 - fully adjusted):** Age, sex, education, residence, smoking, drinking, exercise, fruit/meat/vegetable intake, BMI, MMSE, frailty, hypertension, diabetes, CVD, cholesterol, triglycerides
- **Sensitivity analyses:** Excluding first-year deaths; tertile and quintile divisions -- all showed similar results
- **Subgroup analyses:** Stratified by age (80+/90+/100+), sex, education, residence, smoking, drinking, diet, exercise, BMI, MMSE, frailty

## Limitations

1. Observational design -- cannot exclude reverse causality (hsCRP may be a consequence rather than cause)
2. Single baseline hsCRP measurement -- cannot assess trajectory effects
3. Self-reported chronic disease history -- residual confounding possible
4. Cause-specific mortality unavailable -- could not analyze CVD vs. cancer vs. other deaths
5. 269 participants lost to follow-up (12.2%)
6. Generalizability limited to Chinese longevity-area populations

## Relevance to Research Question

This study directly addresses whether hsCRP predicts all-cause mortality in the oldest-old, providing prospective cohort evidence from a uniquely old population (median age 93). The effect sizes are more modest (HR ~1.5 for highest quartile) compared to studies in middle-aged populations (HR ~2.6 in a comparable Chinese middle-aged study cited by the authors), suggesting the hsCRP-mortality association may attenuate with advancing age. The significant smoking interaction is a novel finding warranting further investigation. The study fills an important gap since the oldest-old are typically underrepresented in CRP-mortality research.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample size of 2,206 is confirmed in both the Methods and Table 1. The breakdown of 1,506 (2012) + 704 (2014) = 2,210, which is 4 more than 2,206. The text says "a total of 2206 participants were enrolled at baseline (1,506 in 2012 and 704 in 2014)" -- the arithmetic discrepancy (2210 vs 2206) is minor and may reflect exclusions applied to the enrollment count.
- Death count of 1,106 out of 2,206 over a median 3.1 years represents a 50.1% mortality rate, which is plausible for a cohort with median age 93 followed for ~3 years.
- The hsCRP quartile boundaries (<0.46, 0.47-1.13, 1.14-2.92, >2.93) are consistent with the reported median of 1.13 mg/L.
