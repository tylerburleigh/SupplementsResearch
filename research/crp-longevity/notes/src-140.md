# src-140: CANTOS Secondary Analysis - CRP Reduction and Cardiovascular Event Reduction with Canakinumab

**Citation:** Ridker PM, MacFadyen JG, Everett BM, Libby P, Thuren T, Glynn RJ. Relationship of C-reactive protein reduction to cardiovascular event reduction following treatment with canakinumab: a secondary analysis from the CANTOS randomised controlled trial. *Lancet* 2018; 391: 319-28. DOI: 10.1016/S0140-6736(17)32814-3.

## Core Findings

This prespecified secondary analysis of the CANTOS trial (n=10,061 post-MI patients with hsCRP >=2 mg/L) demonstrates that the magnitude of hsCRP reduction after a single dose of canakinumab directly predicts the magnitude of cardiovascular benefit. Patients achieving on-treatment hsCRP <2 mg/L at 3 months had a 25% reduction in MACE (adjusted HR=0.75, p<0.0001) and a 31% reduction in both cardiovascular and all-cause mortality (adjusted HR=0.69 for both), while those not achieving this threshold had no significant benefit. The findings were robust across multiple sensitivity analyses and support a "lower is better" paradigm for inflammation reduction.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Sample and Design
- **N = 10,061** patients with prior MI and hsCRP >=2 mg/L, enrolled from >1000 sites in 39 countries
- Randomised to placebo (n=3,182) or canakinumab 50mg (n=2,060), 150mg (n=2,163), or 300mg (n=2,129) subcutaneously every 3 months
- Median follow-up: 3.7 years
- 9,534 participants (95%) had blood samples at 3 months for hsCRP measurement
- hsCRP assays performed in a central laboratory

### Primary Result: hsCRP Threshold and MACE
- Achieving hsCRP <2 mg/L: **25% MACE reduction** (adj HR=0.75, 95% CI 0.66-0.85, p<0.0001)
- hsCRP >=2 mg/L: No significant benefit (adj HR=0.90, 0.79-1.02, p=0.11)
- Incidence rates: 4.39 (placebo), 4.20 (canakinumab hsCRP>=2), 3.28 (canakinumab hsCRP<2) per 100 person-years
- p for trend across strata <0.0001

### Mortality Findings
- Among those achieving hsCRP <2 mg/L:
  - CV mortality: adj HR=0.69 (0.56-0.85, p=0.0004) -- **31% reduction**
  - All-cause mortality: adj HR=0.69 (0.58-0.81, p<0.0001) -- **31% reduction**
  - MI/stroke/all-cause death: adj HR=0.73 (0.65-0.82, p<0.0001)
- Among those NOT achieving hsCRP <2 mg/L:
  - CV mortality: adj HR=0.99 (0.82-1.21, p=0.95) -- no benefit
  - All-cause mortality: adj HR=1.05 (0.90-1.22, p=0.56) -- no benefit

### Dose-Response Relationship
- Proportion achieving hsCRP <2 mg/L at 3 months:
  - Placebo: 22% (701/3,182)
  - Canakinumab 50 mg: 44% (905/2,060)
  - Canakinumab 150 mg: 55% (1,186/2,163)
  - Canakinumab 300 mg: 65% (1,393/2,129)
  - p<0.0001 for trend
- Dose-specific adjusted HRs for MACE among those achieving hsCRP<2: 0.78 (50mg), 0.75 (150mg), 0.74 (300mg)

### Tertile Analysis
- Top tertile hsCRP (>2.6 mg/L): adj HR=0.93, p=0.29
- Middle tertile (1.2-2.6 mg/L): adj HR=0.80, p=0.004
- Lowest tertile (<=1.2 mg/L): adj HR=0.73, p<0.0001
- p for trend <0.0001

### Number Needed to Treat (5-year)
- Overall CANTOS cohort: NNT=24
- hsCRP <2 mg/L achievers: **NNT=16**
- hsCRP >=2 mg/L non-achievers: NNT=57

### Infection Risk
- Fatal infection rates per 100 person-years:
  - Placebo: 0.18
  - Canakinumab hsCRP <2 mg/L: 0.27
  - Canakinumab hsCRP >=2 mg/L: 0.35
- No significant difference between the two canakinumab groups (p=0.33)
- Not dose-dependent; total number of fatal infections was small (limited power)
- No adverse hepatic, renal, or haemorrhagic effects

## Methodology

- **Design:** Prespecified secondary analysis of a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **On-treatment analysis:** Participants stratified by achieved hsCRP at 3 months (not by intention-to-treat)
- **Statistical approach:** Cox proportional-hazards models stratified by time since index MI; multivariable adjustment for age, sex, smoking, hypertension, diabetes, BMI, baseline hsCRP, and baseline LDL cholesterol
- **Sensitivity analyses (8 total):** Alternative hsCRP thresholds (1.8 mg/L median), tertile analysis, 50% reduction threshold, median percent reduction, dose-specific analyses, causal inference analysis, additional endpoints -- all yielded consistent results
- **Limitations acknowledged:** On-treatment analysis is no longer formally randomized; residual confounding possible though multivariable adjustment and causal inference analysis had minimal impact on estimates; applies only to canakinumab; limited to those with elevated baseline hsCRP

## Relevance to Research Question

This source is directly relevant to questions about:
1. **CRP reduction thresholds and clinical outcomes:** Provides strong evidence that achieving hsCRP <2 mg/L with anti-inflammatory therapy (canakinumab) yields substantial cardiovascular benefit (25% MACE reduction, 31% mortality reduction)
2. **Dose-response relationship:** Clear demonstration that greater hsCRP reduction produces greater clinical benefit, supporting the "lower is better" concept for inflammation
3. **Mortality impact:** 31% reduction in both CV and all-cause mortality when hsCRP target achieved -- highly relevant to longevity
4. **Risk-benefit tradeoff:** Increased fatal infection risk with canakinumab, though not dose-dependent and not correlated with hsCRP achievement level
5. **CRP as a treatment target:** Supports using hsCRP as a biomarker to guide therapy, analogous to LDL cholesterol in lipid management
