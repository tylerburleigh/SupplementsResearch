## Core Findings

This is a large Mendelian randomization (MR) study in 78,809 Danish individuals aged 20-100 that tested whether CRP is causally associated with depression and somatic outcomes. The central finding is a dissociation between observational associations and causal estimates: observational analyses show strong positive associations between elevated CRP and all outcomes, but genetic/instrumental variable analyses show no causal effect. CRP is therefore likely a biomarker of underlying inflammation or disease rather than a causal driver.

## Key Evidence

### CRP and Depression
- **Observational:** Doubling of plasma CRP associated with OR 1.28 (95% CI: 1.23-1.33) for hospitalization/death with depression
- **Causal (MR):** Genetically elevated CRP yielded OR 0.79 (95% CI: 0.51-1.22)
- **Difference test:** p = 0.03 (statistically significant divergence between observational and causal estimates)
- **Antidepressant use:** Observational OR 1.12 (1.11-1.15); causal OR 0.98 (0.83-1.15; p = 0.08 for difference)
- **Conclusion:** CRP is not causally related to depression

### CRP and Somatic Outcomes (same individuals)
All showed similar patterns -- strong observational associations but no causal effect:
- **Cancer:** p = 0.002 (observational vs. causal difference)
- **Ischemic heart disease:** p = 4 x 10^-99
- **COPD:** p = 6 x 10^-86
- **All-cause mortality:** p = 0.001

## Methodology

- **Design:** Prospective cohort + instrumental variable (Mendelian randomization) analysis
- **Sample:** 78,809 randomly selected men and women, aged 20-100, from the Danish general population
- **Instrument:** Four CRP genotypes used as genetic instruments
- **Outcomes:** Hospitalization/death with depression, somatic diseases; prescription antidepressant use; all-cause mortality
- **Key strength:** Very large sample from general population; uses MR to address confounding/reverse causation
- **Key limitation:** Only abstract available; cannot verify methodological details (genotype selection criteria, IV strength/F-statistic, sensitivity analyses). Abstract-only source.

## Relevance to Research Questions

This study is directly relevant to whether CRP causally influences longevity-related outcomes. It provides strong evidence that:
1. Observational CRP-depression associations are confounded or reflect reverse causation
2. The same pattern extends to cancer, IHD, COPD, and all-cause mortality
3. CRP-lowering interventions may not produce health benefits for these outcomes

The large sample size (78,809) and general-population design give this study high external validity. The MR design addresses confounding that limits purely observational studies.
