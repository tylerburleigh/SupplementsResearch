## Core Findings (2-3 sentences)

This two-sample bidirectional Mendelian randomization study found a statistically significant causal effect of genetically elevated CRP on hypertensive heart disease (HHD) risk, but no significant causal relationship between CRP and myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease, heart failure, or atherosclerosis. No evidence of reverse causation (CVD causing CRP elevation) was detected. The CRP-HHD causal link was robust to MR-PRESSO outlier correction and multivariable MR adjustment, though results were sensitive to PhenoScanner-based outlier removal.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **Significant causal finding:** CRP -> HHD (hypertensive heart disease) -- statistically significant across primary IVW analysis
- **Null findings (no causal effect):** CRP -> myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease, heart failure, atherosclerosis
- **No reverse causation:** Bidirectional MR found no causal effect of any CVD subtype on CRP levels
- **Robustness:** MR-PRESSO and multivariable MR confirmed CRP-HHD finding
- **Sensitivity to outlier definition:** When PhenoScanner identified outlier IVs were removed, initial MR results changed, but sensitivity analyses stayed congruent with primary analyses

## Methodology

- **Design:** Two-sample bidirectional Mendelian randomization
- **Genetic IV source:** UK Biobank (CRP levels)
- **Outcome GWAS sources:** FinnGen and/or EBI GWAS catalog (CVD subtypes)
- **Primary MR method:** Inverse-variance weighted (IVW)
- **Sensitivity analyses:** Penalized weighted median (PWM), weighted median (WM)
- **Pleiotropy assessment:** MR-Egger intercept
- **Heterogeneity assessment:** Cochran's Q-test
- **IV strength:** F-statistics
- **Outlier correction:** MR-PRESSO
- **Confounding adjustment:** Multivariable MR
- **Outlier identification:** PhenoScanner
- **Two analytical approaches:** Approach 1 and Approach 2 (differing IV selection quality control steps, per Figure 2 description)

## Relevance to Research Question

This source is directly relevant to understanding whether CRP has a causal role in cardiovascular disease. It provides MR evidence that CRP causally affects only hypertensive heart disease among CVD subtypes, and that CRP does NOT cause coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, heart failure, or atherosclerosis. This has implications for interpreting CRP as a biomarker -- it may be specifically causal for HHD rather than broadly causal across CVD. The finding of no reverse causation strengthens the directional interpretation.

For longevity research specifically, the null finding for most CVD subtypes suggests that CRP-lowering interventions may not reduce the majority of CVD risk through causal CRP pathways (except potentially HHD), which tempers the case for CRP as a universal therapeutic target for cardiovascular longevity.
