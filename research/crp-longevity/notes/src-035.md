# src-035: CRP, Ischemic Stroke, and All-Cause Death (Observational + MR)

**Citation:** Rolver MG, Emanuelsson F, Nordestgaard BG, Benn M. "C-reactive protein and prospective cardiometabolic risk: observational and Mendelian randomization study of ischemic stroke and all-cause death." *Cardiovascular Diabetology* (2025). DOI: 10.1186/s12933-025-03028-5

## Core Findings

This large study of 113,491 individuals from the Copenhagen City Heart Study and Copenhagen General Population Study found strong observational associations between elevated CRP and both ischemic stroke and all-cause death, but Mendelian randomization analyses using 3 CRP gene variants across up to 575,531 individuals provided no evidence of a causal effect. The sharp disconnect between observational and genetic estimates demonstrates that CRP is a biomarker of risk rather than a causal driver of these outcomes.

## Key Evidence

### Observational Results (Copenhagen Studies, N=113,491)

- **Quintile analysis:** 5th vs 1st quintile CRP: HR 1.51 (95% CI: 1.34-1.71) for ischemic stroke; HR 1.69 (1.60-1.79) for all-cause death
- **Continuous (per 2-fold higher CRP):** OR 1.13 (p=2x10^-23) for ischemic stroke; OR 1.34 (p=5x10^-490) for all-cause death
- **Cumulative incidence at clinically meaningful ages:**
  - Age 72 (median Danish stroke age): 6.3% vs 4.0% cumulative stroke incidence (CRP >= 2 vs < 2 mg/L), 57% higher risk
  - Age 80 (average Danish lifespan): 33.4% vs 20.7% cumulative death incidence (CRP >= 2 vs < 2 mg/L), 62% higher risk
- **Population median CRP:** 1.4 mg/L; cutoff of 2 mg/L used for categorical analyses

### Mendelian Randomization Results

- **Genetic instruments:** 3 CRP gene variants (rs1205, rs3093077, rs1130864), selected to minimize pleiotropy; max R^2 between variants = 0.24
- **One-sample MR (Copenhagen):** No causal effect on ischemic stroke (OR=0.96, CI: 0.81-1.14, p=0.64) or all-cause death (OR=0.98, CI: 0.90-1.07, p=0.67)
- **Two-sample MR (all consortia combinations):** Consistently null across all outcomes:
  - CHARGE + FinnGen: ischemic stroke OR=1.00 (p=0.89), death OR=1.02 (p=0.49)
  - CHARGE + MEGASTROKE: ischemic stroke OR=1.02 (p=0.55), large artery OR=1.05, small vessel OR=1.03
  - UKBB + FinnGen: ischemic stroke OR=1.01 (p=0.91), death OR=1.04 (p=0.49)
  - UKBB + MEGASTROKE: ischemic stroke OR=1.04 (p=0.55)
- **Meta-analyzed MR estimates:** Any ischemic stroke OR=1.01 (0.97-1.05, p=0.93); all-cause death OR=1.01 (0.98-1.05, p=0.63)
- **Power:** 90.69% power to detect OR of 1.10 at alpha=0.05 in the primary two-sample analysis
- **Sensitivity analyses:** MR Egger and MR weighted median showed similar null results with no indication of pleiotropy

## Methodology

- **Design:** Combined observational cohort study + one-sample MR + two-sample MR
- **Cohort:** CCHS (initiated 1976, follow-up up to 40 years) + CGPS (2003-2015 enrollment), both Danish population-based prospective studies
- **Population:** White individuals of Danish descent, aged 20-100+ years
- **External consortia for two-sample MR:** CHARGE CIWG (204,402 European ancestry), UKBB (575,531), FinnGen (342,499 Finnish ancestry), MEGASTROKE (446,696 European ancestry)
- **Statistical approach:** Restricted cubic splines with Cox models for observational analysis; ivreg2/ivpois for one-sample MR; inverse-variance weighted regression for two-sample MR; fixed-effects meta-analysis (I^2 = 0-16%)
- **Adjustment:** Observational models adjusted for sex, BMI, physical activity, alcohol, tobacco, LDL-C, triglycerides, lipid-lowering drugs, hypertension. MR analyses unadjusted (genetic randomization assumption).

## Limitations

- Observational cohorts were white Danish individuals only -- limited generalizability to other ethnicities
- Up to 8.8% case overlap between consortia may bias toward observational estimates (but low risk for null findings)
- Only 3 CRP gene variants used; limited power for subtype analyses, though calculated power was sufficient for meaningful effects
- rs1205 has reported associations with heart rate variability and COVID-19 severity (potential pleiotropy)
- CRP is a downstream marker; null MR finding does not exclude causal role of upstream inflammatory mediators (e.g., IL-6)

## Clinical and Research Implications

- CRP remains useful for risk stratification but lowering CRP per se unlikely to prevent stroke or reduce mortality
- Results consistent with CANTOS trial (canakinumab reduced cardiovascular events but not stroke or all-cause death)
- IL-6 pathway may be a more promising therapeutic target than CRP itself, as prior MR studies suggest IL6R variants associated with lower stroke risk
- Distinguishes biomarker value from causal mechanism: strong observational prediction does not imply therapeutic target

## Claims to Verify

- N=113,491 stated consistently across abstract and methods. Methods confirm 3,519 excluded for missing CRP/genotype (3.0% of cohort), with 108,636 having genotypes available. Numbers are internally consistent.
- "5x10^-490" p-value for all-cause death observational association seems implausibly precise; likely a reporting artifact from statistical software but the association is clearly highly significant regardless.
- Power calculation claims 90.69% power to detect OR 1.10 -- this is plausible given the sample sizes involved.

## Relevance to Research Question

This source is directly relevant to whether CRP causally influences longevity/mortality. It provides strong evidence from multiple MR approaches that CRP does not causally affect all-cause mortality or ischemic stroke risk, despite being a robust observational predictor. The large sample sizes (>100K observational, >500K in two-sample MR) and consistent null genetic findings make this one of the most definitive studies on the CRP-causality question.
