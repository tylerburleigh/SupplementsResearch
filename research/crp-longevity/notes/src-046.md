# src-046: Framingham Heart Study Proteomics — Protein Biomarkers of CVD and Mortality

## Identification

- **PMID:** 30006491
- **Likely citation:** Ho JE, Lyass A, Courchesne P, et al. Protein biomarkers of cardiovascular disease and mortality in the community. *J Am Heart Assoc.* 2018.
- **Cohort:** Framingham Heart Study (FHS)
- **Source quality:** Abstract only (PubMed landing page); full text not available. Content is substantive abstract-level data.

## Core Findings

1. **CRP has limited multimarker predictive value.** In a multimarker model accounting for clinical factors, CRP was significantly associated only with incident heart failure (alongside NT-proBNP and leptin). CRP was NOT among the biomarkers that retained significance for all-cause mortality, CVD death, or incident ASCVD in the multimarker model.

2. **GDF15 outperforms CRP as a pan-outcome predictor.** Growth differentiation factor 15 (GDF15) was the only biomarker associated with all four outcomes (ASCVD, heart failure, all-cause mortality, CVD death) in the multimarker model.

3. **Many proteins outperform CRP for mortality prediction.** In the multimarker model for all-cause mortality, 12 biomarkers retained significance: CLEC3B (tetranectin), NT-proBNP, AGP1, sRAGE, PMP2, UCMGP, KLKB1, IGFBP2, IGF1, leptin receptor, and cystatin-C. CRP was excluded from this list.

## Key Evidence

### Study Design
- 3,523 Framingham Heart Study participants (mean age 62, 53% women)
- 85 circulating protein biomarkers measured via discovery proteomic platform
- Multivariable-adjusted Cox models; FDR q<0.05 threshold
- Prospective design examining incident events

### Biomarker Counts by Outcome (univariate, multivariable-adjusted)
| Outcome | N significant biomarkers |
|---|---|
| Incident ASCVD | 8 |
| Incident heart failure | 18 |
| All-cause mortality | 38 |
| CVD death | 35 |

### CRP's Position Among Biomarkers
- **CRP in univariate models:** Likely significant for multiple outcomes (abstract does not list individual univariate results; CRP appears among HF biomarkers)
- **CRP in multimarker models:**
  - ASCVD: Not mentioned among multimarker-significant biomarkers
  - Heart failure: Yes (with NT-proBNP, leptin)
  - All-cause mortality: No (12 other biomarkers listed, CRP excluded)
  - CVD death: Not listed among multimarker-significant biomarkers

### Biomarkers That Outperform CRP
- **GDF15:** Only protein associated with ALL four outcomes in multimarker model
- **NT-proBNP:** Associated with heart failure AND all-cause mortality in multimarker model (broader than CRP)
- **Leptin:** Associated with heart failure in multimarker model (same as CRP)
- **12 mortality biomarkers** that CRP did not match: CLEC3B, AGP1, sRAGE, PMP2, UCMGP, KLKB1, IGFBP2, IGF1, leptin receptor, cystatin-C

### Metabolic/Adipocyte Pathway Biomarkers
The study highlighted regulators of metabolic and adipocyte homeostasis as predictive: IGF1, IGFBP1, IGFBP2, leptin, and adipsin.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Prospective cohort study within FHS
- **Proteomics platform:** Discovery platform targeting high-value CVD proteins (85 targets)
- **Statistical approach:** Multivariable-adjusted Cox proportional hazards models; multimarker models accounting for clinical factors; FDR correction (q<0.05)
- **P-value range:** 9.8 x 10^-34 to 3.6 x 10^-2

## Limitations

1. **Abstract-only content:** Full text unavailable; effect sizes (hazard ratios), confidence intervals, and detailed biomarker rankings (e.g., CRP's exact hazard ratio, whether it was near-significant in multimarker mortality models) cannot be extracted.
2. **Discovery cohort only:** Authors note further validation studies are needed.
3. **Selected proteomic panel (85 proteins):** Not a fully agnostic proteomics screen; proteins were pre-selected for CVD relevance.
4. **Single cohort:** FHS is predominantly White/European ancestry; generalizability to other populations is uncertain.
5. **Mean age 62:** Results may not generalize to younger populations.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Where CRP ranks among biomarkers:** CRP ranks relatively low in the multimarker hierarchy. While it has some predictive value for heart failure, it does not survive multimarker adjustment for mortality outcomes, where 12 other proteins dominate.
- **Which biomarkers outperform CRP:** GDF15 is the clear top performer (pan-outcome predictor). NT-proBNP, cystatin-C, sRAGE, IGF1/IGFBP2, and several others outperform CRP for mortality prediction.
- **Implications for CRP utility:** CRP's utility appears limited to heart failure prediction in multimarker contexts. For mortality and ASCVD prediction, other circulating proteins carry substantially more information. This suggests CRP as a standalone biomarker may be insufficient and that multi-protein panels provide superior risk stratification.
