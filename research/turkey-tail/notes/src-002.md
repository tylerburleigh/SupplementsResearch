## Core Findings

1. Turkey tail mushroom contains two key bioactive compounds -- polysaccharide peptide (PSP) and polysaccharide krestin (PSK) -- which may slow cancer growth and boost immune function. PSK taken orally may provide a small improvement in chemotherapy response for certain cancer types, classified as "possibly effective" for this use.

2. The monograph provides specific dosing guidance: PSK at 3 grams/day orally for up to 36 months (adults); whole turkey tail mushroom at 2.4 grams/day for up to 12 weeks. These are the most commonly studied doses.

3. Turkey tail is rated "likely safe" for most healthy adults when taken by mouth. Safety during pregnancy/breast-feeding is unknown (avoid recommended). Vaginal use safety is unestablished.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Active Compounds
- PSP (polysaccharide peptide) and PSK (polysaccharide krestin) are the primary medicinal constituents
- Both are protein-bound polysaccharides derived from Coriolus versicolor

### Dosing (Q4)
- **PSK**: 3 grams/day by mouth, up to 36 months (most studied adult dose)
- **Whole mushroom**: 2.4 grams/day by mouth, up to 12 weeks
- Note: Some supplements contain isolated PSP/PSK; whole mushroom is also used

### Safety Profile (Q6)
- Rated "likely safe" for most healthy adults when taken orally
- Side effects reported in chemotherapy + PSK context: nausea, vomiting, low white blood cell counts, liver problems -- attribution to PSK vs. chemotherapy unclear
- Vaginal use: insufficient safety data; may cause itching and irritation
- Pregnancy/breast-feeding: insufficient data; avoid use recommended

### Drug Interactions (Q6)
- **Moderate interactions**:
  - Cyclophosphamide: PSP may alter clearance rate, changing efficacy and side effects
  - Antidiabetes drugs: Turkey tail may lower blood sugar, risk of hypoglycemia
  - Tamoxifen: May change how tamoxifen works or reduce its effects
- **Minor interaction**:
  - CYP2C9 substrates: PSP may alter hepatic metabolism of drugs metabolized by this enzyme

### Clinical Evidence Assessment
- Cancer (chemotherapy adjunct with PSK): "Possibly Effective" -- may provide small improvement in chemotherapy response
- All other uses (fatigue, UTIs, muscle strength, etc.): "No good scientific evidence"
