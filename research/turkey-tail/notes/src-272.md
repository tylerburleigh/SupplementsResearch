# src-272: PSK induces in vitro cytotoxic activity via cell cycle arrest and apoptosis

**Citation:** Jimenez-Medina E, Berruguilla E, Romero I, Algarra I, Collado A, Garrido F, Garcia-Lora A. BMC Cancer 2008, 8:78. doi:10.1186/1471-2407-8-78

**Quality:** ok -- full paper text with methods, results, discussion. PDF fallback to raw text extraction, but all substantive content is readable.

## Core Findings

1. **PSK directly inhibits tumour cell proliferation in vitro** across 7 cell lines (22-84% inhibition at 100 ug/mL), with highest sensitivity in gastric (AGS, 84%) and lung (A549, 80%) cancer lines.

2. **Mechanisms identified as cell cycle arrest (G0/G1 accumulation, S-phase elimination) and apoptosis induction via caspase-3 activation.** In AGS cells, G0/G1 rose from 32.2% to 60.8% and S-phase dropped to 0%; apoptosis increased from 4.32% to 37.52%.

3. **PSK has a dual anti-tumour mechanism:** direct cytotoxicity on tumour cells (this study's novel finding) plus previously known immunomodulatory activity on NK cells. These are independent mechanisms.

## Key Evidence/Data Points

- **Proliferation inhibition (Table 1):** AGS 84%, A549 80%, Ando-2 67%, B16 62%, Hela 46%, Jurkat 41%, B9 22% at 100 ug/mL PSK (P < 0.001 vs control)
- **Cell cycle arrest (Table 2):** Complete arrest in AGS (S-phase 31.1% to 0%), Ando-2 (32.25% to 4.35%), strong in A549 and B16. Partial slowing in B9, Hela, Jurkat.
- **Apoptosis (Table 3):** AGS 37.52%, A549 35.50%, B16 25.40%, Ando-2 18.35% at 100 ug/mL PSK. No significant apoptosis in B9 (11.37% vs 12.11%) or Hela/Jurkat.
- **Caspase-3 (Table 4):** AGS 36.13%, A549 31.14%, B16 23.35%, Ando-2 15.55%. Negligible in B9, Hela, Jurkat.
- **Synergy with IL-2:** PSK+IL-2 produced 4.5-fold PBL proliferation vs 3-fold for IL-2 alone. PSK alone had no proliferative effect on PBLs.
- **PSK variants:** Neuraminidase-treated, sugar-rich, and protein-rich variants all showed identical cytotoxic activity, indicating the active compound is preserved across preparations.

## Methodology

- **In vitro study** using 7 tumour cell lines: B16 murine melanoma, B9 murine fibrosarcoma, Ando-2 human melanoma, AGS human gastric cancer, A549 human lung cancer, Hela human cervical adenocarcinoma, Jurkat T lymphoma leukemia.
- **Proliferation assays:** BrdU incorporation (ELISA) and viable cell count (Trypan Blue) at PSK concentrations of 50 and 100 ug/mL for 4-6 days.
- **Cell cycle analysis:** Flow cytometry with BrdU/7-AAD staining after 4-day PSK exposure.
- **Apoptosis:** Annexin V-PE/7-AAD dual staining, flow cytometry.
- **Caspase-3:** FITC-conjugated anti-active-caspase-3 antibody, flow cytometry.
- **Statistical analysis:** Student's t-test, P < 0.05 significance threshold. Each experiment repeated at least 3 times.
- **PSK source:** Kureha Chemical Ind. Co. (Tokyo, Japan), hot water extraction from cultured mycelia of Coriolus versicolor.

## Limitations

- **In vitro only** -- no in vivo confirmation of the direct cytotoxic mechanism in this study.
- **Single concentration range** (50-100 ug/mL) tested for most assays; full dose-response curves not reported.
- **Cell cycle data** reported from one typical experiment out of three (Table 2), not pooled data.
- **No molecular pathway analysis** beyond caspase-3; upstream regulators of cell cycle arrest not investigated.
- **PSK is a complex mixture** -- the specific molecular component responsible for cytotoxic activity was not isolated or identified.
- **Funding partially from Kureha Chemical** (PSK manufacturer), declared as competing interest.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Active compounds/mechanisms):** Strongly relevant. Identifies PSK's direct cytotoxic mechanism (cell cycle arrest + apoptosis) as distinct from immunomodulatory mechanism. Provides detailed quantitative data across multiple cancer types.
- **Q2 (Bioavailability/pharmacokinetics):** Not directly addressed (in vitro study). Notes PSK preparation details (hot water extraction, ~12 kDa protein core).
- **Q3 (Synergistic combinations):** Relevant. Demonstrates PSK+IL-2 synergy in lymphocyte proliferation. Also references PSK synergy with docetaxel in pancreatic cancer and with chemotherapeutic agents in clinical trials.
- **Q4 (Safety/toxicity):** Indirectly relevant -- PSK stimulates lymphocytes (no cytotoxic effect on normal PBLs), suggesting selective toxicity toward tumour cells.
- **Q5 (Product quality):** Relevant. Tested neuraminidase-treated, sugar-rich, and protein-rich PSK variants, all showing identical activity. Suggests robustness of cytotoxic compound across preparations.
- **Q6 (Clinical evidence):** References multiple RCTs showing efficacy in gastric, colorectal, lung, esophageal, and breast cancers. Adds mechanistic explanation (dual activity) for clinical observations.
- **Q7 (Comparison with other medicinal mushrooms):** Briefly references Calendula extract LACE as having similar dual activity. Does not directly compare PSK with other mushroom-derived compounds.

## Claims to Verify

- The proliferation inhibition percentages in Table 1 (e.g., AGS 84%, A549 80%) appear internally consistent but are derived from single experiments (each repeated "at least three times" but only one set of numbers reported).
- The working concentration of 100 ug/mL (sometimes rendered as "100 Pg/ml" in the raw text -- appears to be a PDF extraction artifact for "100 ug/mL") was previously titrated in NKL cells and may not be the optimal concentration for all tumour cell lines.
- The statement about PSK being "composed of a single 12-kd protein" that is "highly glycosylated" (line 122-123) is based on the authors' prior work (ref 22) and not independently verified in this study.
