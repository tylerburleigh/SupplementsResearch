# src-078: Mushroom Polysaccharides: a Potent Immune-Modulator

**Citation:** Chakraborty N, Banerjee A, Sarkar A, Ghosh S, Acharya K. (2021). Biointerface Research in Applied Chemistry, 11(2), 8915-8930. DOI: 10.33263/BRIAC112.89158930

**Type:** Review article (open access, CC BY)

**Quality:** Content is substantive -- full text available with methods, tables, and references.

## Core Findings

1. Mushroom polysaccharides (MPs), primarily beta-D-glucans, exert immune-modulatory effects through pattern recognition receptors (dectin-1, CR3, TLR-2/TLR-4) activating innate immune cells (macrophages, NK cells, dendritic cells) and adaptive immune cells (T cells, B cells) via cytokine production and signaling pathways (NF-kB, MAPK, Syk).

2. Turkey tail (Polystictus versicolor / Coriolus versicolor) produces PSK (Krestin) and PSP, which induce cytokine gene expression and stimulate T-cell activation -- these are among the most clinically used mushroom polysaccharides in cancer immunotherapy.

3. Structural properties critically determine bioactivity: optimal molecular weight (500-2000 kDa), degree of branching (20-33%), triple-helical conformation, and water solubility all influence immune-stimulatory potency.

## Key Evidence/Data Points

- **Receptor pathways:** Dectin-1 binds (1->3) beta-D-glucans; works with TLRs to produce TNF-alpha and IL-2. CR3 mediates neutrophil cytotoxicity via Syk pathway. TLR-4 mediates macrophage activation by Ganoderma beta-glucan via NF-kB.
- **Ganoderma lucidum polysaccharide:** 29x increase in TNF-alpha and IL-1 in macrophage culture vs. untreated (line 476).
- **PSK from turkey tail:** High molecular weight fraction showed greater immune-modulatory effects than lower MW fractions (line 325-327).
- **Branching degrees:** Lentinan (40%), Schizophyllan (33%), PSK (20%) -- all show similar bioactivity despite different branching.
- **Chemical modifications** (sulfation, carboxymethylation) can enhance activity of otherwise weakly active polysaccharides.

## Limitations

- **Review only** -- no original data; relies on interpretation of cited studies.
- **No pharmacokinetics discussed** -- absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion of polysaccharides are not addressed. The paper notes MPs "cannot penetrate cells" due to high molecular mass but does not discuss oral bioavailability.
- **No cognition or longevity data** -- the review is exclusively focused on immune modulation and anti-cancer effects. Any cognition/longevity claims would be extrapolation beyond this evidence base.
- **Safety data is thin** -- states nutraceuticals have "very few side effects" without systematic safety analysis. Notes that some MPs (Xylaria nigripes, Ganoderma spores) actually suppress immune function, which could be a risk factor.
- **Most cited studies are in vitro or murine** -- limited human clinical trial evidence discussed.
- **Published in a lower-impact journal** (Biointerface Research in Applied Chemistry).

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q3 (Bioactive compounds, pharmacokinetics, pathways):** Strongly relevant for bioactive compounds and signaling pathways. Provides detailed receptor-level mechanisms (dectin-1, CR3, TLR-2/4, NF-kB, MAPK, Syk). Weak on pharmacokinetics -- no ADME data. Turkey tail PSK/PSP specifically covered.
- **Q5 (Cognition/longevity claims vs. extrapolation):** Directly illustrates the extrapolation concern -- this paper shows mushroom polysaccharides act through immune receptors (not CNS pathways), providing no direct evidence for cognitive or longevity effects. Any such claims would indeed be extrapolation from immune effects.
- **Q6 (Risks, drug interactions, adverse events):** Limited but notable. Notes immune-suppressive effects of some polysaccharides (Xylaria nigripes inhibits IFN-gamma, NO, TNF-alpha, IL-6; Ganoderma spore polysaccharide inhibits antibody-producing cells). These represent potential risks for immunocompromised individuals or those on immunosuppressive drugs.
