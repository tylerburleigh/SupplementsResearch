# src-175: Potential Benefit of Beta-Glucans as Adjuvant Therapy in Immuno-Oncology

**Citation:** Cognigni V, Ranallo N, Tronconi F, Morgese F, Berardi R. Potential benefit of beta-glucans as adjuvant therapy in immuno-oncology: a review. *Explor Target Antitumor Ther.* 2021;2:122-38. https://doi.org/10.37349/etat.2021.00036

**Type:** Narrative review (peer-reviewed, open access)

**Quality Assessment:** Content is substantive and complete -- full text of a well-structured review article with abstract, introduction, preclinical data, clinical data tables, conclusions, and references. No quality concerns.

---

## Core Findings

1. **Beta-glucans function as biological response modifiers (BRMs)** by binding specific immune cell receptors (Dectin-1, CR3, LacCer, NKp30, scavenger receptors), activating both innate and adaptive immunity. They induce trained immunity via epigenetic reprogramming and drive M2-to-M1 macrophage polarization, NK cell activation, T cell proliferation, and cytokine release.

2. **Beta-glucans synergize with immune checkpoint inhibitors** by upregulating PD-L1 on tumor cells and macrophages while simultaneously enhancing costimulatory molecules (CD80, CD86, HLA-DR) and immunostimulatory cytokines. This creates a rationale for combination therapy with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 agents.

3. **Clinical evidence is emerging but limited to early-phase trials.** The most studied compound is BTH-1677 (Imprime PGG), a yeast-derived IV beta-glucan. Phase II results with pembrolizumab in mTNBC (ORR 15.9%, median OS 18.1 months) and melanoma (DCR 45%, median OS 8.8 months) are encouraging but not definitive. Several trials are ongoing.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Molecular Mechanisms
- Beta-glucans are D-glucose polysaccharides with 1->3 linear beta-glycosidic bonds and 1->6 side branches (fungi-specific)
- Receptor binding triggers PI3K/Akt, Syk, and NLRP3 inflammasome pathways
- Dectin-1 mediates non-opsonic phagocytosis; CR3 mediates complement-dependent cytotoxicity (requires dual binding to iC3b and beta-glucan)
- BTH-1677 forms tripartite immune complex with anti-beta-glucan antibodies (ABA) and complement iC3b; ABA levels are a prerequisite for activity and may serve as a patient-selection biomarker

### Preclinical Data
- BTH-1677 + anti-PD-1/PD-L1 significantly repressed tumor growth vs. monotherapy in CT-26 and MC-38 mouse models (Fraser et al.)
- Qiu et al.: T cells treated ex vivo with anti-PD-1 + BTH-1677-treated APCs showed increased proliferation; in vivo combination reduced median tumor volume in mice
- Hong et al.: beta-glucan + anti-GD2 or anti-MUC1 mAbs showed significantly greater tumor regression vs. monotherapy in mouse models

### Clinical Data
| Trial | Population | Treatment | Key Results |
|-------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Phase 1a/b (Halstenson 2015) | Healthy volunteers | BTH-1677 IV | Well tolerated up to 6 mg/kg single, 4 mg/kg x7 days; 67% mild-moderate AEs |
| IMPRIME 1 mTNBC cohort | 44 ABA+ mTNBC | Imprime PGG + pembrolizumab | ORR 15.9% (1 CR, 6 PR); 17/44 SD >1yr; median OS 18.1 mo |
| IMPRIME 1 melanoma cohort | 40 ABA+ melanoma | Imprime PGG + pembrolizumab | DCR 45% (1 CR, 8 SD); median OS 8.8 mo; 12-mo OS 45% |
| Phase 1 (Modak 2013) | 24 chemo-resistant NB | Oral beta-glucan + 3F8 (anti-GD2) | 63% clinical response; well tolerated |
| Phase 1 (Kushner) | 15 high-risk NB in remission | Beta-glucan + ganglioside vaccine | Well tolerated; encouraging serological/MRD responses |

### Ongoing Trials
- NCT03003468: Phase 1b/2, Imprime PGG + pembrolizumab in mNSCLC
- NCT03555149: Phase 1b/2 (Morpheus), Imprime PGG + atezolizumab in mCRC (cohort 3)
- NCT03246685: Phase 2 in SCCHN -- terminated for enrollment failure

## Methodology

This is a narrative literature review covering beta-glucan biology, receptor pharmacology, preclinical studies, and clinical trials. It includes structured tables summarizing preclinical and clinical evidence. The review focuses specifically on BTH-1677 (Imprime PGG) as the most studied beta-glucan in immuno-oncology combinations.

---

## Limitations

- **Narrative review, not systematic:** No formal search strategy, inclusion/exclusion criteria, or quality assessment of included studies reported.
- **Clinical evidence is early-phase:** All cited trials are phase 1 or phase 2 with small sample sizes (15-44 patients). No phase 3 data available.
- **Focus on BTH-1677:** Heavy emphasis on one proprietary beta-glucan product; less coverage of other beta-glucan sources (e.g., turkey tail/Trametes versicolor is not discussed).
- **No turkey tail specific data:** Despite being a beta-glucan review, this paper does not specifically address Trametes versicolor (turkey tail) or its PSK/PSP compounds in any detail.
- **ABA dependency:** The requirement for sufficient anti-beta-glucan antibody levels is a potential limitation for clinical translation -- only ABA-positive patients respond.

## Claims to Verify

1. **"63% clinical response in neuroblastoma"** (line 543-544): This is described as "not striking and of short duration" -- the response criteria are not specified. The 24-patient sample is small.
2. **"median OS 18.1 months"** in mTNBC (line 455-456): The 95% CI is "12 months-not reached," indicating the estimate may not be mature.
3. **"67% of study subjects overall"** experienced AEs (line 391): This includes healthy volunteers in phase 1; may not generalize to cancer patients.
