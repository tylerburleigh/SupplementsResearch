# Source src-097: Polysaccharides as Potential Anti-tumor Biomacromolecules -- A Review

**Citation:** Guo R, Chen M, Ding Y, Yang P, Wang M, Zhang H, He Y, Ma H. (2022). *Frontiers in Nutrition*, 9. DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2022.838179

**Type:** Peer-reviewed review article (Frontiers in Nutrition, Section: Nutrition and Food Science Technology)

**Quality Assessment:** Content is substantive -- full review text with methods, mechanisms, structure-activity relationships, and applications. Quality is "ok."

---

## Core Findings

1. This is a broad review of anti-tumor polysaccharides from many natural sources (mushrooms, plants, algae, etc.), covering extraction, purification, structure-activity relationships, mechanisms of action, bioavailability, and clinical applications.

2. Turkey tail (*Trametes versicolor* / *Coriolus versicolor*) polysaccharides are mentioned only briefly in the introduction as one of three examples of polysaccharides that can be applied as adjuvant cancer therapies with low toxicity and high safety. The paper cites Habtemariam (2020) as the key reference for turkey tail.

3. The paper provides strong mechanistic context relevant to beta-glucan polysaccharides (the structural class to which PSK/PSP belong): beta-(1->3)-glucans with beta-(1->6) branches enhance immunostimulatory and anti-tumor activity; triple-helix conformation is critical for bioactivity.

## Relevance to Turkey Tail Research Questions

- **Q1 (Efficacy/Clinical Evidence):** Low direct relevance. Turkey tail is mentioned once in passing as an adjuvant therapy example. No clinical trial data or specific PSK/PSP efficacy results are discussed.
- **Q2 (Mechanisms):** Moderate indirect relevance. The paper reviews general polysaccharide anti-tumor mechanisms (cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, anti-angiogenesis, immunomodulation) that apply to the broader class including turkey tail polysaccharides.
- **Q3 (Bioactive Compounds):** Moderate indirect relevance. The structure-activity discussion of beta-(1->3)-glucans with beta-(1->6) branches and triple-helix conformation is directly relevant to understanding PSK/PSP structure, though neither is named specifically.
- **Q4 (Dosage/Safety):** Low direct relevance. No turkey tail-specific dosage or safety data. General note that polysaccharides have low toxicity.
- **Q5 (Immune Modulation):** Moderate indirect relevance. Reviews general polysaccharide immune activation pathways (macrophages, NK cells, T/B lymphocytes, cytokines TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma, IL-2/4/6/12).
- **Q6 (Bioavailability):** Low-moderate relevance. Discusses poor oral bioavailability of large polysaccharides and nanoparticle/chitosan carrier approaches.
- **Q7 (Comparison):** Minimal relevance. No direct comparisons between turkey tail and other treatments.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- Turkey tail polysaccharides cited as adjuvant cancer therapy with low toxicity and high safety (line 64-75)
- Beta-(1->3)-glucans with beta-(1->6) branches show prominent anti-tumor activity via receptor interaction (line 437-445)
- Triple-helix lentinan: 49.5% Sarcoma 180 inhibition vs. 5-FU at 50.5%; single-chain form only 12.3% (line 449-455)
- Fungal beta-glucan with chemo/radiotherapy reduces immune depression and accelerates WBC production (line 79-84)
- Anti-tumor mechanisms: cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, anti-angiogenesis, immunomodulation (line 277-383)
- Polysaccharides activate macrophages, T/B lymphocytes, NK cells, and cytokines (TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma, IL-2/4/6/12) (line 349-363)
- Large polysaccharide molecules have poor oral bioavailability due to size preventing intestinal absorption (line 537-597)

## Methodology

- Narrative review covering literature on polysaccharide extraction, purification, structure-activity relationships, anti-tumor mechanisms, bioavailability, and clinical applications.
- No systematic search methodology described (no PRISMA, no inclusion/exclusion criteria).
- Covers studies primarily from the last 5 years at time of publication (2017-2022).

## Limitations

- Turkey tail is only tangentially mentioned; this is NOT a turkey tail-focused review.
- No systematic review methodology; selection bias possible in cited studies.
- Most mechanism evidence comes from in vitro and animal models, with limited clinical data.
- Reference list includes Habtemariam (2020) for turkey tail, suggesting this review relies on secondary sources for turkey tail claims.

## Claims to Verify

- The triple-helix vs. single-chain inhibition percentages (49.5% vs. 12.3%) are attributed to Zhang et al. (ref 85) for lentinan, not turkey tail -- these should not be cited as turkey tail evidence.
- The general claim about fungal beta-glucan reducing immune depression from chemo/radiotherapy (line 79-84) is attributed to ref 16 and is about fungal beta-glucan broadly, not turkey tail specifically.
