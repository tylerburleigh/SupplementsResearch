# src-076: Medicinal Mushroom Supplements in Cancer: A Systematic Review of Clinical Studies

**Citation:** Narayanan S, Rozman de Mores A, Cohen L, et al. Current Oncology Reports. 2023. DOI: 10.1007/s11912-023-01408-2

**Quality:** ok | Peer-reviewed systematic review | 75K chars, 622 sentences

## Core Findings

1. **PSK (turkey tail) was the most studied mushroom extract** in this systematic review of 39 clinical studies (2010-2020), appearing in 10 of 39 studies. A survival benefit was found in 4 gastric cancer studies using PSK in the adjuvant setting.

2. **PSK consistently showed survival benefits in gastric cancer**: improved 3-year RFS, 5-year OS (54.8% vs 45.55%, P=0.031) in stage IIIA/IIIB, and prolonged OS (HR 0.608, P=0.041) in advanced disease. Benefits were particularly pronounced in MHC class I-negative and PD-L1-negative subpopulations.

3. **Evidence is insufficient for routine clinical recommendation**: despite favorable signals, limitations (small sample sizes, lack of RCT design, heterogeneity) preclude definitive clinical guidance. More large RCTs are needed.

## Key Evidence -- PSK/Turkey Tail Specific

### Survival Outcomes (Gastric Cancer)
- Akagi et al.: 3-year OS 62.2% PSK vs 12.5% control (P=0.038), N=31, stage II-III gastric cancer
- Tanaka et al.: Improved OS with PSK in early tumor recurrence (P=0.023) and pN3 metastasis (P=0.032), N=254
- Hsu et al.: 5-year OS improved in stage IIIA/IIIB (P=0.031), especially PD-L1-negative subpopulation, N=918
- Namikawa et al.: OS prolonged with S-1 plus PSK (HR 0.608, P=0.041), N=190
- Ito et al.: 3-year RFS 65% PSK vs 47% chemo-only in MHC-negative cases; in pN2+ cases, 68% vs 28%, N=349
- Fukuchi et al.: PSK combined with S-1 improved RFS with more treatment cycles (>=7), N=136

### Survival Outcomes (Colorectal Cancer)
- Miyake et al.: Phase III RCT, UFT/PSK NOT non-inferior to UFT/LV (3-yr DFS 72.1% vs 82.3%), N=357

### Immune Markers
- PSK lowered CD57+ T cells (16% vs 28%, P=0.048) at 3-month follow-up in stage II/III gastric cancer
- PSK increased NK cell counts in peripheral blood and cytotoxic T cells in peri-tumoral mucosa in rectal cancer (N=30)
- PSK reduced plasma TGF-beta (21.6 to 4.5 ng/mL) as neoadjuvant in advanced gastric cancer (N=31)
- PSK combined with preoperative chemoradiation improved immune function in rectal adenocarcinoma

### Adverse Events
- PSK studies reported mostly grade 2 or lower SEs (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, muscle pain)
- PSK was well-tolerated across studies

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review of clinical studies, PRISMA-guided
- **Databases:** Medline (Ovid), Embase (Ovid), Scopus (Wiley), Cochrane Library
- **Time period:** January 2010 - December 2020
- **Inclusion:** Clinical trial/cohort study >10 patients, English, mushroom as intervention with standard cancer therapy, confirmed cancer diagnosis, reporting cancer-related outcomes
- **Studies included:** 39 (15 RCTs, 11 prospective, 13 retrospective) out of 2349 screened
- **Assessment:** Cochrane risk of bias tool
- **Geographic distribution:** Mostly Asian countries (Japan 20, China 9, Korea 2, Taiwan 2); only 1 US study
- **Analysis:** Descriptive (no meta-analysis due to heterogeneity)

## Limitations (Per Authors)

- Small sample sizes across most studies
- Lack of randomization and blinding in many studies (high/unclear risk of bias)
- Did not search Chinese-language databases
- Excluded non-English articles
- Restricted to 2010-2020 (missed older and newer studies)
- Geographic concentration in Asian populations -- generalizability uncertain
- Heterogeneity of cancers, mushroom types, doses, and outcomes precluded meta-analysis

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Clinical evidence):** Directly relevant -- PSK is the most-studied mushroom in this review with 10 clinical studies, mostly in gastric cancer adjuvant setting
- **Q2 (Efficacy):** Strong evidence for PSK survival benefit in gastric cancer (4 studies showing survival advantage); one negative result in colorectal cancer
- **Q3 (Mechanisms):** Documents immune mechanisms -- CD57+ T cell suppression, NK cell activation, TGF-beta reduction, cytotoxic T cell increases
- **Q4 (Dosing):** PSK dose consistently 3 g/day across studies
- **Q5 (Biomarkers):** MHC class I status, PD-L1 status, CD57+ T cells, TGF-beta, NK cell counts identified as relevant biomarkers
- **Q6 (Comparative):** PSK compared to leucovorin (negative), various chemotherapy regimens (positive)
- **Q7 (Safety):** Generally well-tolerated; grade 2 or lower AEs; no major safety concerns for PSK specifically
