# src-093: Phase 1 Clinical Trial of Trametes versicolor in Women with Breast Cancer

**Citation:** Torkelson CJ, Sweet E, Martzen MR, Sasagawa M, Wenner CA, Gay J, Putiri A, Standish LJ. ISRN Oncology. 2012;2012:251632. doi:10.5402/2012/251632. PMCID: PMC3369477.

**Quality Assessment:** Full text available from PubMed Central (open access). Peer-reviewed. Content is authoritative and complete with methods, results, discussion, and references.

## Core Findings

1. A phase I dose escalation trial demonstrated that oral Trametes versicolor (freeze-dried mycelial powder) at 3, 6, and 9 grams/day for 6 weeks was safe and well tolerated in women with breast cancer following radiotherapy, with no serious treatment-related adverse events.

2. Higher doses (6g and 9g/day) were associated with earlier recovery of radiation-induced lymphopenia and trends toward improved NK cell functional activity; the 9g dose produced statistically significant increases in CD8+ cytotoxic T cells (P=0.0003) and the 6g dose significantly increased CD19+ B cells (P=0.0334).

3. The maximum tolerated dose was not reached -- the study only recruited through the 9g cohort due to strict eligibility criteria and recruitment challenges. The results justify proceeding to phase II randomized controlled dose-response trials.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Safety (Primary Endpoint)
- 9 adverse events total: 7 mild, 1 moderate, 1 severe
- Severe AE: anxiety attack in one participant (6g cohort), likely unrelated to study medication
- Moderate AE: fatigue secondary to UTI (6g cohort), unlikely related
- Mild AEs: heartburn, heart palpitations, constipation, chest pain, fever, radiation dermatitis, cold/flu symptoms
- No nausea or GI upset reported
- Participants tolerated up to 9 capsules three times daily without difficulty
- No dose-limiting toxicity (Grade 2+) observed at any dose level

### Dosing Details
- Product: Trametes versicolor freeze-dried mycelial powder, 500mg capsules
- Source: Fungi Perfecti, Inc. (Paul Stamets), Olympia, WA; encapsulated by Beehive Botanicals, Hayward, WI
- FDA IND approval #75,405 obtained in 2007
- 3g cohort: 2 divided doses daily
- 6g cohort: 2 divided doses daily
- 9g cohort: 3 divided doses daily
- 6 weeks of treatment followed by 3-week washout
- Original escalation plan went up to 24g/day; only 3, 6, and 9g cohorts were recruited

### Immune Results
- Combined analysis with prior observational study (N=23 total): RT significantly reduced lymphocytes from 1.027+/-0.298 to 0.681+/-0.254 (P<0.001, 20% drop)
- RT significantly reduced NK cell activity from 19.941+/-18.959 to 9.872+/-13.454 (P=0.043)
- CD8+ T cells: significant increase in 9g group vs 3g and 6g (F(2,6)=42.04, P=0.0003)
- CD19+ B cells: significant increase in 6g group vs 3g (F(2,6)=6.312, P=0.0334)
- CD4+ T cells and CD16+/56+ NK cell counts: no significant changes
- NK cell functional activity trended upward at 6g dose (not statistically significant due to small N=3 per cohort)

## Methodology

- **Design:** Phase I, two-center, dose escalation study (standard 3+3 design)
- **Sites:** University of Minnesota Cancer Center (Minneapolis, MN) and Bastyr University (Kenmore, WA)
- **Population:** Women aged 21-75 with Stage I-III breast cancer who completed surgery and chemotherapy and were about to start radiotherapy
- **Sample:** 11 enrolled, 9 completed (2 withdrew due to transportation difficulties)
- **Completed participant characteristics:** Age 38-68 years; 6 ER-negative, 3 ER-positive; 3 Stage I, 2 Stage II, 4 Stage III
- **Outcome measures:** Primary: safety (adverse events by CTCAE v3.0). Secondary: CBC with differential, NK cell activity (K562 target lysis), T/B/NK cell subsets, T regulatory cell assay, phagocytic index, cytokine levels
- **Statistical analysis:** Independent sample t-tests, one-way ANOVA with Tukey post hoc; data combined with prior observational study (N=14) to increase power
- **Protocol violation:** One completed participant did not undergo chemotherapy prior to radiation

## Limitations

- Very small sample size: only 3 participants per dose cohort (N=9 completed total)
- Not designed to evaluate dose-dependent changes in immune markers -- immune findings are preliminary trends
- Combined analysis with observational study introduces potential confounding (non-randomized historical controls)
- MTD was not determined because recruitment to 12g cohort and higher was not achieved
- No placebo control group
- Multiple immune endpoints tested without correction for multiple comparisons
- Generalizability limited to post-radiotherapy breast cancer patients who received triple therapy (surgery + chemotherapy + radiation)

## Claims to Verify

- **Combined N=23 analysis:** The combining of observational study (N=14) with dose escalation study (N=9) for immune analysis is methodologically noted. The observational study had identical eligibility criteria but was not randomized. This combined analysis is the basis for the statistically significant lymphocyte and NK cell findings.
- **"3 grams per day is the most commonly used dose in naturopathic medical practice":** Stated without specific citation (line ~301). The reference [9] is a textbook chapter, not a systematic survey.
- **Participant count:** The paper states "Between January 2008 and June 2010 eleven women... were enrolled" and "Nine women completed the study." This is internally consistent.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (What is turkey tail / bioactive compounds):** Background on PSK/PSP as hot water extracts of Tv with anticancer activity; mechanism via Toll-like receptor 2 agonist activity enhancing innate and adaptive immunity.
- **Q2 (Clinical evidence / cancer outcomes):** References PSK use in Japan during and after radiation/chemotherapy; this trial is a preliminary safety study, not powered for clinical outcomes.
- **Q3 (Immune modulation mechanisms):** Strong evidence for lymphocyte recovery, NK cell activity trends, CD8+ T cell and CD19+ B cell increases at higher doses. Shows RT-induced immune deficits that Tv may partially correct.
- **Q4 (Recommended dosing):** Critical source -- tested 3, 6, and 9g/day doses; 9g/day was the highest tested and was safe. 3g/day is noted as the most common clinical practice dose. 6g may optimize NK cell recovery.
- **Q5 (Preclinical data):** References preclinical animal studies supporting Tv benefits for both ER-negative and ER-positive cancers.
- **Q6 (Safety / side effects in humans):** Primary endpoint of this trial -- comprehensive adverse event data across all three dose levels. Strong evidence of safety up to 9g/day.
- **Q7 (Drug interactions):** Not directly addressed in this study.
