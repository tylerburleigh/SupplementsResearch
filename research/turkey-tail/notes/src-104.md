# Source src-104: Trametes Versicolor and Dictyophora Indusiata Champions of Medicinal Mushrooms

**Citation:** Elkhateeb WA, Elnahas MO, Thomas PW, Daba GM (2020). Open Access Journal of Pharmaceutical Research, 4(1). DOI: 10.23880/oajpr-16000192

**Type:** Review article | **Citations:** 7 | **Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

1. **PSP and PSK are the primary bioactive compounds from Trametes versicolor**, with PSP consisting of beta-glucans and proteoglycans showing immunomodulatory, antitumor, antioxidant, antihepatopathic, and antihyperlipidemic activities. PSP molecular weight varies by source: 36 kDa (mycelium), 45 kDa (fermentation broth), 75 kDa (fruiting body). PSK is a proteoglycan of ~100 kDa with beta-(1,4) glucose backbone and beta-(1,3)/(1,6) side chains.

2. **Immunomodulatory mechanisms are well-characterized**: PSK acts as a TLR2/TLR4 agonist; PSP stimulates TLR4/TRAF6 expression, enhances NK cell activity, activates complement, and promotes T/B lymphocyte and macrophage activation. Both innate and adaptive immunity are modulated via beta-glucan recognition by TLR-2, TLR-4, TLR-6, and CR3 receptors.

3. **Dictyophora indusiata polysaccharides show antioxidant and antimicrobial properties**: Water extract achieved 97.35% DPPH scavenging at 2 mg/ml, with documented antimicrobial activity at 200 mg/ml. DIP exhibits anticancer, antitumor, anti-proliferative, and neuroprotection effects per modern studies.

## Key Evidence

- PSP oral LD50 in mice: 20 g/kg; IV LD50: 300.36 mg/kg -- indicating minimum acute toxicity
- No chronic toxicity at 200x clinical dose (rat) and 100x (monkey); no mutagenic/teratogenic effects
- Clinical trial: PSP reduced lipid levels significantly in 240 hyperlipidemia patients
- PSP reduces ALT/AST serum levels and increases HBVDNA/HBeAg/HBsAg negative conversion in hepatitis treatment
- PSP scavenges free radicals in CCl4-induced liver injury in mice; synergistic effect with vitamin E
- 13 T. versicolor-based drugs approved by China SFDA
- Extracellular PSP: ~99.2% D-glucose, beta(1-3) main chain, beta(1-6) branches
- Intracellular PSP: glycopeptide, 30-60% polysaccharide, 10-30% protein

## Methodology

This is a narrative review article synthesizing published literature on Trametes versicolor and Dictyophora indusiata polysaccharides. No original experimental data is presented. The review draws on 57 references covering biochemical characterization, in vitro studies, animal models, and clinical observations. The scope is broad but shallow for any single claim -- most findings are reported as summaries of prior work without detailed methodology.

## Limitations

- Review article with no original data; all claims depend on quality of cited primary sources
- Many cited studies are in Chinese-language journals with limited international accessibility
- Clinical claims (e.g., 240-patient hyperlipidemia trial) reference Chinese-language sources without detailed trial design information
- No systematic methodology for literature search described
- Published in a low-impact open-access journal (Open Access Journal of Pharmaceutical Research)
- Only 7 citations as of retrieval date suggests limited impact/uptake
- Dictyophora indusiata section is notably thinner than Trametes versicolor section
- The hyperlipidemia trial sample size of "240 patients" appears as an inline note without formal trial registration or detailed methodology
