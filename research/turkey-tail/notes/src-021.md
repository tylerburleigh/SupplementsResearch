## Core Findings

1. The best-studied clinical dose for PSK/PSP extract is approximately 3 grams per day, typically divided into three 1-gram doses, based on large meta-analyses and RCTs involving thousands of patients (primarily in Japanese and Chinese oncology settings).
2. For general immune support, 1-2 grams of quality extract or 3-4 grams of whole mushroom powder daily is recommended; whole mushroom powder requires higher doses due to chitin in cell walls limiting bioavailability.
3. Turkey Tail has a strong safety profile with generally mild side effects (bloating, gas, loose stools); benefits are linked to consistent long-term use (minimum 8-12 weeks) with no evidence of diminished effectiveness over time.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Dosing Protocols by Preparation Type
| Preparation | Dose Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| PSK/PSP standardized extract | 1-3.6 g/day | Strongest clinical backing; most studied: 1 g x3/day |
| Hot water / dual extract | 500 mg - 2 g/day | Look for 25-30% polysaccharide standardization |
| Whole mushroom powder | 2-5 g/day | Up to 9 g/day in clinical trials; higher doses needed due to chitin |

### Specific Trial Data
- **Breast cancer trial (Torkelson et al.):** Phase 1, up to 9 g/day whole powder, ~12 weeks, dose-dependent immune changes, good tolerability
- **Colorectal cancer meta-analysis (Eliza et al.):** Improved survival outcomes when PSK combined with chemotherapy
- **Duration guidance:** Short-term 4-12 weeks (tolerance/seasonal), medium-term 3-6 months (immune/gut), long-term 6+ months (oncology)

### Safety Profile
- Mild effects: bloating, gas, loose stools at higher intakes, nausea on empty stomach, darkened stools/fingernails (harmless)
- Rare: allergic reactions, skin irritation
- No serious adverse events consistently attributed to Turkey Tail in major trials
- Contraindications: pregnancy/breastfeeding, autoimmune conditions, immunosuppressants, blood thinners, pre-surgery, mushroom allergies

## Methodology

This is a secondary source / review article, not a primary study. It synthesizes clinical research findings on dosing and cites two specific footnoted studies:
1. Eliza WL, Fai CK, Chung LP -- systematic review and meta-analysis of Yun Zhi (Coriolus versicolor) survival in cancer patients
2. Torkelson CJ et al. -- Phase 1 clinical trial of Trametes versicolor in women with breast cancer

Additional references: Kidd PM (mushroom glucans/proteoglycans in cancer treatment); Cui J, Chisti Y (polysaccharopeptides of Coriolus versicolor).

## Limitations

- **Commercial bias:** Published by Mycobio, a mushroom supplement company; article links to their own products. The dosing guidance may be self-serving (encouraging extract purchases over cheaper powder).
- **Not peer-reviewed:** This is a blog article, not a systematic review in a journal.
- **No direct citations with full bibliographic details:** Footnotes list authors and titles but not journal names, years, DOIs, or volume/page numbers, making verification harder.
- **Dose recommendations for non-oncology use are loosely sourced:** The 1-2 g/day extract recommendation for general immune support is stated without specific study citations.
- **No primary data presented:** The article synthesizes other studies but does not present new experimental findings.

## Claims to Verify

- **"3 grams per day of PSK"** -- This is a widely cited figure but should be cross-checked against the actual meta-analyses (Eliza et al.) to confirm the specific dosing protocols used across included studies.
- **"9 grams daily" in breast cancer trial** -- Verify against Torkelson et al. Phase 1 trial; Phase 1 trials typically use dose escalation, so the 9g may be the maximum rather than the standard dose.
- **"25-30% polysaccharides"** standardization -- This figure appears without citation; verify whether this is an industry norm or has research backing.
- **"No evidence suggesting diminished effectiveness over time"** -- This is a strong negative claim that may simply reflect lack of long-term studies rather than positive evidence of sustained efficacy.
