# src-105: Chemopreventive Effect of PSP Through Targeting of Prostate Cancer Stem Cell-Like Population

**Citation:** Luk S-U, Lee TK-W, Liu J, et al. (2011) PLoS ONE 6(5): e19804. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0019804

**Venue:** PLoS ONE | **Year:** 2011 | **Citations:** 37 | **Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

This study demonstrates that polysaccharopeptide (PSP) from *Coriolus versicolor* (Turkey tail) targets prostate cancer stem cells (CSCs) both in vitro and in vivo, and completely prevents prostate tumor formation in a transgenic mouse model when administered orally for 20 weeks. The anti-CSC mechanism appears to involve inhibition of the Akt/beta-catenin signaling pathway rather than direct cytotoxicity or apoptosis induction.

## Key Evidence

### In vitro findings
- **CSC marker downregulation:** PSP (250-500 ug/mL, 48-72 hr) significantly downregulated CD133 and CD44 protein expression in PC-3 cells in a dose- and time-dependent manner. Notably, mRNA levels were unchanged, suggesting post-transcriptional regulation.
- **No cytotoxicity at effective doses:** MTT assays showed no effect on cell viability at 48 hr despite CSC marker suppression. No cell cycle arrest or apoptosis was observed (no sub-G1 population, no changes in Bax/Bcl-2/PARP cleavage).
- **Prostasphere formation:** PSP completely abolished primary prostasphere formation in both PC-3 and DU145 cells at 500 ug/mL. Secondary prostasphere formation was inhibited by >70% (250 ug/mL) and >90% (500 ug/mL), p<0.001 and p<0.0001 respectively.
- **Signaling pathway:** PSP inhibited Akt phosphorylation at Ser473 and caused complete disappearance of beta-catenin expression, implicating the Pten/Akt/beta-catenin stem cell maintenance pathway.

### In vivo findings
- **Tumorigenicity (xenograft):** PSP pre-treated PC-3-luc cells showed reduced tumor initiation in SCID mice (3/8 mice tumor-free vs 0/8 in control, p=0.07 -- not statistically significant).
- **Chemoprevention (transgenic):** 20-week oral PSP at 300 mg/kg completely prevented tumor formation in TgMAP mice (0/5 tumors in PSP group vs 4/4 in control, p<0.05, Fisher's exact test). Three of five PSP-fed mice had PIN only; two had completely normal prostates.
- **Dose/duration dependence:** 4-week treatment at 200 mg/kg starting at 14 weeks of age failed to inhibit PIN development, indicating duration and dose are critical.
- **Safety:** No adverse effects from 20-week oral PSP (300 mg/kg) as assessed by body weight and physical signs.

## Methodology

- **Cell lines:** PC-3 and DU145 (androgen-independent prostate cancer lines from ATCC)
- **PSP source:** Wonder Herb Health Products, Ltd. (Yun-zhi extract). Dissolved in water at 30 mg/mL stock.
- **In vitro assays:** Western blotting (CD133, CD44, Akt, beta-catenin, Bax, Bcl-2, PARP), MTT viability, flow cytometry for cell cycle, prostasphere formation (primary and secondary), spheroid formation in polyHEMA-coated plates
- **In vivo models:**
  - Orthotopic xenograft: PC-3-luc cells (2.5x10^4) injected into dorsal prostate of 8-week-old SCID mice (n=8/group)
  - Transgenic chemoprevention: TgMAP mice (C57/BL6 background, SV40 Tag-driven prostate tumor model); short-term arm: n=5 PSP + n=4 control; long-term arm: n=5 PSP + n=4 control
- **PSP administration:** Oral gavage, 5 days/week
- **Statistics:** Student's t-test for in vitro; Fisher's exact test for tumor incidence; p<0.05 significance threshold

## Limitations

- **Small sample sizes:** Only 4-5 mice per group in the transgenic model experiments. The tumor prevention result (0/5 vs 4/4) is striking but based on very few animals.
- **Non-significant xenograft result:** The tumorigenicity experiment showed only p=0.07, failing to reach conventional significance with n=8/group.
- **Single cell line focus:** Most in vitro work was on PC-3; DU145 was only used for prostasphere assays. No androgen-dependent cell line (LNCaP) was tested for CSC effects.
- **PSP not fully characterized:** The PSP was provided by a commercial vendor without detailed biochemical characterization (molecular weight, purity, polysaccharide composition).
- **Transgenic model limitations:** TgMAP model uses SV40 Tag, which may not fully recapitulate human prostate carcinogenesis. Authors acknowledge this.
- **No dose-response in vivo:** Only two dose/regimen combinations were tested; the optimal dose and minimum effective duration remain undefined.

## Claims to Verify

- **"0/5 PSP-treated mice developed tumors vs 4/4 controls":** Verified from lines 210-216 and the Figure 5 description (lines 343-348). The p<0.05 by Fisher's exact test is plausible for this distribution but the sample size is very small.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (bioactive compounds):** PSP is the sole compound studied. Demonstrates anti-CSC and chemopreventive properties. No comparison to other turkey tail compounds.
- **Q2 (mechanisms of action):** Identifies Akt/beta-catenin pathway inhibition as a likely mechanism for CSC suppression. Post-transcriptional regulation of CD133/CD44 noted but not fully explained.
- **Q3 (signaling pathways):** Direct evidence for Akt (Ser473) phosphorylation inhibition and beta-catenin downregulation.
- **Q4 (in vitro evidence):** Strong in vitro evidence across multiple assay types (marker expression, functional sphere formation).
- **Q5 (in vivo evidence):** Key study for in vivo evidence -- complete tumor prevention in transgenic mice with oral PSP.
- **Q6 (safety/toxicity):** No adverse effects noted in mice over 20 weeks of oral administration. No formal toxicology data.
- **Q7 (clinical evidence):** No clinical data. Authors recommend clinical trials based on preclinical results.
