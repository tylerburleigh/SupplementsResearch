**Citation:** Mironczuk-Chodakowska, I., Kujawowicz, K., & Witkowska, A.M. (2021). *Nutrients*, 13, 3960. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13113960

**Type:** Peer-reviewed review article (MDPI, open access)
**Quality:** ok -- full text extracted, substantive content across all sections

## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive review of mushroom beta-glucans covering structure-function relationships, immunomodulatory mechanisms, and potential applications including COVID-19. It provides a dedicated section on Krestin (PSK) from *Trametes versicolor* and references PSP alongside it. The review synthesizes the receptor-mediated pharmacokinetics of mushroom beta-glucans and their triple helix conformation as a key determinant of bioactivity.

## Turkey-Tail-Specific Evidence

### PSK (Krestin) Structure and Properties
- PSK is a protein-bound beta-glucan extracted from *Trametes versicolor* (L.) Lloyd
- Classified as a heteroglycan; molecular weight ~100,000 Da on average
- Contains 1,3-beta-glucan and 1,4-beta-glucan structural elements (multi-sugar-protein complex)
- Table 1 lists both PSK and PSP as products of *Trametes versicolor* with these structural features
- Popular drug in Japan; used as complementary cancer therapy
- Antitumor mechanism: stimulates T lymphocytes and antigen-presenting cells for recognition and destruction of neoplastic cells
- Clinical trials confirm positive effects in chemotherapy patients for breast, liver, stomach, colon, lung, and prostate cancer
- Also shows immune-boosting, antiviral, hypocholesterolemic, and prebiotic activity

### Turkey Tail Antioxidant Properties
- Polysaccharides isolated from *Trametes versicolor* show significant antioxidant properties
- Exhibit chelating properties that reduce lipid oxidation

## General Beta-Glucan Pharmacology (Relevant to Q3)

### Structure-Activity Relationships
- Mushroom beta-glucans: beta-1,3-glucans with short beta-1,6-side chains
- Triple helix conformation is a key determinant of immunomodulatory and anticancer properties
- Beta-1,3-glucans with beta-1,6 branches form triple helix in natural form and aqueous solutions
- High-MW molecules with beta-1,3-bonds in base chain show best anticancer properties
- Bioactivity varies with monomer type, molecule size, branching degree, solubility, and conformation
- Mushroom beta-glucans gained BRM (biological response modifier) status

### Pharmacokinetics / Oral Bioavailability
- Most beta-1,3-glucans resist gastric juice; pass unchanged to small intestine
- Bind to macrophage dectin-1 receptors in intestinal wall
- Transported to spleen, lymph nodes, and bone marrow
- In macrophages, high-MW beta-glucans are degraded to smaller fragments
- Smaller fragments bind complement receptor 3 (CR3) on immune cells including granulocytes
- This stimulates immune response directed against tumor cells

### Receptor Mechanisms
- **Dectin-1:** Most abundant receptor on dendritic cells, monocytes, macrophages, neutrophils, T lymphocytes. Activates phagocytosis, endocytosis, ROS production, and cytokine production (TNF-alpha, IL-2, IL-10, IL-12)
- **TLRs (2, 4):** Activate innate immune response; TLR2 via NF-kB induces TNF-alpha and IL-12
- **CR3:** On neutrophils, monocytes, NK cells (not macrophages). Increases leukocyte adhesion and activates cytotoxicity against tumor cells
- **Scavenger receptors:** On endothelial cells; activate MAPK, PI3K, eNOS signaling
- **LacCer:** On neutrophils and endothelial cells; activates NF-kB, MIP-2, TNF-alpha production

### Trained Immunity (TRIM)
- Beta-glucans act as TRIM inducers
- Innate immune cells undergo metabolic, mitochondrial, and epigenetic reprogramming
- Results in memory phenotype with enhanced immune response to secondary pathogen exposure
- Protective activity demonstrated against secondary fungal, bacterial, or viral infections

### Prebiotic Properties
- Beta-glucans resist human digestive enzymes, pass through GI tract retaining structure
- Both soluble and insoluble mushroom-derived beta-glucans support probiotic bacteria growth
- Stimulate growth of *Lactobacillus acidophilus* and *Bifidobacterium bifidum*
- Increase production of short-chain fatty acids (propionate, butyrate)

### COVID-19 Potential
- Beta-glucans proposed to activate macrophages and dendritic cells via dectin-1, TLR, CR3
- Enhance antigen presentation on MHC to T lymphocytes, promoting antibody production
- Most studies on COVID-19 used yeast-derived or shiitake-derived glucans, not turkey tail specifically
- Mushroom glucans can be administered orally with high safety profile

## Methodology

- Narrative review covering literature through 2021
- No systematic search methodology described
- Covers multiple mushroom species and beta-glucan types
- Includes Table 1 (studied beta-glucans), Table 2 (COVID-19 studies), and multiple figures of chemical structures and mechanisms

## Limitations

- Review, not primary data; relies on secondary analysis of cited studies
- Turkey tail section is relatively brief compared to lentinan and other glucans
- Most COVID-19 evidence comes from yeast or shiitake glucans, not turkey tail specifically
- No quantitative meta-analysis or systematic quality assessment of cited studies
- Claims about clinical trials for PSK reference other reviews (Maehara et al. 2012) rather than primary trial data
- Published 2021; does not cover more recent clinical evidence

## Claims to Verify

1. **PSK molecular weight of 100,000 Da** -- appears as "on average" without specifying range or measurement method in this review; verify against Maehara et al. 2012.
2. **"Numerous clinical trials" for PSK** -- this review cites Maehara et al. 2012 (a review itself); the actual number and quality of trials should be verified from primary sources.
3. **Beta-glucan content range of 3.1%-46.5% in macrofungi** -- cited from references [25,35,36]; verify the upper bound (46.5%) as it seems exceptionally high.
