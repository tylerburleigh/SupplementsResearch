## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive review examining the dual roles of TLR2 and cGAS-STING innate immune pathways in breast cancer progression and therapy. The authors argue that both pathways exhibit context-dependent pro- and anti-tumor effects, creating a complex therapeutic landscape. TLR2 is the most expressed TLR in triple-negative breast cancer and correlates with poor prognosis, yet TLR2 agonists like PSK can stimulate antitumor immunity in specific contexts.

## Key Evidence on PSK (Turkey Tail / Trametes versicolor)

- **PSK as TLR2 agonist**: The mushroom-derived polysaccharide krestin (PSK) acts as a natural TLR2 ligand, stimulating NK cell-mediated antitumor responses in multiple cancer models (lines 174-176).
- **PSK + trastuzumab synergy**: PSK potentiates trastuzumab-induced antibody-dependent cell cytotoxicity (ADCC) of HER2+ breast cancer cells by stimulating NK cell activation. Demonstrated in mouse models and confirmed with human NK cells and MDA-MB-231 cells (lines 176-179, 574-579).
- **No clinical trials**: Despite promising preclinical data, no clinical trials have been conducted with TLR2 agonists (including PSK) in breast cancer patients (lines 580-581).

## Key Evidence on TLR2 Dual Role

- TLR2 mRNA is significantly higher in breast cancer vs. normal tissue, especially in triple-negative and HER2+ subtypes (line 204-206).
- High TLR2 expression correlates with poor relapse-free and overall survival in breast cancer (lines 215-216).
- **Pro-tumor mechanisms (intrinsic)**: NF-kB activation promotes survival/proliferation; MAPK pathway induces EMT; promotes cancer stem cell (CSC) self-renewal via HMGB1-TLR2-NF-kB-IL-6/TGF-beta-STAT3/Smad3 axis; mediates chemoresistance to doxorubicin.
- **Pro-tumor mechanisms (extrinsic)**: Expands Tregs, induces MDSCs from bone marrow precursors, polarizes macrophages to M2, promotes Breg differentiation -- all creating immunosuppressive TME.
- **Anti-tumor mechanisms**: TLR2 agonists stimulate DC maturation, NK cell proliferation/cytotoxicity, M1 macrophage differentiation, and CD8+ T cell responses -- but predominantly in non-breast-cancer models or when combined with vaccines.

## Key Evidence on cGAS-STING Pathway

- Acute activation produces type I IFNs with antitumor effects; chronic activation shifts to NF-kB signaling promoting tumor progression.
- STING agonists in clinical trials have largely failed; DMXAA (the only phase III candidate) only binds murine STING, not human.
- Proposed strategy: combine STING agonists with agents restoring canonical IRF3 signaling (e.g., APR-246 for p53 reactivation) plus ICB.
