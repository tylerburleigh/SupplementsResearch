# src-134: In-vivo and in-vitro wound healing and tissue repair effect of Trametes versicolor polysaccharide extract

**Authors:** Teymoorian, Nouri, Moghimi (University of Tehran)
**Venue:** Scientific Reports (2024), 15 citations
**DOI:** 10.1038/s41598-024-54565-0

## Core Findings

T. versicolor polysaccharide extract demonstrated significant wound healing properties both in vitro and in vivo. In vitro, the extract promoted dose-dependent fibroblast proliferation (63% viability at 1250 ug/ml), achieved 100% scratch closure in 24h, and upregulated key wound healing genes (TGF-beta1, FGF2, IL-1beta). In vivo, topical application of 0.01 g extract achieved 100% wound closure in mice at 14 days versus 60% in untreated controls, with histological evidence of increased fibroblasts, collagen, and epidermal thickness.

## Methodology

- **Extract preparation:** T. versicolor cultured on PDA (14 days), transferred to PDB broth (14 days, 30 C, 150 rpm). Polysaccharide extracted by cold ethanol precipitation (95% ethanol, 4 C overnight), freeze-dried 2 days. Protein removed by TCA method, lipids by chloroform extraction. Final powder was 40% polysaccharides (quantified by phenol-sulfuric acid method).
- **Antimicrobial:** MIC and MBC against E. coli ATCC8739 and S. aureus ATCC25923 using tetrazolium chloride indicator.
- **Antioxidant:** DPPH assay at concentrations 80-2560 ug/ml; ascorbic acid positive control.
- **Cell proliferation:** MTT assay on L929 fibroblasts (IBRC C10102) at 156.25-1250 ug/ml, 48h incubation.
- **Cell migration:** Scratch assay with 1250 ug/ml extract, 24h, crystal violet staining.
- **Gene expression:** qRT-PCR for FGF2, IL-1beta, TGF-beta1 at 24h and 48h (1250 ug/ml). h-HPRT1 internal control. 2(-delta delta Ct) method via REST-2009.
- **In vivo:** 6 male BALB/c mice (18-20 g, 6 weeks); 4 wounded (2 treated, 2 untreated controls), 2 healthy controls. Two 5 mm punch wounds per mouse. 0.01 g extract applied topically. 14-day observation. Histopathology with H&E and Masson's trichrome staining.

## Key Evidence

### Antimicrobial (Q2)
- MIC: E. coli = 1.1 mg/ml; S. aureus = 4.38 mg/ml
- MBC: E. coli = 2.19 mg/ml; S. aureus = 4.38 mg/ml
- More effective against gram-negative (E. coli) than gram-positive (S. aureus)

### Antioxidant (Q2)
- 53.7% DPPH inhibition at 2560 ug/ml
- IC50 ~ 1300 ug/ml
- Dose-dependent antioxidant activity

### Cell Proliferation (Q4)
- L929 viability: 63 +/- 3% at 1250 ug/ml, 28 +/- 3% at 156.25 ug/ml (48h)
- IC50: 345.7 ug/ml
- No cytotoxicity at any concentration tested
- Dose-dependent proliferation

### Cell Migration (Q4, Q5)
- 100% scratch closure at 24h with 1250 ug/ml extract
- Negative control scratch area: 1.5 x 10^7 um^2 at T0

### Gene Expression / Mechanisms (Q3, Q5)
- TGF-beta1: 6.08-fold (24h), 5.09-fold (48h) -- early-phase wound healing driver
- FGF2: 3.87-fold (24h), 6.38-fold (48h) -- increasing over time, cell proliferation
- IL-1beta: 0.05-fold (24h), 1.82-fold (48h) -- low early inflammation, strong immune response later
- Overall gene expression at 48h was 4x that at 24h
- Discussed potential pathways: PI3K/PCK, MAPKAPK-2, TGF-beta/SMAD

### In Vivo Wound Healing (Q6, Q7)
- 100% healing in treated mice vs 60% in untreated at 14 days
- Fibroblasts: 191 +/- 4 (treated) vs 122 +/- 2 (control) -- ~2-fold increase
- Epidermal thickness: 93 +/- 6 um (treated) vs 46 +/- 2 um (control) -- ~3-fold increase
- Collagen fibers: 49 +/- 1 (treated) vs 22 +/- 2 (control) -- ~3-fold increase
- Vessels: 27 +/- 1 (treated) vs 18 +/- 2 (control) -- ~2-fold increase
- Neutrophils: 39 +/- 3 (treated) vs 72 +/- 6 (control) -- lower inflammation in treated
- Hair follicles: 1.5 +/- 0.5 (treated) vs 0.5 +/- 0.5 (control) -- slight difference
- Dose: 0.01 g applied topically to 5 mm diameter wounds

## Limitations

1. **Very small in vivo sample size:** Only 2 treated mice and 2 untreated controls (plus 2 healthy controls) -- total n=4 wounded animals. This severely limits statistical power and generalizability.
2. **Single dose tested in vivo:** Only 0.01 g was applied; no dose-response curve in the animal model.
3. **No vehicle control:** The untreated group received no treatment at all, rather than a vehicle (e.g., base ointment without extract), making it unclear whether the healing effect is specific to the polysaccharide or partly due to the physical application.
4. **Extract characterization limited:** Only 40% polysaccharide content reported; no detailed structural characterization (monosaccharide composition, molecular weight, linkage analysis) provided.
5. **No comparison to standard wound care:** No positive control (e.g., standard antibiotic cream or commercial wound dressing) was included.
6. **Short observation period:** 14 days only; no long-term follow-up for scar quality or wound recurrence.
7. **No mechanism experiments in vivo:** Gene expression was only measured in vitro; no signaling pathway inhibitors or knockdown experiments to confirm mechanistic claims.

## Claims to Verify

- **"40% polysaccharides" in the final extract** (line 89): Stated once without replication data or detailed compositional analysis.
- **"100% healing" at 14 days** (line 310): Based on only n=2 mice per group; individual mouse data not shown.
- **"60% healing in positive control"** (line 311): Stated as 2 mm diameter remaining from 5 mm initial wound. This is (1-2/5)*100 = 60%. Appears internally consistent but from n=2.
- **P-value < 0.05** claimed for MTT assay and gene expression (lines 263, 351), but with n=2 in vivo, statistical tests are underpowered.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Bioactive compounds):** Polysaccharide extract (40% polysaccharide content) from cultured mycelium; cold ethanol precipitation method.
- **Q2 (In vitro effects):** Strong data on antimicrobial (MIC/MBC), antioxidant (DPPH IC50 ~1300 ug/ml), and cell proliferation (MTT, scratch assay).
- **Q3 (Mechanisms):** Gene expression data for TGF-beta1, FGF2, IL-1beta at 24h/48h; discussion of PI3K, MAPKAPK-2, TGF-beta/SMAD pathways (no direct pathway validation).
- **Q4 (Dose-response):** Dose-dependent proliferation at 156.25-1250 ug/ml; antimicrobial at 0.02-17.5 mg/ml range.
- **Q5 (Synergistic mechanisms):** Combination of antimicrobial, antioxidant, cell proliferative, and anti-inflammatory effects documented.
- **Q6 (In vivo evidence):** Mouse wound healing model with 100% vs 60% closure at 14 days; histopathology data. Very small n.
- **Q7 (Dosing for humans):** 0.01 g topical on 5 mm wound in mice; no human equivalent dose calculated.
