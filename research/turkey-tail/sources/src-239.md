Abstract 
Purpose: Polysaccharide peptide (PSP) extracted from the Coriolus versicolor mushroom is 
frequently suggested as an adjunct to the chemoα or radiotherapy in cancer patients. In 
previous study we have shown that PSP induced a tumor necrosis factorαα (TNFαα)αdependent 
an anapyrexiaαlike response in rats.  Thus, PSP appears a factor which modifies number of 
pathophysiologic responses.  Because of the fact, the PSP is suggested as an potential adjuvant 
used in the cancer therapy during which frequently cancer patients contract a microbial 
infections accompanied by fever, the aim of the present study was to investigate whether or not 
the PSP can modulate a course of the fever in a response to the antigen, such as LPS. 
Materials and methods: Body temperature (Tb) of the male Wistar rats was measured by 
biotelemetry system.  PSP was injected intraperitoneally (i.p.) at a dose of 100 mg kgα1, 2h 
before LPS administration (50 µg kgα1; i.p.).  The levels of interleukin (IL)α6 and TNFαα in
the plasma of rats were estimated 3h and 14h postαinjection of PSP using a standard sandwich 
ELISA kits. 
Results: We report that i.p. preαinjection of PSP 2h before LPS administration expanded the 
duration of endotoxin fever in rats.  This phenomenon was accompanied by a significant 
elevation of the blood ILα6 level of rats both 3h and 14h postαinjection of PSP.  Preαtreatment 
i.p. of the rats with antiαILα6 antibody (30 µg/rat) prevented the PSPαinduced prolongation of
endotoxin fever. 
Conclusions: Based on these data, we conclude that PSP modifies the LPSαinduced fever, in 
ILα6αrelated fashion.

1. Introduction
Polysaccharide peptide (PSP) isolated from Coriolus versicolor strain COVα1, has been 
widely used as adjunct therapy in cancer patients undergoing chemoα or radioαtherapy [1] and 
its nonαtoxic properties under acute and chronic conditions have been confirmed [2].  Clinical 
trials showed that PSP improved the quality of life of patients by decreasing cancer treatmentα 
related symptoms such as fatigue, loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, and pain [3].  This 
mushroomαderived polysaccharide exert its activities primarily via immunomodulation [4]. 
Therefore, it can be classified as a biological response modifier, which is defined as an agent 
capable of modifying the host's biological response by stimulating the immune system and 
thereby eliciting various therapeutic effects [5].  Immunostimulatory effect of PSP (in vitro and 
in vivo) includes elevation of proαinflammatory cytokines, such as interleukinα1β (ILα1β), 
interleukinα6 (ILα6) and tumor necrosis factor α (TNFαα) as well as prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) 
and histamine [3], increase in the production of reactive oxygen and nitrogen intermediates [6], 
natural killer cells (NK) activity, activation of complementα3, Tαcell proliferation [7] and many 
others. 
The above mentioned cytokines and PGE2 secreted by PSPαstimulated cells are important 
components of the physiological mechanism of fever.  This phenomenon is regarded as a part 
of the acuteαphase response to infection, inflammation, injury and trauma [8].  The increase of 
body temperature (Tb) during fever has several advantages over infections: inhibition of 
bacterial growth, increase bactericidal activities of neutrophils and macrophages, T cells 
proliferation and differentiation, B cells proliferation and the production of antibodies or 
stimulation of acuteαphase protein synthesis [9α10].  The initial step in the cascade of events 
leading to fever is considered to be a stimulation of a large number of various immune types of 
cells, including monocytes, macrophages and neutrophils by exogenous stimuli, called 
exogenous pyrogens [11].  These stimuli are represented by bacteria walls components such as 
lipopolisaccharide (LPS), viral components such as doubleαstranded RNA and bacterial

a microbial infections accompanied by fever, the aim of the present study was to investigate 
whether or not the PSP can modulate the course of the fever.  To the best of our knowledge, 
this phenomenon has not yet been studied.  Moreover, our studies aimed to explore the role of 
PSP as a modulator of endotoxin fever in a response to the antigen, such as LPS.

2.2. Polysaccharide peptide and lipopolisaccharide preparation and administration
Polysaccharide peptide (PSP; extract from the Cov 1 strain of Coriolus versicolor; 
MycoMedica, Czech Republic) was dissolved in sterile 0.9% sodium chloride (saline) and 
injected intraperitoneally (i.p.) at a dose of 100 mg kgα1.  As we described previously, this was
the dose of PSP, which modulated the normal Tb in male Wistar rats [18].  In our studies, we 
also tested the lower dose of PSP (50 mg kgα1) causing the smaller decrease of Tb of rats.
However, since the lower dose of PSP did not provoke any significant effect on the LPSα 
induced febrile response in rats (data not shown), the dose of 100 mg kgα1 of PSP was selected
for further experiments. 
LPS extracted from Escherichia coli (0111:B4, Sigma Chemicals) was dissolved in sterile 
0.9% sodium chloride.  Before injection, the stock solution of LPS (2.5 mg mlα1) was diluted in 
a warm sterile saline to the desired concentration, and injected i.p. at a dose of 50 µg kgα1, as 
described previously [19].  All injection solutions were warmed to 37oC before
administration.  PSP was injected at 7:00 a.m., 2h prior to the LPS administration (9:00 a.m.). 
The control rats were administered i.p. with an equivalent volume of pyrogenαfree saline.  The 
rats were briefly restrained and not anesthetized during the injections.  Immediately after the 
injections, the animals were placed in their home cages.

3. Results
3.1. Pre*treatment with PSP expands the duration of endotoxin fever in rats 
Effect of PSP on changes of Tb in male Wistar rats during endotoxin fever is illustrated in 
Fig. 1.  The rats were injected i.p. with PSP at a dose of 100 mg kgα1 at 7:00 a.m., 2h prior to
the LPS administration.  Preαtreatment of the animals with PSP resulted in a significant 
alterations of the postαLPS Tb that can be regarded as a protraction of the timeαcourse of fever 
response to the administration of endotoxin.  As can be seen in Fig. 1, the rats treated with 
PSP followed by LPS responded with fever, which started 3,5h postαinjection of LPS (12:30), 
whereas this phenomenon in the saline/LPSαinjected animals was observed 1,5h postαinjection 
of LPS (10:30).  Moreover, as we described previously [18], PSP administration caused the 
drop in Tb.  However, the Tb of PSP/LPSαtreated rats (38.2±0.2oC) was comparable to the Tb 
of saline/LPSαinjected rats (38.3±0.1oC) measured from 13:30 to 18:00 (p=0.25).  On the
other hand, the rats preαtreated with saline 2h prior to LPS administration returned to Tb 
observed in the nonαtreated group of animals (NT) 12h postαinjection of PSP (19:00), whereas 
this phenomenon was observed in the PSP/LPSαtreated rats only after 21h from injection 
(04:00).  The average Tb of the rats counting from 19:00 to 4:00 for the PSP/LPSαtreated 
animals was 38.3±0.1oC vs. 37.8±0.2oC in the saline/LPS treated rats (p<0.01).  Injection i.p.
of sterile 0.9% sodium chloride (solvent for PSP) 2h prior to the i.p. saline administration 
(solvent for LPS) did not induce alterations in Tb of rats (data not shown).

Immunostimulatory effects of PSP (in vitro and in vivo) include elevation of proα 
inflammatory cytokines, such as ILα6 and TNFαα [3].  Similarly, it is wellαknown, that 
stimulation of immune cells by exogenous stimuli such as LPS leads to synthesis of proα 
inflammatory mediators, among which the most important are cytokines such as ILα6 and 
TNFαα [11, 16, 20].  Experimental data strongly suggest important role of ILα6 as endogenous 
mediators in LPSαinduced fever.  The presence of ILα6 is critical for fever, as seen by the 
absence of the febrile response to peripheral immune challenge in ILα6 knockαout (KO) mice 
as well as in animals treated with ILα6 antiserum [20α21].  In the present data, we showed that 
the preαtreatment of the rats with PSP expands the duration of LPSαinduced fever, and the 
response is ILα6αrelated.  Therefore, we suppose that PSP may intensify the production of ILα
6 by the immune cells such as monocytes, macrophages and neutrophils.  However, further in 
vitro studies are needed to investigate the reactivity of peripheral blood mononuclear cells 
(PBMCs) isolated from the rats preαtreated with PSP and then injected with LPS.  This
