Hericium erinaceus
mushroom, also known as Lion’s Mane, has the ability to promote nerve regeneration, prevent neuronal degeneration, and enhance synaptic plasticity – all crucial factors for optimal brain function.

In this post, we summarise some of the key findings from joint research between Australian and South Korean universities published in the Journal of Neurochemistry in 2023

Ref: DOI: 10.1111/jnc.15767: Hericerin derivatives activate a pan-neurotrophic pathway in central hippocampal neurons converging to ERK1/2 signaling enhancing spatial memory.

The Power of
Hericium Erinaceus
Extracts

The study has revealed that certain extracts from
Hericium erinaceus
hold neurotrophic properties akin to the well-known brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF). These extracts have been proven effective in encouraging neuronal growth, both in lab-grown hippocampal neurons and in live (animal) subjects undergoing learning tasks, ultimately leading to enhanced memory.

The Bioactive Compounds in Lion’s Mane

One of the key factors contributing to the mushroom’s neurotrophic effects lies in its rich composition of terpenoids. Compounds like erinacines, hericenones, hericerins, hericenes, hericenols, and erinacerins have been identified for their bioactivity. Among these, four aromatic compounds – NDPIH, hericene A, corallocin A, and 4-[3′,7′-dimethyl-2′,6′-octadienyl]-2-formyl-3-hydroxy-5-methoxybenzylalcohol – have displayed strong neurotrophic effects.

Promoting Neurotrophic Factors

Neurotrophins play a pivotal role in regulating axonal growth and plasticity within the nervous system. Unfortunately, their expression is typically reduced in the adult central nervous system, presenting a challenge for therapeutic applications. However, the study suggests that supplementing with neurotrophic factors like NGF and BDNF may hold potential in creating conducive environments for post-injury nerve regeneration.

Hericene A: A Game-Changing Compound

A standout revelation of the research is the identification of hericene A, a compound derived from
Hericium erinaceus
, as a potent enhancer of pro-BDNF signalling. This compound has shown promise in significantly improving recognition memory.

Gains in Recognition Memory with Lion’s Mane

In the experiments, Lion’s Mane mushroom extract (A1) and hericene A (A2-C2) were administered to mice over 31 days. The results modestly, but significantly, improved recognition memory. While not a miracle cure, these findings hold potential for those seeking natural ways to support cognitive function.

Future Prospects and Implications

The research paves the way for potential breakthroughs in brain health and neurodegenerative disease treatment. By exploring methods to increase the production of hericene A derivatives and establishing standardised markers, we could unlock sustainable use of these compounds in dietary supplements and medicinal products.

Conclusion & Comments

This research supports the commonly held perception that specific extracts of Lion’s Mane are a promising avenue for improving brain health, memory, and
potentially mitigating cognitive decline associated with ageing and neurodegenerative diseases
. Further research is required to confirm if certain compounds isolated from lion’s mane extracts can be harnessed to improve memory performance and/or for slowing down the cognitive decline associated with ageing and neurodegenerative diseases.
