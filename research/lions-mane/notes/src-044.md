# src-044: Four Weeks of Hericium erinaceus Supplementation Does Not Impact Markers of Metabolic Flexibility or Cognition

**Citation:** Grozier, C., Alves, V., Killen, L., O'Neal, E., Simpson, J., & Waldman, H. (2022). International Journal of Exercise Science, 15(2), 1366-1380. DOI: 10.70252/XZKO8571

**Content quality:** Abstract only (landing page, no full text). Abstract is substantive and contains key methods and results.

## Core Findings

A single-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-longitudinal trial (N=24) found that 4 weeks of daily Lion's Mane supplementation (10 g/day) had **no significant effect** on metabolic flexibility or cognitive performance in a college-age cohort (all p > 0.05). This is a clear null result from one of the few human RCTs on Hericium erinaceus.

## Key Evidence

- **Dose:** 10 g HE per day for 4 weeks (delivered via two muffins identical in taste and appearance to placebo)
- **Sample:** 24 college-age participants, stratified into HE and placebo groups
- **Outcome measures:** Substrate oxidation rates and cardiorespiratory fitness via graded cycle ergometer test; dual-task cognitive challenges (Stroop Word Challenge + Mental Arithmetic Challenge) performed pre- and post-exercise in a fatigued state
- **Statistical analysis:** Repeated-measures ANOVA; no significant interactions or main effects for any dependent variable (all p > 0.05)
- **Baseline imbalance noted:** Group differences existed at baseline, which the authors acknowledged but which complicates interpretation
- **Context:** As of 2022, only 4 human studies on HE existed; none had previously examined metabolic flexibility or cognitive performance outcomes

## Methodology

- **Design:** Single-blind (participants blinded, not researchers), placebo-controlled, parallel-longitudinal
- **Supplement delivery:** Two muffins per day, identical in taste and appearance between groups
- **Cognitive testing:** Dual-task paradigm (Stroop + Mental Arithmetic), assessed pre- and post-fatiguing exercise
- **Metabolic testing:** Graded exercise test on cycle ergometer for substrate oxidation and cardiorespiratory markers

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size (N=24):** Limited statistical power, especially with parallel design splitting into two groups (~12 per group)
2. **Baseline group differences:** Acknowledged by authors; may confound interpretation of null results
3. **Single-blind design:** Researchers were not blinded, introducing potential bias
4. **College-age population:** Results may not generalize to older adults or clinical populations where cognitive effects are more relevant
5. **Duration uncertainty:** 4 weeks may be insufficient; authors call for dose-finding and longer-duration studies
6. **Dose uncertainty:** 10 g/day may be too low, too high, or correct — no established effective human dose exists
7. **Abstract-only source:** Full text unavailable; cannot verify methods details, exact statistical values, or discussion nuances

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q6: Adversarial evidence -- null results, failed effects on cognition
**Highly relevant.** This is a direct null result on cognitive outcomes from a controlled human trial. Key counterpoints to positive findings from other studies:
- 10 g/day for 4 weeks produced no cognitive improvement
- Dual-task cognitive challenge (Stroop + arithmetic) showed no benefit
- Suggests either insufficient dose, insufficient duration, or no real cognitive effect in healthy young adults
- Does not rule out effects in older/clinical populations or at different doses/durations

### Q1: Human trial evidence quality
**Moderately relevant.** Provides methodological data point on the state of human HE research:
- Single-blind (weaker than double-blind) design
- Small sample (N=24)
- At time of publication (2022), only 4 human studies existed total
- Highlights the gap between promising animal data and limited human evidence
