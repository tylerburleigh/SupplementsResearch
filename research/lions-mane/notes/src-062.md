# src-062: Mori et al. (2011) - Hericium erinaceus and Aβ(25-35)-Induced Cognitive Deficits in Mice

**Citation:** Mori K, Obara Y, Moriya T, Inatomi S, Nakahata N. (2011). Effects of Hericium erinaceus on amyloid β(25-35) peptide-induced learning and memory deficits in mice. *Biomedical Research*, 32(1), 67-72. DOI: 10.2220/biomedres.32.67

**Quality:** ok (full paper text extracted; citation count: 59)

## Core Findings

Oral administration of dried H. erinaceus fruiting bodies (5% w/w in diet) for 23 days prevented both spatial short-term memory deficits (Y-maze) and visual recognition memory impairment (novel-object recognition test) induced by intracerebroventricular injection of amyloid β(25-35) peptide in mice. The authors propose the mechanism involves promotion of NGF synthesis, based on their prior work showing H. erinaceus induces NGF mRNA and protein expression in vitro and in the mouse hippocampus in vivo.

## Key Evidence

### Animal Model (Q7)
- **Model:** Male 5-week-old ICR mice; Aβ(25-35) peptide (10 μg) injected ICV on days 7 and 14 to induce cognitive impairment modeling Alzheimer's pathology. Aβ(35-25) (reversed sequence) used as negative control.
- **Y-maze test (day 21):** Aβ(25-35) + control diet mice showed significantly decreased alternation behavior (P < 0.01). Aβ(25-35) + H. erinaceus diet mice showed no significant difference from Aβ(35-25) controls, indicating prevention of spatial memory deficit.
- **Novel-object recognition test (days 21-23):** Aβ(25-35) + control diet mice failed to discriminate familiar vs. novel objects. Aβ(25-35) + H. erinaceus diet mice showed significant novel-object preference (P < 0.01) and higher discrimination ratio, comparable to controls.
- **Sample sizes:** Y-maze: n = 18/15/19/13 per group; Novel-object: n = 20/18/21/20 per group (Aβ(35-25)+control / Aβ(35-25)+H.e. / Aβ(25-35)+control / Aβ(25-35)+H.e.)
- **No locomotor confound:** Total arm entries (Y-maze) and total exploration time (novel-object) did not differ across groups, ruling out locomotor/motivational effects.

### Mechanism (Q2)
- **NGF secretion:** Prior work by the same group (Mori et al., 2008) showed H. erinaceus extract induced NGF mRNA and protein expression in 1321N1 human astrocytoma cells, and oral administration increased NGF mRNA in mouse hippocampus.
- **Active compounds:** Hericenones C-H (from fruiting body) and erinacines A-I (from mycelium) are known to promote NGF synthesis in astrocytes. HPLC analysis confirmed hericenone C (4.12 mg/g), D (0.21 mg/g), and E (0.49 mg/g) in the fruiting bodies used.
- **Proposed pathway:** Lipid-soluble active compounds are absorbed and cross the blood-brain barrier to promote NGF activity in the CNS. However, this mechanism is proposed/inferred from prior in vitro work rather than directly demonstrated in this study.

## Methodology

- **Study design:** 4-group parallel design (2x2 factorial: Aβ peptide type x diet). 23-day feeding period.
- **Diet preparation:** Freeze-dried H. erinaceus fruiting bodies powdered and mixed with normal powdered diet at 5% w/w. Control diet contained 5% dextrin.
- **Aβ preparation:** Peptides dissolved in sterile saline (1 mg/mL), incubated 4 days at 37°C to induce aggregation. Fibril formation confirmed by light microscopy for Aβ(25-35) but not Aβ(35-25).
- **ICV injection:** Light ether anesthesia, 50-μL Hamilton microsyringe with 28-gauge needle, 2.5 mm depth, 1 mm lateral to bregma. Injection site verified with trypan blue in preliminary experiments.
- **Statistics:** One-way ANOVA with Tukey's post-hoc (Y-maze alternation, discrimination ratio); Student's paired t-test (within-group object comparisons). Data expressed as means +/- SEM.
- **Food intake:** No significant difference across groups (~5.4 g/day/mouse).

## Limitations

1. **Single dose level:** Only 5% w/w tested; no dose-response relationship established.
2. **No direct NGF measurement in this study:** The NGF mechanism is inferred from prior work (Mori et al., 2008), not measured in the current experiment.
3. **Short-term study:** 23-day duration; no assessment of long-term effects or sustained benefits.
4. **ICV injection model:** While Aβ(25-35) ICV injection is an established acute model, it does not fully replicate the progressive pathology of Alzheimer's disease (no plaque formation, no chronic neurodegeneration).
5. **Whole fruiting body:** Used crude dried fruiting body powder rather than isolated active compounds, making it difficult to attribute effects to specific molecules or determine pharmacokinetics.
6. **Young animals only:** 5-week-old mice do not model age-related cognitive decline.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q7 (Animal models of cognitive decline prevention):** Directly relevant. Demonstrates preventive efficacy of orally administered H. erinaceus against Aβ-induced cognitive deficits in mice using well-validated behavioral tests. Strong evidence with appropriate controls (reversed-sequence peptide, locomotor controls).
- **Q2 (Mechanisms / NGF secretion):** Moderately relevant. The study itself does not measure NGF directly but references prior work by the same group showing NGF induction. Identifies hericenone content of the fruiting bodies used. The proposed mechanism (NGF promotion via BBB-crossing active compounds) remains inferential.

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample sizes:** The n-values listed in figure captions (18/15/19/13 for Y-maze; 20/18/21/20 for novel-object) include mice that may have been excluded (e.g., <9 arm entries in Y-maze). The methods state exclusions but the exact exclusions are not specified.
- **Hericenone concentrations (4.12, 0.21, 0.49 mg/g):** Described as "unpublished observations" by the authors, so these values have not been peer-reviewed independently.
