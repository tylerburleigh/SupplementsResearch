# src-052: Neurogenesis-dependent antidepressant-like activity of Hericium erinaceus

**Citation:** Chong et al. (2021). *Chinese Medicine*, 16:132. DOI: 10.1186/s13020-021-00546-8
**Citations:** 41 | **Quality:** ok | **Type:** Peer-reviewed open-access research article

## Core Findings

Hericium erinaceus (HE) standardised aqueous extract exerts antidepressant-like effects in a chronic restraint stress (CRS) mouse model through a neurogenesis-dependent mechanism involving the BDNF-TrkB-CREB signalling pathway. The neurogenesis blocker temozolomide completely abolished all behavioral effects of HE, providing strong causal evidence that neurogenesis is required for the antidepressant action. HE also reduced GFAP+ astrocyte activation in the hippocampus, and this anti-neuroinflammatory effect was also neurogenesis-dependent (abolished by temozolomide).

## Key Evidence

### Bioactive Compounds
- HE aqueous extract (Nev-Gro): 20.66% beta-1,3-1,6 glucan, 0.17% adenosine
- Isolated compounds: adenosine (C10H13N5O4), herierin III, and herierin IV (novel, C8H10O4, yield 0.005%)
- Total polyphenol: 2.26 +/- 0.20 mg GAE/g; total flavonoid: 0.73 +/- 0.03 mg CE/g

### Mechanism (BDNF-TrkB-CREB Signaling)
- HE at 10 and 25 mg/kg (i.p., 4 weeks) upregulated Bdnf, Syp, Nes, Psd-95 gene expression in dorsal hippocampus
- Trkb and Dcx upregulated only at 10 mg/kg (not 25 mg/kg)
- Protein level: HE restored pTrkB, mBDNF, pCREB, pERK1/2 reduced by CRS
- 10 mg/kg elevated TrkB, CREB proteins; 25 mg/kg elevated mBDNF, pCREB

### Neurogenesis (BrdU Evidence)
- 10 mg/kg HE significantly increased BrdU+ cells in dentate gyrus (F(2,170)=23.11, p<0.001)
- 25 mg/kg HE did NOT increase BrdU+ cells; showed decreased BrdU+/NeuN+ co-localization
- Suggests high dose may delay neuronal maturation

### Neurogenesis-Dependent Validation (TMZ)
- Temozolomide (TMZ) blocked neurogenesis for first 3 weeks of treatment
- TMZ completely abolished ALL behavioral effects of HE (no significant differences among any TMZ groups)
- TMZ also abolished the anti-neuroinflammatory (GFAP-reducing) effect of HE

### Anti-inflammatory Effects
- HE reduced GFAP+ astrocytes in CA1, CA3, and DG of dorsal hippocampus at both 10 and 25 mg/kg
- This reduction was abolished by TMZ, indicating neurogenesis-dependent mechanism

### Network Pharmacology
- GO and KEGG analysis predicted HE targets overlap with depression genes in MAPK, IL-17, TNF, neurotrophin pathways
- IL, BCL2L1, BCL2, TNF, CASP9, PIK3CA, CASP3, NGF were top-connected gene targets

## Methodology

- **Subjects:** Male C57BL/6 mice (8-10 weeks, n=76 total)
- **Depression model:** CRS (6h/day immobilization, 14 consecutive days)
- **Treatment:** HE aqueous extract at 10 or 25 mg/kg i.p. daily for 4 weeks
- **Controls:** Non-CRS + saline; CRS + saline
- **Behavioral tests:** Cage emergence, novelty-suppressed feeding, sucrose preference, tail suspension
- **Molecular assays:** qPCR (8 genes), Western blot (BDNF, TrkB, pTrkB, CREB, pCREB, ERK1/2, pERK1/2, AKT, pAKT), immunofluorescence (BrdU/NeuN), immunohistochemistry (GFAP)
- **Validation:** TMZ administered on days 1,3,5,8,10,12,15,17,19,22,24,26 to block neurogenesis
- **Statistics:** One-way ANOVA with LSD post-hoc; Student's t-test for CRS vs non-CRS

## Dose-Response Nuance

An important finding is the non-linear dose response: 10 mg/kg showed stronger neurogenic effects (BrdU+, Dcx, Trkb upregulation) while 25 mg/kg showed stronger effects on behavioral despair and BDNF/CREB protein activation. The 25 mg/kg dose decreased BrdU+/NeuN+ co-localization, suggesting it may delay neuronal differentiation.

## Limitations

1. Only male mice studied; sex differences not examined
2. Intraperitoneal route (not oral); clinical relevance of dosing uncertain
3. Only two doses tested; full dose-response curve not established
4. HE extract characterization limited to glucan and adenosine content; full compound profile not quantified
5. Compounds responsible for BDNF upregulation not specifically identified
6. No direct measurement of inflammatory cytokines (only GFAP as astrocyte marker)
7. AKT results were unexpected (HE reduced AKT protein without affecting pAKT)
