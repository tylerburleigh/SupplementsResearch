## Core Findings

Oral supplementation with *Hericium erinaceus* in **wild-type mice** (non-pathological, non-AD model) produced two key effects: (1) significant improvement in recognition memory in behavioral tests (novel object recognition), and (2) increased spontaneous and evoked excitatory synaptic transmission at the mossy fiber-CA3 hippocampal synapse. This is notable as one of the first studies to examine *H. erinaceus* effects in healthy (non-disease-model) subjects.

## Key Evidence

### Behavioral (Q7: wild-type cognitive enhancement)
- **Emergence test:** Hr-supplemented mice (n=22) vs dextrin controls (n=12) showed increased novelty exploration (more complete exits, longer exploring time, shorter latency to first exit) during 8-min sessions.
- **Novel object recognition (NOR):** Hr-supplemented mice (n=15) vs dextrin controls (n=10) showed more approaches to novel objects, longer approach duration, and shorter latencies during 10-min sessions.

### Electrophysiological (Q2: hippocampal neurotransmission mechanisms)
- **Spontaneous EPSCs:** Increased frequency/amplitude of sEPSCs recorded from CA3 pyramidal cells in Hr mice (n=5) vs dextrin controls (n=6).
- **Evoked EPSCs:** Minimal stimulation at mossy fiber-CA3 synapse showed reduced failure rates in Hr mice (n=6) vs controls (n=8), indicating enhanced synaptic efficacy.
- **Paired-pulse ratio:** A2/A1 ratio assessed at 50 ms interpulse interval -- changes suggest presynaptic modulation (n=8 controls, n=6 Hr).
- **Pharmacological validation:** DCG-IV (2 uM, Group II mGluR agonist) selectively blocked mossy fiber responses, confirming the mossy fiber origin of recorded currents.

## Methodology

- **Subjects:** Wild-type mice (not AD model or transgenic)
- **Design:** Dietary supplementation with *H. erinaceus* vs dextrin control
- **Electrophysiology:** Patch-clamp recordings from CA3 pyramidal cells in hippocampal slices; mossy fiber-CA3 pathway specifically isolated using DCG-IV pharmacological validation
- **Behavioral tests:** Emergence test and novel object recognition (NOR)
