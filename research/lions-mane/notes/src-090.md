# src-090: Nerve Growth Factor-Inducing Activity of Hericium erinaceus in 1321N1 Human Astrocytoma Cells

**Citation:** Mori K, Obara Y, Hirota M, Azumi Y, Kinugasa S, Inatomi S, Nakahata N. Biol. Pharm. Bull. 31(9) 1727-1732 (2008).

**Quality:** ok -- full paper text recovered via raw text extraction from PDF.

## Core Findings

1. **H. erinaceus ethanol and ethyl acetate extracts promote NGF mRNA expression and protein secretion in 1321N1 human astrocytoma cells** in a concentration-dependent manner, while H2O extract does not. Among four edible mushrooms tested (H. erinaceus, P. eryngii, G. frondosa, A. blazei), only H. erinaceus showed NGF-inducing activity.

2. **The NGF induction mechanism operates via the JNK signaling pathway.** The JNK inhibitor SP600125 significantly blocked NGF mRNA induction, while MEK, p38 MAPK, PKA, and PKC inhibitors did not. H. erinaceus induced time-dependent phosphorylation of JNK and c-Jun, and enhanced c-fos expression, suggesting AP-1 transcription factor activation downstream of JNK.

3. **The active compounds are NOT hericenones C, D, or E.** These known constituents failed to promote NGF mRNA expression at 10-100 ug/ml in 1321N1 cells or in primary rat astroglial cells. HPLC analysis showed hericenone concentrations in the active extract (20, 4, and 2 ng/ml for C, D, E respectively) were far below the previously reported effective concentration of 33 ug/ml.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### In vitro evidence
- **Cell line:** 1321N1 human astrocytoma cells (and PC12 neurite outgrowth assay)
- **Active concentrations (NGF mRNA):** Ethanol extract at 50, 100, 150 ug/ml (concentration-dependent); significant at 100 ug/ml
- **Active concentrations (NGF protein secretion):** 50, 100, 150 ug/ml measured by ELISA
- **Neurite outgrowth in PC12 cells:** Conditioned medium from 1321N1 cells treated with 125 and 250 ug/ml extract promoted significant neurite outgrowth
- **Hericenones inactive at:** 10, 30, 100 ug/ml (hericenones C, D, E)
- **JNK pathway confirmed by:** SP600125 (30 uM) inhibition of NGF mRNA induction; Western blot showing JNK and c-Jun phosphorylation; RT-PCR showing c-fos upregulation
- **Kinase inhibitors that did NOT block NGF induction:** U0126 (MEK, 10 uM), SB203580 (p38, 3 uM), H89 (PKA, 10 uM), GF109203X (PKC, 5 uM)

### In vivo evidence
- **Animals:** Male ddY mice, 5 weeks old, 33-35 g
- **Dose:** 5% freeze-dried H. erinaceus powder in diet (vs. 5% dextrin control)
- **Duration:** 1 or 7 days
- **Sample sizes:** n=8 per group (Day 1 and Day 7), n=3 (Day 21)
- **Result:** Significant increase in NGF mRNA in hippocampus at Day 7 (p<0.05); no increase in cortex at any time point

### Bioavailability implications (Q3)
- The in vivo hippocampal NGF increase after oral administration suggests active compounds cross the BBB, but this is indirect evidence only
- No direct pharmacokinetic data (plasma levels, tissue distribution, metabolism) are provided
- The authors note the selectivity for hippocampus over cortex may reflect tissue-specific JNK signaling differences or pharmacokinetic differences in brain distribution

## Methodology

- **Extract preparation:** 5 g lyophilized powder extracted with 150 ml ethanol for 2 h at room temperature (yield: 499 mg for H. erinaceus)
- **Hericenone isolation:** From 6.0 kg fresh fruiting bodies via ethanol extraction, chloroform/water partition, silica gel column chromatography
- **NGF mRNA quantification:** RT-PCR (real-time) normalized to beta-actin
- **NGF protein quantification:** ELISA (Promega Emax Immunoassay System)
- **Signaling analysis:** Western blot for phospho-JNK, phospho-c-Jun; RT-PCR for c-fos
- **Statistical analysis:** One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's test; p<0.05 significance

## Limitations

1. **Active compounds not identified.** The paper demonstrates hericenones are not responsible but does not identify the actual active constituents. The authors suggest unknown lipid-soluble compounds.
2. **No direct pharmacokinetic data.** BBB penetration is inferred from in vivo results only, with no measurement of compound levels in blood or brain tissue.
3. **Single in vivo dose level.** Only 5% dietary inclusion tested; no dose-response in animals.
4. **Small in vivo sample sizes** (n=8 per group at the primary time points, n=3 at Day 21).
5. **In vitro concentrations are relatively high** (50-250 ug/ml crude extract), making translational relevance uncertain.
6. **NGF protein levels were too low to account for PC12 neurite outgrowth alone**, suggesting other neurotrophic factors are also released by astrocytes stimulated with H. erinaceus.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q2 (Compounds and pathways):** Directly addresses this. Identifies JNK as the critical signaling pathway for NGF induction. Shows hericenones are NOT the active compounds in this cell system (contradicting earlier rodent astrocyte studies). Suggests unidentified lipid-soluble active compounds in fruiting bodies. Provides specific concentration data for in vitro activity.
- **Q3 (Pharmacokinetics/bioavailability):** Provides indirect evidence of oral bioavailability and BBB penetration via in vivo hippocampal NGF mRNA increase, but no direct PK data.

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample sizes for in vivo work:** Stated as n=8 for Day 1 and Day 7, n=3 for Day 21. The Day 21 group (n=3) is unusually small and results at this time point should be interpreted cautiously.
- **Extract yields:** 499 mg from 5 g dry powder for ethanol extract seems reasonable but should be cross-checked if cited.
- **Hericenone concentrations by HPLC:** 20, 4, and 2 ng/ml for C, D, E in 100 ug/ml extract. These are key quantitative data that undermine the hericenone hypothesis and should be verified.
