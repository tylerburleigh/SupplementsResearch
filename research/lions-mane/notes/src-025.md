# src-025: Mori 2009 - Yamabushitake (Hericium erinaceus) for Mild Cognitive Impairment

**Source type:** PubMed abstract (full text behind paywall)
**PMID:** 18844328
**Year:** 2009 (published online 2008, copyright John Wiley & Sons)
**Quality note:** Abstract-only source; no access to full text. Key details (effect sizes, exact p-values, individual timepoint scores) are unavailable.

## Core Findings

This double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT found that oral supplementation with Yamabushitake (Hericium erinaceus) dry powder significantly improved cognitive function scores on the HDS-R in adults aged 50-80 with mild cognitive impairment at weeks 8, 12, and 16 compared to placebo. However, cognitive gains were not sustained -- scores decreased significantly at 4 weeks post-treatment, indicating the effects are reversible and require continued supplementation. No adverse effects were observed.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **Sample:** 30 Japanese men and women, aged 50-80, diagnosed with MCI; randomized into two groups of 15
- **Design:** Double-blind, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial; 2-week run-in, 16-week treatment, 4-week post-treatment observation
- **Dosage:** Four 250 mg tablets (96% dry powder purity) taken 3x/day = ~3 g/day total
- **Outcome measure:** Revised Hasegawa Dementia Scale (HDS-R)
- **Results:** Significant cognitive improvement at weeks 8, 12, and 16 vs placebo; dose-duration relationship (scores increased with longer intake)
- **Persistence:** Scores decreased significantly at 4 weeks after treatment cessation
- **Safety:** No adverse effects on laboratory tests

## Methodology

- RCT with proper blinding and placebo control
- Cognitive assessment via HDS-R (standard Japanese cognitive screening tool)
- Small sample size (n=15 per group) limits statistical power and generalizability
- Specific p-values, effect sizes, and confidence intervals not reported in abstract

## Limitations

- **Small sample:** Only 30 participants (15 per arm) -- quite underpowered for definitive conclusions
- **Abstract only:** No access to effect sizes, p-values, confidence intervals, or individual timepoint data
- **Population specificity:** Japanese adults aged 50-80; generalizability to other ethnicities/age groups uncertain
- **Short follow-up:** Only 4 weeks post-treatment; long-term persistence and safety unknown
- **Single cognitive measure:** HDS-R only; no secondary cognitive endpoints reported in abstract
- **Potential conflicts:** Not assessable from abstract

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (Human clinical trials -- MCI trial, cognitive function outcomes, persistence after stopping):** This is the primary study addressing Q1. It directly documents:
1. Cognitive function outcomes in an MCI population (significant improvement at weeks 8-16)
2. Persistence after stopping supplementation (scores decreased significantly at 4 weeks post-treatment)
3. Dose-duration relationship (scores increased with longer intake period)

The persistence finding is particularly important -- it demonstrates that Lion's Mane cognitive benefits are not durable after cessation, requiring ongoing supplementation to maintain effects.
