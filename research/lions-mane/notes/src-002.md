# src-002: Acute effects of Hericium erinaceus on cognition and mood in healthy younger adults

**Citation:** Surendran G, Saye J, Binti Mohd Jalil S, Spreadborough J, Duong K, Shatwan IM, Lilley D, Heinrich M, Dodd GF and Surendran S (2025). *Front. Nutr.* 12:1405796. doi: 10.3389/fnut.2025.1405796

**Quality:** ok -- full text available from Frontiers in Nutrition (open access).

## Core Findings

This double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, cross-over study found that a single 3g dose of H. erinaceus fruiting body extract (10:1, water/ethanol extraction) did **not** significantly improve composite measures of global cognitive function or mood in 18 healthy young adults (aged 18-35) at 90 minutes post-consumption. However, individual task analysis revealed a significant improvement in psychomotor skill (Grooved Pegboard Test, dominant hand: partial eta-squared = 0.48, p < 0.001) alongside impairments in executive function tasks (Flanker and Trails B), making the overall acute cognitive picture inconclusive. No mood effects (PANAS) were detected.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Study design
- **Design:** Double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, cross-over with 7-day washout
- **Population:** 18 healthy adults (10 male, 8 female), aged 18-35, mean age 22.78 years, BMI range corresponding to 45.5-85 kg
- **Intervention:** 3g H. erinaceus fruiting body extract (95% extract, 5% maltodextrin; 10:1 concentrate) in 250mL drink vs matched placebo
- **Extract:** Fruiting body, water:ethanol extraction (8:2), spray-dried (Cambridge Commodities Ltd, sourced from China)
- **Testing timepoints:** Baseline and 90 minutes post-consumption
- **Power:** Calculated via G*Power for effect size f=0.3, alpha=0.05, power=80%, yielding N=17 minimum; 20 recruited, 18 completed

### Primary outcome (negative)
- Global cognition composite (Z-scored sum across all cognitive tests): No significant main effect of Intervention or Intervention x Time interaction (all p > 0.05)
- Mood composite (PANAS): No significant effects

### Significant individual-task findings
1. **Pegboard Dominant hand:** Large effect (partial eta-squared = 0.48). Active drink significantly better than placebo post-drink (mean diff = -0.83, p < 0.001). Significant improvement from baseline in active condition only.
2. **Pegboard Non-dominant hand:** Significant improvement from baseline in active condition (mean diff = -0.52, p < 0.001); significant decline in control condition.
3. **Flanker task (executive function):** Significantly WORSE performance with active vs control post-drink (mean diff = 0.84, p < 0.001). Partial eta-squared = 0.22.
4. **Trails B (executive function):** Significant decline from baseline in active condition (mean diff = 0.42, p = 0.01).

### Mood results
- No significant effects on PANAS positive or negative affect. Authors note mood benefits may require chronic supplementation (citing Docherty et al. 2023 showing acute cognitive but only chronic mood/stress effects).

## Methodology

- **Cognitive battery:** TMT A & B, Digit Span Test, DSST, Grooved Pegboard Test, Deary-Liewald Task, Flanker Task -- covering executive function, working memory, psychomotor skills, attention, processing speed, and inhibitory control
- **Mood:** PANAS (20-item, 1-5 scale, used in acute "how you feel right now" mode)
- **Statistical analysis:** Z-scored composite measures analysed via 2-factor repeated-measures ANOVA; individual tasks via 2-factor repeated-measures MANOVA with Bonferroni corrections
- **Blinding:** Confederate prepared drinks; drinks consumed through black straw with obscured vessel

## Dosage and Animal-to-Human Translation (Q3)

- **Dose:** 3g of 10:1 extract = equivalent to 30g fresh fruiting body. Authors note culinary consumption can be up to 300g fresh fruiting body.
- **Fruiting body vs mycelium:** Fruiting bodies are rich in hericenones; mycelia contain higher concentrations of erinacines. EU Novel Foods regulations prohibit mycelial extracts in food products/supplements, constraining this study to fruiting body extract.
- **Pharmacokinetics gap:** Erinacine S reaches peak concentration in rats at 8 hours post-oral administration (Li et al. 2019). Human pharmacokinetics are completely unknown -- the 90-minute testing window may have missed the peak effect entirely.
- **Extraction method:** Water:ethanol (8:2) was used to capture both polar and non-polar compounds, followed by spray drying.

## Limitations

1. **Very small sample (N=18):** Even though powered for the expected effect size, this is a pilot-scale study. Authors explicitly state all cognitive results should be interpreted with caution.
2. **Single timepoint (90 min):** Given rat PK data showing peak at 8 hours, 90 minutes may be far too early to detect cognitive effects. The authors acknowledge this as a major limitation.
3. **Conflict of interest:** Funded by Beyond Alcohol Ltd (trading as Three Spirit Drinks), which uses H. erinaceus in commercial products. Two authors (GS, DL) are employees of this company.
4. **Acute-only design:** Cannot address chronic supplementation effects; authors recommend future chronic studies.
5. **Healthy young population:** Floor/ceiling effects may limit detection of cognitive changes in already high-functioning individuals.
6. **No biochemical measures:** No blood draws, no BDNF/NGF measurement, no quantification of bioactives in the extract administered.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (Human clinical trials):** Directly relevant as a human RCT. Provides evidence that acute (single-dose) supplementation with fruiting body extract at 3g does not produce global cognitive or mood benefits at 90 minutes in healthy young adults, though domain-specific effects (psychomotor improvement, executive function impairment) were observed. Small sample and early testing timepoint are important caveats. Effect sizes reported (partial eta-squared 0.22-0.53 for significant individual tests).

**Q3 (Animal-to-human translation):** Highlights a critical translational gap -- rat PK data suggest peak bioactive concentration at 8 hours, but the human study tested at only 90 minutes. Human bioavailability and blood-brain barrier penetration of H. erinaceus bioactives remain completely unknown. Fruiting body extracts (hericenones) have different bioactive profiles than mycelial extracts (erinacines), complicating cross-study comparisons with animal work that often uses mycelial/erinacine-enriched preparations.

## Claims to Verify

- N=18 completer count is clearly stated and corroborated across multiple sections (recruited 20, 2 dropped before familiarization).
- Partial eta-squared values (0.48, 0.53, 0.22, 0.21) are large -- worth noting these may be inflated in a small sample.
- The 10:1 extraction ratio equivalence (3g extract = 30g fresh) is stated without direct analytical verification in the paper.
- The claim that erinacine S peaks at 8h in rats is cited to Li et al. (2019) reference 40; this was not verified in the primary source.
