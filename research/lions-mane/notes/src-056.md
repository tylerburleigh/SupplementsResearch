## Core Findings

Hericium erinaceus mycelium (HEM) and its isolated compound erinacine C demonstrated neuroprotective effects in a rat model of mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI), primarily through activation of the Nrf2-mediated antioxidant pathway. Erinacine C facilitated Nrf2 binding to promoter regions of key antioxidant genes (BDNF, catalase, thioredoxin reductase, superoxide dismutase), resulting in reduced brain inflammation, inhibition of neuron cell death, and inactivation of microglia.

## Methodology

### In vivo (animal model)
- **Animals:** Male Sprague-Dawley rats, n=6 per group
- **Injury model:** Mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI)
- **Treatments:**
  - H. erinaceus mycelium (HEM): oral administration at 108.5 and 217 mg/kg
  - Erinacine C: intraperitoneal injection at 2 mg/kg
- **Duration:** Supplements continued throughout the recovery phase (6 days of behavioral testing)
- **Behavioral test:** Motor beam walking test (time-to-right from supine position), measured days 2-6
- **Histology:** Immunohistochemical staining for Iba1 (microglia marker) and NeuN (neuronal marker) in cortex and subcortex; 10 fields at 200x magnification per brain sample
- **Nrf2 protein expression:** Immunohistochemical analysis with integrated optical density quantification

### In vitro (cell culture)
- **Cell types:** BV2 microglial cells and primary mixed glia cultures
- **Stimulus:** LPS (endotoxin)
- **Erinacine C concentrations:** 0.5 and 1.0 uM (in vitro), 24h treatment
- **Assays:**
  - ChIP-qPCR for Nrf2 binding to gene promoters (BDNF, CAT, TrxR, SOD)
  - Flow cytometry for apoptosis (Annexin V/PI), ROS/Ca2+ levels, mitochondrial potential (JC-1 staining)
  - Iba1 fluorescence staining for microglial activation

## Key Evidence

1. **Behavioral improvement:** HEM-treated mTBI rats showed significant decreases in time-to-right on beam walking test (p < 0.05 vs. mTBI-only group)
2. **Histological protection:** HEM inhibited neuron cell death and microglial activation (Iba1 and NeuN staining) in both cortex and subcortex
3. **Antioxidant enzyme upregulation:** HEM upregulated catalase, glutathione reductase, thioredoxin reductase, SOD, and p-CREB levels
4. **Nrf2 pathway activation (ChIP-qPCR):** Erinacine C facilitated Nrf2 binding to promoter regions of BDNF, CAT, TrxR, and SOD genes (confirmed via ChIP-qPCR with delta-delta Ct method)
5. **Anti-apoptotic effects (in vitro):** Erinacine C inhibited apoptosis, reduced ROS/Ca2+ production, and restored mitochondrial potential in BV2 cells
6. **Anti-inflammatory effects (in vitro):** Erinacine C at 0.5 and 1.0 uM reduced activated microglia cell numbers in LPS-treated cultures

## Mechanism of Action (Proposed)

As described in the schematic (Figure 10): In the nucleus, Nrf2 promotes transcriptional activation of antioxidant enzymes (BDNF, CAT, TrxR, SOD) by erinacine C through binding to promoter regions of downstream target genes. This triggers the antioxidant defense system and suppresses microglial activation, protecting against mTBI-induced neuronal damage.
