# src-026: Reduction of depression and anxiety by 4 weeks Hericium erinaceus intake

**Authors:** Nagano M, Shimizu K, Kondo R, Hayashi C, Sato D, Kitagawa K, Ohnuki K
**Year:** 2010 | **Venue:** Biomedical Research | **Citations:** 101
**DOI:** 10.2220/biomedres.31.231

## Core Findings

This was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial examining the effects of 4-week H. erinaceus (lion's mane) fruiting body intake on depression, anxiety, sleep quality, and menopausal symptoms in 26 Japanese females (mean age ~40). The HE group showed significant within-group reductions on the CES-D depression scale (P=0.033) and the Indefinite Complaints Index (P=0.004), but **no between-group differences reached statistical significance** for primary outcomes. Subscale analysis of the ICI showed significant between-group differences for "palpitation" (P=0.032) and "insentive" (P=0.047), with trends for "anxious" (P=0.067) and "irritating" (P=0.076). No effect on sleep quality (PSQI) was observed.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled
- **Duration:** 4 weeks
- **Population:** 26 females (of 30 enrolled; 4 excluded), age 41.3 +/- 5.6 years, with indefinite complaints and no specified diseases
- **Intervention:** Powdered fruiting body of H. erinaceus, 0.5 g per cookie, 4 cookies/day = **2 g/day total**, supplied by Aso Biotech Inc
- **Control:** Placebo cookies (no H. erinaceus powder)
- **Groups:** HE (n=12), Placebo (n=14)
- **Outcome measures:**
  - CES-D (depression, 20 items, 0-3 scale)
  - ICI (Indefinite Complaints Index, 40 items, 0-5 scale)
  - KMI (Kupperman Menopausal Index, 17 items)
  - PSQI (Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, 19 items)
- **Statistics:** Wilcoxon signed-rank test (within-group), ANCOVA adjusting for baseline (between-group), SPSS

## Limitations

1. **Very small sample size** (n=26 analyzed; 12 treatment, 14 placebo) -- underpowered for moderate effects
2. **No between-group significance** on primary outcomes (CES-D, ICI total) -- only within-group improvements were significant
3. **No effect sizes reported** -- impossible to gauge clinical magnitude
4. **Specific population** -- perimenopausal Japanese females with indefinite complaints; generalizability is limited
5. **Short duration** -- only 4 weeks
6. **Formulation concerns** -- used cookies as delivery vehicle; delivered as powdered whole fruiting body at only 2 g/day
7. **PDF extraction quality** -- raw text extraction with some garbling (e.g., "insentive" likely means "insensitive/loss of incentive")

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (Human clinical trials -- mood outcomes, effect sizes, durations, populations):** Directly relevant. This is one of the early RCTs examining lion's mane for mood outcomes. It provides evidence of within-group improvements in depression (CES-D) and indefinite complaints (ICI) after 4 weeks at 2 g/day fruiting body powder, but notably **fails to demonstrate between-group superiority** on primary outcomes. The study is frequently cited (101 citations) but its evidence for efficacy is limited by small sample size and lack of between-group significance. The authors suggest possible anxiolytic and antidepressant effects based on ICI subscales but acknowledge the mechanism is unclear and may differ from NGF-enhancement.
