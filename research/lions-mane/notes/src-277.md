# Source src-277: Preclinical Bioavailability, Tissue Distribution, and Protein Binding of Erinacine A

**Citation:** Tsai PC, Wu YK, Hu JH, Li IC, Lin TW, Chen CC, Kuo CF. (2021). *Molecules*, 26(15), 4510. doi: 10.3390/molecules26154510. PMC8347307.

**Type:** Peer-reviewed original research (open access, MDPI)

**Quality:** ok -- full text available with methods, results, discussion.

---

## Core Findings

This is the first preclinical pharmacokinetic study of erinacine A from *Hericium erinaceus* mycelia in Sprague-Dawley rats. The study demonstrates that erinacine A has moderate oral bioavailability (24.39%), can cross the blood-brain barrier via passive diffusion (peak brain concentration 0.205 ug/g at 8 h), and is primarily eliminated via feces. Only ~2.84% of the administered dose was recovered as unchanged compound, suggesting extensive metabolism.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Pharmacokinetic Parameters (PO: 50 mg/kg erinacine A as mycelia extract; IV: 5 mg/kg pure erinacine A)

| Parameter | PO (n=6) | IV (n=6) |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Cmax (ug/mL) | 1.40 +/- 1.14 | 4.53 +/- 3.42 |
| Tmax (min) | 360.00 +/- 131.45 | -- |
| T1/2 (min) | 491.22 +/- 111.70 | 4.37 +/- 4.55 |
| AUC (min*ug/mL) | 457.26 +/- 330.50 | 187.50 +/- 105.29 |
| Absolute bioavailability | **24.39%** | -- |

- Multiple plasma peaks observed at 90, 150, and 360 min after oral dosing, possibly due to enterohepatic circulation or variable GI absorption
- Large standard deviations indicate high inter-individual variability

### Blood-Brain Barrier Penetration

- Erinacine A detected in brain at 1 h post-dose, peaked at 8 h (0.205 +/- 0.079 ug/g)
- Unbound brain-to-plasma concentration ratio close to unity, indicating passive diffusion as dominant transport
- Brain had highest tissue protein binding (28.94 +/- 9.29% bound) among all tissues
- Brain concentration lower than erinacine S from prior study, but still detectable

### Tissue Distribution (4 h post-oral dose)

| Tissue | Concentration (ug/g) |
|--------|---------------------|
| Stomach | 56.493 +/- 27.593 |
| Small intestine | 25.375 +/- 20.359 |
| Liver | 2.826 +/- 0.641 |
| Kidney | 1.279 +/- 0.438 |
| Heart | 1.109 +/- 0.264 |
| Lung | 1.176 +/- 0.339 |
| Large intestine | 0.726 +/- 0.336 |
| Brain (peak at 8h) | 0.205 +/- 0.079 |

- At 24 h, erinacine A still detectable in GI tract, heart, and liver
- Low tissue protein binding (except brain) supports rapid distribution

### Protein Binding

- Plasma: 84.00% free (low binding), increasing availability for tissue diffusion
- Brain: highest binding at 28.94% bound
- Stomach: lowest binding at 11.68% bound

### Excretion

| Route | Cumulative excretion (% dose) | Peak concentration | Peak time |
|-------|------------------------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Feces | 2.823% | 271.237 ug/g | 8-12 h |
| Urine | 0.017% | 0.248 ug/mL | 0-4 h |

- Total recovery as unchanged compound: ~2.84% -- implies extensive metabolism/biotransformation
- Feces is the dominant elimination route (fecal >> urinary)

## Methodology

- **Animals:** 8-week-old male Sprague-Dawley rats (280-300 g), n=6 per group for PK, n=3 per time point for tissue distribution
- **Dosing:** PO = 2.381 g/kg mycelia extract (50 mg/kg erinacine A); IV = 5 mg/kg pure erinacine A
- **Analytical method:** Validated LC-MS/MS (Agilent 1100 HPLC + API 3000 triple quadrupole), MRM mode, ESI positive ionization. Linear range 5-500 ng/mL. Intra- and inter-day precision/accuracy within +/-15%
- **Protein binding:** Rapid equilibrium dialysis (8000 Da MWCO), 37 deg C, 4 h equilibrium
- **IACUC approved:** Protocol #10405, Shih Chien University

## Limitations

1. **Rat model only** -- no human pharmacokinetic data; translation to humans uncertain
2. **High dose** (50 mg/kg PO) -- much higher than typical human supplementation doses
3. **Large variability** -- SDs often 50-100%+ of means (e.g., Cmax SD = 1.14 for mean 1.40), suggesting significant inter-individual variability
4. **Only parent compound measured** -- no metabolite profiling; ~97% of dose unaccounted for as unchanged erinacine A
5. **Single dose study** -- no steady-state or chronic dosing PK data
6. **Industry affiliation** -- several authors affiliated with Grape King Bio Ltd., a supplement manufacturer
7. **Very short IV half-life** (4.37 min) with enormous SD (4.55 min) raises questions about IV PK reliability

## Claims to Verify

- **Absolute bioavailability of 24.39%**: Computed from AUCpo (457.26) x Doseiv (5) / AUCiv (187.50) x Dosepo (50) = (2286.3 / 9375) x 100 = 24.39%. Arithmetic checks out but relies on very variable AUC values (SD ~70% of mean for PO AUC).
- **IV half-life of 4.37 +/- 4.55 min**: This is extremely short and highly variable; the SD exceeds the mean. This may indicate rapid distribution rather than true elimination, or methodological issues with IV sampling.
- **n=6 for PK groups, n=3 for tissue distribution**: Small sample sizes, especially for tissue distribution where n=3 per time point.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q3 (Bioavailability/Translation Gap)
**Highly relevant.** This is the primary study for erinacine A preclinical pharmacokinetics. Key data:
- Absolute bioavailability: 24.39% (moderate)
- BBB penetration demonstrated via passive diffusion
- Brain peak concentration: 0.205 ug/g at 8 h
- Feces dominant elimination route
- ~97% of dose undergoes biotransformation (metabolites unknown)
- Highlights the gap: rat PK data exist, but no human PK or metabolomic data

### Q2 (Mechanistic Implications)
**Moderately relevant.** The passive diffusion BBB transport mechanism is established. The enterohepatic circulation hypothesis (multiple plasma peaks) and extensive biotransformation suggest metabolites may contribute to neuroprotective effects. The comparison with erinacine S (higher lipophilicity, lower bioavailability, different transport) provides mechanistic contrast.
