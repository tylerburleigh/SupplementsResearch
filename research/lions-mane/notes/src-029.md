# src-029: Benefits, side effects, and uses of Hericium erinaceus as a supplement: a systematic review

**Citation:** Menon A, Jalal A, Arshad Z, Nawaz FA, Kashyap R (2025). Frontiers in Nutrition 12:1641246. DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2025.1641246
**Type:** Systematic Review (PRISMA-guided, PROSPERO registered: CRD42024571250)
**Quality:** ok -- full text available, substantive content (82K chars, 622 sentences)

## Core Findings

This systematic review of 26 studies (5 RCTs, 3 PCTs, 15 lab studies, 1 cohort, 1 case report, 1 computer analysis) covering literature from 2000-2024 concludes that Hericium erinaceus (HE) "shows limited effectiveness in clinical trials" for cognitive enhancement, with the majority of studies reporting "only limited improvement in neural functional enhancement." The combined weighted mean MMSE increase across one RCT and one PCT was 1.17 in intervention groups. Side effects are generally mild (stomach discomfort, headache, nausea) but one case report documented serious ARDS.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Cognitive Function (Q1)
- **Saitsu et al. 2019 RCT** (Japan, N=31, 84 days, 0.8g fruiting powder/day): MMSE scores changed from 30 to 29.19; improved Benton visual retention; no S-PA differences. Healthy older adults.
- **Mori et al. 2009 RCT** (Japan, N=30, 154 days, 3.0g/day tablets): Effective for mild cognitive impairment using HDS-R scale. Patients aged 50-80.
- **Li et al. 2020 PCT** (Taiwan, N=41, 365 days, 3 capsules/day with 5 mg/g erinacine A): Placebo group declined on CASI (MD: 3.6); HE group improved MMSE (MD: 1.45); IADL improvement (0.71 vs 0.35). Early Alzheimer's patients, mean age ~75.
- **La Monica et al. 2023 RCT** (USA, N=40 [I=40, C=40 listed], 7-14 days, 1g single dose): No significant improvements in VAS mood, VAS mental clarity, or SHS general happiness. Headache reported. Healthy adults, mean age 34.
- **Docherty et al. 2023 PCT** (UK, N=43, 28 days, 1.8g/day): Acute single dose improved Stroop speed (p=0.005); chronic 28-day showed trend toward reduced stress (p=0.051) but no cognition/memory gains. Healthy young adults, mean age ~26.
- **Grozier et al. 2022 Cohort** (USA, N=24, 28 days, 10g/day in muffins): No significant effects on metabolic flexibility or cognition.

### Mood and Mental Health (Q1)
- **Nagano et al. 2010 RCT** (Japan, N=26 females, 28 days, 2.0g/day): CES-D scores decreased (MD: 3.6 intervention vs 2.5 placebo); KMI and ICI scores improved. No PSQI difference.
- **Vigna et al. 2019 RCT** (Italy, N=77, 120 days, 1.5g/day): Reduced depression and anxiety scores (Zung's scales) in overweight/obese patients; increased pro-BDNF without change in mature BDNF.

### Safety Profile (Q4)
- Generally well-tolerated across clinical trials
- Mild side effects: stomach discomfort and diarrhea (Mori 2009, 7 in HE group vs 6 in placebo), headache (La Monica 2023), nausea/abdominal discomfort/skin rash (Li 2020), epimenorrhea (Nagano 2010, participant excluded)
- **Serious adverse event**: 63-year-old Japanese man developed ARDS after prolonged HE supplementation (Nakatsugawa 2003 case report). Lymphocyte reaction test was positive, suggesting allergic mechanism.
- Dosing across studies: ranged from 0.5g to 10g/day, durations from 7 days to 365 days

### Mechanistic Plausibility (Q5)
- Erinacines (A, B, C) from mycelia stimulate NGF synthesis; hericenones from fruiting bodies also promote NGF
- HE increased circulating pro-BDNF without corresponding change in mature BDNF
- Molecular docking suggests erinacine A and hericenone B have AChE-inhibiting potential similar to donepezil and galantamine
- Gut-brain axis: HE polysaccharides increase SCFA-producing bacteria (butyrate, acetate, propionate), reduce inflammation
- However, clinical evidence remains limited; most mechanistic data from in vitro and animal studies

### Adversarial Evidence / Null Results (Q6)
- Grozier et al. 2022: No significant effects on cognition at 10g/day for 4 weeks
- La Monica et al. 2023: No improvements in VAS mood, mental clarity, or happiness after single 1g dose
- Docherty et al. 2023: No significant cognitive or memory gains after 28 days despite acute Stroop improvement
- Meta-analysis was not possible due to heterogeneous study designs and incomplete reporting
- Review's own conclusion: HE "shows limited effectiveness in clinical trials"

## Methodology

- PRISMA-guided systematic review, registered on PROSPERO
- PubMed-only search (Jan 2000 - Jun 2024)
- ROBIS, Cochrane RoB-2, ROBINS-I, NOS, SYRCLE tools for bias assessment
- Inclusion: peer-reviewed, English, full-text, 2000-2024
- Exclusion: non-English, non-peer-reviewed, reviews/systematic reviews, no HE benefits/side effects
- Pooled odds ratios with 95% CI calculated
- Could not perform formal meta-analysis due to study heterogeneity

## Limitations

1. PubMed only -- potential selection bias (authors argue overlap with Embase/Scopus)
2. Meta-analysis not possible due to incomplete reporting, crossover design data gaps
3. Small sample sizes across individual studies (N=13 to N=77)
4. Heterogeneous study designs, populations, dosages, and outcome measures
5. Most cancer findings limited to in vitro -- no clinical trial evidence for anti-tumor effects
6. Only one human study examined gut microbiota effects (Xie 2021)
7. Short study durations in several trials (7-28 days)
8. No power analysis conducted in some studies, risking type II error

## Claims to Verify

- The La Monica et al. 2023 study lists N=40 for both intervention and control (total 80) in Table 1, which is inconsistent with "N=40" as the total -- the actual design may be crossover or the table has a formatting error.
- The MMSE scores reported for Saitsu (30 pre vs 29.19 post) suggest a decrease rather than improvement -- this may be a reporting issue where the numbers represent different groups or the direction was misinterpreted in the review text.
- The "combined weighted mean increase of 1.17" for MMSE is reported in the abstract but the calculation method is not clearly described in the results section.
