# Source src-032: Hericium erinaceus Extract Exerts Beneficial Effects on Gut-Neuroinflammaging-Connected Processes in Aging Mice

**Citation:** PMID 38248449, published 2024 in MDPI (open access), PubMed Central.
**Quality note:** Metadata marks this as "degraded" (paywall detected), but the PubMed landing page contains a rich abstract and detailed figure legends with statistical data. The content is sufficient for evidence extraction but lacks full methods text.

## Core Findings

This study demonstrated that two months of oral supplementation with a standardized Hericium erinaceus extract (He1) prevented cognitive decline in elderly frail mice. The beneficial effects were mediated through the gut microbiome-brain axis: while overall gut microbiome composition remained unchanged, specific genera involved in cognition and inflammation were significantly altered. In the hippocampus, He1 treatment reduced key markers of neuroinflammation (CD45, GFAP, IL6), cellular senescence (p62), and DNA damage (gamma-H2AX) across dentate gyrus and Cornus Ammonis subfields.

## Key Evidence

### Cognitive outcomes
- He1 supplementation reverted cognitive frailty (knowledge component of recognition memory) in elderly mice, p < 0.01 (Figure 1)
- Treatment duration: 2 months oral supplementation

### Gut microbiome
- Overall gut microbiome composition was not significantly changed by He1 (alpha diversity, NMDS ordination)
- Specific genera associated with cognition and inflammation showed significant differential abundance at T2 in treated vs. control mice (Figure 2c)

### Neuroinflammation markers (immunohistochemistry)
- **CD45** (microglial marker): Significant decrease in DG and CA subregions, p < 0.01 to p < 0.0001 (Figure 5)
- **GFAP** (astrocyte activation): Significant decrease in cell density and OD in DG and CA, p < 0.05 to p < 0.0001 (Figure 7)
- **IL6** (pro-inflammatory cytokine): Significant decrease in DG and CA, p < 0.05 to p < 0.0001 (Figure 7)
- **GFAP + IL6 co-localization**: Significantly reduced, p < 0.001 to p < 0.0001 (Figure 7E)

### Cellular senescence markers
- **p62** (autophagy dysfunction): Significant decrease in DG and CA, p < 0.001 to p < 0.0001 (Figure 8)
- **gamma-H2AX** (DNA damage): Significant decrease in DG, CA subregions, and cerebral cortex, p < 0.0001 (Figure 9)

### Neuroprotection
- Shrunken cell density significantly reduced in DG and CA subregions, p < 0.0001 (Figure 3)
- PSR staining showed reduced collagen/fibrosis in hippocampal parenchyma and blood vessels, p < 0.01 to p < 0.0001 (Figure 4)

## Methodology

- **Model:** Elderly frail mice (aging model)
- **Treatment:** He1 standardized extract, oral supplementation for 2 months
- **Design:** Longitudinal with T1 (baseline) and T2 (post-treatment) assessments; control vs. He1-treated groups
- **Gut microbiome analysis:** 16S rRNA sequencing, Faith's PD alpha diversity, Bray-Curtis NMDS ordination, differential abundance analysis
- **Brain tissue analysis:** Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence on hippocampal sections
- **Cognitive assessment:** Cognitive Frailty Index measuring recognition memory knowledge component
- **Statistical analysis:** Unpaired Student's t-test
