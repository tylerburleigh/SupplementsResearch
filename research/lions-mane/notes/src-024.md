# src-024: Improvement of cognitive functions by oral intake of Hericium erinaceus

**Citation:** Saitsu Y, Nishide A, Kikushima K, Shimizu K, Ohnuki K. (2019). Biomedical Research (Tokyo), 40(4), 125-131. DOI: 10.2220/biomedres.40.125. Cited 66 times.

## Core Findings

A 12-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel-group trial found that oral intake of Hericium erinaceus fruiting body (3.2 g/day) significantly improved cognitive function as measured by MMSE total score in healthy adults over 50. The ANCOVA interaction between groups and time was significant (P = 0.029), and the between-group difference at week 12 was significant (P = 0.0328). However, two other cognitive tests (Benton visual retention test, Standard verbal paired-associate learning test) showed no significant effects. The study is notable for using fruiting body (hericenones) rather than mycelial extract (erinacines).

## Key Evidence

### Study Design
- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel-group RCT
- Duration: 12 weeks with assessments at baseline (ex-ante), week 6 (interim), and week 12 (ex-post)
- Dose: 4 tablets/day x 0.8 g powdered H. erinaceus fruiting body = 3.2 g/day total
- Placebo: identical tablets without HE content
- Conducted at two sites in Fukuoka, Japan (Kyushu University Hospital and USL Health-up Salon)

### Participants
- 34 enrolled; 3 excluded (2 could not complete ex-post evaluation, 1 had MMSE < 24)
- 31 analyzed: HE group n=16 (6M/10F), placebo n=15 (5M/10F)
- Age: all over 50; mean 61.8 (HE, SE=1.7) vs 60.8 (placebo, SE=2.2), P=0.732
- Inclusion: healthy, age >50, voluntary participation
- Exclusion: regular supplement/drug users, night-shift workers, those with metabolic/neurological disorders, pregnant/lactating women
- Compliance: HE group 99.6%, placebo group 98.1%

### Cognitive Outcome Measures
1. **MMSE** (primary): HE group total scores increased from 29.19 to 30.00; placebo from 28.73 to 29.53. Between-group difference at ex-post: P = 0.0328. ANCOVA interaction P = 0.029.
2. **Benton visual retention test**: No significant changes within or between groups at any time point (all P > 0.8).
3. **S-PA (verbal paired-associate learning)**: No significant changes within or between groups.

### Statistical Methods
- Paired t-test within groups across time points (ex-ante/interim, ex-ante/ex-post)
- Unpaired t-test between groups; Welch's t-test where appropriate
- Bonferroni correction for multiplicity
- ANCOVA with age as covariate for MMSE
- Significance threshold: P = 0.05; trend threshold: P < 0.10

## Methodology

- Supplementation: powdered fruiting body tablets (not mycelial extract)
- The authors attribute effects primarily to hericenones (abundant in fruiting body), distinguishing this from studies using erinacine-containing mycelial extracts
- Three assessments at 6-week intervals over 12 weeks
- Pre-registration and ethics: followed Declaration of Helsinki (Fortaleza 2013 revision), Japanese Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Health Research

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size**: n=31 analyzed (16 HE, 15 placebo) -- underpowered for moderate effects
2. **Ceiling effects**: MMSE scores were already very high at baseline (29.19/30 and 28.73/30), limiting room for improvement and clinical meaningfulness
3. **Single positive outcome**: Only MMSE showed significance out of 3 cognitive tests; Benton and S-PA were null
4. **Healthy population only**: Participants had normal cognitive function (MMSE >= 24 excluded); unclear if results generalize to MCI or dementia
5. **No biomarker data**: No NGF levels, BDNF, neuroimaging, or blood markers collected
6. **Short duration**: 12 weeks may be insufficient to assess sustained cognitive effects

## Relevance to Q1 (Human Clinical Trials)

This is a directly relevant human clinical trial on Lion's Mane and cognitive function. It provides evidence that HE fruiting body supplementation at 3.2 g/day for 12 weeks can improve MMSE scores in cognitively normal older adults. The effect size is modest (~0.47 point between-group difference on a 30-point scale). The study distinguishes itself from Mori et al. (2009) by using fruiting body (hericenones) rather than mycelial culture (erinacines), providing comparative data on preparation type.
