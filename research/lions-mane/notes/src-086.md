# src-086: Protective Effects of Hericium erinaceus Mycelium and Its Isolated Erinacine A against Ischemia-Injury-Induced Neuronal Cell Death

**Citation:** Lee K-F, Chen J-H, Teng C-C, et al. *Int J Mol Sci.* 2014;15(9):15073-15089. doi:10.3390/ijms150915073. PMCID: PMC4200813.

**Quality note:** Metadata flags "degraded" (paywall_page EUR), but the actual PMC content is full-text open-access with complete methods, results, and discussion. Treating as full-quality source.

## Core Findings

H. erinaceus mycelium and its active compound erinacine A provide significant neuroprotection in a rat global ischemic stroke model, reducing infarcted brain volume by 22% (50 mg/kg) and 44% (300 mg/kg). The neuroprotective mechanism involves inhibition of the iNOS/p38 MAPK/CHOP signaling pathway and reduction of reactive nitrogen species (nitrotyrosine) and pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha).

## Key Evidence / Data Points

### Infarct Volume Reduction (Q7)
- H. erinaceus mycelium at 50 mg/kg: 22% reduction in total infarcted volume vs SAM control
- H. erinaceus mycelium at 300 mg/kg: 44% reduction in total infarcted volume vs SAM control
- Erinacine A at 1, 5, and 10 mg/kg also significantly reduced infarcted volume (p < 0.05)
- n = 6 per group, TTC staining at 24 h post-reperfusion

### Neuron Survival (Q7)
- NeuN-positive neurons increased by 15% (50 mg/kg HEM) and 45% (300 mg/kg HEM) vs untreated SAM
- Erinacine A at 1, 5, and 10 mg/kg also significantly increased NeuN-positive cells (p < 0.05)
- Evaluated in both cortical and subcortical regions

### iNOS/p38 MAPK Mechanism (Q2)
- Erinacine A dose-dependently inhibited iNOS expression and p38 MAPK phosphorylation
- CHOP (CCAAT enhancer-binding protein homologous protein) expression was attenuated
- Nitrotyrosine (marker of reactive nitrogen species/RNS) was reduced
- Pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha were all significantly decreased by erinacine A
- Western blot confirmed pathway modulation; immunohistochemistry confirmed iNOS and nitrotyrosine reduction in brain tissue

### Proposed Mechanism Pathway
ROS scavenging by erinacine A -> iNOS inhibition -> reduced NO/RNS -> decreased p38 MAPK phosphorylation -> reduced CHOP expression -> decreased ER stress-induced apoptosis -> neuroprotection

## Methodology

- **Animal model:** Adult male Sprague-Dawley rats (280 +/- 20 g), global cerebral ischemia via two-vessel occlusion (2VO) for 90 min, followed by 24 h reperfusion
- **Groups:** 8 groups of 6 rats each: control, SAM, three HEM groups (50, 300, 1000 mg/kg), three erinacine A groups (1, 5, 10 mg/kg)
- **Treatment timing:** HEM given orally for 5 days before ischemia; erinacine A given intraperitoneally for 5 days before ischemia
- **Erinacine A content:** 3 mg/g (0.3%) in HEM ethanol extract, confirmed by HPLC
- **Assays:** TTC staining for infarct volume, NeuN immunohistochemistry for neuron counts, Western blot for iNOS/p38 MAPK/CHOP/nitrotyrosine, serum cytokine ELISA
- **Statistics:** Mean +/- SD, *p < 0.05 vs SAM group

## Limitations

1. No positive control drug (e.g., known neuroprotectant) was included for comparison
2. Anti-hyperglycemic effects of H. erinaceus were observed but not fully validated; the authors note that further studies with glucose addition are needed
3. The study used a global ischemia model (2VO), which may not fully represent focal ischemic stroke in humans
4. The paper contains some apparent errors/inconsistencies: references to "magnolol" in Figure 4 and Figure 6 descriptions appear to be copy-paste errors (should be erinacine A)
5. Only acute (24 h) outcomes were assessed; long-term functional recovery was not evaluated
