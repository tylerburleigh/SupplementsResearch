## Core Findings

A two-month oral supplementation with standardized H. erinaceus extracts (containing Erinacine A, Hericenone C, Hericenone D, and L-ergothioneine) partially recovered age-related locomotor decline in aged mice and ameliorated cerebellar neurodegeneration. The treatment reduced inflammatory and oxidative stress markers while increasing SIRT1 (a key longevity regulator) in frail mice, demonstrating a non-pharmacological dietary intervention for neuro-healthy aging.

## Key Evidence

### Locomotor/Frailty Outcomes (Q7)
- Mean speed decline during senescence was reverted by H. erinaceus treatment (two-way ANOVA: p < 0.05, p < 0.01)
- Resting time increase during aging was reverted (p < 0.05)
- Locomotor frailty index improved (p > 0.05 and p < 0.01 at different timepoints) -- note: p > 0.05 at T1 suggests baseline equivalence, with significant improvement at T2
- Model: aged C57BL mice, 2-month oral supplementation, comparing healthy aging vs. frail mice

### Cerebellar Histopathology (Q7)
- Milder cerebellar volume reduction in treated frail mice (p < 0.05)
- Slighter molecular layer thickness decrease (p < 0.01)
- Lower percentage of shrunken Purkinje neurons (p < 0.001)
- Reduced Picrosirius Red staining (collagen/fibrosis) in meningeal fibers (p < 0.01, p < 0.001)

### Inflammatory/Oxidative Stress Pathways (Q2)
- IL-6 immunostaining reduced in treated mice (p < 0.05, p < 0.001) -- anti-inflammatory effect
- GFAP (astrocyte activation marker) reduced -- indicating reduced reactive gliosis (p < 0.01, p < 0.001)
- COX-2 reduced in treated mice (p < 0.01, p < 0.001)
- NOS2 (inducible nitric oxide synthase) reduced in treated mice (p < 0.01, p < 0.001)
- SOD1 increased in treated mice (p < 0.05) -- enhanced antioxidant defense
- HIF-1alpha and VEGF also assessed, suggesting vascular/angiogenic pathway modulation

### Ergothioneine and Longevity Mechanisms (Q2)
- L-ergothioneine quantified in H. erinaceus extracts by HPLC-UV-ESI/MS
- Extract also contained Erinacine A, Hericenone C, Hericenone D
- SIRT1 (sirtuin 1, a key longevity regulator) increased in treated mice in both molecular layer cells and Purkinje neuron soma (p < 0.01, p < 0.001)
- This is notable as SIRT1 activation is a well-established longevity pathway

## Methodology

- **Design:** In vivo study, aged C57BL male mice, 2-month oral supplementation
- **Extract:** Standardized H. erinaceus (He1) containing Erinacine A, Hericenone C, Hericenone D, and L-ergothioneine
- **Behavioral tests:** Open arena test and emergence task for locomotor assessment
- **Histology:** H&E and Nissl staining, Picrosirius Red (PSR) staining
- **Immunohistochemistry:** IL-6, GFAP, HIF-1alpha, VEGF, SOD1, NOS2, COX-2, SIRT1
- **Statistics:** Two-way ANOVA for behavioral data; unpaired Student's t-test for histological data
