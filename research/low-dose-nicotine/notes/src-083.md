## Key Findings

### Cardiovascular Safety (Relevant to Q2)

1. **EAGLES Trial (Benowitz et al., 2018):** Double-blind RCT of 8,058 smokers comparing varenicline, bupropion, and nicotine patch (21 mg/day) vs placebo. The incidence of cardiovascular events during treatment was low: <0.5% for MACE (cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke) and <0.8% for MACE+. No significant treatment differences in time to cardiovascular events, blood pressure, or heart rate. Varenicline HR for MACE vs placebo: 0.29 (95% CI 0.05-1.68); bupropion HR: 0.50 (95% CI 0.10-2.50). **No evidence that smoking cessation pharmacotherapies increased cardiovascular risk.**

2. **Sterling et al. (2016) Meta-analysis:** 38 RCTs, N=12,706. Varenicline 1 mg BID showed no significant difference in cardiovascular SAEs vs placebo (RR 1.03, 95% CI 0.72-1.49; I2=0%). No significant difference in all-cause mortality (RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.50-1.52).

3. **Eisenberg et al. (2016) ACS Trial:** 302 patients hospitalized with acute coronary syndrome. SAE rates similar: varenicline 11.9% vs placebo 11.3%; MACE: varenicline 4.0% vs placebo 4.6%.

### Cognitive Effects (Marginally Relevant to Q1)

4. **Smith et al. (2016):** 87 schizophrenic smokers. Varenicline (2 mg/day) significantly decreased smoking but did NOT improve MATRICS cognitive battery scores vs placebo. This suggests nicotinic agonism at therapeutic doses does not enhance cognition in this population.

### Dependence/Treatment Comparison (Relevant to Q5)

5. **Baker et al. (2016):** N=1,086. 12 weeks of nicotine patch, varenicline, or C-NRT showed no significant differences in abstinence at 26 or 52 weeks. All well tolerated; varenicline had more adverse events than patch.

## Doses Used

All doses are standard therapeutic smoking cessation doses:
- Nicotine patch: 21 mg/day (with tapering in some studies)
- Varenicline: 1 mg twice daily (2 mg/day)
- Bupropion: 150 mg twice daily (300 mg/day)
- These are NOT low/subthreshold doses relevant to the research brief

## Population

All studies enrolled **established tobacco-dependent smokers**, not healthy non-smokers. Subpopulations included:
- General population smokers
- COPD patients
- Psychiatric patients (schizophrenia, PTSD, depression, SMI)
- Substance use disorder patients
- Acute coronary syndrome patients
- Pregnant smokers
