# Source src-055: Double Dissociation of Working Memory and Attentional Processes in Smokers and Non-Smokers

**Citation:** Grundey, J., Amu, R., Ambrus, G.G. et al. (2015). Psychopharmacology, 232, 2491-2501. DOI: 10.1007/s00213-015-3880-7

**Quality Note:** Metadata marks this as `"degraded"` due to paywall. The abstract is substantive and contains key results, but full methods/results sections are unavailable. Dose of nicotine patch not specified in abstract.

## Study Design

- **Type:** Placebo-controlled crossover experiment
- **Intervention:** Transdermal nicotine patches vs. placebo patches
- **Tasks:** 3-back letter task (working memory) and Stroop interference test (attentional processes)
- **Participants:** 16 smokers + 16 non-smokers (n-back); 21 smokers + 21 non-smokers (Stroop); total N = 74 unique participants
- **Setting:** Department of Clinical Neurophysiology, Georg-August-University Medical Center Gottingen, Germany
- **Funding:** Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG grants NI 683/4-1 and NI683/4-2)

## Key Findings

1. **Non-smokers showed cognitive impairment with nicotine:** Non-smoking subjects displayed decreased accuracy and increased errors on the 3-back working memory task after nicotine administration. This is direct evidence AGAINST cognitive enhancement from nicotine in healthy non-smokers.

2. **Smokers showed cognitive restoration with nicotine:** Nicotine-deprived smokers had compromised working memory and attentional performance compared to non-smokers at baseline. Nicotine administration restored these functions in smokers.

3. **Double dissociation:** Nicotine has opposite effects on cortical excitability and cognition in smokers vs. non-smokers. In non-smokers, nicotine enhances cortical inhibition (associated with impaired performance); in deprived smokers, nicotine restores cortical facilitation (associated with improved performance).

4. **Working memory more affected than attention:** The effects were more apparent for the 3-back working memory task than for the Stroop attentional interference test, suggesting task-specificity of nicotine effects.

## Relevance to Brief Questions

### Q1: Low-dose cognitive effects in healthy non-smokers
**Highly relevant.** This study provides direct evidence that nicotine IMPAIRS rather than enhances working memory in healthy non-smokers. This challenges the common narrative of nicotine as a cognitive enhancer and supports the "restoration not enhancement" hypothesis.

### Q6: Evidence against low-dose nicotine
**KEY SOURCE.** This is one of the clearest demonstrations that nicotine can harm cognitive performance in non-smokers. The decreased accuracy and increased errors on a well-validated working memory task (3-back) provide quantitative evidence of cognitive impairment. The double dissociation (smokers improve, non-smokers worsen) is particularly compelling.

### Q5: Dependence/withdrawal risk
Moderately relevant. The study shows that nicotine-deprived smokers have compromised cognition, illustrating withdrawal-related cognitive deficits that nicotine "resolves" - consistent with dependence-driven self-medication rather than true enhancement.

## Limitations

5. **Task specificity:** Effects were stronger for working memory than attention, suggesting the negative effects may not generalize across all cognitive domains.
