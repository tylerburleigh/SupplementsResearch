# src-013: Nicotine and Its Potential Role in Treating COVID-19 and Long COVID

**Source:** EDS Clinic article by David Harris, updated October 2024
**URL:** https://www.eds.clinic/articles/nicotine-long-covid-treatment
**Type:** Clinical review / blog article (not peer-reviewed)
**Quality flag:** Metadata marked "degraded" due to EUR detection, but actual content is a full substantive article. Not a paywall page.

## Study Design

This is not a primary research study. It is a clinical review article published on the EDS Clinic website summarizing the potential role of nicotine patches in treating long COVID symptoms. It references the Leitzke 2023 case series (n=4), patient self-reports, and general nicotine pharmacology. The article cites university and NIH sources but is itself an opinion/review piece from a clinical practice.

## Key Findings

1. **Cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway:** Nicotine may compete with viral interference at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs), helping restore autonomic nervous system balance disrupted by COVID-19. Based on the Leitzke hypothesis.
2. **Cognitive benefits:** A referenced meta-analysis found nicotine effective in improving focus, memory, and attention. Nicotine enhances dopamine and serotonin levels relevant to cognitive function.
3. **Leitzke 2023 case series:** Four patients using 7.5 mg nicotine patches experienced improvements in cognitive function and fatigue after several days; some sustained relief for months.
4. **Patient-reported outcomes:** Approximately 20% of patients reported substantial symptom improvement; 36% reported moderate relief from brain fog and fatigue with nicotine patches.
5. **Cardiovascular effects:** Nicotine's mild stimulant effect may increase heart rate and improve blood flow, potentially helping exercise intolerance in long COVID, but can also raise blood pressure and requires caution.
6. **Withdrawal/dependence:** Quitting nicotine can cause brain fog and concentration difficulties. Slow-release patches minimize but do not eliminate addiction risk compared to smoking.

## Doses Used

- 7 mg nicotine patch (recommended starting dose per Leitzke)
- 7.5 mg nicotine patch (used in Leitzke case series)
- Dosing approach: start low, assess tolerance, titrate as needed

## Population

- Long COVID patients with symptoms including brain fog, fatigue, exercise intolerance, autonomic dysfunction
- Not a healthy non-smoker population; patients are clinical cases with post-viral syndrome

## Relevance to Brief Questions

- **Q1 (Cognitive effects in healthy non-smokers):** Low relevance. The article discusses cognitive improvement but in a disease population (long COVID), not healthy non-smokers. The referenced meta-analysis may be more relevant but is not named specifically.
- **Q2 (Cardiovascular/metabolic at <5mg/day):** Moderate relevance. Notes cardiovascular caution and stimulant effects, but doses discussed (7-7.5 mg) exceed the 5mg/day threshold. No metabolic data.
- **Q3 (Longevity pathways):** Not addressed.
- **Q4 (Intermittent vs daily):** Not addressed.
- **Q5 (Dependence/withdrawal risk):** Moderate relevance. Discusses withdrawal symptoms (brain fog, concentration difficulty) and notes that patches minimize but do not eliminate addiction risk.
- **Q6 (Evidence against):** Some cautionary evidence regarding mixed findings on ACE2 receptor expression and cardiovascular risks.
- **Q7 (Expert disagreement):** Minimal. References the Leitzke hypothesis but does not present opposing expert views.

## Limitations

- Not peer-reviewed; a clinic blog article
- The Leitzke study referenced is a case series of only 4 patients
- Patient outcome figures (20% substantial, 36% moderate improvement) appear to be from online self-reports, not controlled trials
- No named meta-analysis is cited for the cognitive improvement claim
- Population is long COVID patients, not healthy non-smokers
- No dosing below 5mg/day discussed

## Claims to Verify

- "A meta-analysis found nicotine to be effective in improving focus, memory, and attention" -- unnamed meta-analysis; needs identification and verification of scope (population, dose, delivery method)
- "Approximately 20% of patients report substantial improvement...36% experiencing moderate relief" -- source of these figures is unclear; appears to be from Health Rising blog aggregation, not a formal study
- "7.5 mg nicotine patches" in the Leitzke study -- standard patch sizes are 7 mg and 14 mg; 7.5 mg is unusual and should be verified against the original paper
