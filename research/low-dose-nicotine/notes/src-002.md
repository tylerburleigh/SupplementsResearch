# src-002: Chronic Transdermal Nicotine in Alzheimer's Disease

**Source:** PubMed abstract only (paywall -- full text unavailable). PMID: 10326778. Published in Psychopharmacology (Berlin), 1999.

**Quality:** Degraded. Only abstract available from PubMed landing page. No full text methods, results tables, or discussion.

---

## Study Design

- **Design:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled, cross-over trial
- **Duration:** Two 4-week treatment periods separated by a 2-week washout
- **Patch:** Nicotrol transdermal patch, worn 16 hours/day
- **Dose schedule:** 5 mg/day (week 1), 10 mg/day (weeks 2-3), 5 mg/day (week 4)
- **N:** 8 subjects (very small)

## Population

- Mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease patients
- Otherwise healthy
- Aged (MeSH terms: Aged; Aged, 80 and over)

**Key limitation for the research brief:** This study involves Alzheimer's patients, NOT healthy non-smokers. Relevance to Q1 (healthy non-smokers) is indirect at best. The population is clinical, not healthy.

## Key Findings

1. **Attention improved significantly:** Chronic nicotine reduced errors of omission on the Conners' Continuous Performance Test (CPT). The reduction was sustained throughout the 4-week administration period.
2. **Reaction time variability reduced:** Hit reaction time variability on the CPT was significantly reduced by nicotine.
3. **No improvement in motor or memory function:** Other cognitive domains (motor, memory) showed no significant improvement.
4. **Attention effect sustained:** The attentional benefit persisted across the full 4-week period, suggesting no rapid tolerance to this effect.

## Doses Used

- 5-10 mg/day via transdermal patch (Nicotrol)
- The initial dose of 5 mg/day falls within the brief's <5mg/day threshold; however, most of the treatment period used 10 mg/day, which exceeds it.
- Dose was titrated up and then back down, which is a clinically motivated protocol rather than a fixed low-dose design.

## Relevance to Brief Questions

- **Q1 (Cognitive effects in healthy non-smokers):** INDIRECT. Shows nicotine improves attention (sustained, not just acute) in AD patients, but population is clinical, not healthy. Only n=8. Cannot directly generalize to healthy non-smokers.
- **Q2 (Cardiovascular/metabolic at <5mg/day):** Not addressed. No health outcomes reported in abstract.
- **Q3 (Longevity/neurodegeneration):** Weakly relevant. Sustained attention improvement in AD suggests nicotinic mechanisms remain responsive in neurodegeneration, but no direct longevity pathway data.
- **Q4 (Intermittent vs daily):** Not addressed. Continuous daily dosing.
- **Q5 (Dependence/withdrawal at <5mg):** Not addressed in abstract.
- **Q6 (Evidence against):** The null effects on memory and motor function are modest counter-evidence to broad cognitive enhancement claims. Small sample limits interpretability.
- **Q7 (Expert disagreement):** Not addressed.

## Claims to Verify

- The n=8 sample size is extremely small. Any null findings (memory, motor) are unsurprising given low power.
- Dose was 10 mg/day for the majority of treatment -- this exceeds the <5mg/day threshold in the research brief. Only week 1 and week 4 were at 5 mg/day.
- No data on whether subjects were current or former smokers, which would confound results.

## Summary Assessment

This is a small clinical trial in AD patients with only abstract-level data available. It provides moderate evidence that chronic nicotine improves sustained attention (reduced omission errors, reduced RT variability) in AD, but does NOT address healthy non-smokers, cardiovascular health, longevity pathways, dependence risk, or intermittent dosing. Its direct relevance to the research brief is limited.
