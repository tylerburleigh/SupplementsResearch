## Study Design

This is a popular-science overview article, not a primary research study. It references:
- A small German case series (n=4) using low-dose nicotine patches for long COVID symptoms
- General literature on nicotine and cognition
- Mechanistic speculation about nicotinic acetylcholine receptor disruption by SARS-CoV-2

## Key Findings

1. **German case series (n=4):** Low-dose nicotine patches administered once daily improved long COVID symptoms (tiredness, weakness, shortness of breath, exercise intolerance) by day 6. Lost taste/smell recovered within 16 days. Very small sample; cannot establish causation.

2. **Attention enhancement:** The article states "most studies agree that [nicotine] can enhance attention" but notes mixed results on memory and concentration. No specific studies cited with data.

3. **Dependence risk with patches:** Transdermal delivery keeps blood nicotine steady without spikes; participants in the German study did not appear to develop dependence. However, gum/lozenges can cause spikes and user-controlled dosing.

4. **Cardiovascular side effects:** Nicotine can cause nausea, dizziness, increased heart rate, and higher blood pressure even in gum/patch form. Long-term use may affect heart health.

5. **Expert caution:** Experts advise against self-medicating with nicotine due to lack of standardized dosing, addiction potential, and unknown long-term effects.
