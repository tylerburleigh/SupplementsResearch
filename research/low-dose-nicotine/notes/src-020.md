## Study Design

The MIND Study is described as "the largest and longest-running study of its kind" testing whether nicotine delivered via transdermal patch can safely improve memory and functioning in people diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment (MCI). Funded by NIH. No tobacco company involvement. The study is no longer accepting participants. No results are reported on this page.

## Key Findings

No quantitative results are presented. The page makes general statements:
- Nicotine does not cause cancer, heart disease, or respiratory illness (those are attributed to tar and other chemicals in cigarettes).
- In a prior study using nicotine patches to treat MCI in non-smokers, "there were no signs of withdrawal symptoms or cravings after stopping use."
- In that same prior study, "participants using nicotine patches showed more improvement in attention and memory than those using the placebo patches."
- Side effects from nicotine patch in memory studies are "minimal" -- nausea, dizziness, headaches that "typically go away very quickly."
- Nicotine patch treatment "has not yet been studied and is not recommended to prevent memory loss in healthy people."

People diagnosed with mild memory loss or mild cognitive impairment (MCI). Not healthy non-smokers.
