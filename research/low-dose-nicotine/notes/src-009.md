# Source src-009: Nicotine Patch Protocol (covidinstitute.org)

**URL:** https://covidinstitute.org/nicotine-patch-protocol
**Type:** Web page (non-peer-reviewed)
**Quality Assessment:** Low. This is a consumer-facing health website page, not a research paper. No original data, no controlled studies, no peer review. Claims are based on "early studies and anecdotal evidence" with one cited case study of n=4.

## Study Design

Not a study. This is an informational/practitioner web page summarizing mechanisms and protocols for using nicotine patches to manage Long COVID symptoms. No methodology section, no controlled design.

## Key Findings

- Proposes a 7mg nicotine patch protocol for 14-30 days for Long COVID symptom relief
- Claims nicotine acts via nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs) to reduce inflammation and restore nervous system function
- One case study from late 2023 reported improvement in 4 patients, 1 with complete recovery
- Explicitly states nicotine does NOT interact with spike proteins, bind ACE2 receptors, or flush spike protein
- Acknowledges no FDA approval and need for clinical trials

## Doses Used

- 7mg transdermal nicotine patch (standard smoking cessation dose)
- Duration: 14-30 days maximum
- The site explicitly warns against use beyond 30 days, preferring 14 days

## Population

Long COVID patients with symptoms including brain fog, fatigue, anosmia, dysgeusia. No specific demographic data provided.

## Relevance to Brief Questions

- **Q1 (Cognitive effects in healthy non-smokers):** Marginally relevant. Discusses cognitive improvement (clarity, focus) but in Long COVID patients, not healthy non-smokers. Evidence is anecdotal only.
- **Q2 (Cardiovascular/metabolic at <5mg/day):** Marginally relevant. Mentions ACE2 upregulation and blood pressure modulation as mechanisms, but provides no quantitative data. Dose discussed (7mg) exceeds the 5mg/day threshold in the research question.
- **Q3 (Longevity pathways):** Tangentially relevant. Mentions cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway and vagus nerve activation, which are mechanisms potentially relevant to longevity, but no longevity-specific evidence.
- **Q4 (Intermittent vs daily):** Not addressed.
- **Q5 (Dependence/withdrawal risk):** Weakly relevant. The 14-30 day duration limit implicitly acknowledges dependence risk, but no systematic evidence on dependence is provided.
- **Q6 (Evidence against):** Notes risks include mild skin irritation and immune suppression. States nicotine patches are not FDA-approved for this use.
- **Q7 (Expert disagreement):** Quotes Dr. Colin Furness expressing cautious optimism but emphasizing need for comprehensive studies.

## Limitations

- Non-peer-reviewed consumer health content
- No original research data
- Anecdotal evidence only (n=4 case study, unreferenced)
- Potential conflict of interest: site promotes consultations and a paid community
- Dose (7mg) exceeds the research brief's <5mg/day threshold
- Population (Long COVID patients) differs from the brief's focus on healthy non-smokers
