# Source src-003: WhyNicotine (VUMC MIND Study Page)

## Status
Brief informational/promotional web page from Vanderbilt University Medical Center. Not a primary research paper; summarizes findings from a prior MCI nicotine patch study and references the MIND Study. Very short content (~20 lines).

## Study Design

**Prior study referenced (not the MIND Study itself):**
- Design: Randomized, placebo-controlled trial
- Population: 74 adults diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment (MCI)
- Intervention: Nicotine patch vs placebo
- Duration: 6 months
- Outcomes measured: Attention, memory

**MIND Study:**
- Described as studying nicotine for MCI to possibly delay/prevent progression to Alzheimer's disease
- No longer enrolling; results expected December 2025

## Key Findings

1. Nicotine patch users with MCI showed improvement in **attention and memory** over 6 months
2. No serious side effects reported
3. No signs of withdrawal reported
4. Medical research community is also exploring nicotine for ADHD, depression, Parkinson's disease, and sarcoma

## Doses Used

Not specified on this page. The delivery method was transdermal patches.

## Population

Adults diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment (MCI). Not healthy non-smokers. This is an important limitation for direct applicability to the research brief, which focuses on healthy non-smokers.
