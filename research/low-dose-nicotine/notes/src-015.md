# src-015: Dautzenberg et al. (2021) - Systematic Review of Transdermal Nicotine in Non-Smokers

## Citation
Dautzenberg B, Levi A, Adler M, Gaillard R. "Transdermal nicotine in non-smokers: A systematic review to design COVID-19 clinical trials." Respir Med Res. 2021 Jun 7;80:100844. doi: 10.1016/j.resmer.2021.100844. PMCID: PMC8183099.

## Study Design
Systematic review of Medline and ScienceDirect databases. 298 articles screened, 35 publications (33 unique studies) included. PRISMA methodology. Two independent reviewers. Studies published 1992-2020 of transdermal nicotine in non-smokers for >48 hours. 16 randomized controlled trials, 7 crossover, 1 case-control, 9 open studies.

## Population
- **Total participants:** 987 non-smokers (534 never-smokers, 326 ex-smokers, 127 unspecified non-smokers)
- **Nicotine group:** 608 non-smokers received transdermal nicotine
- **Comparator group:** 465 received placebo or other comparator
- **Age range:** 10-80 years (mean 44.79 +/- 21.25)
- **Sex:** 502 male / 410 female
- **Conditions studied:** ADHD (2), autism (1), cognitive impairment in elderly (6), post-chemotherapy (1), major depressive disorder (3), Parkinson's disease (5), Tourette syndrome (4), ulcerative colitis (8), healthy volunteers (3)

## Doses Used
- **Initial dose range:** 2.5 mg/day to 22 mg/day (mean 7.28 +/- 4.78 mg/day)
- **Target dose range:** 5 mg/day to 105 mg/day (mean 19.06 +/- 20.89 mg/day)
- **Lowest targets (under 25 years):** 5-7 mg/day
- **Median dose used across studies:** 14 mg/day
- **Treatment duration:** 2 to 238 days (mean 56.61 +/- 71.10 days)
- **Dose titration:** 22/33 studies used gradual increase in 2-7 steps over 3-96 days
- **Patch types:** 20 studies used 16-hour patches, 13 used 24-hour patches

## Key Findings

### Safety Profile
- 7.1% dropout rate for nicotine group vs 1.9% for comparator due to adverse events
- Nausea was most common side effect: 38.35% (nicotine) vs 7.53% (comparator), 3.02x more frequent
- Itching under patches: 30.83% (nicotine) vs 17.06% (comparator), 1.85x more frequent
- No hospitalizations or deaths attributed to transdermal nicotine
- Cardiovascular monitoring in 9 studies showed mostly stable hemodynamics; heart rate increase of 5 bpm in one study; slight BP changes in two studies
- No severe cardiovascular events even in elderly at-risk populations or at very high doses (90 mg/day in Parkinson's)

### Dependence Risk
- **Critical finding:** No withdrawal symptoms or addictive behavior reported across all 33 trials in 608 non-smokers
- No craving reported at end of patch use
- No cigarette or other nicotine source use reported after trials
- Includes 12-week follow-up data from one study
- Authors note that transdermal route avoids the nicotine peaks that drive receptor multiplication and dependence
- However, most studies did not formally assess dependence risk

### Efficacy
- 28 studies had clinical efficacy objectives
- No significant deleterious effects of nicotine reported
- 6 studies: no effect; 12 studies: trend toward improvement; 10 studies: significant improvement
- Tourette syndrome: most consistent improvement (4/4 studies), effect persisted 2-8 weeks post-treatment
- Cognitive studies: mixed results, generally trends toward improvement
- Ulcerative colitis: meta-analysis showed non-significant trend (RR 1.40, 95% CI 0.63-3.12)
- Overall authors' assessment: "absent or marginal and anecdotal"

### Never-Smokers vs Ex-Smokers
- Only 4 studies reported separate side-effect data
- Never-smokers had higher side-effect rates (ratio 0.86) than ex-smokers (ratio 0.47)
- Insufficient data for separate efficacy analysis

## Relevance to Brief Questions

### Q1: Cognitive effects in healthy non-smokers
- 3 studies involved healthy volunteers (biological effects only)
- 6 studies on cognitive impairment in elderly showed trends
- Evidence is indirect (clinical populations, not healthy non-smokers specifically for cognition)
- Relevant as safety and dosing foundation for cognitive studies

### Q2: Cardiovascular/metabolic health at <5 mg/day
- Most studies used doses well above 5 mg/day (mean target ~19 mg/day)
- Cardiovascular data from 9 studies shows mostly benign profile
- No severe CV events in any study
- Cannot isolate effects specifically at <5 mg/day from this review

### Q3: Longevity pathways
- Not directly addressed. Mentions anti-inflammatory hypothesis via alpha-7 nicotinic receptors and cytokine storm suppression (COVID-19 context)

### Q4: Intermittent vs daily
- All studies used daily administration with gradual dose changes
- No intermittent protocols identified
- Provides titration schedules (2-7 steps over 3-96 days) as dosing reference

### Q5: Dependence/withdrawal risk
- **Strong relevance.** Key finding: zero dependence cases in 608 non-smokers across 33 studies
- Important caveat: formal dependence assessment was absent in most studies
- Transdermal route characterized as lower-risk than smoking for inducing dependence

### Q6: Evidence against low-dose nicotine
- 38.35% nausea rate in nicotine group (vs 7.53% comparator)
- 7.1% treatment discontinuation rate
- Authors conclude efficacy is "absent or marginal and anecdotal"
- Not recommended for routine use in any disease

### Q7: Expert disagreement
- Authors are cautious: "can only be used in clinical trials"
- Propose 14 mg/day target with gradual titration for COVID-19 trials
- Acknowledge lack of formal dependence assessment as a critical gap

## Limitations
- Only 5/33 studies were registered clinical trials (NCT numbers)
- 11/33 studies did not report side effects at all
- Only 13/33 had detailed adverse event reporting
- Most studies did not formally assess dependence risk
- Insufficient data to separate never-smoker vs ex-smoker outcomes
- Heterogeneous conditions prevent disease-specific conclusions
- Small sample sizes per study (largest single study: 102 patients)
