# src-666: Nicotine-induced Upregulation of Nicotinic Receptors

**Govind AP, Vezina P, Green WN (2009). Biochemical Pharmacology, 78(7), 756-765.**
**PMCID:** PMC2728164 | **PMID:** 19540212 | **Citations:** 414
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.bcp.2009.06.011

---

## Study Design

This is a **review/commentary article** (not an original empirical study) synthesizing evidence on nicotine-induced upregulation of nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs). The authors are from the University of Chicago (Neurobiology and Psychiatry departments). The paper reviews in vitro, in vivo (animal), and postmortem human brain data to evaluate six proposed mechanisms of upregulation and their relevance to nicotine addiction.

**Methodology:** Literature synthesis with the authors' own experimental data on alpha4beta2 receptors expressed in HEK cells, including radioligand binding assays, surface biotinylation, pulse-chase analysis, and antibody binding studies.

## Key Findings

### 1. Nicotine upregulates high-affinity nAChR binding sites (primarily alpha4beta2)
- Postmortem brains of smokers show increased 3H-nicotine binding sites compared to non-smokers.
- Upregulation is **posttranscriptional** -- no change in receptor subunit mRNA levels observed for up to 10 days of nicotine exposure.
- Magnitude of upregulation varies: ~2.5-fold in M10 cells to 15-fold in HEK cell lines.
- Blood nicotine levels in smokers (100-200 nM) reach and maintain levels sufficient for upregulation.

### 2. Subtype-specific upregulation patterns (critical for intermittent vs. daily)
- **alpha4beta2**: Upregulates with **sustained, low nicotine** concentrations.
- **alpha6beta2**: Requires **higher nicotine concentrations** but upregulates **10-fold faster** than alpha4beta2 -- suited to **transient, high nicotine** exposures.
- **alpha3beta2**: Requires higher concentrations than alpha4beta2; similar speed to alpha6beta2.
- This differential kinetics is highly relevant to intermittent vs. continuous dosing patterns.

### 3. Intermittent vs. continuous exposure produces different receptor changes
- **Moderate intermittent nicotine** produced transient upregulation in the VTA (not NAcc) -- observed at 3 hours but gone by 3 days and 3 weeks.
- **Chronic continuous nicotine** upregulated alpha4 receptors specifically in midbrain **GABA neurons** (not DA neurons), increasing inhibition of mesoaccumbens dopamine activity -- linked to withdrawal symptoms.
- Different exposure regimens may show different **cell-specific** receptor upregulation patterns.

### 4. Upregulation does not directly cause sensitization or tolerance
- Temporal correlation between upregulation and locomotor sensitization does not always hold.
- Upregulation may represent an **early step** in a cascade leading to sensitization rather than being the primary mechanism.
- Blocking VTA nicotine receptors during exposure blocks induction of long-term locomotor sensitization.

### 5. Six proposed posttranslational mechanisms of upregulation
1. Slowed cell surface receptor turnover
2. Increased receptor trafficking to surface
3. Enhanced subunit maturation/assembly in ER
4. Changes in subunit stoichiometry (2:3 vs 3:2 alpha:beta ratio)
5. Block of subunit degradation in ER (proteasome inhibition)
6. Conformational change increasing agonist affinity without increasing receptor number

The authors favor mechanism #6 (conformational change), noting that their data shows upregulation of 125I-epibatidine binding without significant changes in surface or intracellular receptor numbers. However, they acknowledge that multiple mechanisms likely coexist.

### 6. Alpha4beta2 receptors in VTA dopamine neurons may resist upregulation
- Alpha5 accessory subunit association may prevent upregulation of alpha4beta2 receptors in DA neurons.
- Only a small percentage of receptors contain alpha5; the impact on overall population upregulation may be limited.

## Doses Used

This is a review paper; doses discussed include:
- **In vitro:** 0.1-10 uM nicotine for alpha4beta2 Ca2+ entry inhibition; IC50 of 0.7 uM for dopamine release suppression
- **Smoker blood levels:** 100-200 nM (steady-state), higher immediately post-cigarette
- **Nicotine half-life:** 2-3 hours in humans
- **In vivo (rat):** 14-day subcutaneous nicotine producing upregulation

## Population

Review article covering:
- Postmortem human brain tissue (smokers vs. non-smokers)
- Rat and mouse brain preparations
- Heterologous expression systems (HEK cells, M10 fibroblasts, Xenopus oocytes)
- Cultured VTA neurons

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1 (Low-dose cognitive effects)
- **Indirect relevance.** The paper discusses functional upregulation where alpha4beta2 receptors become "hyperfunctional" after nicotine removal, with increased evoked currents and higher affinity. This could relate to cognitive enhancement mechanisms but is not directly addressed.

### Q4 (Intermittent vs. daily -- tolerance development)
- **Highly relevant.** The subtype-specific upregulation kinetics are key: alpha4beta2 receptors (the predominant brain subtype, ~90% of high-affinity sites) upregulate with sustained low nicotine, while alpha6beta2 receptors upregulate faster but require higher concentrations. Intermittent dosing produced transient VTA-specific upregulation (3h only), while continuous dosing produced GABA-neuron-specific upregulation linked to withdrawal. This suggests intermittent low-dose exposure may produce fundamentally different (and potentially more benign) receptor adaptations than continuous exposure.

### Q5 (Dependence/withdrawal risk)
- **Core relevance.** This is one of the foundational papers on nicotine receptor upregulation. Key insight: upregulation occurs at smoking-relevant nicotine concentrations, is posttranslational (rapid onset), and the pattern depends on exposure regimen. Continuous exposure upregulates GABA neuron receptors, increasing inhibition of reward pathways during withdrawal. The paper also establishes that upregulation magnitude (up to 15-fold) far exceeds what could be explained by increased receptor assembly alone, suggesting conformational changes that fundamentally alter receptor pharmacology.

### Q6 (Evidence against)
- **Indirectly relevant.** The paper notes that upregulation does NOT directly correlate with sensitization/tolerance behaviors, and that alpha4beta2 receptors in VTA DA neurons may resist upregulation (due to alpha5 subunit association). These qualify the simple model that upregulation = tolerance/dependence.

## Claims to Verify

1. **"alpha6beta2 upregulation occurred 10-fold faster than alpha4beta2"** (line 554): This is a specific quantitative claim from the authors' own work (reference [126]). Verify in the original source study.
2. **"alpha4beta2 receptors in dopaminergic neurons in the VTA are not upregulated by nicotine"** (line 542-543): From reference [57] (Nashmi et al.). This is an important qualification -- if VTA DA neuron receptors resist upregulation, the behavioral implications differ from the general upregulation narrative.

## Limitations

- Review/commentary format: synthesizes diverse study designs with different methodologies, making direct quantitative comparisons difficult.
- Much of the mechanistic data comes from heterologous expression systems (HEK cells), not native neurons.
- The authors advocate for their preferred mechanism (conformational change) over alternatives, which may introduce bias.
- Published in 2009; subsequent research may have resolved some of the debated mechanisms.
- Does not directly address low-dose cognitive effects or human behavioral outcomes.
