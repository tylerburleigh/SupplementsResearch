# src-005: Nicotine Patch in Mild Cognitive Impairment (Newhouse et al., 2012)

**Source type:** Press release from American Academy of Neurology (aan.com)
**Quality:** Degraded -- metadata flags paywall; content is a press release summarizing the study, not the full paper.
**Reference:** Newhouse, P. et al. (2012). Published in *Neurology*, January 10, 2012 print issue.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **Duration:** 6 months with assessments at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months
- **Intervention:** Transdermal nicotine patch 15 mg/day vs placebo
- **Outcome measures:** Several tests of memory and thinking skills (specific tests not named in press release)

## Key Findings

- Nicotine-treated group **regained 46% of normal performance for age** on long-term memory after 6 months
- Placebo group **worsened by 26%** over the same period
- No serious side effects reported in the nicotine group
- Author cautions against self-medication and notes that long-term benefit persistence is unknown

## Doses Used

- 15 mg/day transdermal nicotine patch
- **Note:** This dose exceeds the brief's threshold of <5 mg/day. It is 3x the upper bound of interest. This limits direct applicability to the low-dose research question.

## Population

- N = 74 non-smokers with mild cognitive impairment (MCI)
- Mean age: 76 years
- MCI defined as stage between normal aging and dementia with mild memory/thinking problems but no significant disability

## Relevance to Brief Questions

- **Q1 (Cognitive effects in healthy non-smokers):** Partially relevant. Shows cognitive benefit (long-term memory) from nicotine in non-smokers, but population is older adults with MCI, not healthy adults. Dose (15 mg/day) far exceeds the <5 mg/day threshold.
- **Q2 (Cardiovascular/metabolic health at <5 mg/day):** Limited. Reports no serious side effects at 15 mg/day over 6 months, but dose is above threshold and no specific cardiovascular/metabolic markers mentioned.
- **Q3 (Longevity pathways, neurodegeneration):** Tangentially relevant. Demonstrates nicotine receptor mechanism in memory and notes receptor loss in Alzheimer's. Suggests neuroprotective potential but no direct longevity pathway data.
- **Q5 (Dependence/withdrawal at <5 mg/day):** No data on dependence or withdrawal at this dose.
- **Q6 (Evidence against use):** Author explicitly cautions against self-medication.
- **Q7 (Expert disagreement):** Author recommends medical supervision and notes uncertainty about long-term benefits.

## Limitations

1. **Source is a press release**, not the full paper. Details of methodology, statistical analysis, and full results are unavailable.

3. **Population is MCI patients (mean age 76)**, not healthy non-smokers as specified in the brief.
4. No information on dependence, withdrawal, or long-term follow-up.
5. Pfizer Inc. provided the transdermal nicotine patches (industry involvement noted).
