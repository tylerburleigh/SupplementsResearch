# src-011: The Effects of Transdermal Nicotine on Cognition in Nonsmokers with Schizophrenia and Nonpsychiatric Controls

**Citation:** Barr RS, Culhane MA, Jubelt LE, et al. (2008). Neuropsychopharmacology, 33, 480-490. DOI: 10.1038/sj.npp.1301423

**Source Quality:** DEGRADED (paywall). Only abstract and references accessible. Full text not available. Evidence limited to abstract-reported findings.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled, crossover trial
- **Dose:** 14 mg transdermal nicotine patch (single dose)
- **Timing:** Cognitive battery conducted before and 3 hours after patch application
- **Population:** Nonsmokers only (to eliminate withdrawal/reinstatement confounds)
  - 28 individuals with schizophrenia
  - 32 healthy non-psychiatric controls
- **Primary outcome:** Continuous Performance Test Identical Pairs (CPT-IP) Version
- **Secondary outcomes:** Card Stroop task

## Key Findings

1. **Attention improvement (both groups):** Nicotine significantly improved CPT-IP performance as measured by hit reaction time, hit reaction time standard deviation, and random errors in both schizophrenia and control groups.
2. **Greater benefit in schizophrenia:** Nicotine reduced commission errors on CPT-IP and improved Card Stroop performance to a greater extent in schizophrenia patients vs controls, suggesting enhanced response inhibition where baseline deficits exist.
3. **Mechanism:** The nAChR system is integral to regulation of attentional processes; schizophrenia involves dysregulation of this system.

## Doses Used

- **14 mg transdermal nicotine** (single dose)
- NOTE: This dose is substantially above the low-dose threshold of <5 mg/day defined in the research brief. It approximates the nicotine delivery of ~10-14 cigarettes. Results may not generalize to low-dose regimens.

## Population

- Nonsmokers only (critical design strength)
- schizophrenia group: n=28
- Healthy controls: n=32
- Eliminates withdrawal/reinstatement confounds inherent in smoker studies

## Limitations

- **Paywall restricted:** Only abstract available; full methods, statistical details, and adverse event data inaccessible.
- **Dose mismatch:** 14 mg is nearly 3x the low-dose threshold of 5 mg/day. Findings may overstate effects achievable at low doses.
- **Acute only:** Single-dose study; no data on chronic effects, tolerance, or dependence.
- **Mixed population:** Primary focus is schizophrenia; healthy controls are a comparison group, not the primary target.
- **Sample size moderate:** n=60 total is adequate but not large.

## Claims to Verify

- **Dose:** Abstract states "14 mg transdermal nicotine" -- this is a standard commercial patch dose, not a low dose. Any extrapolation to low-dose effects must be explicitly qualified.
- **Statistical significance:** Abstract says "significantly improved" but no p-values, effect sizes, or confidence intervals available without full text.
