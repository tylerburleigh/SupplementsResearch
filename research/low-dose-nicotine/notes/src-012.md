# src-012: Wignall & de Wit (2011) - Nicotine Effects on Attention and Inhibitory Control in Healthy Nonsmokers

**Citation:** Wignall ND, de Wit H. Effects of Nicotine on Attention and Inhibitory Control in Healthy Nonsmokers. Exp Clin Psychopharmacol. 2011 Jun;19(3):183-191. doi:10.1037/a0023292. PMCID: PMC4266322.

**Note:** Metadata quality field says "degraded" due to paywall detection, but the full text is available from PMC and is complete and substantive.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Two-session, within-subject, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover
- **N:** 40 healthy nonsmokers (50% female), mean age 23.9 (SD 3.8)
- **Dose:** 7mg transdermal nicotine (Nicoderm patch) vs. placebo (capsaicin to mimic tingling)
- **Absorption period:** 3 hours after patch placement before cognitive testing began
- **Sessions separated by:** minimum 48 hours, maximum 14 days
- **Population:** 35 never-smokers, 3 occasional smokers, 2 former smokers (quit >3 years ago); none had smoked in past 30 days
- **Screening:** Physical exam, ECG, psychiatric interview, drug/alcohol screening, BDI, MAST
- **Cognitive tasks:** Attention Network Test (ANT), Stroop Test, Stop Signal Task, Modified Signal Detection Task, Balloon Analogue Risk Task (BART)
- **Physiological measures:** Heart rate, mean arterial pressure (recorded at arrival, 60, 120, 180, 260, 280 min)
- **Subjective measures:** POMS, Drug Effects Questionnaire (DEQ)

## Key Findings

### Cognitive Effects
1. **Stroop test improved:** Nicotine significantly improved the Stroop effect (F(1,35)=6.18, p=.018), driven by increased color-word score (F(1,35)=8.13, p=.007). This reflects improved interference control/behavioral inhibition.
2. **Orienting attention impaired:** Nicotine significantly decreased orienting effect on ANT (F(1,35)=8.79, p=0.01), meaning selective attention was worsened.
3. **No effect on:** Alerting attention, conflict attention, overall accuracy (ANT), stop signal reaction time, go reaction time (stop task), risk-taking (BART), mood (POMS).
4. **Reward responsiveness decreased:** Nicotine reduced response bias on signal detection task (F(1,35)=6.08, p=0.019), contradicting prior work (Barr et al. 2008) that found increased reward responsiveness with 14mg.

### Physiological Effects
5. **Heart rate increased:** F(1,34)=7.45, p=0.01 (mean: 67.14 nicotine vs. 64.90 placebo bpm)
6. **Mean arterial pressure increased:** Treatment x time interaction F(1,34)=7.56, p=.009, significant at 60, 120, 240, and 280 min (mean: 88.00 vs. 85.86 mmHg)

### Subjective Effects
7. **"Feel" drug effect increased:** F(4,36)=8.10, p<.001, significant at 60-260 min
8. **"Like" drug effect decreased:** F(4,36)=2.96, p=.033 -- participants disliked the nicotine effect, possibly due to mild nausea
9. **No mood changes on POMS** scales

## Doses Used

- 7mg transdermal nicotine (Nicoderm patch) -- single acute dose
- This is at the upper boundary of what the research brief considers "low-dose" (<5mg/day via patches/pouches)
- Note: transdermal delivery provides sustained release over hours, so plasma levels differ from gum/pouch delivery

## Population

- Healthy young adult nonsmokers (N=40, 50% female)
- Mean age 23.9 (SD 3.8), range 18-40
- Mean BMI 23.4 (SD 2.4)
- Race: 26 Caucasian, 5 Asian, 5 Hispanic, 2 African-American, 2 Other
- Mean education 15.4 years
- 35 never-smokers, 5 with minimal past exposure
- Excluded: Axis I disorders, heavy drinking, BDI >9, MAST >5

## Relevance to Brief Questions

### Q1 (Cognitive effects in healthy nonsmokers)
**Highly relevant.** Directly tests attention and inhibitory control in healthy nonsmokers at 7mg transdermal nicotine. Findings are mixed: improved interference control (Stroop) but impaired orienting attention (ANT), with no effects on other measures. The overall conclusion is that nicotine produces **minimal cognitive enhancement** in healthy nonsmokers, affecting only specific subtypes of cognition. This challenges optimistic claims about broad cognitive enhancement.

### Q2 (Cardiovascular/metabolic at <5mg/day)
**Partially relevant.** The 7mg dose exceeds the brief's <5mg/day threshold, but the cardiovascular effects are still informative. Demonstrated significant increases in heart rate (+2.24 bpm) and mean arterial pressure (+2.14 mmHg) -- modest but statistically significant acute cardiovascular effects.

### Q5 (Dependence/withdrawal risk at sub-5mg/day)
**Moderately relevant.** The finding that participants **disliked** the drug effect (decreased DEQ "like" ratings) and experienced mild nausea suggests low abuse potential at this dose in nonsmokers. No pleasurable subjective effects were detected.

### Q6 (Evidence against low-dose nicotine use)
**Relevant.** The study provides evidence that (a) cognitive benefits are minimal and task-specific, (b) orienting attention is impaired, (c) reward responsiveness is decreased (not increased), and (d) cardiovascular effects occur even at this dose. The authors conclude that cognitive enhancement is unlikely to be a significant motivating factor for nicotine use in nonsmokers.

## Limitations

1. Only a single dose (7mg) tested -- no dose-response characterization
2. Possible ceiling effects on some tasks (very high accuracy)
3. Physiological and subjective effects dissipating by end of ~90-minute task battery
4. Small monetary rewards for BART/signal detection ($4.80 and $6.20) relative to study compensation ($150) may have weakened motivational salience
5. No baseline performance assessment independent of drug condition (regression to the mean concern)
6. BART data collected from only 27 of 40 participants
7. Acute single-dose study; no chronic dosing data

## Claims to Verify

- N=40 is reported consistently throughout. Verified: demographics table shows 40 (20 female). Consistent.
- Dose of 7mg transdermal Nicoderm patch. Verified in methods and confirmed by brand name.
- The authors note that the 7mg dose is comparable to doses used in other studies with nonsmokers, but the dose-response function of nicotine is described as "complex and non-linear" (line 336-337), which is important context for extrapolating to lower doses.
