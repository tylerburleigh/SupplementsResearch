# src-663: Baker et al. (2013) - Intermittent nicotine, nAChR upregulation, and locomotor sensitization

**Citation:** Baker LK, Mao D, Chi H, Govind AP, Vallejo YF, Iacoviello M, Herrera S, Cortright JJ, Green WN, McGehee DS, Vezina P. (2013). Intermittent nicotine exposure upregulates nAChRs in VTA dopamine neurons and sensitizes locomotor responding to the drug. *European Journal of Neuroscience*, 37(6), 1004-1011. doi:10.1111/ejn.12114. PMID: 23331514. PMCID: PMC3604051. 56 citations.

## Study Design

- **Subjects:** Male Sprague-Dawley rats (180-250g at arrival)
- **Species:** Rat (preclinical)
- **Exposure regimen (intermittent):** 4 injections of nicotine (0.4 mg/kg, base, i.p.), one every other day over 7 days
- **Comparison regimen (continuous):** 3.0 mg/kg/day via osmotic minipump for 14 days
- **Methods:** Locomotor activity monitoring, 125I-epibatidine radioligand binding, whole-cell slice electrophysiology (nAChR currents, AMPAR/NMDAR ratios), in vivo microdialysis (NAcc DA), precipitated withdrawal assessment (mecamylamine challenge), intracranial nAChR blockade (mecamylamine, DHbE+MLA)
- **Analysis:** 2-way ANOVA with post-hoc Scheffe tests; independent t-tests for withdrawal and DA data

## Key Findings

### 1. VTA-selective nAChR activation required for sensitization (Q4)
- Bilateral mecamylamine infusion into VTA before each nicotine exposure injection dose-dependently blocked the induction of locomotor sensitization (exposure x mecamylamine interaction: F(2,30)=7.15, P<0.01)
- The same blockade in NAcc had NO effect on sensitization induction
- Confirmed with DHbE+MLA (non-alpha7 + alpha7 antagonists) in VTA

### 2. Intermittent nicotine upregulates nAChRs selectively in VTA DA neurons (Q4, Q6)
- Epibatidine binding: >2-fold increase in VTA at 2 hrs post-exposure, NOT at 3 days or 3 weeks
- NO upregulation in NAcc at any time point
- Functional nAChR upregulation (electrophysiology): selective in DA neurons (large Ih; exposure F(1,86)=6.03, P<0.05; interaction F(2,86)=6.68, P<0.01), NOT in GABA neurons (F(1,42)=0.86, NS)
- Effect was transient: significant only within 2 hours of last injection

### 3. Intermittent nicotine induces LTP at excitatory synapses onto VTA DA neurons (Q4)
- AMPAR/NMDAR ratio increased 17-26 hrs post-exposure (t(25)=2.05, P<0.05)
- Consistent with induction of long-term potentiation
- No differences in AMPA receptor rectification (GluR1 vs GluR2 composition unchanged)

### 4. No withdrawal from intermittent exposure; withdrawal from continuous exposure (Q5)
- Intermittent (4 x 0.4 mg/kg over 7 days): NO somatic withdrawal signs (t(10)=0.82, NS)
- Continuous (3.0 mg/kg/day for 14 days): SIGNIFICANT withdrawal signs (t(9)=1.84, P<0.05)
- Directly compared in same experimental framework

### 5. Enhanced DA overflow after intermittent but NOT continuous exposure (Q4, Q6)
- Intermittent: enhanced nicotine-evoked NAcc DA overflow 1 day post-exposure (t(12)=2.27, P<0.05)
- Continuous: NO enhancement of DA overflow (t(19)=0.39, NS)

### 6. Conceptual distinction: intermittent vs. continuous exposure (Q4, Q6)
- Intermittent exposure: VTA DA neuron nAChR upregulation, LTP, enhanced DA overflow, locomotor sensitization, no withdrawal -- models INITIAL drug exposure and development of addiction liability
- Continuous exposure: VTA GABA neuron nAChR upregulation, DA neuron inhibition, withdrawal symptoms -- models MAINTENANCE of established smoking
- Continuous stimulant exposure is NOT known to produce sensitization

## Doses Used

| Regimen | Dose | Route | Duration |
|---------|------|-------|----------|
| Intermittent | 0.4 mg/kg (base) | i.p. | 4 injections over 7 days (every other day) |
| Continuous (comparison) | 3.0 mg/kg/day | s.c. (minipump) | 14 days |
| Challenge/test dose | 0.4 mg/kg | i.p. | Single injection at test |

The 0.4 mg/kg i.p. dose is considered moderate. The paper frames this as modeling initial drug exposure patterns.

## Population

- Preclinical: Male Sprague-Dawley rats (180-250g)
- Not human data; but the conceptual framework maps intermittent exposure to initial tobacco experimentation

## Limitations

1. Single dose tested for the intermittent regimen (0.4 mg/kg); no dose-response for the sensitization/upregulation effects
2. Transient nature of nAChR upregulation (hours) means it cannot directly explain long-lasting sensitization (weeks-months); authors propose it initiates a cascade
3. No female subjects; sex differences not assessed
4. The paper does not test doses lower than 0.4 mg/kg, so cannot speak to whether ultra-low doses (e.g., 0.01-0.1 mg/kg) would produce similar effects
5. nAChR subunit composition of the upregulated receptors was not directly determined (speculated to involve alpha6-containing receptors)
6. The continuous comparison (3.0 mg/kg/day) is a much higher total dose and different route, making the comparison confounded by both pattern and total dose

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q4 (Intermittent vs daily dosing effects on receptor upregulation)
**Directly and centrally relevant.** This is one of the key papers showing that intermittent nicotine exposure produces a fundamentally different pattern of nAChR upregulation than continuous exposure. The intermittent pattern selectively upregulates nAChRs in VTA DA neurons (not GABA neurons), producing sensitization rather than withdrawal. This is critical evidence for why intermittent/episodic exposure (as might occur with low-dose products) could have different neuroadaptation consequences than continuous exposure (as with smoking).

### Q5 (Dependence/withdrawal risk)
**Highly relevant.** Directly demonstrates that moderate intermittent nicotine exposure does NOT produce somatic withdrawal signs, while continuous exposure does. This supports the argument that intermittent/low-dose patterns carry less withdrawal risk. However, the paper also notes that this same intermittent pattern promotes sensitization and enhances subsequent drug self-administration, which is a liability concern.

### Q6 (Evidence against safety)
**Relevant as a counterpoint.** The paper shows that intermittent nicotine exposure, while not producing withdrawal, DOES produce locomotor sensitization, LTP at excitatory synapses, enhanced DA overflow, and enhanced subsequent self-administration of nicotine, amphetamine, and cocaine. These are pro-addiction neuroadaptations. The paper explicitly states this pattern "may contribute to the development of increased liability for nicotine addiction."

## Claims to Verify

- The ">2-fold increase" in epibatidine binding (line ~528) should be verified against the actual figure data. The text states this but the exact values come from a figure that is not fully reproduced.
- The dose 0.4 mg/kg is described as "moderate" -- this is consistent with other literature but the characterization is interpretive rather than empirical.
- Sample sizes vary across experiments (n=5-11 per group typically); the total N across all experiments is not stated.
