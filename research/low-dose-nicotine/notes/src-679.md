# src-679: Jubelt et al. (2008) - Nicotine effects on episodic memory in non-smokers

**Citation:** Jubelt LE, Barr RS, Goff DC, Logvinenko T, Weiss AP, Evins AE. Effects of transdermal nicotine on episodic memory in non-smokers with and without schizophrenia. *Psychopharmacology (Berl)*. 2008;199(1):89-98. doi:10.1007/s00213-008-1133-8

**PMCID:** PMC4078257 | **PMID:** 18548234 | **Citations:** 124 | **Quality:** ok

---

## Core Findings

A single 14-mg transdermal nicotine dose selectively improved novelty detection in non-smokers on an episodic memory task, reducing false alarm rates (Cohen's d = 0.71, p < 0.01) and speeding reaction time to novel items (d = 0.71, p = 0.007). Nicotine had **no effect** on hit rate, source memory accuracy, discriminability (d'), or reaction time to previously-viewed items. The effect appears to reflect enhanced inhibitory control rather than general memory enhancement.

## Study Design

- **Design:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover
- **Dose:** Two 7-mg transdermal nicotine patches (total 14 mg) vs. two identical placebo patches
- **Timing:** Episodic memory task administered pre-dose and 4 hours post-dose (peak serum nicotine); patches removed ~4.5 h after application
- **Counterbalancing:** Order of nicotine/placebo was randomized and counterbalanced
- **Washout:** Mean 9.3 days between visits (6-15 days) for males/postmenopausal females; 42.3 days for cycling females
- **Statistical approach:** Repeated-measures linear mixed models; Bonferroni-corrected alpha = 0.0125 for four primary outcomes

## Doses Used

- **Route:** Transdermal (Nicoderm CQ)
- **Dose:** 14 mg total (two 7-mg patches applied simultaneously)
- **Serum nicotine achieved:** Mean 6.14 +/- 2.44 ng/ml (vs. 0.43 +/- 0.87 for placebo), measured ~4.5 h post-application
- **Note:** This dose produces serum levels well below typical smoking levels (~10-50 ng/ml), making it relevant to low-dose nicotine questions

## Population

- **Healthy controls:** N=12 non-smokers (8F/4M), mean age 35 +/- 2 years, mean IQ 111.8 +/- 6.8
- **Schizophrenia group:** N=10 non-smokers (5F/5M), mean age 46 +/- 10 years, mean IQ 97 +/- 14.2
- **Non-smoking status confirmed by:** Self-report, salivary cotinine <10 ng/ml, expired CO <9 ppm
- **Exclusion:** Tobacco use within 3 months, substance abuse/dependence (except caffeine), major depression, head injury, dementia
- **Note:** Most schizophrenia patients were former smokers (8/10); 4/12 controls were former smokers. Groups differed significantly in age, IQ, and prior smoking history.

## Key Findings

### Significant nicotine effects (both groups combined)
1. **False alarm rate reduction:** F(1,17)=8.47, p < 0.01, Cohen's d = 0.71
2. **Faster reaction time to novel items:** F(1,18)=9.11, p = 0.007, Cohen's d = 0.71
3. **Conservative shift in response bias (C):** F(1,18)=10.06, p = 0.005 (less likely to call items "old")

### Null findings
- No effect on hit rate (F(1,19)=1.11, p=0.31)
- No effect on source memory accuracy (F(1,18)=0.42, p=0.53)
- No effect on discriminability/d' (F(1,19)=0.49, p=0.49)
- No effect on reaction time for old items (F(1,18)=2.06, p=0.17)

### Diagnosis interaction
- Trend-level interaction on false alarm rate: F(1,17)=3.13, p=0.095, Cohen's d = 0.43
- Effect was stronger in schizophrenia group, but did not reach significance

## Mechanism Discussion

The authors propose that nicotine enhances novelty detection via stimulation of alpha-7 nAChRs on GABA interneurons in the hippocampus, increasing GABAergic inhibition of repeated stimuli (repetition suppression). This is analogous to nicotine's known effects on P50 sensory gating and prepulse inhibition. The conservative response bias shift supports an inhibitory control mechanism.

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size** (N=22): Limited power, especially for detecting diagnosis-by-treatment interactions
2. **Not purely never-smokers:** Most participants were former smokers (particularly in schizophrenia group), limiting generalizability to never-smokers
3. **Clozapine confound:** 50% of schizophrenia patients were on clozapine, which may attenuate nicotine's cognitive effects
4. **Single-dose study:** Cannot address effects of chronic nicotine administration
5. **Potential fatigue/order effects:** Other cognitive tests administered before the memory task
6. **Group differences:** Age, IQ, and smoking history differed between groups

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: Low-dose nicotine cognitive effects in healthy non-smokers
**Directly relevant.** Provides evidence that a single 14-mg transdermal nicotine dose (producing serum levels of ~6 ng/ml) selectively improves novelty detection and inhibitory control in healthy non-smokers. The effect is specific -- not a general cognitive enhancer, but targeted at stimulus discrimination and response inhibition. Moderate effect sizes (d=0.71) for key outcomes. Key caveat: most "non-smokers" were former smokers, not never-smokers.

### Q5: Dependence/withdrawal risk
**Tangentially relevant.** Two of 25 randomized participants dropped out due to nausea from the 14-mg patch (8% dropout from adverse effects), suggesting tolerability concerns at this dose in nicotine-naive individuals.

Not directly addressed.

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample size:** The paper reports 12 controls and 10 schizophrenia patients completing the study, with 25 randomized and 2 dropping from nausea and 1 from technical error. These numbers are internally consistent (25 - 3 = 22 completers).
- **Serum nicotine levels:** 6.14 ng/ml from 14-mg transdermal patch is plausible and consistent with known pharmacokinetics of nicotine patches at ~4-5 hours post-application.
- **Effect sizes:** Cohen's d = 0.71 reported for both false alarm rate and novel-item RT -- these are large effects, plausible given the crossover design but should be interpreted cautiously given the small N.
