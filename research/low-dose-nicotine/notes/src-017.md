# src-017: Transdermal Nicotine for Mood and Cognitive Symptoms in Non-Smokers with Late-Life Depression

**Citation:** Gandelman JA, Kang H, Antal A, et al. "Transdermal Nicotine for the Treatment of Mood and Cognitive Symptoms in Non-Smokers with Late-Life Depression." J Clin Psychiatry. 2018;79(5):18m12137. PMCID: PMC6129985.

**Design:** 12-week open-label clinical trial (NCT02816138)

## Study Design

- **Type:** Open-label, single-arm, proof-of-concept trial
- **Duration:** 12 weeks treatment + 3-week taper + follow-up at week 15
- **N enrolled:** 15 non-smoking older adults started patches; 14 completed all 12 weeks
- **Dosing:** Rigid dose escalation: 3.5mg (week 1), 7mg (weeks 2-3), 14mg (weeks 4-6), 21mg (weeks 7-12). Dose reductions allowed for tolerability. Patches worn ~16 hours/day.
- **Primary mood outcome:** MADRS change every 3 weeks
- **Primary cognitive outcome:** Conners CPT hit reaction time SE
- **Analysis:** Linear mixed effects model with AR(1) for repeated measures; Wilcoxon signed-rank for baseline vs week 12 comparisons

## Key Findings

1. **Mood:** Robust antidepressant response. MADRS decreased significantly over time (beta = -1.51, p < 0.001). Mean MADRS reduction = 18.45 points. Response rate = 86.7% (13/15); remission rate = 53.3% (8/15). Improvement significant by week 3 (Bonferroni-adjusted p = 0.004). Change not related to dose, smoking history, or concurrent antidepressant use.

2. **Secondary mood measures:** Significant improvement in apathy (p < 0.001) and all rumination subscales (total p = 0.002). Anhedonia and anxiety showed trends (p = 0.084 and 0.073 respectively). These changes were not correlated with MADRS change.

3. **Cognitive -- subjective:** Both MFQ (p = 0.049) and PROMIS (beta = 0.46, p = 0.001) showed significant improvement in subjective cognitive performance. However, these were correlated with MADRS improvement, raising questions about independence from mood effects.

4. **Cognitive -- objective:** Primary outcome (CPT) did not improve (p = 0.761). Secondary measures showed improvement in working memory speed (one-back, p = 0.049) and episodic memory immediate recall (shopping list task, p = 0.049). Authors note these should be viewed cautiously due to multiple comparisons.

5. **Negativity bias:** Significant increases in good adjectives endorsed (p = 0.046) and bad adjectives rejected (p = 0.004), plus decreased reaction times for both.

6. **Safety:** No serious adverse events. No significant BP or HR changes. Significant weight loss (-6.7 lb, p < 0.001) and BMI decrease (-1.0 kg/m2, p < 0.001). No withdrawal symptoms or cravings reported at 15-week follow-up.

## Doses Used

- Escalation from 3.5mg to 21mg/day (transdermal patch, ~16hr wear)
- Mean final dose: 15.4mg (SD = 6.3, range 7-21mg)
- Only 8/15 participants tolerated the full 21mg dose
- Monotherapy group reached lower mean final dose (8.8mg) vs augmentation group (19.8mg)

## Population

- N = 15 non-smoking older adults (age >= 60), mean age 64.9 (SD 4.6)
- All met DSM-IV-TR criteria for Major Depressive Disorder with MADRS >= 15
- Mean baseline MADRS = 27.7 (moderate-severe depression)
- Subjective cognitive impairment required (>= 20% on CCI)
- MoCA >= 24 required (mean 27.9, so objectively non-impaired)
- 66.7% female, mean education 18.2 years
- Primarily early-onset depression (mean age at first episode = 26.0)
- 33.3% past smokers (none in past year); 60% on concurrent antidepressant

## Relevance to Brief Questions

- **Q1 (Cognitive effects in healthy non-smokers):** Partially relevant. Population was depressed older adults, not healthy. However, all were non-smokers, and MoCA scores were normal (27.9). Working memory speed and episodic memory showed improvement. Primary cognitive outcome (attention/CPT) was negative. Mood improvement confounds subjective cognitive gains. Only moderate relevance to Q1 due to clinical population.

- **Q2 (Cardiovascular/metabolic at <5mg/day):** Doses used (7-21mg) exceed the <5mg/day threshold substantially. Finding of no BP/HR change is reassuring but at much higher doses than the brief targets. Weight loss of 6.7 lbs could be notable. Limited direct relevance to low-dose question.

- **Q3 (Longevity pathways):** Not addressed. No measurement of sirtuins, mTOR, AMPK, or other longevity biomarkers.

- **Q4 (Intermittent vs daily):** Not addressed. Patches were worn daily (~16hr).

- **Q5 (Dependence/withdrawal risk):** Directly relevant and positive finding. After 12 weeks of daily transdermal nicotine (7-21mg) followed by 3-week taper, no withdrawal symptoms or cravings reported. This is a clinical population but supports the notion that transdermal nicotine at moderate doses for 12 weeks does not produce dependence in non-smokers.

- **Q6 (Evidence against):** Primary cognitive outcome (CPT attention) was negative. Open-label design is a major limitation (response rates inflated vs placebo-controlled). Small sample (N=15). Only 53% could tolerate max dose. Multiple comparisons weaken cognitive findings.

- **Q7 (Expert disagreement):** Not directly addressed, though authors note that specific alpha7 nAChR agonists have shown limited efficacy, suggesting broad nAChR agonism (as with nicotine) may be needed, which some in the field may dispute.

## Claims to Verify

- **Mean weight loss of 6.7 lbs (p < 0.001):** This seems large for a 12-week trial. Confirmed in the text at lines 884-890. Worth cross-checking whether this is clinically plausible with nicotine at these doses.
- **86.7% response rate:** Verified as 13/15 participants. However, open-label design substantially inflates this compared to placebo-controlled trials. Authors acknowledge this.
- **No cravings at follow-up:** Stated but not quantified with a standardized craving scale. Relies on spontaneous report.
