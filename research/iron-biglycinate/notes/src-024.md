# src-024: Ferrous bisglycinate 18 mg vs ferrous sulfate 60 mg in Cambodian women

**Source:** PubMed abstract (PMID 37271416), RCT registered at NCT04017598
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37271416
**Venue:** American Journal of Clinical Nutrition (Elsevier for ASN), 2023
**Content quality:** Abstract only (PubMed landing page). No full text available, but abstract contains complete methods, results, and conclusions.

## Core Findings

A double-blind RCT in 480 nonpregnant Cambodian women (18-45 y) found that ferrous bisglycinate 18 mg/day was **not noninferior** to ferrous sulfate 60 mg/day for raising ferritin over 12 weeks. The adjusted mean ferritin difference was 14.6 ug/L (95% CI: 7.6, 21.6), against a noninferiority margin of 20 ug/L. Mean 12-week ferritin was 99 ug/L (ferrous sulfate), 84 ug/L (bisglycinate), and 78 ug/L (placebo). No gut health differences were observed between groups.

## Key Evidence

- **Dose comparison:** 18 mg ferrous bisglycinate (chelated form, ~1x RDA) vs 60 mg ferrous sulfate (standard therapeutic dose, ~3.3x RDA) vs placebo, 12 weeks
- **Primary outcome:** Adjusted mean ferritin difference of 14.6 ug/L between iron groups, within the 20 ug/L noninferiority margin by point estimate but favoring ferrous sulfate
- **Population:** Predominantly iron-replete (only 6% iron deficient, 17% anemic at baseline)
- **Gut safety:** Fecal calprotectin and 11-pathogen molecular panel (subset n=100) showed no differences across groups
- **Funding:** CIHR (Canadian Institutes of Health Research), Grant ID400771

## Methodology

- Double-blind, randomized placebo-controlled noninferiority trial
- n=480 nonpregnant women, 18-45 y, Cambodia
- 12-week supplementation period
- Blood: ferritin by ELISA (nonfasting)
- Stool: fecal calprotectin by ELISA; 11 enteropathogens by molecular assay (n=100 subset)
- Generalized linear mixed-effects models for adjusted estimates
- Noninferiority margin set a priori at 20 ug/L

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q3 (biglycinate vs other forms):** Direct head-to-head RCT evidence. Bisglycinate at 18 mg raised ferritin less than 60 mg ferrous sulfate, though both increased ferritin vs placebo. The ~3:1 dose ratio confounds pure form comparison -- the study cannot distinguish whether the difference is due to dose or form.
- **Q4 (low-dose protocols):** Tests a low-dose (18 mg) strategy explicitly. Shows it produces measurable but smaller ferritin increases than standard 60 mg.
- **Q5 (risks in non-deficient):** In a mostly iron-replete population, neither form worsened gut inflammation markers. No signal of harm, but also no demonstration of clinical benefit from supplementation.
- **Q6 (absorption optimization):** Chelated form (amino acid chelate) did not overcome the 3x dose disadvantage, suggesting bioavailability advantage of bisglycinate may not fully compensate for lower elemental iron dose at this ratio.

## Limitations

- Abstract only; full-text details (adverse events, hemoglobin changes, compliance) not available
- Noninferiority was not met -- bisglycinate 18 mg was inferior to ferrous sulfate 60 mg
- The ~3:1 dose ratio makes it impossible to isolate form effects from dose effects
- Population had low baseline iron deficiency (6%), limiting generalizability to deficient populations
- Enteropathogen analysis was only in a subset of n=100
- 12-week duration may be insufficient for full ferritin repletion assessment

## Claims to Verify

- **n=480:** Stated in abstract methods. Reasonable for an RCT of this type. Verify against clinicaltrials.gov NCT04017598 if full text becomes available.
- **Baseline prevalence figures (17% anemia, 6% iron deficiency):** Stated in results section. Should be verifiable from baseline characteristics table in full text.
- **Ferritin values at 12 wk:** 99, 84, 78 ug/L for the three groups -- these are means with 95% CIs, not medians. Ferritin is typically right-skewed, so mean values could be influenced by outliers. Full text should clarify whether log-transformation was used.
