# src-023: Ferrous Bisglycinate vs Ferrous Fumarate in Pregnant Women with Iron Deficiency

**Source type:** PubMed abstract page (PMID 35276810)
**Full text status:** Abstract only; full text not available in source file
**Study design:** Randomized Controlled Trial, n=120
**Quality note:** Metadata was largely empty (no title/authors/year extracted), but the abstract is substantive and identifiable by PMID.

## Core Findings

Ferrous bisglycinate (24 mg elemental iron equivalent) with folinic acid and multivitamins achieved significantly better bioavailability and numerically greater improvements in all hematological biomarkers compared to ferrous fumarate (66 mg elemental iron) over 6 months in iron-deficient pregnant women (n=120). Despite containing roughly one-third the elemental iron, the bisglycinate formulation produced higher serum iron at 1 and 2 hours post-dose (p<0.001) and significantly fewer GI side effects (p<0.001).

## Key Evidence

- **Bioavailability:** Serum iron increases were significantly higher in the bisglycinate group vs fumarate group at both 1h and 2h post-dose (p<0.001), despite 24 mg vs 66 mg elemental iron.
- **Hematological efficacy:** Both groups showed significant increases (p<0.001) in Hb, ferritin, TSAT, MCV, MCH, MCHC, erythrocytes, and reticulocytes at 3 and 6 months. The bisglycinate group had numerically larger increases at every timepoint.
- **Tolerability:** Bisglycinate group reported significantly fewer side effects: nausea, abdominal pain, bloating, constipation, metallic taste (p<0.001).
- **Dose advantage:** 24 mg bisglycinate matched or outperformed 66 mg fumarate, suggesting substantially higher bioavailability per mg of elemental iron.

## Methodology

- RCT, n=120 pregnant women at 12-16 weeks gestation with iron deficiency
- Test group (n=60): ferrous bisglycinate (equiv. 24 mg iron) + folinic acid + multivitamins, daily after breakfast
- Control group (n=60): ferrous fumarate (equiv. 66 mg iron), daily after breakfast
- Absorption assessed via fasted serum iron at 1h and 2h post-supplementation
- Iron status biomarkers assessed at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months
- Side effects monitored throughout

## Limitations

1. **Industry-funded:** Study funded by Max Biocare Pty Ltd (supplement manufacturer), introducing potential funding bias.
2. **Pregnant population only:** Results may not generalize to non-pregnant adults or older populations interested in longevity.
3. **Abstract-only content:** Full text not available; cannot verify detailed methods, randomization procedure, or exact statistical analyses.
4. **Co-interventions:** Test group received folinic acid and multivitamins alongside bisglycinate, confounding the isolated effect of the iron formulation.
5. **"Numerically larger" claims:** The abstract states bisglycinate was "numerically larger" on biomarkers -- this may not have reached statistical significance for individual endpoints.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q3 (biglycinate vs other forms):** Strong direct evidence. Head-to-head RCT showing bisglycinate outperforms ferrous fumarate at roughly 1/3 the dose on both absorption and tolerability.
- **Q4 (low-dose protocols):** Relevant. Demonstrates that 24 mg/day bisglycinate is effective over 6 months, supporting low-dose approaches.

## Claims to Verify

- The abstract reports "numerically larger" increases for the test group on all biomarkers but does not provide p-values for between-group comparisons on these endpoints. The p<0.001 values cited appear to be within-group changes. Whether between-group differences were statistically significant is unclear without full text.
- Exact serum iron values at each timepoint are not reported in the abstract.
- Sample size of 120 (60 per group) is adequate but modest for a superiority trial; whether it was powered for the observed between-group differences is unknown.
