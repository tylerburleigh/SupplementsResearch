# src-066: Subclinical Iron Deficiency Prevalence and Screening in Non-Anemic Individuals

**Citation:** PubMed PMID 31302652. Published 2019 in Annals of Hematology (S. Karger AG). URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31302652
**Content quality:** Abstract only (PubMed landing page). No full text available. Quality assessment based on structured abstract with Methods, Results, and Conclusions.

## Core Findings

Subclinical iron deficiency (SID) -- defined by low ferritin combined with low serum iron or low transferrin saturation -- affects 3.3% of non-anemic females and 0.6% of non-anemic males overall, but prevalence rises sharply in reproductive-age females (7.6% in ages 15-49) and elderly males (7.8% in ages >=70). MCH (mean corpuscular hemoglobin) is a reliable screening index for SID with AUC >0.87 in both sexes.

## Key Evidence

- **Sample:** 16,485 non-anemic health examinees (3,567 males, 12,918 females) across 16 health-promotion centers in 13 Korean cities, January 2017 - June 2018
- **SID definition:** Ferritin <24 ug/L (males) / <15 ug/L (females) PLUS serum iron <44 ug/dL (males) / <29 ug/dL (females) OR transferrin saturation <20%
- **Prevalence:** 0.6% males, 3.3% females overall; 7.8% in males >=70 years; 7.6% in females 15-49 years
- **Male risk factors:** Older age (OR 1.069, p=0.004), lower Hb (OR 0.58, p=0.04), lower MCH (OR 0.433, p<0.001), higher RDW (OR 1.374, p=0.049)
- **Female risk factors:** Lower BMI (OR 0.929, p<0.001), younger age (OR 0.954, p<0.001), lower Hb, lower MCH, higher RDW
- **Best screening index:** MCH with AUC 0.877 (males) and 0.872 (females); cut-offs 29.2 pg (males) and 29.3 pg (females)

## Methodology

- Retrospective cross-sectional study at 16 health-promotion centers in 13 Korean cities
- All participants were non-anemic (normal hemoglobin) but underwent comprehensive iron panel testing
- SID defined by dual criteria: low ferritin PLUS either low serum iron or low transferrin saturation
- Logistic regression used to identify factors associated with SID
- ROC analysis used to evaluate RBC indices for SID screening

## Limitations

- **Korean population only:** Results may not generalize to other ethnicities/regions with different iron status distributions
- **Retrospective design:** Cannot establish causality for risk factors
- **Abstract only:** Full text unavailable; unable to verify detailed methods, examine sensitivity analyses, or assess potential confounders in multivariable models
- **No clinical outcomes:** Study identifies SID prevalence but does not link SID to health consequences, symptoms, or clinical endpoints
- **SID definition is conservative:** Using both ferritin AND serum iron/transferrin saturation may underestimate true prevalence compared to ferritin-only criteria

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (health consequences of low-normal iron):** Indirectly relevant. Demonstrates that SID exists in non-anemic individuals at meaningful rates, but does not quantify health consequences of SID. The conclusion that screening is needed "to prevent the development of anemia" implies SID is a pre-clinical state, but no outcome data is presented.
- **Q7 (clinical guidelines):** Relevant. Proposes MCH as a practical screening tool for SID in non-anemic populations, with specific cut-offs. Identifies high-risk groups (reproductive-age females with low BMI, elderly males) who should be screened.
- **Q2-Q6:** Not directly relevant. Does not address iron-longevity relationships, iron form comparisons, dosing protocols, risks of supplementation, or absorption optimization.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample size of 16,485 is large and well-specified (3,567 males + 12,918 females). Appears internally consistent.
- The SID prevalence figures (0.6%, 3.3%, 7.8%, 7.6%) come from the abstract and cannot be cross-checked against methods without full text.
- OR values are plausible for a large cross-sectional study.
