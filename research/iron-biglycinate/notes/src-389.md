# src-389: Iron Absorption from Supplements Is Greater with Alternate Day Than with Consecutive Day Dosing in Iron-Deficient Anemic Women

**Citation:** Stoffel NU, Zeder C, Brittenham GM, Moretti D, Zimmermann MB. Haematologica. 2020;105(5):1232-1239. doi:10.3324/haematol.2019.220830
**Citations:** 170 | **Quality:** ok | **Type:** Academic (peer-reviewed)

## Core Findings

In iron-deficient anemic women, alternate-day dosing of oral iron (100 or 200 mg as ferrous sulfate) produced 40-50% higher fractional iron absorption compared to consecutive-day dosing. This effect is mediated by serum hepcidin, which increases ~24 hours after oral iron doses and returns to baseline by 48 hours, suppressing absorption of a dose given the next day. Total iron absorption from 200 mg given on alternate days was approximately twice that from 100 mg on consecutive days, suggesting equivalent total absorption can be achieved with less frequent, higher doses.

## Key Evidence

1. **Alternate-day FIA advantage:** FIA on day 5 (alternate) was 40-50% higher than day 3 (consecutive) for both 100 and 200 mg doses (P<0.001). FIA did not differ between day 2 (baseline) and day 5 (alternate), confirming no residual mucosal block at 48 hours.

2. **Hepcidin kinetics:** SHep was significantly higher on day 3 (consecutive) than day 2 (baseline) or day 5 (alternate) for both doses (P<0.001, P<0.01 respectively). No difference between days 2 and 5 confirms hepcidin returns to baseline by 48h.

3. **Dose efficiency:** FIA was significantly lower from 200 mg vs 100 mg (P<0.001). Geometric mean FIA: 100mg = 23.5%/17.0%/25.0% (days 2/3/5); 200mg = 17.3%/11.9%/16.8%.

4. **Total absorption equivalence:** TIA from 200 mg alternate-day was ~2x that from 100 mg consecutive-day (P<0.001). FIA from 100 mg daily was not significantly different from 200 mg alternate-day (P=0.792).

5. **GI side effects:** 40% lower incidence with 100 mg vs 200 mg but not statistically significant (P=0.105). Most common: nausea/epigastric pain. All adverse events were grade I-II.

6. **Hepcidin blunting in IDA:** SHep increase of ~0.4 nM in IDA women was much smaller than ~2 nM seen in non-anemic iron-depleted women after similar doses, likely due to stronger suppression by erythropoietic drive.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Crossover study with two 6-day supplementation periods separated by 16-day incorporation period
- **Population:** 19 women with IDA (median Hb 11.5 g/dL, mean SF 10 mg/L), mostly blood donors, age 19-26
- **Intervention:** 100 or 200 mg FeSO4 given on days 2, 3 (consecutive), and 5 (alternate), labeled with stable isotopes (57Fe, 58Fe, 54Fe)
- **Absorption measurement:** Erythrocyte isotope incorporation 16 days post-administration (gold standard)
- **Statistical analysis:** Linear mixed effects model with Bonferroni correction
- **Registration:** NCT03623997

## Limitations

- Participants were only mildly/borderline anemic (Hb 8.6-12.5 g/dL); results may not generalize to severe anemia (Hb <8 g/dL)
- Most participants had recently donated blood, which may influence EPO and hepcidin response
- Whether results apply to chronic anemia (vs acute blood loss) cannot be concluded
- Iron form was ferrous sulfate only; results may not directly transfer to iron biglycinate
- Study was not designed with washout between consecutive and alternate-day testing within each period

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q4 (low-dose/alternate-day protocols):** Directly and strongly relevant. This is a landmark study demonstrating the alternate-day dosing advantage in IDA women. Shows 40-50% higher FIA with alternate-day vs consecutive-day dosing at 100-200 mg doses. Supports the mechanistic rationale for alternate-day dosing via hepcidin kinetics.
- **Q6 (absorption optimization):** Highly relevant. Shows hepcidin suppression of absorption lasts 24h but not 48h, that lower doses are absorbed more efficiently (fractionally), and that no mucosal block exists beyond 48h. Provides mechanistic basis for timing optimization.
- **Q5 (risks of supplementing non-deficient):** Moderately relevant. Notes GI side effects are dose-dependent and that high unabsorbed iron causes gut inflammation, free radical production, and adverse gut microbiome changes. However, study population was iron-deficient, not iron-replete.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample size: 19 participants is stated consistently throughout (Methods, Results, Tables); 3 dropped out after first period but data included from first period. Verified.
- "40-50% higher FIA": Geometric mean FIA comparisons (day 5 vs day 3) show: 100mg = 25.0/17.0 = 47% higher; 200mg = 16.8/11.9 = 41% higher. Confirmed.
- "Total iron absorption approximately twice": This is a comparison of TIA from 200 mg alternate-day vs 100 mg consecutive-day. The P<0.001 is stated for TIA comparison. Appears consistent with data.
- Hepcidin increase of ~0.4 nM in IDA vs ~2 nM in non-anemic: These are approximate values from discussion comparing to prior study (Moretti 2015). Not directly measurable from this study's data alone.
