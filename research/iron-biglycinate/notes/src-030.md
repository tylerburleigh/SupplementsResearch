## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive JAMA review on iron deficiency (ID) and iron-deficiency anemia (IDA). It covers epidemiology, clinical presentation, diagnosis, and treatment. The abstract provides high-quality clinical guidance but does not address iron biglycinate specifically, longevity/U-curve relationships, or low-dose/alternate-day protocols beyond a brief mention of ferrous sulfate 325 mg daily or on alternate days.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

- **Global prevalence:** Absolute iron deficiency affects ~2 billion people worldwide and 14% of US adults. IDA affects ~1.2 billion worldwide and 10 million in the US.
- **Women at high risk:** 38% of nonpregnant reproductive-age women in high-income countries have ID without anemia; 13% have IDA.
- **Pregnancy:** Up to 84% of pregnant women in the third trimester have ID in high-income countries.
- **Chronic disease prevalence:** ID comorbidity rates are high -- IBD (13%-90%), CKD (24%-85%), HF (37%-61%), cancer (18%-82%).
- **Symptom prevalence:** Restless legs syndrome in 32%-40% of ID patients; pica in 40%-50%.
- **Diagnostic thresholds:** Serum ferritin <30 ng/mL (without inflammation) or transferrin saturation <20%.
- **First-line treatment:** Ferrous sulfate 325 mg/day or on alternate days.
- **IV iron indications:** Oral iron intolerance, malabsorption (celiac, post-bariatric), chronic inflammatory conditions, ongoing blood loss, 2nd/3rd trimester pregnancy.
