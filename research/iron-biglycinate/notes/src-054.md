# src-054: Iron Depletion Without Anemia and Performance in Female Collegiate Rowers

**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22089308
**Type:** PubMed abstract only (no full text)
**Quality:** Abstract-level content; no methods, detailed results, or discussion available.

## Core Findings

Iron depletion without anemia (serum ferritin <20 microg/L) was present in 30% of nonanemic female collegiate rowers (n=44 of 147 nonanemic), and these iron-depleted rowers reported 2-km rowing performance times approximately 21 seconds slower than rowers with normal iron stores (p < .004). An additional 10% (n=16) were outright anemic (Hgb <12.0 g/dL). The study concluded that screening for low iron stores in female endurance athletes at the start of training programs may be clinically useful.

## Key Evidence

- **Sample:** 165 female collegiate rowers from 5 colleges/universities in central New York State; screened across three time points (August 2008, January 2009, September 2009).
- **Prevalence:** 10% anemic (Hgb <12.0 g/dL); 30% of nonanemic had serum ferritin <20 microg/L.
- **Performance gap:** Iron-depleted (nonanemic) rowers were ~21 s slower on self-reported 2-km simulated race times (p < .004).
- **Measures:** Hemoglobin, serum ferritin, soluble transferrin receptor; self-reported habitual physical activity and best 2-km time in previous 3 months.

## Methodology

- Cross-sectional screening study; no intervention.
- Self-reported performance outcome (not objectively measured), which is a notable limitation.
- Ferritin cutoff of <20 microg/L for iron depletion; hemoglobin <12.0 g/dL for anemia.

## Limitations

- **Abstract only** -- no access to full text; cannot verify detailed methods, statistical approach, or confounders.
- Performance was self-reported, introducing recall and social desirability bias.
- Cross-sectional design cannot establish causation between iron depletion and slower times.
- Specific to female collegiate rowers (young adults, endurance athletes); generalizability to broader populations is uncertain.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (health consequences of low-normal iron):** Moderate relevance. Provides evidence that iron depletion without anemia is associated with measurable performance decrements in athletes (~21 s over 2 km). Supports the idea that subclinical iron deficiency has functional consequences.
- **Q7 (clinical guidelines):** Weak-moderate relevance. Authors recommend screening female endurance athletes for low iron stores, but this is an opinion based on a cross-sectional study.
- Other questions (Q2, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6): Not directly addressed.

## Claims to Verify

- The ~21 s performance difference relies on self-reported 2-km times, not measured times. Full text would be needed to confirm whether any objective performance data were collected.
- Sample size of 165 with 5 institutions across 3 time points needs clarification on whether individuals were counted more than once (repeated measures vs. independent samples).
