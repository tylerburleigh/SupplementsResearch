# src-097: FORTE Trial - Ferritin-guided Iron Supplementation in Whole Blood Donors

## Core Findings

This large (n=830) double-blind RCT compared six ferrous bisglycinate supplementation protocols (placebo daily/alternate day, 30mg daily/alternate day, 60mg daily/alternate day) over 56 days in blood donors with ferritin <=30 ug/L. All active iron groups significantly reduced iron deficiency vs placebo (ORs 0.60-0.65). For low ferritin specifically, 60mg daily was markedly superior (OR 0.52), while 30mg alternate day was least effective (OR 0.82). No differences were found in GI side effects, symptoms, adherence, or donation intention across groups -- a result attributed to the favorable tolerability of ferrous bisglycinate compared to ferrous sulfate.

## Key Evidence

- **Sample**: 830 whole blood donors (464 women) with ferritin <=30 ug/L, randomized to 6 arms (n~138 each), 56-day supplementation period with follow-up at 122 and 180 days.
- **Iron deficiency (ferritin <15 ug/L) at 56 days**: All iron groups significantly superior to placebo. OR range 0.60 (60mg daily) to 0.65 (60mg alternate day). Placebo groups: ~49-51% still had ID; 60mg daily: 0.9%.
- **Low ferritin (15-30 ug/L)**: Strong dose-frequency gradient. 60mg daily OR 0.52 (0.47-0.57) -- clearly best; 30mg alternate day OR 0.82 (0.74-0.91) -- least effective among active treatments.
- **Low Hb**: All iron groups superior to placebo (ORs 0.74-0.80), no significant differences between active groups.
- **GI side effects**: No significant differences vs placebo overall. Only minor constipation increase in 60mg daily and placebo alternate day groups. Attributed to ferrous bisglycinate's chelated form reducing colonic iron exposure.
- **Symptoms (fatigue, cognition, RLS, general health)**: No improvement vs placebo in any group. Authors note possible underreporting due to positive donation feelings.
- **Adherence**: High across all groups (~70-80% of capsules taken). Only 60mg alternate day showed modestly higher adherence vs placebo (+6%).
- **Hepcidin mechanism**: Cited Galetti et al. showing absorption drops from 21.9% to 10.0% as ferritin rises from 1 to 30 ug/L, explaining why higher/more frequent dosing is needed for low ferritin correction.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Double-blind RCT with 6 arms (2x3 factorial: dosage x frequency), stratified by sex and age.
- **Iron form**: Ferrous bisglycinate (elemental iron 30mg or 60mg per capsule).
- **Population**: Dutch whole blood donors with ferritin <=30 ug/L (deferred from donation). Median baseline ferritin ~20 ug/L.
- **Duration**: 56-day supplementation; follow-up at 122 and 180 days.
- **Analysis**: GEE for binary outcomes, LMM for continuous outcomes, adjusted for baseline levels. Sensitivity analyses with 60mg daily as reference.
- **Limitations**: Only 830 of planned 1200 enrolled; limited ethnic diversity (9 non-Western European); no PICA data; ~1/3 lost to follow-up (balanced across groups); self-reported symptoms may lack sensitivity; population limited to blood donors with low ferritin (not general population).

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q3 (biglycinate vs other forms)**: Directly relevant. Shows ferrous bisglycinate at 30-60mg has minimal GI side effects compared to known ferrous sulfate literature. Authors explicitly attribute favorable tolerability to the bisglycinate chelate formulation.
- **Q4 (low-dose/alternate-day protocols)**: Central study. Compares 30mg vs 60mg, daily vs alternate day. Alternate-day dosing was consistently less effective for ferritin repletion. 30mg daily was intermediate. 60mg daily was optimal. This challenges the alternate-day hypothesis for ferritin correction, though the study population had already-depleted stores.
- **Q5 (risks of supplementing non-deficient)**: Not directly addressed -- population was iron-depleted donors (ferritin <=30). No adverse signals observed in this population.
- **Q6 (absorption optimization)**: Instructed avoidance of dairy, coffee, tea near supplementation. Hepcidin-mediated absorption decrease cited as mechanistic explanation for dose-response findings.
- **Q7 (clinical guidelines)**: Sanquin medical advisory board considering 60mg daily ferrous bisglycinate as standard policy for donors with ferritin <=30 ug/L.

## Source Quality

Preprint (medRxiv, January 2025), not yet peer-reviewed. Large, well-designed RCT with robust statistical methods. Relevant to iron supplementation in a depleted-but-not-anemic population. The use of ferrous bisglycinate (not sulfate) makes this particularly relevant to the biglycinate research focus.
