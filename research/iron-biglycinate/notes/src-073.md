# src-073: Iron supplementation with Lactobacillus plantarum 299v in female athletes

**PMID:** 32365981
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32365981
**Year:** 2020 (inferred from PMID)
**Study type:** Randomized Controlled Trial
**Venue:** MDPI / PubMed Central
**Content quality:** Abstract only (PubMed landing page; no full text available in source file)

## Core Findings

This RCT examined whether co-administering the probiotic Lactobacillus plantarum 299v (Lp299v) with 20 mg/day iron improves iron status, mood, and physical performance in healthy non-anemic female athletes with low iron stores (ferritin < 30 ug/L). The combination produced numerically greater ferritin increases at 4 weeks (13.6 vs 8.2 ug/L, p=0.056) and reticulocyte hemoglobin content at 12 weeks (1.5 vs 0.82 pg, p=0.083), but neither reached statistical significance. The only significant finding was increased vigor on the POMS mood questionnaire at 12 weeks (3.5 vs 0.1, p=0.015).

## Key Evidence

- **Ferritin change at 4 weeks:** Lp299v+iron = +13.6 ug/L, iron alone = +8.2 ug/L (p=0.056, NS)
- **Reticulocyte Hgb at 12 weeks:** Lp299v+iron = +1.5 pg, iron alone = +0.82 pg (p=0.083, NS)
- **Vigor (POMS) at 12 weeks:** Lp299v+iron = +3.5, iron alone = +0.1 (p=0.015, significant)
- **No conclusive effects on physical performance**
- **Iron dose:** 20 mg/day (low dose, consistent with low-dose supplementation protocols)
- **Population:** 53 healthy non-anemic female athletes with ferritin < 30 ug/L; 39 completed (26% dropout)

## Methodology

- **Design:** 12-week randomized controlled trial, two parallel arms
- **Intervention:** 20 mg iron alone vs. 20 mg iron + Lp299v probiotic
- **Population:** Healthy non-anemic female athletes with ferritin < 30 ug/L
- **Outcome measures:** Plasma ferritin (primary), reticulocyte hemoglobin content, POMS mood questionnaire, physical performance tests
- **Assessments:** Baseline, 4 weeks, 8 weeks, 12 weeks
- **Note:** Iron form (e.g., biglycinate vs. other) is not specified in the abstract

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size:** 39 completed out of 53 randomized -- underpowered for the observed effect sizes, which is likely why most comparisons were non-significant despite clinically meaningful numerical differences
2. **High dropout rate (26%):** Raises potential for attrition bias
3. **Conflict of interest:** Authors employed by Probi AB (probiotics company) and Nature's Bounty Co. (supplement manufacturer)
4. **Abstract-only source:** Full text not available; cannot verify methods details (iron form used, randomization procedure, blinding, adverse effects)
5. **Specific population:** Female athletes with low ferritin -- generalizability to broader populations or non-athletes is uncertain
6. **Iron form unspecified:** Cannot determine if the iron was biglycinate or another form

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (health consequences of low-normal iron):** Moderate relevance. Study population had low ferritin (<30 ug/L) without anemia. The vigor improvement suggests subclinical low iron may affect mood/energy even without anemia.
- **Q4 (low-dose/alternate-day protocols):** Moderate relevance. Used 20 mg/day (a low dose), which produced meaningful ferritin increases even alone (+8.2 ug/L at 4 weeks). Demonstrates low-dose iron can replete stores in mildly deficient individuals.
- **Q6 (absorption optimization):** Moderate relevance. Probiotic (Lp299v) co-administration is a novel absorption-enhancement strategy. Results trend positive but are inconclusive.
- **Q5 (risks of supplementing non-deficient):** Low relevance. All participants had ferritin < 30 ug/L (low stores), so this does not address risks in iron-replete individuals.
- **Q3 (biglycinate vs other forms):** Low relevance. Iron form not specified in abstract.
- **Q2 (iron-longevity U-curve):** Not addressed.
- **Q7 (clinical guidelines):** Low relevance. Not a guideline document.

## Claims to Verify

- **Iron form used:** The abstract does not specify what form of iron was administered (biglycinate, sulfate, etc.). This is critical for the research question and should be verified from full text.
- **Dropout reasons:** 14 of 53 dropped out (26%); the abstract does not state whether dropouts were due to GI side effects (relevant to Q5 about risks).
- **"Significantly increases intestinal iron absorption in meal studies"** (line 16-17): This prior claim about Lp299v is stated as background but should be verified against cited studies.
