# src-477: Ferrous Bisglycinate vs Electrolytic Iron as Fortificants in Bread (van Stuijvenberg et al., 2006)

## Core Findings

This RCT (n=160, aged 6-11, serum ferritin <20 mcg/L) found that ferrous bisglycinate fortified into brown bread significantly improved hemoglobin (P=0.013), serum iron (P=0.041), and transferrin saturation (P=0.042) in iron-deficient South African school children over 7.5 months, while electrolytic iron showed no significant treatment effects on any measure. However, neither form significantly improved serum ferritin (iron stores), likely because the dose (~3.66 mg Fe/day average) was sufficient to improve circulating iron but not to replenish stores.

## Key Evidence

- **Sample**: 160 iron-deficient children (serum ferritin <20 mcg/L), 153 completed; 72% had serum ferritin <15 mcg/L at baseline
- **Dose**: 2.52 mg Fe/day for 75 days, then 5.04 mg Fe/day for 62 days; average 3.66 mg/day on 137 school days over 7.5 months
- **Hb effect**: Bisglycinate group +3.1 g/L change from baseline; intervention effect 2.6 g/L (95% CI 0.6-4.6) vs control (P=0.013), four times the electrolytic Fe effect
- **Serum Fe effect**: Bisglycinate intervention effect 1.50 mmol/L (P=0.041); electrolytic Fe not significant
- **Transferrin saturation**: Bisglycinate intervention effect 2.48% (P=0.042); electrolytic Fe not significant
- **Serum ferritin**: No significant change in either group
- **Compliance**: 95-96% across all groups
- **Bioavailability**: Ferrous bisglycinate estimated at 2-4x ferrous sulfate, 4-8x electrolytic Fe

## Methodology

- **Design**: Single-blind RCT with three arms (control, electrolytic Fe, ferrous bisglycinate), stratified by school grade
- **Population**: 6-11 year old children in Northern Cape, South Africa, low socio-economic status; ~30% stunted or underweight; only 7.5% anemic, <1% vitamin A deficient
- **Vehicle**: Brown bread (88% extraction wheat flour), 4 slices (120 g) per school day; high phytate environment
- **Measurements**: Hb, serum ferritin, serum Fe, transferrin saturation, CRP (excluded if >=10 mg/L), serum retinol; at baseline and 7.5 months
- **Analysis**: ANCOVA adjusting for baseline and gender; intention-to-treat for Hb
- **Funding**: Partially supported by CELANEM and Albion Laboratories (manufacturer of Ferrochel)

## Limitations

- Study conducted in iron-deficient children, not adults -- generalizability to longevity/health optimization context is limited
- Single-blind (not double-blind); project leader aware of group allocation
- Dose was very low (~3.66 mg/day average) and delivered only on school days; may not reflect supplement protocols
- No sensory testing conducted on fortified bread
- Study partially funded by Albion Laboratories (maker of Ferrochel ferrous bisglycinate)
- Serum ferritin did not significantly improve, suggesting dose/duration may have been insufficient to replenish stores
- Population was iron-deficient but largely non-anemic and well-nourished for vitamin A

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q3 (biglycinate vs other forms)**: STRONG. Direct RCT comparison of ferrous bisglycinate vs electrolytic iron in a high-phytate food vehicle. Bisglycinate clearly superior for Hb, serum Fe, and transferrin saturation. Also reviews bioavailability literature showing 2-4x ferrous sulfate, 4-8x electrolytic Fe.
- **Q4 (low-dose protocols)**: MODERATE. Demonstrates efficacy at ~3.66 mg/day average dose in food fortification context, but this is intermittent (school days only) and in deficient children, not a supplement protocol for non-deficient adults.
- **Q6 (absorption optimization)**: MODERATE. Demonstrates bisglycinate's advantage in high-phytate settings where absorption inhibitors are present, supporting the chelate mechanism of protection from dietary inhibitors.

## Claims to Verify

- Bioavailability claim of "two to four times that of ferrous sulfate" is attributed to Olivares et al. 1997, Bovell-Benjamin et al. 2000, and Layrisse et al. 2000 -- these are secondary citations and should be verified against the primary sources.
- "Four to eight times better than electrolytic Fe" is an extrapolation, not directly measured; derived from comparing separate studies against a common reference (ferrous sulfate).
