# src-047: Iron-biofortified beans, brain activity, and cognition

**Source:** PMID 30926992 (PubMed abstract) - Luna et al., 2019, American Journal of Clinical Nutrition
**Study type:** Double-blind randomized controlled trial (NCT01594359)
**Quality:** Abstract-only (PubMed landing page); full text not available. Substantive abstract with detailed methods, results, and effect sizes.

## Core Findings

Iron-biofortified beans consumed daily for 18 weeks significantly improved iron status biomarkers, cognitive performance (attention speed, memory retrieval), and brain dynamics (EEG amplitude/spectral power) in 55 women aged 18-27 with low iron status (serum ferritin <20 ug/L). Mediation analysis supported the hypothesis that iron status changes improve cognition through changes in brain activity.

## Key Evidence

- **Sample:** 55 Rwandan women, 18-27 y, serum ferritin <20 ug/L
- **Intervention:** Iron-biofortified beans (86.1 ppm iron) vs comparison beans (50.1 ppm iron), consumed daily for 18 weeks
- **Iron status improvements:** Hemoglobin (eta2=0.16), ferritin (eta2=0.17), body iron (eta2=0.10), all P<0.05
- **Cognitive improvements:** Attentional/mnemonic response speed (eta2=0.04-0.29), memory sensitivity/efficiency (eta2=0.12-0.55)
- **EEG improvements:** Amplitude and spectral power (eta2=0.08-0.49)
- **Mediation:** Changes in brain activity mediated the relationship between iron status changes and cognitive changes

## Methodology

- Double-blind, randomized controlled trial
- Assessed at baseline and endline (18 weeks)
- Iron biomarkers: hemoglobin, ferritin, transferrin receptor, body iron
- Cognition: 5 computerized tasks
- Brain dynamics: concurrent EEG
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Limitations

- Full text not available; analysis based on abstract only
- Study used dietary iron biofortification (beans), not iron supplements (e.g., biglycinate)
- Population was iron-depleted women (ferritin <20), not iron-replete or mildly deficient
- 18-week duration may not capture longer-term effects
- No direct comparison to oral iron supplements

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (health consequences of low-normal iron):** Strong evidence. Demonstrates that low iron status (ferritin <20) impairs cognitive performance and brain function. Mediation analysis supports causal pathway.
- **Q2 (iron-longevity U-curve):** Tangential. No longevity data; focuses on cognitive/brain outcomes.
- **Q3 (biglycinate vs other forms):** Not directly relevant. Uses biofortified food, not supplements.
- **Q4 (low-dose/alternate-day protocols):** Not relevant. Daily consumption of biofortified beans.
- **Q5 (risks of supplementing non-deficient):** Not addressed. All participants were iron-depleted.
- **Q6 (absorption optimization):** Not addressed.
- **Q7 (clinical guidelines):** Indirectly relevant. Supports screening and intervention for low ferritin (<20) in young women.

## Claims to Verify

- Sample size of 55: Stated in abstract methods, but unclear if this is completers or enrolled. Flow diagram mentioned but not visible.
- Effect sizes (eta2 values): Large ranges (e.g., 0.12-0.55 for memory) should be verified in full text for specific task-level breakdowns.
