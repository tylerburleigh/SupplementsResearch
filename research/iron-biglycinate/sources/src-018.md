Iron forms haemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that binds to oxygen and carries it to tissues and organs throughout the body. This process is vital for energy production and the number one reason people supplement iron.

Iron helps your body release energy from food and combat tiredness, so you feel more awake, focused and ready for whatever the day brings.

Menstruation & Heavy Periods

Memory & Headaches

Iron is involved in making neurotransmitters such as dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine. They maintain a positive mood and influence memory and concentration. This is why 80% of people with low iron levels get regular headaches as a symptom.

Immunity

Iron is essential for a well-functioning immune system. It plays a role in the production and maturation of white blood cells, which are crucial for the body's defence against infections and diseases.

Detox & Itching Relief

Iron helps detox drugs and toxins in the liver, through its role in various enzymatic reactions. This may be why iron deficiency can cause persistent and intolerable itching in some people - it's a particularly common symptom of iron deficiency during perimenopause.

FAQs About Iron Bisglycinate Supplements

What are the benefits of taking iron Bisglycinate supplements?

Iron is essential for energy, vitality and concentration. It supports the formation of red blood cells and haemoglobin, which carry oxygen around the body, helping to reduce tiredness and fatigue. Iron also contributes to normal immune function and supports brain performance, making it one of the most important minerals for everyday health.

What are the symptoms of iron deficiency?

Low iron levels can cause persistent tiredness, pale skin, shortness of breath, dizziness, cold hands and feet, brittle nails or hair loss. Some people may also notice poor concentration or frequent infections. Women of childbearing age, vegetarians and those with heavy periods are particularly prone to low iron.

What is a safe daily dose of iron?

For most adults, the recommended daily intake is around 14 mg of elemental iron. However, therapeutic doses for diagnosed deficiency may be higher and should only be taken under medical supervision. It’s best not to exceed the dose on your supplement label unless advised by your doctor, as excess iron can build up in the body.

Why do some iron supplements upset the stomach?

Iron can irritate the stomach when taken without food, and tends to irritate the colon for the first few weeks until it adapts. This is partly caused because it makes changes to the gut flora, feeding various bacteria, so it’s helpful to take a probiotic at the same time as iron supplements in order to help counteract this effect. Forms like ferrous sulphate are more likely to cause nausea, constipation or stomach cramps. Gentle, bioavailable forms such as iron bisglycinate or iron citrate are much easier to absorb and kinder on the stomach. Taking iron with food can also help.

Are there any cautions to know about when taking iron supplements?

Yes. Iron should be taken only as directed, as excessive intake can be harmful. Keep out of reach of children — iron overdose can be dangerous for young children. If you suspect your child may have taken iron supplements, take them to hospital immediately.

When is the best time to take iron?

Iron is best absorbed on an empty stomach, but if that causes discomfort, take it with a meal. Avoid taking it at the same time as tea, coffee, calcium or high-fibre foods, as these can reduce absorption. Pairing it with Vitamin C — such as a glass of orange juice — can help your body absorb more iron effectively.

What is the best type of iron supplement?

The best iron supplements provide high absorption with minimal digestive side effects. Iron bisglycinate is widely regarded as one of the most gentle and effective forms, offering excellent uptake without constipation or nausea. Look for supplements made in GMP or BRC-accredited UK or EU facilities, and ideally are fully chelated to leave absolutely no elemental iron in the supplement.
