# src-025: Haghighatdoost & Hariri (2019) - Can resveratrol supplement change inflammatory mediators?

**Citation:** Haghighatdoost F, Hariri M. *Can resveratrol supplement change inflammatory mediators? A systematic review and meta-analysis on randomized clinical trials.* European Journal of Clinical Nutrition 73, 345-355 (2019). DOI: 10.1038/s41430-018-0253-4

**Content quality note:** Metadata flagged as "degraded" due to paywall. However, the abstract and key results are fully available. The full methods and discussion sections are behind a paywall. The reference list provides useful context about included studies.

## Core Findings

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 15 RCTs (658 adults aged 18-75) found that resveratrol supplementation significantly reduced serum CRP levels (WMD = -0.54; 95% CI: -0.78, -0.30; I2 = 77.7%) but had no significant overall effect on IL-6 (WMD = -0.06; 95% CI: -0.27, 0.14) or TNF-alpha (WMD = -0.20; 95% CI: -0.55, 0.16). Subgroup analyses showed resveratrol reduced TNF-alpha in young subjects (WMD = -0.34; 95% CI: -0.57, -0.12) and obese individuals (WMD = -1.52; 95% CI: -2.87, -0.16).

## Key Evidence/Data Points

- **15 RCTs, 658 participants** aged 18-75 years included
- **CRP reduction:** WMD = -0.54 mg/L (95% CI: -0.78 to -0.30), highly significant (P < 0.0001), but high heterogeneity (I2 = 77.7%)
- **IL-6:** No significant effect (WMD = -0.06; 95% CI: -0.27 to 0.14; I2 = 62.0%)
- **TNF-alpha:** No significant overall effect (WMD = -0.20; 95% CI: -0.55 to 0.16; I2 = 87.2%), but significant in young subjects and obese individuals
- **PROSPERO registered:** CRD42018085098
- Search conducted up to October 2017 across ISI Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, EMBASE, Google Scholar
- Heterogeneity threshold: fixed-effect model when I2 < 25%; random-effects model otherwise

## Methodology

- Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs
- PROSPERO registered (CRD42018085098)
- Databases: ISI Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, EMBASE, Google Scholar
- Search through October 2017
- Fixed-effect model for I2 < 25%; random-effects model for I2 >= 25%
- WMD estimated by subtracting baseline from post-intervention or as post-intervention values
- Included 15 trials with 658 adult participants aged 18-75

## Limitations

- Full text behind paywall -- only abstract, results summary, and references available
- High heterogeneity across studies (I2 ranging from 62% to 87.2%)
- Some included RCTs showed null results (Bo et al. 2016, Chachay et al. 2014, Kjaer et al. 2017, Yoshino et al. 2012)
- Subgroup analyses for TNF-alpha in young/obese subgroups had small sample sizes and high heterogeneity
- Authors call for more studies stratified by sex, obesity, and age

## Claims to Verify

- **658 participants across 15 trials:** This is stated in the abstract but cannot be cross-checked against the methods section due to paywall. The number is plausible for a meta-analysis of this scope.
- **Subgroup TNF-alpha reduction in obese (WMD = -1.52):** This is a very large effect size compared to the overall null finding. Worth verifying whether this is driven by one or two small studies.
