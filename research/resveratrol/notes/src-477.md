# src-477: No effect of resveratrol supplementation after 6 months on insulin sensitivity in overweight adults

**Citation:** de Ligt M, Bergman M, Mancilla Fuentes R, et al. Am J Clin Nutr. 2020;112(4):1029-1038. doi:10.1093/ajcn/nqaa125. PMCID: PMC7528554.

**Quality:** Full paper text available from PMC. Peer-reviewed, high-quality journal (Am J Clin Nutr). Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT02565979).

## Core Findings

In a 6-month RCT of 150 mg/day trans-resveratrol (resVida) in overweight adults (n=41 completers), resveratrol had **no effect on insulin sensitivity** (primary outcome, Matsuda index) or on intrahepatic lipid content, body composition, resting energy metabolism, blood pressure, physical performance, quality of life, or sleep. The only significant finding was a modestly lower HbA1c in the resveratrol arm vs placebo (35.8 vs 37.6 mmol/mol, P=0.007), though the authors caution this may reflect altered hemoglobin glycation or erythrocyte life span rather than true glycemic improvement.

## Key Evidence/Data Points

- **Participants:** 41 overweight men and postmenopausal women (BMI 27-35, age 40-70 y; mean age 62, mean BMI ~29-30). 24 men, 17 women. Resveratrol arm n=20, placebo arm n=21.
- **Dose:** 150 mg/day trans-resveratrol (resVida, 99.9% purity, provided by DSM Nutritional Products), split as 2x75 mg capsules with lunch and dinner.
- **Primary outcome -- Matsuda index:** Adjusted mean 5.18 +/- 0.35 (resveratrol) vs 5.50 +/- 0.34 (placebo), P=0.549. No difference.
- **HbA1c:** Significantly lower in resveratrol arm (35.8 +/- 0.43 vs 37.6 +/- 0.44 mmol/mol, P=0.007). Fasting glucose and insulin were unchanged.
- **Secondary outcomes -- all null:** Glucose/insulin AUC and iAUC during OGTT, IHL content (by 1H-MRS), body composition (DXA), blood pressure, resting energy expenditure, fat/carbohydrate oxidation, lipid profile (total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, triglycerides, free fatty acids), 6-min walk test, timed chair-stand test, muscle strength (isometric), muscle endurance (isokinetic extension), quality of sleep (PSQI), quality of life.
- **One marginal secondary finding:** Muscle endurance for flexion movement was better preserved in resveratrol arm (0.58 vs 0.88 Nm/contraction decline slope, P=0.044). Small effect; no multiple-testing correction applied.
- **Safety:** No serious adverse events. Liver/kidney safety markers (bilirubin, creatinine, urea, AST, ALT) all unchanged between arms. 150 mg/day for 6 months deemed safe.
- **Compliance:** Confirmed by capsule counting and monthly plasma resveratrol/DHR levels. Resveratrol always below detection in placebo arm.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Parallel-group, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial. 1:1 randomization stratified by sex and age.
- **Duration:** 6 months (recruitment April 2016 to August 2018). 2-week run-in period to eliminate resveratrol-containing foods.
- **Insulin sensitivity assessment:** Matsuda index calculated from duplicate 2-hour 75-g OGTTs (performed twice pre- and twice post-intervention to reduce intraindividual variability). This is a notable methodological strength vs single OGTT, though still inferior to hyperinsulinemic euglycemic clamp.
- **Statistical analysis:** ANCOVA adjusting for pre-intervention values. No multiple-testing correction (acknowledged as limitation).
- **Sample size:** Powered at 80% to detect a 0.3 difference in Matsuda index with significance level 0.05, accounting for 15% dropout. 42 recruited, 41 completed (low dropout).
- **Funding:** Diabetes Fonds and Dutch Heart Foundation. DSM Nutritional Products provided capsules but apparently no other involvement.

## Limitations

- Small sample size (n=41), which may explain null findings for some secondary outcomes (e.g., IHL content, which was borderline P=0.122).
- OGTT-based Matsuda index rather than gold-standard clamp for insulin sensitivity.
- No multiple-testing correction; the HbA1c finding and flexion endurance finding could be false positives.
- Only one dose tested (150 mg/day). Authors note studies finding glycemic effects typically used 480-2000 mg/d.
- Mixed population (both sexes, some with impaired glucose tolerance) may have diluted effects.
- Not powered for sex differences.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (clinical trials):** Directly relevant. High-quality 6-month RCT showing null result for most outcomes. Adds to evidence that resveratrol at moderate dose (150 mg/d) does not improve metabolic health in overweight adults.
- **Q2 (bioavailability):** Plasma resveratrol and DHR were measured monthly, confirming absorption and compliance. Mean parental resveratrol ~365 ng/mL and DHR ~552 ng/mL at end of intervention.
- **Q4 (mechanism debates):** Authors discuss SIRT1 pathway and mitochondrial function. Resveratrol previously improved ex vivo mitochondrial function at this dose in shorter studies, but did not translate to whole-body metabolic improvements here.
- **Q5 (challenging evidence):** Strongly challenging. A well-designed 6-month trial with predominantly null results, despite prior shorter studies showing mitochondrial benefits at the same dose. The HbA1c effect is ambiguous.
- **Q6 (dosing/safety):** Confirms safety of 150 mg/d for 6 months. No adverse effects on liver/kidney markers. Suggests higher doses (480-2000 mg/d) may be needed for metabolic effects.

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample size n=41 completers:** Verified from Table 1 (n=20 resveratrol, n=21 placebo). Original recruitment target was 21 per arm = 42 total, with 15% dropout expected. Actual dropout was only 1 participant.
- **HbA1c P=0.007:** Reported in both Results and Table 2. No multiple-testing correction, which is a concern given the number of outcomes tested.
- **Dose of 150 mg/d as consistently improving mitochondrial function in prior work:** Referenced to the same research group's earlier studies (refs 8, 14, 23). This is self-citation; the claim should be evaluated against independent replication.
