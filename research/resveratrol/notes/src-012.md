# src-012: Randomized controlled trial of resveratrol in obese men

**Source:** PubMed PMID 23193181 (url: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23193181)
**Trial registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01150955
**Content quality:** Abstract + figure legends only (PubMed landing page). No full text available. Quality is moderate -- abstract provides key outcomes but methods details (exact dose) are missing from the scraped content.

## Core Findings

In a randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blinded, parallel-group trial, 4 weeks of high-dose resveratrol supplementation in 24 obese but otherwise healthy men produced **no significant metabolic benefits**. The study explicitly contradicts the persuasive positive data from rodent models and casts doubt on resveratrol's justification as a human nutritional supplement for metabolic disorders.

## Key Evidence

- **Primary outcome (insulin sensitivity):** Assessed via hyperinsulinemic euglycemic clamp. Insulin sensitivity deteriorated insignificantly in both resveratrol and placebo groups. No treatment effect detected.
- **Secondary outcomes -- all null:**
  - Endogenous glucose production: unchanged
  - Glucose turnover and oxidation rates: unchanged
  - Blood pressure: no effect (trend toward increase in resveratrol group, systolic P=0.39, diastolic P=0.36)
  - Resting energy expenditure: unchanged
  - Lipid oxidation rates: unchanged
  - Ectopic fat (hepatic and intramyocellular lipid via 1H-MR spectroscopy): unchanged
  - Visceral fat (MRI): unchanged
  - Inflammatory and metabolic biomarkers: unchanged
- **Molecular markers (muscle and adipose biopsies):**
  - AMPK phosphorylation in muscle: no effect
  - Total lysine acetylation in muscle: no effect
  - PGC1alpha mRNA expression in muscle: no effect
  - GLUT4 mRNA expression in muscle: **significantly decreased** in resveratrol group during clamp (P < 0.05) -- a negative finding
  - TNF-alpha and NF-kB mRNA in adipose tissue: no effect

## Methodology

- **Design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blinded, parallel-group
- **Sample:** 24 obese but otherwise healthy men (12 per group)
- **Duration:** 4 weeks
- **Dose:** Described as "high-dose" but exact dose not stated in abstract/available content
- **Primary measure:** Hyperinsulinemic euglycemic clamp (gold standard for insulin sensitivity)
- **Analysis:** Two-way repeated-measures ANOVA
- **Additional assessments:** DXA body composition, MRI visceral fat, 1H-MR spectroscopy for ectopic lipid, ambulatory blood pressure, muscle/adipose biopsies with Western blot and RT-PCR

## Limitations

- Small sample size (n=12 per group) limits statistical power
- Short duration (4 weeks) may be insufficient to detect metabolic changes
- Only obese men studied; generalizability limited
- Exact resveratrol dose not recoverable from scraped content
- Only full abstract available, not complete paper text

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (clinical trials):** Directly relevant. A negative/null-result RCT of resveratrol in humans with metabolic endpoints.
- **Q4 (mechanism debates):** Relevant. Tested SIRT1-pathway markers (AMPK, PGC1alpha, acetylation) and found no activation, contradicting the proposed mechanism from animal studies.
- **Q5 (challenging evidence):** Highly relevant. This is a key negative study. The authors explicitly state the results "raise doubt about the justification of resveratrol as a human nutritional supplement in metabolic disorders" and highlight the translational gap between rodent and human data.
- **Q6 (dosing/safety):** Partially relevant. Described as "high-dose" but exact dose unknown from available content. No adverse events reported, but a concerning trend toward increased blood pressure and a significant decrease in GLUT4 expression.
