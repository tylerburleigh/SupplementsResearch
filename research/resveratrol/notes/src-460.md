# src-460: Resveratrol improves health and survival of mice on a high-calorie diet

**Authors:** Joseph A. Baur, Kevin J. Pearson, Nathan L. Price, ... Rafael de Cabo, David A. Sinclair
**Venue:** Nature, Vol 444, 16 November 2006
**DOI:** 10.1038/nature05354
**Quality note:** Metadata flagged as "degraded" (paywall detection), but full paper text is present and readable. This is the original landmark Nature paper.

## Core Findings

Resveratrol (at ~22.4 mg/kg/day) shifted the physiology of middle-aged male C57BL/6NIA mice on a high-calorie (60% fat) diet toward that of standard-diet mice, reducing mortality risk by 31% (hazard ratio = 0.69, P = 0.020) and improving insulin sensitivity, organ pathology, mitochondrial number, and motor function -- without significantly reducing body weight. Gene expression analysis showed resveratrol opposed the effects of the high-calorie diet in 144 out of 153 significantly altered pathways.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **Survival:** At 114 weeks, 58% of HC controls had died (median lifespan 108 weeks) vs. 42% of HCR and 42% of SD. Cox proportional hazards: HCR hazard ratio = 0.69 vs HC (P = 0.020); HCR vs SD hazard ratio = 1.03 (P = 0.88, not significant).
- **Insulin sensitivity:** HOMA scores: SD = 2.5, HC = 8.8, HCR = 3.5 (HCR vs HC, P = 0.01). Oral glucose tolerance tests showed significantly reduced AUC for both glucose and insulin in HCR vs HC.
- **Liver pathology:** Blinded pathology scores (0-4 scale): SD = 1.3, HC = 2.8, HCR = 0.8. Resveratrol prevented fatty liver development.
- **Heart pathology:** Blinded scores: SD = 1.6, HC = 3.2, HCR = 1.2.
- **Mitochondria:** HCR mice had considerably more hepatic mitochondria than HC controls, comparable to SD. PGC-1alpha acetylation was 3-fold lower in resveratrol-fed mice (short-term cohort at 186 mg/kg/day), indicating enhanced SIRT1 activity.
- **AMPK activation:** Resveratrol showed strong tendency toward AMPK phosphorylation in liver, with downstream indicators (ACC phosphorylation, decreased fatty acid synthase).
- **Motor function:** Rotarod performance improved steadily with age in HCR group, becoming indistinguishable from SD.
- **Gene expression:** 782 of 41,534 genes significantly changed. PAGE analysis: resveratrol opposed HC diet effects in 144/153 pathways. Downregulated pathways included insulin/IGF-1 signaling, mTOR signaling, oxidative phosphorylation, glycolysis.
- **Plasma markers:** Resveratrol significantly reduced insulin, glucose, IGF-1, and amylase levels compared to HC controls (Table 1).

## Methodology

- **Animals:** One-year-old male C57BL/6NIA mice, assigned to standard diet (SD), high-calorie diet (HC, 60% fat), or HC + resveratrol (HCR) at 0.04% in diet providing ~22.4 +/- 0.4 mg/kg/day.
- **Design:** Lifelong feeding study starting from middle age. Primary analyses at 18-24 months.
- **Sample sizes:** Varying by assay; rotarod n = 15 (SD), 6 (HC), 9 (HCR); PAGE analysis n = 5 (SD), 5 (HC), 4 (HCR, one discarded due to RNA degradation); AMPK/PGC-1alpha n = 5 or 3 per group.
- **Microarray:** Agilent 44k whole-genome arrays on liver RNA. Z-normalization, PAGE, principal component analysis.
- **Statistics:** ANOVA with Fisher's post-hoc tests. Cox proportional hazards for survival.
- **Note:** The lower dose group (5.2 mg/kg/day) was not reported in this paper; authors stated those results would be presented later.

## Limitations

1. **Male-only, single strain:** Only male C57BL/6NIA mice studied; sex-specific and strain-specific effects unknown.
2. **Incomplete survival data:** Colony age was 114 weeks at publication; ultimate mean lifespan extension could not be confidently determined. Survival curves still separated but final endpoint not reached.
3. **No body weight reduction:** Resveratrol did not significantly reduce weight on the high-calorie diet, which may limit translational relevance for obesity treatment per se.
4. **PGC-1alpha acetylation measured in separate cohort:** Used a short-term (6-week) high-dose (186 mg/kg/day) cohort rather than the main study cohort, due to tissue requirements.
5. **Mechanism not fully resolved:** Authors note it remains unclear whether effects are primarily through SIRT1 or a combination of interactions (xenohormesis hypothesis). Direct vs. indirect Sir2 activation debated.
6. **Competing financial interests declared:** Authors affiliated with Sirtris Pharmaceuticals.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (clinical trials):** Preclinical; landmark animal study that motivated subsequent human trials. Not a clinical trial itself.
- **Q2 (bioavailability):** Reports doses in mg/kg/day (22.4 mg/kg/day as main dose, noted as "feasible daily doses for humans"). Does not directly address pharmacokinetics or bioavailability.
- **Q3 (longevity vs disease):** Directly relevant. Shows survival benefit (31% reduced death risk) on high-calorie diet but effect brought survival to par with standard diet, not beyond it. Benefits were health-span-related (insulin sensitivity, organ pathology, motor function) rather than extension beyond normal lifespan.
- **Q4 (mechanism debates):** Highly relevant. implicates SIRT1/PGC-1alpha pathway, AMPK activation, mitochondrial biogenesis, and opposes HC diet in 144/153 gene pathways. Notes debate over direct vs. indirect SIRT1 activation.
- **Q5 (challenging evidence):** Relevant as a foundational positive study. Key caveat: resveratrol rescued HC-diet pathology but did not extend lifespan beyond normal diet controls.
- **Q6 (dosing/safety):** Reports two doses: 5.2 and 22.4 mg/kg/day. Describes doses as "feasible daily doses for humans." No adverse effects reported; no significant changes in body temperature, plasma lipids, or markers of hepatic damage (AST, ALT).

## Claims to Verify

- **"22.4 +/- 0.4 mg/kg/day" dose:** Calculated from 0.04% resveratrol in diet. Verify this conversion is reasonable for mouse food intake.
- **"144 out of 153 pathways" opposed:** Reported from PAGE analysis with n = 4-5 per group. Statistical power for pathway-level analysis with these sample sizes could be questioned.
- **Hazard ratio 0.69 (P = 0.020):** Survival data was interim (colony at 114 weeks). The P-value and hazard ratio may change with complete survival data.
- **Threefold reduction in PGC-1alpha acetylation:** Measured in a separate cohort at much higher dose (186 mg/kg/day), not the main study dose. Cannot directly attribute the main study's effects to this mechanism at the lower dose.
