# src-475: Effects and Mechanisms of Resveratrol on Aging and Age-Related Diseases

**Citation:** Zhou DD et al. Oxid Med Cell Longev. 2021 Jul 11;2021:9932218. doi: 10.1155/2021/9932218. PMCID: PMC8289612, PMID: 34336123.

**Type:** Comprehensive narrative review (open-access, peer-reviewed)

**Quality:** ok -- full text available from PMC, substantive content throughout.

## Core Findings

This review synthesizes preclinical and limited clinical evidence that resveratrol extends lifespan in multiple animal models (yeast, nematodes, fruit flies, mice, fish) and protects against age-related diseases (neurodegenerative, cardiovascular, sarcopenia, cancers, infertility, osteoporosis) primarily through four mechanisms: suppression of oxidative stress, inhibition of inflammation, improvement of mitochondrial function, and regulation of apoptosis. The review also notes that clinical evidence remains sparse, and one clinical trial found resveratrol abolished exercise-induced cardiovascular benefits in aged men.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Lifespan Extension (Q3: Longevity vs Disease)
- Meta-analysis of 19 papers across 6 species found resveratrol acts as a life-extending agent (Hector et al. 2012, Biology Letters)
- Resveratrol prolonged C. elegans lifespan via Sirt1-dependent autophagy (Morselli et al. 2010, Cell Death & Disease)
- Transgenic resveratrol rice DJ526 prolonged Drosophila median longevity by up to 50% (Khan et al. 2019)
- HtrA2 knockout mice: oral resveratrol increased median survival by 10 days (32 to 42 days) at 25 mg/kg BW (Gerhardt et al. 2011)
- **Inconsistent evidence:** Red wine and equivalent oral pharmacological doses of resveratrol delayed vascular aging but had no effect on lifespan in rats (da Luz et al. 2012)

### Mechanisms (Q4: Mechanism Debates)
- **Sirt1 activation** is the most consistently cited pathway, appearing in lifespan extension, neuroprotection, cardioprotection, sarcopenia, and apoptosis regulation
- **Oxidative stress suppression:** Resveratrol activates Nrf2, promotes antioxidant enzymes (SOD, CAT, GSH, GPx), reduces ROS; in erythrocytes, dose-dependent activation of plasma membrane redox system (0.1-100 uM)
- **Anti-inflammation:** Reduces IL-1beta, IL-8, TNF-alpha, MCP-1; resveratrol reduced age-associated inflammation independently of PGC-1alpha (Olesen et al. 2013), unlike exercise which was PGC-1alpha-dependent
- **Mitochondrial function:** Increases mtDNA copy number, ATP content, promotes mitochondrial biogenesis; downregulates Akt/mTOR pathway (zebrafish retina study)
- **Apoptosis regulation:** Upregulates Sirt1, promotes FoxO1, inhibits p53; modulates Bax/Bcl2 balance
- **Gut microbiota:** Identified as a novel but under-explored mechanism; resveratrol modulated gut microbiota in obesity/diabetes models but anti-aging effects via gut microbiota have little evidence

### Age-Related Diseases
- **Neurodegenerative:** Improved memory, learning, mood in multiple rodent studies; enhanced hippocampal neurogenesis; Sirt1-dependent and Akt/PKC pathway involvement; doses ranged from 5 ug/uL (intraventricular) to 200 mg/kg oral
- **Cardiovascular:** Enhanced NO bioavailability, reduced superoxide, improved endothelial function; lowered aorta media thickness, inflammation, fibrosis (40 mg/kg for 6 months in aged mice); cardioprotection via Sirt1-USP7 axis
- **Sarcopenia:** Resveratrol combined with exercise showed superior effects vs either alone on muscle strength and endurance; AMPK/Sirt1 pathway activation
- **Cancer:** In vitro evidence across ovarian, gastric, prostate, colon cancer cell lines; inhibited proliferation (AMPK/mTOR), induced apoptosis (caspase 3), suppressed metastasis (NF-kappaB, Akt/GSK-3beta/Snail)
- **Other:** Improved oocyte quality and ovarian reserve (12-month study in mice); increased bone volume and trabecular number

### Clinical Trials (Q1: Clinical Trials)
- **Witte et al. 2014 (J Neuroscience):** 200 mg/d resveratrol for 26 weeks in 23 healthy overweight older adults enhanced memory performance with improved glucose metabolism and hippocampal functional connectivity
- **Topical resveratrol cream:** Visible improvement of clinical conditions with significant decrease of aging signs in human volunteers
- **Gliemann et al. 2013 (J Physiology):** Resveratrol combined with exercise **abolished** the positive effects of exercise on cardiovascular health in aged men; negative effects partly related to resveratrol's antioxidant properties blunting exercise-induced adaptations

### Safety/Dosing (Q6: Dosing and Safety)
- Animal doses ranged widely: 0.1 mg/kg to 4 g/kg depending on study
- **Nephrotoxicity concern:** Chronic resveratrol treatment (12 months) caused significant increase in oxidative damage markers in kidneys of F2 hybrid mice (Wong et al. 2009); authors emphasize need for attention to safe dosage and duration
- In vitro cancer studies used concentrations of 25-800 uM (potentially supra-physiological)

### Challenging Evidence (Q5)
- Resveratrol did not extend lifespan in rats despite delaying vascular aging (da Luz et al. 2012)
- Resveratrol abolished exercise-induced cardiovascular benefits in aged men (Gliemann et al. 2013)
- Resveratrol's anti-inflammatory effects operated independently of PGC-1alpha, unlike exercise (Olesen et al. 2013)
- Cardiac tissue antioxidant effects may not result from CAT/SOD activation (Aguilar-Alonso et al. 2018)

## Methodological Notes

- This is a **narrative review**, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. No formal search strategy, quality assessment, or inclusion/exclusion criteria reported.
- The lifespan extension meta-analysis cited (Hector et al. 2012) is a separate publication -- this review does not perform its own meta-analysis.
- Most evidence is from **animal models** (mice, rats, Drosophila, C. elegans, zebrafish, honey bees, lemurs); only three clinical trials are discussed.
- In vitro cancer studies used concentrations (25-800 uM) that may far exceed achievable plasma levels in humans.
- No discussion of bioavailability limitations or pharmacokinetics of resveratrol.
- The review does not address the Sirt1 direct-activation controversy (whether resveratrol directly activates Sirt1 or acts through upstream mechanisms like PDE inhibition).

## Limitations

- Narrative review design limits reproducibility and may introduce selection bias.
- Clinical evidence is extremely limited (only 3 trials briefly mentioned).
- No pharmacokinetic/bioavailability discussion -- a major gap given resveratrol's known poor oral bioavailability.
- Animal dose ranges are not translated to human equivalent doses.
- Does not address the debate about whether resveratrol directly activates Sirt1 (the PDE4 inhibition mechanism proposed by Park et al. 2012 is cited in the references but not discussed in context of mechanism).

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Clinical trials):** Provides details on 3 clinical trials including the key finding that resveratrol improved memory in older adults (Witte et al. 2014) and blunted exercise benefits (Gliemann et al. 2013). Limited clinical coverage.
- **Q3 (Longevity vs disease):** Strong coverage of lifespan extension across multiple species and distinction between lifespan effects and disease-specific effects.
- **Q4 (Mechanism debates):** Good coverage of four primary mechanisms (oxidative stress, inflammation, mitochondrial function, apoptosis) and the central role of Sirt1. Notes gut microbiota as under-explored.
- **Q5 (Challenging evidence):** Reports inconsistent lifespan findings in rats and the exercise-blunting effect, but does not deeply engage with mechanism controversies.
- **Q6 (Dosing/safety):** Notes nephrotoxicity concern and wide dose ranges but lacks systematic safety analysis or human equivalent dose calculations.
