# src-463: The therapeutic potential of resveratrol: a review of clinical trials

**Citation:** Berman AY, Motechin RA, Wiesenfeld MY, Holz MK. NPJ Precision Oncology. 2017;1:35. doi:10.1038/s41698-017-0038-6. PMCID: PMC5630227.

**Type:** Review article (peer-reviewed, open access)

**Quality:** ok -- full text available from PMC, substantive content with detailed trial summaries and Table 1.

## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive 2017 review of clinical trials investigating resveratrol across six disease areas: cancer, neurological disorders, cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, NAFLD, and obesity. The authors found that resveratrol was well tolerated and beneficially influenced disease biomarkers in neurological disorders, cardiovascular diseases, and diabetes. However, resveratrol had ambiguous or detrimental effects in certain cancers (notably multiple myeloma, where SRT501 caused renal failure) and in NAFLD. Poor bioavailability was identified as the major obstacle across all disease areas.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Cancer (Q1, Q5)
- Only 15 clinical trial results on PubMed for "resveratrol and cancer" as of March 2017 (vs. 2600+ total publications)
- **Prostate cancer:** MPX (muscadine grape skin) lengthened PSA doubling time by 5.3 months, but results not statistically significant. A second trial found no effect on prostate volume or PSA levels (n=66, 4 months, 150 or 1000 mg/day)
- **Colorectal cancer:** SRT501 (5 g) increased cleaved caspase-3 (apoptosis marker) in 9 patients; 500-1000 mg reduced Ki-67 staining in 20 patients. Tissue concentrations matched preclinical effective levels
- **Multiple myeloma:** SRT501 (5 g) caused severe adverse events including renal failure in phase 2 trial (n=24). Authors conclude SRT501 and likely any resveratrol version cannot be used for multiple myeloma
- **Breast cancer:** Resveratrol safe and well tolerated (n=39, 5 or 50 mg twice daily, 12 weeks); decreased RASSF-1a methylation correlating with circulating resveratrol levels -- suggests chemopreventive epigenetic mechanism
- Resveratrol combined with rapamycin showed cytotoxicity in bladder and breast cancer cell lines and TSC2-deficient mouse xenograft tumors (from authors' own lab)

### Neurological Disorders (Q1)
- **Alzheimer's disease:** Two phase 2 trials (119 patients each, 12 months, escalating dose from 500 mg/day to 1000 mg twice daily) showed reduced CSF MMP-9, increased IL-4, attenuated decline in A-beta 42 and A-beta 40. Significant resveratrol and metabolites found in CSF, confirming BBB penetration
- **Ischemic stroke:** 312 patients, resveratrol given with r-tPA; reduced MMP-9 and MMP-2, extended therapeutic window of r-tPA. Dose: 2.5 mg/kg body weight

### Cardiovascular Diseases (Q1)
- **Coronary artery disease:** 40 patients, 10 mg/day for 3 months -- improved left ventricular function, improved FMD, lowered LDL
- **Atherosclerosis:** 44 healthy subjects, 400 mg trans-resveratrol + grape skin extract + quercetin for 1 month -- decreased ICAM, VCAM, IL-8 expression
- **Hypertension:** 18 patients, combination supplement including 60 mg resveratrol -- reduced diastolic pressure
- **Inflammation in smokers:** 50 smokers, 500 mg/day for 3 months -- reduced systemic airway inflammation, decreased CRP
- **Null results:** No effect on cardiovascular markers in schizophrenic patients (n=19, 200 mg/day) or overweight/obese subjects (n=45, 150 mg/day)

### Diabetes (Q1)
- **Type 2 diabetes:** 62 patients, 250 mg/day for 3 months -- decreased HbA1c, systolic BP, total cholesterol (Bhatt et al.)
- 19 patients, 5 mg twice daily for 1 month -- decreased insulin resistance and blood glucose, delayed glucose peaks, activated Akt insulin signaling pathway (Brasnyo et al.)
- **Impaired glucose tolerance:** 10 older adults, 1000-2000 mg/day for 1 month -- decreased peak postmeal glucose, increased insulin sensitivity (Crandall et al.)
- **Null result:** 14 patients, 500 mg twice daily -- no effect on GLP-1 secretion, glycemic control, or body weight (Thazhath et al.)
- Systematic review by Hausenblas et al. found significant improvements in systolic BP, HbA1c, and creatinine, but not fasting glucose, HOMA-IR, diastolic BP, insulin, triglycerides, LDL, or HDL

### NAFLD (Q5)
- **Dose-dependent paradox:** Lower doses (300 mg twice daily for 3 months) showed benefit (reduced AST, ALT, cholesterol, glucose, TNF-alpha). Higher doses (1500 mg/day for 6 months or 3000 mg/day for 2 months) showed no benefit or increased ALT/AST
- Most effective as supplement to lifestyle modifications (exercise + diet), not as standalone treatment

### Obesity (Q5)
- Mixed results: Timmers et al. found improved metabolic markers (150 mg/day, 30 days, obese men); Poulsen et al. failed to reproduce findings (500 mg/day, 4 weeks, 24 obese men)

## Bioavailability (Q2)
- Rapid conjugation of trans-resveratrol to glucuronic acid and sulfates produces glucuronides and sulfate conjugates accumulating in plasma and urine
- More effective topically than orally due to rapid metabolism and excretion
- SRT501 (micronized oral resveratrol) developed to improve bioavailability
- Nonlinear dose response effects noted -- low dose (dietary) vs high dose (pharmacological) may differ qualitatively
- Resveratrol successfully crosses the blood-brain barrier (confirmed by CSF detection in AD trials)

## Mechanisms Discussed (Q4)
- SIRT1 activation (histone and nonhistone protein deacetylation)
- NF-kB pathway inhibition (inflammation, immune response)
- PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway inhibition (cell proliferation, survival)
- IGF-1R/Akt/Wnt pathway inhibition; p53 activation
- COX inhibition (anti-inflammatory)
- VCAM inhibition; vascular smooth muscle cell effects
- Platelet aggregation inhibition
- RASSF-1a methylation modulation (epigenetic, breast cancer)

## Safety and Dosing (Q6)
- Generally well tolerated across most trials
- Doses ranged from 5 mg/day to 5 g/day
- Severe adverse events (renal failure) in multiple myeloma patients with SRT501 at 5 g
- NAFLD: higher doses (1500-3000 mg) showed no benefit or possible harm (increased ALT/AST)
- Some studies showed nonlinear dose-response, suggesting more is not always better
- No standardized dosing established; bioavailability remains key barrier

## Limitations
- Review is from 2017; newer trials not included
- Many reviewed trials had very small sample sizes (9-66 patients)
- Most trials were short duration (weeks to a few months)
- Heterogeneity in dosing, formulations, and outcome measures across trials
- No meta-analysis performed; narrative review only

## Relevance to Research Questions
- **Q1 (clinical trials):** Highly relevant -- comprehensive catalog of clinical trials across 6 disease areas
- **Q2 (bioavailability):** Discusses poor bioavailability extensively; confirms BBB penetration; nonlinear dose response
- **Q3 (longevity vs disease):** Touches on caloric restriction mimicry and SIRT1 activation but does not directly address longevity endpoints
- **Q4 (mechanism debates):** Covers multiple mechanisms (SIRT1, NF-kB, PI3K/Akt/mTOR, COX) but does not engage in debate about which is primary
- **Q5 (challenging evidence):** Strong -- documents null results, conflicting findings, adverse events (renal failure in myeloma, worsening lipids in schizophrenic patients, dose-dependent paradox in NAFLD)
- **Q6 (dosing/safety):** Covers dosing ranges, safety profile, adverse events; notes nonlinear dose response

## Claims to Verify
- PSA doubling time increase of 5.3 months -- described as not statistically significant, so the magnitude is less important than the null finding
- AD trials: both reported as 119 patients each -- these appear to be the same trial reported in two publications (Muossa et al. and Turner et al.), so not independent evidence
- Stroke trial: 312 patients is a reasonably large sample, but no effect size or p-values reported in the review
- The "2600 results" for PubMed search on "resveratrol and cancer" was as of March 2017 and is now outdated
