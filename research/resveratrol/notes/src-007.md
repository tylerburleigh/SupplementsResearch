# src-007: Resveratrol supplementation and cardiac remodeling in hypertensive patients

**Status:** Abstract only (PubMed landing page)
**PMID:** 36854725
**Journal:** Hypertension Research (2023), The Japanese Society of Hypertension
**Study type:** Randomized Controlled Trial
**Full text:** Not available -- only abstract retrieved

## Core Findings

Resveratrol supplementation (400 mg/day) for 6 months as an adjunct to conventional antihypertensive therapy alleviated left atrial remodeling, improved left ventricular diastolic function, and reduced biomarkers of cardiac fibrosis (procollagen type I C-peptide, galectin-3) in hypertensive patients. The study also found increased serum levels of sirtuin3, superoxide dismutase, and klotho in the resveratrol group compared to controls, suggesting activation of antioxidant and anti-aging pathways.

## Key Evidence

- **Sample:** 80 hypertensive patients randomized to RES group (400 mg/d + conventional therapy, n=43) vs. control group (conventional therapy alone, n=37). 6-month duration.
- **Cardiac structure:** RES group had smaller left atrial size and improved left ventricular global longitudinal strain vs. control after 6 months.
- **Diastolic function:** Lower E/e' ratio in RES group (indicating improved LV diastolic function).
- **Fibrosis biomarkers:** Significant decreases in procollagen type I C-peptide and galectin-3 in RES group vs. control.
- **No effect on:** Left ventricular structure (no significant difference between groups). Brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity decreased within RES group but was not significantly different from control.
- **Mechanism biomarkers:** Serum sirtuin3, superoxide dismutase (SOD), and klotho all significantly increased in RES group vs. control.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Randomized controlled trial, parallel-group
- **Population:** Hypertensive patients (specific inclusion/exclusion criteria not available from abstract)
- **Intervention:** Resveratrol 400 mg/day oral supplementation + standard antihypertensive therapy
- **Comparator:** Standard antihypertensive therapy alone
- **Duration:** 6 months
- **Primary outcomes:** Changes in cardiac-remodeling parameters (echocardiographic)
- **Secondary outcomes:** Anthropometric parameters, arterial stiffness parameters, mechanism indices (biomarkers)

## Limitations

- Only abstract available; full methods, statistical analysis details, adverse events, and complete results are inaccessible
- Sample size of 80 is relatively modest for a clinical trial
- 6-month follow-up is short; long-term effects unknown
- No placebo control mentioned (control received conventional therapy only)
- Imbalanced randomization (43 vs. 37) not explained in abstract
- Arterial stiffness improvement was not significant between groups

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (clinical trials):** Directly relevant -- RCT of resveratrol supplementation in humans with hypertension (n=80, 6 months).
- **Q2 (bioavailability):** Indirectly relevant -- study used 400 mg/day oral dosing and measured serum biomarker changes, suggesting systemic bioavailability, but no pharmacokinetic data.
- **Q4 (mechanism debates):** Relevant -- found increases in sirtuin3, SOD, and klotho, supporting sirtuin-activation and antioxidant mechanisms.
- **Q6 (dosing/safety):** Relevant -- used 400 mg/day dose, appeared well-tolerated (no adverse events mentioned in abstract, though full safety data unavailable).

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample size (n=80):** Stated in abstract as 80 subjects with 43 in RES group and 37 in control. Sum is 80. The imbalance (43 vs 37) is unusual for simple randomization and may suggest dropouts or block randomization details not reported.
- **Sirtuin3 increase:** Abstract claims "significantly increased" but no p-values, effect sizes, or confidence intervals provided. Cannot verify statistical significance from abstract alone.
- **Klotho increase:** Same concern -- "significantly increased" stated without quantitative support.
- **Safety:** Abstract does not mention adverse events or safety outcomes at all. Absence of mention does not confirm safety.
