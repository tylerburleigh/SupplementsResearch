# src-005: Meta-Analysis of Resveratrol on Inflammation and Oxidative Stress in T2DM

**Citation:** Zhu P, Jin Y, Sun J, Zhou X (2025). "The efficacy of resveratrol supplementation on inflammation and oxidative stress in type-2 diabetes mellitus patients: randomized double-blind placebo meta-analysis." *Frontiers in Endocrinology*, 15:1463027. doi: 10.3389/fendo.2024.1463027

**Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs (peer-reviewed, open access)

**Quality Assessment:** Full paper text available -- methods, results, discussion, and references all present. Content is substantive and authoritative.

## Core Findings

This meta-analysis of 6 RCTs (533 participants with T2DM) found that resveratrol supplementation significantly reduced several markers of inflammation and oxidative stress, including C-reactive protein (SMD = -1.40, P = 0.02), lipid peroxide (SMD = -0.99, P < 0.00001), 8-isoprostanes (SMD = -0.79, P < 0.0001), and oxidative stress score (SMD = -1.62, P = 0.0003). Resveratrol also increased antioxidant enzymes: glutathione peroxidase (SMD = 0.38, P = 0.04) and catalase (SMD = 0.33, P = 0.03). However, no significant effects were found for IL-6, TNF-alpha, SOD, TAC, or MDA in the primary random-effects analysis. Critically, GRADE evidence quality was "low" for 5 outcomes and "very low" for the remaining 6, limiting confidence in these findings.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **6 RCTs included**, total 533 participants, all randomized double-blind placebo-controlled
- **Dose range:** 40 mg to 1000 mg/day resveratrol
- **Duration range:** 4 to 24 weeks
- **Geographic distribution:** 3 trials from Iran, 1 from Mexico, 1 from Pakistan, 1 from Italy
- **Significant reductions in:** CRP (SMD = -1.40, 95% CI [-2.60, -0.21]), lipid peroxide (SMD = -0.99), 8-isoprostanes (SMD = -0.79), oxidative stress score (SMD = -1.62)
- **Significant increases in:** glutathione peroxidase (SMD = 0.38), catalase (SMD = 0.33)
- **Non-significant results (primary analysis):** IL-6 (P = 0.06), TNF-alpha (P = 0.12), SOD (P = 0.24), TAC (P = 0.21), MDA (P = 0.29)
- **Sensitivity analysis:** Several non-significant outcomes became significant under fixed-effect models, suggesting heterogeneity may mask effects
- **Subgroup analysis (dose):** No significant difference between <500 mg and >=500 mg doses for CRP or IL-6
- **Safety:** All studies reported no adverse events -- resveratrol had a high safety profile
- **Publication bias:** Funnel plot for CRP was asymmetrical, suggesting possible publication bias

## Methodology

- **Databases searched:** PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library, ClinicalTrials, ProQuest Dissertations and Theses (through November 6, 2024)
- **Inclusion criteria:** T2DM patients, oral resveratrol, placebo-controlled, outcomes related to oxidative stress/inflammation, RCT design
- **Quality assessment:** Cochrane RoB 2 tool and GRADE system
- **Statistical analysis:** RevMan 5.3, SMD with 95% CI, random-effects model when heterogeneity high (I2 > 50%, P <= 0.1), fixed-effects otherwise
- **Subgroup analysis by dose:** <500 mg vs >=500 mg
- **Sensitivity analysis:** Swapping random/fixed effect models to test robustness

## Limitations

1. **Very few RCTs** (only 6) -- sample sizes within each outcome were often small (125-563 participants depending on the biomarker)
2. **Heterogeneity was high** for many outcomes (e.g., CRP I2 not reported directly but random-effects model used, TNF-alpha had very wide CI [-7.47, 0.87])
3. **GRADE evidence quality was low or very low** for ALL outcomes -- downgraded for inconsistency, small sample size, few studies, and publication bias
4. **Geographic concentration** -- most studies from Middle Eastern countries (4 of 6 from Iran/Pakistan), limiting generalizability
5. **Variable dosing and duration** across studies (40-1000 mg/day, 4-24 weeks)
6. **Sensitivity analysis reversals** -- several outcomes that were non-significant became significant under fixed-effect models, indicating instability

## Claims to Verify

- **"533 participants" total sample**: The Methods/abstract state 533, but the CRP analysis reports 563 participants (293 treatment + 270 control from 7 studies). This discrepancy may be because two three-arm trials were split into separate comparisons, inflating the count in some analyses. The actual unique participant count is likely lower than 563 but should be reconciled.
- **Participant counts per outcome vary widely** (125-563), and some studies contributed multiple arms, making total participant counts potentially misleading.
- **No adverse events reported**: This is based on all 6 included studies, which is reassuring but the statement is somewhat vague ("resveratrol had a high safety profile with no adverse events") without specific numbers or types of adverse events monitored.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Clinical Trials):** Highly relevant. Directly synthesizes 6 RCTs of resveratrol supplementation in T2DM patients with specific biomarker outcomes.
- **Q2 (Bioavailability):** Not directly addressed. No pharmacokinetic data or blood resveratrol levels reported.
- **Q3 (Longevity vs Disease):** Relevant to disease-specific effects (T2DM). Not about longevity per se, but inflammation/oxidative stress are aging-related pathways.
- **Q4 (Mechanism Debates):** Moderately relevant. Discussion section reviews proposed mechanisms (NF-kB, SIRT1, PI3K-Akt, Nrf-2 pathways) but no original mechanistic data.
- **Q5 (Challenging Evidence):** Highly relevant. Multiple outcomes showed NO significant effect in primary analysis (IL-6, TNF-alpha, SOD, TAC, MDA). Low/very low GRADE ratings highlight the weakness of evidence. Sensitivity analysis showed results were unstable.
- **Q6 (Dosing/Safety):** Highly relevant. Dose range 40-1000 mg/day, subgroup analysis by dose (<500 vs >=500 mg) found no significant dose-response for CRP or IL-6. Safety profile reported as good with no adverse events.
