# src-008: Safety and Metabolic Outcomes of Resveratrol Supplementation in Older Adults

**Citation:** Anton SD, Embry C, Marsiske M, Lu X, Doss H, Leeuwenburgh C, Manini TM. "Safety and metabolic outcomes of resveratrol supplementation in older adults: results of a twelve-week, placebo-controlled pilot study." *Exp Gerontol.* 2014;0:181-187. doi: 10.1016/j.exger.2014.05.015. PMCID: PMC4149922.

**Study type:** Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled pilot trial (NCT01126229)

**Population:** 32 overweight older adults (age >=65, mean 73 +/- 7 years), BMI 25-34.9, sedentary, non-smokers. 97% Caucasian, 50% female.

## Core Findings

Resveratrol at both 300 mg/day and 1000 mg/day was well tolerated over 90 days in overweight older adults with no significant differences in adverse events vs. placebo. Blood chemistry values remained within normal ranges. The primary metabolic finding -- significantly lower glucose vs. placebo (p=0.02 for 300mg, p=0.01 for 1000mg) -- is ambiguous because treatment group glucose did not change from baseline; rather, the placebo group's glucose increased. No significant effects on blood pressure, body weight, or waist circumference were observed.

## Key Evidence

- **Safety (Q6):** Adverse event rates were low and similar across groups (no statistically significant differences). Two withdrawals in 1000 mg group for GI issues. Blood chemistries remained in normal range. Some minor changes: moderate dose reduced hemoglobin (-0.41 g/dL, p=0.04) and MCHC; high dose increased alkaline phosphatase (+7.90 IU/L, p=0.03) -- all within normal ranges.
- **Glucose/metabolism (Q1, Q5):** Significant glucose difference vs. placebo at Day 90 (300mg: p=0.02; 1000mg: p=0.01 adjusted). However, treatment groups did NOT differ from their own baselines. Placebo group glucose increased significantly (p=0.02). The authors note this finding appears driven by the placebo rise rather than a treatment effect.
- **No blood pressure effect:** No significant changes in systolic or diastolic BP in any group.
- **Adherence:** 93% across all groups, indicating feasibility of supplementation in older adults.
- **Hepatic markers:** High dose showed increased AST vs. moderate dose (p=0.04), but ALT unchanged. Bilirubin decreased in both treatment groups vs. placebo (p=0.01 for 300mg, p=0.04 for 1000mg) -- clinical significance unclear.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT with 3 arms (placebo, 300 mg/day, 1000 mg/day)
- **Duration:** 90 days supplementation + 30-day follow-up
- **Randomization:** 10 placebo, 12 moderate dose, 10 high dose completed the trial (39 enrolled, 7 withdrew)
- **Product:** Naturally-derived resveratrol from grapes and Japanese knotweed (Reserveage Organics). HPLC verified content within 7% of stated dose.
- **Monitoring:** Intensive safety monitoring -- blood draws every 3 days for first 10 days, then monthly. Adverse events graded via NCI criteria.
- **Statistics:** Wilcoxon rank sum, t-tests, ANCOVA adjusting for baseline values. SAS 9.2.

## Limitations

- Small sample size (n=32 completers across 3 arms), not powered for subgroup analyses
- Predominantly Caucasian sample -- limited generalizability
- Short duration (90 days) -- no long-term safety data
- Product was a blend (grape polyphenols + resveratrol), not pure resveratrol; effects may not be attributable solely to resveratrol
- Glucose finding is ambiguous (driven by placebo increase, not treatment decrease)
- Conflict of interest: Anton serves as scientific advisor/consultant for Reserveage Organics (product provider)

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (clinical trials):** Directly relevant. RCT in older adults with two dose arms, placebo control. Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT01126229). Adds to evidence base for resveratrol RCTs in aging populations.
- **Q5 (challenging evidence):** The ambiguous glucose finding (driven by placebo rise) and the absence of blood pressure effects challenge some of the more optimistic claims about resveratrol's metabolic benefits. Mixed results on hepatic markers add nuance.
- **Q6 (dosing/safety):** Strong direct relevance. One of few studies testing two different doses in older adults. Supports safety at 300-1000 mg/day for 90 days. Notes GI issues at 1000 mg leading to 2 withdrawals. Provides detailed blood chemistry and adverse event tables.

## Claims to Verify

- **Sample size of 32 completers:** Verified from text. 39 enrolled, 7 withdrew. Breakdown: 10 placebo, 12 x 300mg, 10 x 1000mg. Consistent throughout.
- **93% adherence:** Reported but not independently verified (pill counts at clinic visits).
- **Glucose significance (p=0.02, p=0.01):** Confirmed in Table 5, adjusted comparisons. But note the within-group change for placebo was p=0.02, while treatment groups showed no significant change from own baseline.
