# src-009: New Perspectives on Resveratrol in Metabolic and Estrogen-Dependent Conditions

**Citation:** Renke G, Fuschini AC, Clivati B, Teixeira LM, Cuyabano ML, Erel CT, Rosado EL. *Curr Issues Mol Biol.* 2025 Aug 27;47(9):692. doi: 10.3390/cimb47090692. PMCID: PMC12468622.

**Type:** Narrative review (63 articles, 2000-2025, from PubMed, ScienceDirect, SciELO)

**Quality assessment:** Full text available from PMC. Peer-reviewed open-access journal (MDPI). Content is substantive with methods, results/discussion, and conclusions sections.

---

## Core Findings

This review examines resveratrol's therapeutic potential for metabolic and estrogen-dependent conditions (endometriosis, adenomyosis, lipedema, PCOS, breast cancer) through SIRT-1 activation, estrogen receptor modulation, and anti-inflammatory pathways. The authors conclude resveratrol shows promise but current evidence is insufficient to support routine clinical use, with a predominance of preclinical data and heterogeneous, small clinical trials. A key contribution is framing resveratrol as a "selective estrogen receptor modulator" with a dual agonist/antagonist profile dependent on dose, tissue, and receptor subtype ratio (ERalpha/ERbeta).

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Bioavailability (Q2)
- Poor aqueous solubility, rapid hepatic metabolism, low oral bioavailability are key limitations
- Natural self-emulsifying hydrogel formulation (fenugreek galactomannan hydrogel matrix) retains ~37.9% at pH 6.5 and 28.5% at pH 2.0 after 24h; conventional resveratrol has minimal stability under gastric/intestinal pH
- Nanotechnology strategies reviewed: liposomes, PLGA nanoparticles, solid lipid nanoparticles, polymeric micelles, dendrimers, mesoporous silica nanoparticles, carbon nanotubes -- all under 500 nm
- CS/Res/SeNPs (chitosan-stabilized resveratrol/selenium nanoparticles) showed superior efficacy in murine T2DM model, improving glycemic control, insulin sensitivity, lipid profiles via PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway

### Dosing and Safety (Q6)
- Biphasic dose-dependent (hormetic) response: low-moderate doses (<=500 mg/day) produce beneficial effects; >=1000 mg/day associated with GI adverse events (diarrhea, nausea, abdominal discomfort)
- Meta-analysis of RCTs: treatment periods >12 weeks associated with greater lipid profile improvements (time-dependent effect)
- High-dose long-term (>90 days) occasionally associated with mild hepatic enzyme elevations; rare reports of leukopenia without confirmed causality
- In T2DM patients, 500-1000 mg/day combined with oral antidiabetic agents associated with mild hypoglycemic episodes (possible synergistic effect)
- No serious adverse events conclusively attributed to resveratrol
- Favorable safety profile in elderly populations within appropriate therapeutic window

### Mechanism of Action (Q4)
- **SIRT-1 pathway activation** leads to: mitochondrial biogenesis (PGC-1alpha, AMPK, ERRalpha), anti-inflammatory effects (reduced IL-1, IL-6, CRP, NF-kB, COX-1, COX-2), antioxidant enzyme stimulation (SOD, catalase, glutathione peroxidase), eNOS activation/NO production
- **Estrogen receptor modulation**: "Estrogenic Paradox" -- dual agonist/antagonist on ERalpha and ERbeta, structurally mimics 17beta-estradiol. Low nanomolar doses stimulate ER-positive cell proliferation; higher micromolar concentrations show cytostatic/cytotoxic effects via ER-independent mechanisms
- **Gut microbiota**: improves intestinal barrier integrity, increases Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium, reduces Clostridia, Lachnospiraceae, Enterococcus faecalis; biotransformation into 4-HPA and 3-HPP improves lipid metabolism

### Clinical Trials Evidence (Q1)
- Table 2 summarizes 14 preclinical and clinical studies with varying designs
- Notable RCTs: Abdollahi 2019 (71 overweight T2DM, 1g/day, 8 weeks -- reduced fasting glucose); Mahjabeen 2019 (110 T2DM, 200 mg/day, 24 weeks -- decreased glucose, HbA1c, insulin, HOMA-IR); Thaung 2021 (125 postmenopausal women, 75 mg/day, 24 months -- improved insulin resistance)
- Gorabi 2021 meta-analysis: 35 RCTs, 1741 individuals, average >=500 mg/day -- significant CRP reduction
- **No RCTs directly examining resveratrol in estrogen-dependent conditions** (except PCOS)
- Menopause-specific RCTs: 75 mg twice daily for 24 months enhanced cognition, cerebrovascular function, insulin sensitivity in postmenopausal women; resveratrol reduced hot flashes in 78.6% of patients; improved osteoarthritis pain and quality of life

### Challenging Evidence / Limitations (Q5)
- Current evidence insufficient for routine clinical use
- Predominance of preclinical studies; few clinical trials in humans
- Significant methodological heterogeneity: small sample sizes, non-standardized dosing, short durations, variable patient characteristics
- Dual ER activity adds complexity -- potential estrogenic stimulation in hormone-sensitive tissues is a safety concern
- Limited commercial interest (non-patentable compound) reduces funding for large-scale trials
- No studies directly linking resveratrol to estrogen-dependent conditions beyond PCOS

## Methodology

- Literature review of 63 articles from PubMed, ScienceDirect, SciELO (2000-2025)
- Prioritized systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and RCTs
- Primary outcome: antioxidant action of resveratrol
- Secondary outcomes: types, effects, and safety in metabolic conditions
- No systematic review protocol registered; narrative review approach

## Limitations

- Narrative review rather than systematic review or meta-analysis
- No formal quality assessment of included studies
- Heterogeneous study designs make direct comparisons difficult
- Most evidence for metabolic effects, not estrogen-dependent conditions
- Authors acknowledge gap between proposed mechanisms and clinical validation

## Claims to Verify

- "78.6% of patients" reported reduction in hot flashes (reference 61) -- specific percentage cited but original study details not provided
- "metabolic syndrome currently affects approximately 30% of the global population, with projections suggesting an increase to over 50%" -- broad epidemiological claim referenced to a single source
- Sample sizes in Table 2: 1741 individuals in Gorabi 2021 meta-analysis (35 RCTs) -- this is a pooled sample from meta-analysis, not individual participants
- The hormetic dose threshold of <=500 mg/day as beneficial vs >=1000 mg/day as potentially harmful is presented as consistently observed across studies but based on only 4 cited references

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (clinical trials)**: Moderate -- reviews multiple RCTs but focuses on metabolic/PCOS endpoints, not longevity; Table 2 useful for trial-level data
- **Q2 (bioavailability)**: Strong -- discusses poor oral bioavailability, formulation strategies (nanotechnology, hydrogel), isomeric differences (trans vs cis)
- **Q4 (mechanism debates)**: Strong -- detailed mechanistic discussion of SIRT-1, ER modulation, "Estrogenic Paradox," gut microbiota pathway
- **Q5 (challenging evidence)**: Moderate -- explicitly states evidence is insufficient, cites limitations, dual ER activity as concern
- **Q6 (dosing/safety)**: Strong -- hormetic dose-response framework, specific dose thresholds, time-dependent effects, adverse event profile
