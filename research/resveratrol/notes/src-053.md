# src-053: Marx et al. (2018) - Resveratrol, Cognitive Performance, and Mood Meta-Analysis

**Citation:** Marx W, Kelly JT, Marshall S, Cutajar J, Annois B, Pipingas A, Tierney A, Itsiopoulos C. "Effect of resveratrol supplementation on cognitive performance and mood in adults: a systematic literature review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials." *Nutrition Reviews*. 2018 Jun 1;76(6):432-443. doi: 10.1093/nutrit/nuy010. PMID: 29596658.

**Content quality:** Abstract-only (PubMed landing page). No full text available. Key quantitative results are present in the abstract.

## Core Findings

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 10 RCTs found that resveratrol supplementation may improve select measures of cognitive performance (specifically delayed recognition) and reduce negative mood, but the overall literature is inconsistent and limited. Only 3 of 10 included studies found significant improvements in some cognitive measures; 2 reported mixed findings; 5 found no effect.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **10 RCTs included** in the systematic review
- **Delayed recognition:** SMD 0.39 (95% CI: 0.08-0.70; I2=0%; P=0.01; 3 studies, 166 participants)
- **Negative mood:** SMD -0.18 (95% CI: -0.31 to -0.05; I2=0%; P=0.006; 3 studies, 163 participants)
- **3/10 studies** found significant improvement in some cognitive measures
- **2/10 studies** reported mixed findings
- **5/10 studies** found no effect
- Studies generally had **low risk of bias** and were of **moderate or high quality**
- Quality assessed using GRADE; bias assessed using Cochrane Collaboration Risk of Bias tool

## Methodology

- Systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA guidelines
- Population: adults (healthy and clinical settings)
- Outcomes: processing speed, number facility, memory, and mood
- Risk of bias: Cochrane Collaboration tool
- Evidence quality: GRADE framework
