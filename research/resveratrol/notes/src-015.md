# src-015: Zhou et al. (2022) - Efficacy of Resveratrol Supplementation on Glucose and Lipid Metabolism: A Meta-Analysis and Systematic Review

**Citation:** Zhou Q, Wang Y, Han X, Fu S, Zhu C, Chen Q. (2022). *Frontiers in Physiology*, 13:795980. doi: 10.3389/fphys.2022.795980

**Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs (peer-reviewed, open access)

**Quality:** Full paper text available with methods, results, discussion, and references.

## Core Findings

This meta-analysis of 25 RCTs (1,171 total participants) found that resveratrol supplementation significantly decreased waist circumference (SMD = -0.36, P = 0.002), HbA1c (SMD = -0.48, P <= 0.001), total cholesterol (SMD = -0.15, P = 0.003), LDL-C (SMD = -0.42, P <= 0.001), and increased HDL-C (SMD = 0.16, P = 0.03). However, resveratrol had no significant effect on body weight, BMI, fasting glucose, insulin, HOMA index, triglycerides, leptin, or adiponectin. The authors conclude resveratrol has its major clinical value for obese and diabetic patients.

## Key Evidence/Data Points

- **Sample:** 25 RCTs, 1,171 participants (578 placebo, 593 intervention), group sizes ranged from 9 to 53
- **Doses across included trials:** 50 mg/day to 1000 mg/day, durations from 4 weeks to 6 months
- **Significant reductions:** WC (SMD -0.36, 95% CI: -0.59 to -0.14, I2=88%), HbA1c (SMD -0.48, 95% CI: -0.69 to -0.27, I2=94%), TC (SMD -0.15, 95% CI: -0.30 to -0.01, I2=94%), LDL-C (SMD -0.42, 95% CI: -0.57 to -0.27, I2=92%), HDL-C (SMD 0.16, 95% CI: 0.02 to 0.31, I2=81%)
- **No significant effects on:** Body weight (P=0.18), BMI (P=0.83), fasting glucose (P=0.14), insulin (P=0.42), HOMA index (P=0.13), fat percentage (P=0.06), TG (P=0.53), adiponectin (P=0.89), leptin (P=0.38)
- **Subgroup findings for HbA1c:** Strongest effect in T2DM patients (SMD -1.10) vs. obese (SMD -0.44); strongest with 200-500 mg/day dose (SMD -0.81) and >17 weeks duration (SMD -1.40)
- **Subgroup findings for WC:** Significant in obese patients; 200-500 mg/day dose more effective than <200 mg/day; 9-16 weeks duration most effective (SMD -0.73)
- **Publication bias:** Significant for WC (P=0.035), HbA1c (P=0.012), and TC (P=0.034), but Duval and Tweedie trim-and-fill method did not significantly change pooled SMDs
- **Heterogeneity:** Very high across all outcomes (I2 ranging from 46% to 98%), indicating substantial variability between studies

## Methodology

- **Databases searched:** PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Google Scholar, Embase (inception to October 2021)
- **Inclusion criteria:** Human RCTs (parallel or crossover), resveratrol vs. placebo, reporting mean changes in metabolic biomarkers (TC, TG, LDL-C, HDL-C, BW, BMI, WC, HbA1c, HOMA, insulin, fasting glucose, fat %, leptin, adiponectin)
- **Statistical approach:** Random-effects model (inverse variance), SMD with 95% CI, RevMan V.5.3 and STATA V.15
- **Quality assessment:** Cochrane Collaboration risk of bias tool; 8 studies low bias risk, 19 unclear, 9 high bias risk
- **Subgroup analyses by:** Disease type (obesity, T2DM, other), BMI category (<30 vs >=30), dosage (<200, 200-500, >500 mg/day), duration (<8, 9-16, >=17 weeks)
- **Registered:** PROSPERO CRD42021244904
- **Note:** A corrigendum was issued for this article (nature of correction not specified in the text)

## Limitations

1. **Very high heterogeneity** (I2 46-98%) across nearly all outcomes, limiting confidence in pooled estimates
2. Small sample sizes within individual trials (9-53 per group)
3. Wide variation in dosages (50-1000 mg/day), durations (4 weeks - 6 months), and patient populations
4. Publication bias detected for three key outcomes (WC, HbA1c, TC)
5. Only English-language publications included
6. Quality assessment showed 9 of 25 studies with high bias risk
7. Sensitivity analysis showed significant changes in pooled results for body weight, fasting glucose, and HOMA index after removing individual studies, indicating fragility of some findings
8. Does not address bioavailability, which may explain inconsistent trial results

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Clinical Trials):** Directly relevant. Comprehensive synthesis of 25 human RCTs covering metabolic outcomes. Shows which biomarkers respond and which do not.
- **Q2 (Bioavailability):** Indirectly relevant. The wide dose range (50-1000 mg/day) and inconsistent results across trials could reflect bioavailability issues, though not explicitly discussed.
- **Q3 (Longevity vs Disease):** Partially relevant. Shows strongest effects in T2DM and obese populations, with no benefits in healthy populations (consistent with Yoshino et al. 2012 finding of no improvement in nonobese women with normal glucose tolerance).
- **Q4 (Mechanism Debates):** Discusses SIRT1/AMPK pathway, ATF6-dependent mechanism, ATM-AMPK axis in skeletal muscle, and lipid metabolism-related miRNAs as potential mechanisms, though these are from referenced animal studies rather than the meta-analysis itself.
- **Q5 (Challenging Evidence):** Highly relevant. Shows that resveratrol had NO significant effect on body weight, BMI, fasting glucose, insulin, HOMA index, triglycerides, adiponectin, or leptin. Notes contradictions with prior meta-analyses and individual RCTs. Some included studies (Kjaer et al., de Ligt et al., Poulsen et al.) found no benefits.
- **Q6 (Dosing/Safety):** Relevant for dosing. Subgroup analysis suggests 200-500 mg/day is most effective for HbA1c and WC. Does not address safety/adverse events directly.

## Claims to Verify

- Total participant count of 1,171 is stated in Results; verified from Table 1 (sum of individual study group sizes is consistent)
- Corrigendum was applied to this article but its content/nature is not described -- downstream verification may need to check what was corrected
- HDL-C result reported as "0.16 (-0.31, -0.02)" in abstract but "0.16 (0.02 to 0.31)" in main results section -- the abstract appears to have a sign error in the CI bounds; the main text version (0.02 to 0.31) is consistent with a significant increase in HDL-C
