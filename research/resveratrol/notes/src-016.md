# src-016: Preliminary RCT of Resveratrol in Hypertensive Patients

**Citation:** Shafiei E, Rezaei M, Mahmoodi M, et al. "Preliminary, randomized, double-blinded, placebo-controlled cross-over study with resveratrol in hypertensive patients." *Scientific Reports* 15, 31297 (2025). DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-17305-6

**Quality:** Full text available (open access, Nature Scientific Reports). Peer-reviewed.

## Core Findings

1. Resveratrol supplementation (500 mg twice daily = 1 g/day) for 4 weeks did NOT produce a statistically significant reduction in systolic or diastolic blood pressure compared to placebo in pre-hypertensive and stage-1 hypertensive patients.
2. Nitric oxide (NO) levels were significantly higher in the resveratrol-treated group vs placebo (P = 0.042), demonstrating a biological effect on the NO/eNOS pathway even without clinical BP benefit.
3. No other biochemical parameters (FBS, TG, TC, HDL, creatinine, liver enzymes, oxidative stress markers MDA/TAC, hematologic indices) showed significant treatment effects.

## Key Evidence / Data Points

- **Design:** Single-center, randomized, double-blinded, placebo-controlled, 2x2 cross-over trial (4 weeks treatment - 4 weeks washout - 4 weeks treatment).
- **Sample:** 30 patients allocated (21 men, 9 women); only 16 completed both phases (4 females, 12 males), mean age 49.0 +/- 9.2 years. 13 noncompliant participants were excluded.
- **Dose:** 500 mg resveratrol (99% pure, Biotivia) twice daily = 1 g/day total, orally.
- **Primary outcome (BP):** No statistically significant effect of resveratrol vs placebo on systolic or diastolic BP (multivariate ANOVA crossover model).
- **Secondary outcome (NO):** Statistically significant increase in nitric oxide (P = 0.042).
- **Safety:** 1 g/day was generally well-tolerated. Two adverse events: (1) one female reported heaviness, hyperthermia, discomfort (on Hydrochlorothiazide + Amlodipine); (2) one male reported pain/swelling in feet and face (on Losartan, Metoral, Atorvastatin, Aspirin). Both discontinued. Authors speculate possible drug-resveratrol interactions.
- **Compliance:** 100% among the 16 completers (capsule count method).
- **Trial registration:** IRCT201407078129N7 (registered 15/08/2014).

## Methodology

- Cross-over design with stratified complete block randomization (blocks of 4).
- BP measured twice on right arm after 15-min rest using standard mercury sphygmomanometer.
- Biochemical analysis: enzymatic methods with Selectra 2 autoanalyzer; NO and MDA via colorimetric assays.
- Statistical analysis: ANOVA for 2x2 crossover (Stata/SE 13.0), treatment/period/interaction effects.
- Original sample size target was 24 completers; only 16 completed due to recruitment challenges (COVID-19 pandemic).

## Limitations

- **Severely underpowered:** Only 16 completers vs target of 24. Authors explicitly acknowledge heightened Type II error risk and frame findings as preliminary/hypothesis-generating.
- **Short duration:** Only 4 weeks per treatment period; authors acknowledge longer treatment may be needed.
- **Single center, homogeneous population:** Iranian adults only, limiting generalizability.
- **No sex-stratified analysis:** Too few females (n=4 completers) for meaningful subgroup analysis.
- **Concomitant medications:** Most participants were on standard antihypertensive medications (Captopril, Losartan, Atenolol, etc.), introducing potential confounding through drug-supplement interactions.
- **Recruitment period:** 2014-2018 (4 years for a small trial suggests persistent recruitment difficulties beyond just COVID).

## Claims to Verify

- **"30 participants" allocated but only "16 completed":** The text states 30 allocated and 16 completed, with 13 noncompliant. That accounts for 29 (30 - 1 unaccounted for). Minor discrepancy but worth noting.
- **NO significance P = 0.042:** Reported in text but tables not fully visible in the extracted content. Should be verified against Table 4 if accessible.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (clinical trials):** Directly relevant. A well-designed (though underpowered) RCT of resveratrol in hypertensive patients. Shows null BP result but positive NO biomarker effect.
- **Q2 (bioavailability):** Indirectly relevant. The NO increase demonstrates systemic biological activity at 1 g/day oral dose, though the paper does not measure resveratrol metabolite levels directly.
- **Q4 (mechanism debates):** Relevant. The dissociation between increased NO and absent BP effect is discussed in detail -- authors invoke NO resistance, ROS scavenging of NO, and RAAS/SNS override as explanations. Supports the complexity of translating eNOS activation to clinical outcomes.
- **Q5 (challenging evidence):** Highly relevant. This is a null-result clinical trial -- resveratrol failed to lower BP despite promising preclinical data. Explicitly challenges efficacy claims for hypertension.
- **Q6 (dosing/safety):** Relevant. 1 g/day dose was generally well-tolerated but produced adverse events in 2 participants potentially due to drug interactions. Supports the view that 1 g/day is at the upper boundary of tolerability.
