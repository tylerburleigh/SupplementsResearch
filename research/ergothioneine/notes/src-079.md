# src-079: Ergothioneine is associated with reduced mortality and decreased risk of cardiovascular disease

**Authors:** Smith E, Ottosson F, Hellstrand S, Ericson U, Orho-Melander M, Fernandez C, Melander O
**Year:** 2020 | **Venue:** Heart (BMJ) | **Citations:** 140
**DOI:** 10.1136/heartjnl-2019-315485
**Quality:** ok -- full paper text available (raw text extraction, some formatting artifacts)

## Core Findings

Higher plasma ergothioneine was independently associated with reduced risk of coronary artery disease, cardiovascular mortality, and all-cause mortality over 21.4 years of follow-up in a population-based Swedish cohort of 3,236 individuals. Ergothioneine was the metabolite most strongly linked to a Health Conscious Food Pattern (HCFP) among 112 measured metabolites, suggesting it serves as a biomarker connecting healthy dietary intake to cardiometabolic protection. The authors explicitly call for ergothioneine supplementation trials to test causality.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Prospective population-based cohort study (Malmo Diet and Cancer -- Cardiovascular Cohort, MDC-CC)
- **Population:** 3,236 participants free of CVD and type 2 diabetes at baseline; mean age 57.4 years; 61% female
- **Baseline period:** 1991--1996; median follow-up 21.4 years
- **Metabolomics:** 112 plasma metabolites measured via liquid chromatography mass spectrometry (LC-MS)
- **Exposure identification:** Linear regression of metabolite levels against HCFP score, adjusted for age, sex, BMI, fasting glucose, LDL, HDL, triglycerides, SBP, antihypertensive treatment, dietary season, alcohol, smoking. Bonferroni correction (p < 0.00044).
- **Outcome analysis:** Cox proportional hazards models, first age/sex-adjusted, then fully adjusted for same covariates plus alcohol and smoking. Schoenfeld residuals used to verify proportional hazards assumption.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** Physical activity adjustment in subset (n=2,993 for Cox models; n=2,323 for HCFP regressions) -- results unchanged.

## Key Evidence

### Fully adjusted Cox regression results (ergothioneine per 1 SD increment)

| Outcome | HR | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| Coronary artery disease | 0.85 | 0.01 |
| Cardiovascular mortality | 0.79 | 0.002 |
| All-cause mortality | 0.86 | 4e-5 |

Note: The age/sex-adjusted models showed even stronger estimates (CAD HR=0.80, 95% CI 0.72-0.90, p=3e-4; stroke HR=0.86, 95% CI 0.76-0.97, p=0.01; all-cause mortality HR=0.82, 95% CI 0.76-0.88, p=2e-8; CV mortality HR=0.76, 95% CI 0.67-0.88, p=7e-4). The fully adjusted model did not retain stroke significance.

### Event counts during follow-up

- 603 incident CVD cases (316 CAD, 271 stroke)
- 362 incident type 2 diabetes cases
- 843 total deaths (229 cardiovascular)

### Dietary associations

- Ergothioneine most strongly correlated with vegetable intake (Pearson r=0.2, p=2e-29)
- Also correlated with seafood intake; negatively associated with solid fats and added sugar
- Ergothioneine had strongest association with HCFP among all 112 metabolites

## Mechanistic Discussion

- Ergothioneine has a specific transporter (OCTN1/SLC22A4) suggested to be upregulated in areas of inflammation, providing potential for controlled antioxidant function
- Previous rodent studies show protection from ischemic reperfusion injury
- Cited Cheah et al. 2017: oral ergothioneine in healthy humans increased circulating levels and decreased biomarkers of oxidative damage
- Dietary sources: mushrooms, tempeh, garlic, vegetables, seafood

## Limitations

1. **Observational design:** Cannot establish causation despite prospective design and multivariable adjustment
2. **Single timepoint measurement:** Diet, risk factors, and metabolomics assessed only at baseline; changes over 21 years not captured
3. **Population specificity:** Swedish middle-aged cohort -- generalizability to other ethnicities/ages uncertain
4. **Modest diet-metabolite correlations:** Pearson r=0.2 for ergothioneine-vegetables, though consistent with prior literature
5. **Confounding by healthy lifestyle:** Despite extensive adjustment, residual confounding possible (ergothioneine may simply be a marker of overall health-conscious behavior)
6. **Alcohol association:** Positive correlation between ergothioneine and alcohol intake is unexplained and could confound results
7. **Proportional hazards violated** for urobilin-overall mortality model (though not for ergothioneine models)

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Dietary sources/bioavailability):** Identifies ergothioneine as the metabolite most strongly associated with a health-conscious food pattern; strongest food correlation with vegetables (r=0.2); dietary sources include mushrooms, tempeh, garlic. Supports ergothioneine as a dietary biomarker.
- **Q2 (Human health outcomes):** Primary evidence source -- large prospective cohort with 21+ year follow-up showing independent associations with reduced CVD, cardiovascular mortality, and all-cause mortality. This is one of the strongest epidemiological studies linking ergothioneine to hard clinical endpoints.
- **Q4 (Deficiency/insufficiency):** Demonstrates a dose-response relationship (per 1 SD increment) suggesting that lower ergothioneine levels correspond to higher risk.
- **Q5 (Longevity/aging):** Directly addresses mortality outcomes with long follow-up; all-cause mortality HR=0.86 (fully adjusted), suggesting ~14% reduction per SD of ergothioneine.
- **Q6 (Supplementation safety/efficacy):** Cites Cheah et al. 2017 showing oral ergothioneine increases circulating levels and reduces oxidative damage biomarkers. Does not directly test supplementation.
- **Q7 (Practical recommendations):** Authors explicitly recommend ergothioneine supplementation trials; identify ergothioneine as a candidate substance for future RCTs.

## Claims to Verify

- Event counts (603 CVD, 316 CAD, 271 stroke, 362 diabetes, 843 deaths, 229 CV deaths) appear in multiple places and are consistent between abstract and results sections.
- Fully adjusted HR for CAD (0.85, p=0.01) appears in the abstract but the age/sex-adjusted value (0.80) is more prominent in the body text. The difference between age/sex-adjusted (0.80) and fully adjusted (0.85) is expected given additional covariates.
- Sample size of 3,236 is well-documented: 3,833 randomly selected for metabolite measurement, minus exclusions for prevalent CAD (n=80), stroke (n=26), diabetes (n=348), incomplete covariates (n=43), incomplete diet (n=120). These exclusions are internally consistent.
