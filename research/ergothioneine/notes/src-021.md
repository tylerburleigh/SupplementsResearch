# src-021: Ergothioneine: an underrecognised dietary micronutrient required for healthy ageing?

**Authors:** Tian X, Thorne JL, Moore JB | **Year:** 2023 | **Venue:** British Journal of Nutrition | **Citations:** 68 | **Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive horizon-scanning review arguing ergothioneine should be considered a conditionally essential micronutrient for healthy ageing. The strongest epidemiological evidence comes from a Swedish prospective cohort (n=3,236; median follow-up 21.4 years) showing higher plasma ergothioneine associated with reduced coronary disease (HR 0.85/SD), cardiovascular mortality (HR 0.79/SD), and all-cause mortality (HR 0.86/SD). Cognitive data from a Singapore elderly cohort (n=470, mean age 73) show lower baseline ergothioneine predicts faster cognitive decline over 5 years. The authors highlight a critical gap: only one human pharmacokinetic study exists (n=45, doses 5-25 mg/day for 7 days), and no controlled human intervention trials with clinical endpoints have been published.

## Key Evidence

### Mortality and Cardiovascular Disease
- Swedish prospective cohort (n=3,236, 21.4-year median follow-up): HR per 1 SD ergothioneine increment for coronary disease 0.85, CV mortality 0.79, all-cause mortality 0.86 (Smith et al. 2020, ref 80).
- Meta-analysis of prospective cohorts (n=601,893): mushroom consumption associated with lower all-cause mortality (pooled RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.91-0.98) (ref 82).
- In vitro: ergothioneine reduces adhesion molecules (E-selectin, ICAM-1, VCAM-1) in human aortic endothelial cells and reduces monocyte adhesion, a key atherogenesis step (ref 90).
- Diabetic rat models: 6 weeks ergothioneine supplementation decreased cardiac injury biomarkers, lipid peroxidation, and inflammation (ref 87).

### Cognitive Decline and Ageing
- Blood ergothioneine declines linearly after age 60 in humans (ref 75).
- Case-control studies: lower ergothioneine in mild cognitive impairment, dementia, and Parkinson's disease vs age-matched controls (refs 75-77).
- Singapore prospective elderly cohort (n=470, mean age 73): lower baseline ergothioneine predicted poorer cognitive performance and faster decline over 5 years (ref 78).
- Animal models: ergothioneine protects neurons from cisplatin, beta-amyloid, and age-related learning/memory deficits (refs 72-74).
- SLC22A4 knockout in C. elegans: reduced lifespan and increased oxidative damage (ref 52).

### Pharmacokinetics and Supplementation
- Only one human PK study (Cheah et al. 2017, ref 39): 45 healthy subjects, placebo/5 mg/25 mg per day for 7 days, 4-week follow-up. Ergothioneine rapidly absorbed, largely retained, minimal urinary excretion (<4% increase).
- Whole blood ergothioneine continued increasing for 3 weeks post-supplementation withdrawal, sustained at 4 weeks.
- Mouse study: 70 mg/kg/day for 28 days accumulated in liver, whole blood, kidney, and brain (ref 38).
- Safety approval: Europe 2016 (EFSA), USA 2018 (FDA GRAS).

### Mechanism of Action
- SLC22A4 transporter: highly specific for ergothioneine, expressed on intestinal apical membrane and renal proximal tubules; avid absorption and retention.
- Second transporter SLC22A15 de-orphaned, expressed highly in brain (ref 28).
- Scavenges diverse ROS/RNS, chelates divalent cations, deactivates singlet oxygen at higher rates than other thiols.
- Predicted to directly bind and stabilize Nrf2 via molecular docking simulations (ref 67).
- Redox repair of oxidized ergothioneine can be provided by ascorbate, glutathione reductase, or thioredoxin reductase.

### Food Sources
- Mushrooms: richest dietary source, ranging from ~0.06 mg/kg wet weight (chanterelle) to 11,800 mg/kg dry weight (golden oyster in grape marc).
- Fermented foods: variable, up to 1,775 mg/kg wet weight (fermented shiitake).
- Other sources: asparagus, garlic, organ meats, spirulina.
- Wide variation due to strain, growing conditions, cultivation substrates, soil health, and analytical methods.

## Methodology

Narrative review (horizon-scanning manuscript) synthesizing preclinical and epidemiological evidence. No systematic search protocol described. Includes extensive tables of food ergothioneine content from published literature. Discusses the framework for conditionally essential nutrients (Grimble 1993 criteria) applied to ergothioneine.

## Limitations

1. **No human intervention trial data** -- the most critical gap. The review repeatedly acknowledges that controlled human trials with clinical endpoints are just beginning.
2. **Narrative review, not systematic** -- potential for selection bias in evidence presented.
3. **Preclinical-to-human extrapolation** -- significant species differences exist (e.g., SLC22A4 expressed in adult mouse/rat liver but barely detectable in adult human liver).
4. **Unresolved transporter biology** -- basolateral transporter for ergothioneine efflux unknown; mitochondrial transport unconfirmed; possible additional transporters uncharacterized.
5. **Observational epidemiological data only** -- the mortality and cognitive associations cannot establish causation.
6. **Conflicting results** noted (e.g., rabbit heart reperfusion study showing no benefit from ergothioneine, ref 86).

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Health benefits/longevity):** Strong relevance. Provides the most comprehensive epidemiological evidence linking higher ergothioneine to reduced mortality and cognitive decline. Reviews preclinical evidence across multiple disease models (CVD, diabetes, NAFLD, neurodegeneration).
- **Q2 (Supplementation protocols):** Moderate relevance. Only one human PK study documented. Notes ongoing clinical trials (cognitive decline: 25 mg 3x/week for 52 weeks; ErgMS metabolic syndrome: 5 or 30 mg/day for 12 weeks). Safety approvals documented.
- **Q3 (Mechanism of action):** Strong relevance. Reviews antioxidant activity, metal chelation, Nrf2 activation, SIRT1/SIRT6 involvement, anti-inflammatory pathways. Discusses transporter biology and tissue distribution in detail.
- **Q4 (Dietary sources):** Strong relevance. Tables 1 and 2 provide comprehensive ergothioneine content data for mushrooms, fermented foods, vegetables, grains, meats, and other foods.
- **Q5 (Safety/side effects):** Moderate relevance. Notes regulatory safety approvals (EFSA 2016, FDA GRAS 2018). Single human PK study showed no adverse effects. No dedicated safety trial data reviewed.
- **Q6 (Deficiency states):** Strong relevance. Reviews SLC22A4 knockout phenotypes across organisms. Notes blood levels decline after age 60. Argues for conditionally essential nutrient framework. Discusses polymorphisms affecting transporter function.

## Claims to Verify

- The statement that "only one study has examined ergothioneine supplementation in humans" (line 182-183) was accurate at time of publication (2023) but may be outdated given ongoing trials.
- Hazard ratios (0.85, 0.79, 0.86) from Smith et al. 2020 should be cross-checked against the original paper for confidence intervals and adjustment variables.
- The meta-analysis pooled RR of 0.94 (95% CI 0.91-0.98) for mushroom consumption and all-cause mortality is attributed to Ba et al. 2021 (ref 82) -- verify the original n=601,893 figure.
- Mouse dose of 70 mg/kg/day (line 191) is quite high; verify from Tang et al. 2018 (ref 38) whether this is the correct dose and duration.
