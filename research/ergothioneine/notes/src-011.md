## Core Findings

This is a consumer-facing supplement guide that synthesizes published research on ergothioneine into practical dosing and usage recommendations. It does not present original data but provides a structured summary of human trials, observational studies, and mechanistic evidence. The guide is notable for its specificity on dosage ranges studied in clinical trials and its frank acknowledgment of evidence limitations.

## Main Topics Covered

1. **Mechanism of action:** SLC22A4 (OCTN1) transporter-mediated uptake, selective tissue accumulation in high-stress tissues (bone marrow, immune cells, eye, gut), thione-form stability, radical scavenging, metal chelation, Nrf2 pathway activation, mitochondrial protection.
2. **Cognitive aging:** Observational cohorts linking lower plasma ergothioneine to faster decline; pilot RCT (25 mg 3x/week for 1 year in MCI) showing improved learning and stabilized neurofilament light chain.
3. **Sleep quality:** 8 mg/day for 16 weeks improved subjective sleep (randomized, placebo-controlled); 20 mg/day for 4 weeks also improved sleep parameters.
4. **Cardiometabolic outcomes:** Large cohort analyses showing higher plasma levels associated with lower coronary disease risk, CV mortality, and all-cause mortality (observational only).
5. **Safety and dosing:** European regulatory review found synthetic L-ergothioneine safe at proposed intakes. PK studies at 5-25 mg/day showed no serious adverse events. Side effects rare and mild.

## Limitations of This Source Type

1. **Not peer-reviewed:** This is a commercial supplement guide, not a scientific publication. Claims are unsourced within the text (references listed but not linked to specific claims).
2. **No primary data:** All findings are secondary summaries of published research; no new experiments or analyses.
3. **Potential commercial bias:** VitaLibrary is a supplement information site that may have commercial interests in promoting supplementation.
4. **Unverified trial specifics:** The pilot RCT and sleep studies are described but no trial identifiers, sample sizes, or effect sizes are provided.
5. **Dosing recommendations mix evidence with practical advice:** The "practical starting points" section extrapolates beyond what trials have directly tested.
