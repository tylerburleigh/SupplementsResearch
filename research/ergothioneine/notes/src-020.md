# src-020: Is Ergothioneine Your Next Longevity Supplement? (WellnessPulse)

**Source type:** Health journalism article (not peer-reviewed)
**Author:** Egle Kristopaityte, fact-checked by Dominyka Sitaviciute, MSc
**Date:** March 6, 2025
**Venue:** WellnessPulse (online health media)

## Core Findings

This article synthesizes recent evidence on ergothioneine (ET) supplementation for longevity and cognitive health, drawing on multiple published studies but adding no new data. It concludes that ET supplements appear safe but longevity benefits in humans remain unproven. The strongest evidence supports cognitive benefits in older adults with mild cognitive impairment.

## Key Claims and Cited Evidence

### Longevity / Lifespan (Animal Data Only)
- **2025 trial** (Cell Metabolism): ET extended lifespan and enhanced mobility in worms; aged rats showed enhanced exercise endurance, muscle mass, and blood vessel growth; mice showed higher NAD+ levels in muscles.
- **2024 study** (GeroScience): Male mice on daily oral ET had lower age-related weight/fat mass decline, slower movement velocity decline, and slower learning/memory impairment.

### Human Observational Data
- **Swedish study** (Heart journal, n=3,236): Higher plasma ET associated with lower risk of coronary disease, cardiovascular mortality, and all-cause mortality.
- Low ET levels associated with dementia and Parkinson's disease (citing observational studies).
- **Caveat**: Halliwell and Cheah (FEBS Letters) warn that correlation does not imply causation -- disease could cause low ET via dietary changes or gut microbiota shifts.

### Cognitive Decline (Human RCT)
- **Pilot RCT** (Journal of Alzheimer's Disease, n=19, age 60+, MCI): 25 mg ET or placebo 3x/week for 1 year. ET group showed improved Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test performance (working/long-term memory) and stabilized neurofilament light chain (NfL) levels. No changes in blood counts, kidney, or liver function.
- **Singapore cross-sectional study** (2019, n=600 Chinese seniors): >2 standard mushroom portions/week (~150g) associated with 50% lower MCI odds.

### Safety
- FDA granted ET GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) designation.
- EFSA: No adverse effects at 800 mg/kg/day; concluded safe up to 30 mg/day for adults, 20 mg/day for children 3+.
- Human studies used up to 25 mg with no side effects observed.
- **Potential concern**: One 2022 study found fecal bacteria may metabolize ET to TMAO, but only in 1 of 25 samples.

### Other Benefits
- **Skin**: 2024 trial (n=77 women, ~48 years), 25 mg/day for 12 weeks showed better wrinkle/texture scores vs placebo. Study funded by mushroom company with researcher COI.
- **Sleep**: 2022 trial (n=92, ages 40-75, anxiety/sleep complaints), 20 mg/day for 4 weeks significantly alleviated sleeping difficulties.

### Dietary Sources
Highest ET levels in king oyster, maitake, oyster, shiitake, and porcini mushrooms. Also found in liver, black beans, red beans, and oat bran.

## Expert Commentary
- **Robert Beelman, PhD (Penn State)**: Takes ET himself; believes sufficient evidence to recommend supplements especially for low-mushroom consumers prone to neurological disease.
- **Irwin Cheah (NUS)**: Acknowledges associative evidence but says clinical studies "urgently needed" before recommending for healthy longevity. Notes ET levels not routinely measured and no official deficiency guidelines exist.

## Limitations of This Source

1. **Health journalism, not peer-reviewed** -- claims are summaries of other studies, not primary data.
2. **No effect sizes or p-values reported** -- quantitative precision is low.
3. **Key RCT is a pilot study** with only n=19 participants.
4. **Conflict of interest noted** for the skin study (funded by mushroom company).
5. **Causation vs. correlation caveat** is appropriately highlighted but many headline claims imply causation.
6. **No systematic methodology** -- study selection criteria not described.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Supplementation efficacy**: Moderate relevance -- cites key human trials and animal studies but lacks quantitative detail.
- **Human health/longevity**: Moderate -- covers mortality associations, cognitive decline, and safety data.
- **Dosage**: Relevant -- reports 25 mg human trial dose and EFSA safe upper limit of 30 mg/day.
- **Safety profile**: Relevant -- covers GRAS status, EFSA assessment, and minimal side effects in human studies.

## Claims to Verify
- "Americans consume only about 1 mg of ET daily" -- no primary source cited; should verify against dietary survey data.
- "50% less likely to have MCI" from Singapore mushroom study -- this is a large effect; verify against the original paper.
- Swedish study sample size of 3,236 -- verify against original publication.
