# src-003: Is ergothioneine a 'longevity vitamin' limited in the American diet?

**Citation:** Beelman RB, Kalaras MD, Phillips AT, Richie JP Jr. (2020). *Journal of Nutritional Science*, 9, e52. doi:10.1017/jns.2020.44. PMCID: PMC7681161.

**Source quality:** Full-text open-access review/opinion article from Penn State authors. Peer-reviewed. Content is substantive with referenced data throughout.

---

## Core Findings

This paper argues that ergothioneine (ERGO) should be classified as a "longevity vitamin" -- a micronutrient whose insufficiency in the diet does not cause acute disease but may compromise long-term health and reduce life expectancy. The authors synthesize evidence from epidemiological, metabolomic, and nutritional studies to show that (1) ERGO intake is low in the American diet relative to European countries, (2) low ERGO status correlates with increased mortality, cardiovascular disease, neurodegeneration, and frailty, and (3) agricultural practices may further deplete ERGO from the food supply.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Dietary Intake (Cross-Country Comparison)
- Americans consume an estimated **1.1 mg/day** of ERGO vs. up to **4.6 mg/day** in Italy (refs 14, 15).
- These lower intakes coincide with higher prevalence of chronic neurological diseases and lower life expectancies.
- Ecological analysis using WHO 2010 data shows negative association between estimated ERGO consumption and total mortality, and positive association with longevity. **Authors explicitly note these associations cannot be interpreted as causal.**

### Mortality and Cardiovascular Disease
- Longitudinal metabolomic study (Sweden, n > 3200, 21.4-year follow-up): out of 112 baseline metabolites, **plasma ERGO was the most strongly associated with decreased CVD risk and reduced mortality** (Smith et al., 2020, *Heart*).

### Neurodegeneration and Aging
- ERGO blood levels decline with age and decline faster in cognitively impaired individuals (Cheah et al., 2016).
- Blood ERGO levels are lower in Parkinson's Disease patients than age-matched controls (Hatano et al., 2016).
- ERGO crosses the blood-brain barrier; ETT transporter is found in the brain.

### Frailty
- Study of 131 blood metabolites in elderly hospital patients: ERGO was lower in frail vs. non-frail individuals and lower in those with impaired cognition (Kameda et al., 2020, *PNAS*).

### Food Sources
- Mushrooms are the dominant source: white button (630 mg/kg d.w.), oyster (1310 mg/kg d.w.).
- Other foods have far less: oats (1.8 mg/kg d.w.), kidney beans (2.1 mg/kg d.w.), chicken liver (47 mg/kg d.w.).
- Tempeh (fermented soyabean, 201 mg/kg d.w.) is moderately high but rarely consumed in the US.
- In the US, ~97% of mushrooms consumed are Agaricus bisporus (white button), which has lower ERGO than oyster mushrooms.

### Agricultural Practices
- Excessive tillage disrupts mycorrhizal fungal networks in soil that are thought to pass ERGO to plants.
- Regenerative agriculture (minimal tillage, cover crops, reduced chemicals) may maintain healthy soil fungal populations and thus ERGO levels in crops.
- This is presented as a hypothesis requiring further research, not yet supported by direct experimental data.

### Biological Mechanisms
- ERGO is not synthesized by animals/humans -- obtained only through diet.
- Specific transporter (ETT/OCTN1, gene SLC22A4) is evolutionarily conserved across mammals.
- ERGO uptake into erythrocytes occurs within 1 hour of consumption.
- Bruce Ames proposed ERGO as a "longevity vitamin" under his Triage Theory (2018, *PNAS*).

## Methodology

This is a narrative review/opinion article, not a primary study. It synthesizes evidence from published literature (metabolomic studies, cohort studies, food composition analyses) and presents a hypothesis about ERGO deficiency in the American diet. The cross-country mortality comparisons use estimated intake data and WHO mortality statistics -- these are ecological correlations, not causal evidence.

## Limitations

1. **Ecological fallacy risk:** Cross-country comparisons of ERGO intake and mortality are ecological correlations. The authors acknowledge this cannot be interpreted causally.
2. **No original clinical data:** This is a review article; all quantitative data come from other studies.
3. **Agricultural hypothesis is speculative:** The claim that regenerative agriculture increases ERGO in crops is presented without direct experimental evidence.
4. **Estimated intake data:** ERGO intake figures are estimated from food composition data and dietary surveys, not from direct measurement of individual intake.
5. **US-centric framing:** Focus is primarily on the American diet; applicability to other populations varies.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Supplementation effects on human health):** Provides correlative evidence linking low ERGO status to poor health outcomes. No direct supplementation trial data.
- **Q2 (Dietary sources and bioavailability):** Strong source for food composition data (mushroom vs. other food ERGO content) and bioavailability (rapid erythrocyte uptake via ETT).
- **Q3 (Dose-response, optimal intake):** Cross-country intake comparison (1.1 vs 4.6 mg/day) provides ecological evidence but no dose-response data from controlled studies.
- **Q4 (Disease prevention):** Cites key studies linking low ERGO to CVD, neurodegeneration, frailty, and mortality. Does not provide intervention data.
- **Q5 (Longevity/anti-aging):** Directly addresses the "longevity vitamin" concept. Cites the Ames Triage Theory and cross-country mortality correlations.
- **Q6 (Safety/side effects):** Not addressed in this paper.
- **Q7 (Interactions):** Briefly discusses mushroom consumption patterns and how agricultural practices affect food ERGO content, but no supplementation interaction data.

## Claims to Verify

- The cross-country ERGO intake figures (1.1 mg/day US, 4.6 mg/day Italy) are sourced from secondary analyses (refs 14, 15), not from direct measurement. These are widely cited but their methodology should be checked in the original sources.
- The statement that "97% of all mushrooms produced [in the US]" are Agaricus bisporus should be verified against USDA agricultural statistics.
- The Smith et al. (2020) finding that ERGO was "the most strongly associated" metabolite out of 112 tested with CVD/mortality should be verified in the original paper for the exact statistical measure used (hazard ratio, p-value, etc.).
