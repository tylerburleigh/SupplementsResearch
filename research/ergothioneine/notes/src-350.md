# src-350: Ergothioneine, Recent Developments (Cheah & Halliwell, 2021)

**Citation:** Cheah IK, Halliwell B. Ergothioneine, recent developments. *Redox Biology*. 2021;42:101868. DOI: 10.1016/j.redox.2021.101868

**Source quality:** Full paper text available (Journal Pre-proof). Substantive review article covering transport, physiology, disease associations, and therapeutic potential.

---

## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive narrative review by two leading ergothioneine (ET) researchers covering advances since their prior 2012 review. The central thesis is that ET -- a unique dietary thiol/thione with a dedicated transporter (OCTN1/ETT) -- plays important physiological roles in human health, and declining blood levels with age and disease suggest deficiency may elevate disease risk. The authors argue ET should be considered a "longevity vitamin" (per Ames' classification) and call for randomized controlled trials to establish causation.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Transporter Biology (OCTN1/ETT)
- OCTN1 (encoded by slc22a4) is primarily an ET transporter, reaffirmed using natively expressed and over-expressed OCTN1 in human cells. Proposal to rename it ETT (ergothioneine transporter).
- OCTN1 knockout mice excreted **>50%** of IV-administered ET in urine vs **<10%** in wildtype, demonstrating avid body retention.
- An alternative transporter SLC22A15 can transport ET with lower efficiency than OCTN1; may be important for brain deposition, but not dietary uptake.
- OCTN1 polymorphisms affect ET transport kinetics; the L503F variant has up to 50% greater transport efficiency and 3-fold greater affinity for ET.
- ET is found in all tissues of lab rats and mice despite only trace levels in their diet, indicating efficient uptake and retention.

### Blood Levels, Aging, and Disease
- Blood ET increases from infancy, peaks at early adulthood, then gradually declines with age (studies in Saudi males, elderly Asian populations, Australian 55-85 year olds).
- Lower blood ET associated with: **Parkinson disease**, **mild cognitive impairment**, **Crohn's disease**, **frailty**, and **vascular dementia** (unpublished data).
- Higher blood ET correlated with: lower risk of **cardiometabolic disorders and mortality** (longitudinal study, **3,236 subjects over 20 years**; out of 112 metabolites, ET had the most significant protective association, independent of age, gender, glucose, cholesterol, triglycerides, alcohol, smoking), and lower prevalence of **peripheral neuropathy**.
- A study of 131 blood metabolites identified declining ET as correlating with frailty markers.
- Blood ET levels correlated with self-reported mushroom consumption in an elderly Singaporean population, but no significant trend between age and mushroom consumption, suggesting the decline is not purely dietary.
- **Causation vs. consequence remains unresolved** -- long-term studies needed.

### Supplementation and Human Trials
- Oral ET administration to young healthy males (21-29 years) showed uptake and retention, but **no significant decrease in oxidative damage biomarkers** (basal levels already low). Trend toward lower biomarkers relative to placebo.
- ET in erythrocytes is **>50 times higher than plasma**; whole blood levels continued increasing for up to 4 weeks after cessation of administration, consistent with bone marrow reservoir hypothesis.
- Subjects with higher basal blood ET had greater uptake and accumulation following oral administration (possibly transporter polymorphism effect).

### Neuroprotection and Neurogenesis
- ET promotes neuronal stem cell differentiation via S6K1 and neurotrophin 4/5-TrkB signalling.
- Mice administered ET for 14 days showed dose-dependent increases in hippocampal ET, with enhanced memory and learning and increased neuronal spines in dentate gyrus at doses **>20 mg/kg**.
- Antidepressant-like effects in mouse and rat models of depression; improved sleep abnormalities and social avoidance behaviors.
- ET decreased beta-amyloid accumulation in hippocampus and lipid peroxidation in D-galactose mouse model.
- Silencing OCTN1 in neuroblastoma cells decreased proliferation/differentiation, upregulated oxidative stress markers.

### Tissue Protection and Fibrosis
- Multiple animal and cell models demonstrate ET protects against liver, kidney, and lung injury and fibrosis.
- OCTN1 knockout mice had exacerbated indoxyl sulfate-induced kidney and DMN-induced liver injury, with aggravated oxidative damage, inflammation, and fibrosis.
- ET protected against cigarette smoke-induced lung damage over 6 months; effects more severe in OCTN1 knockout mice.

### Cardiovascular Health
- Longitudinal study (3,236 subjects, 20 years): higher blood ET was the most significant metabolite associated with lower cardiometabolic disease and mortality risk.
- ET protects endothelial cells from oxidative damage by paraquat, H2O2, high glucose, and oxidized LDL.
- ET inhibited proinflammatory cytokine induction of VCAM-1, ICAM-1, and E-selectin.

### Reproductive Health
- High ET levels in seminal plasma of animals and humans (much greater than GSH and cysteine).
- In Wistar rats, oral ET supplementation for 14 days before cisplatin abolished testicular damage; ET alone increased sperm counts and motility, decreased abnormalities, significantly decreased basal malondialdehyde.
- 4-fold upregulation of OCTN1 in lactating vs. non-lactating human mammary epithelial cells; ET present in human breast milk.

### Ocular Health
- High ET in ocular tissues (lens, retina, cornea, retinal pigmented epithelium); some of the highest OCTN1 expression found in the eye.
- ET levels in human eye lens decreased significantly with increasing severity of cataract formation.

### COVID-19
- Authors hypothesize ET could alleviate COVID-19 symptoms due to protection against acute respiratory distress, excessive oxidative damage, inflammation, and endothelial dysfunction. No clinical data presented.

### Regulatory Status
- **EFSA approved ET** as supplement for pregnant/breastfeeding women, children, and infants.
- **US FDA GRAS status** as food additive and supplement.

### Dietary Sources and Biosynthesis
- Highest dietary levels in certain mushrooms and Spirulina.
- Mushrooms contain many other beneficial compounds (beta-glucans, polysaccharides); health benefits cannot be solely attributed to ET.
- Whether gut microbiota produce ET remains unresolved; authors' unpublished isotopically labelled histidine studies did not reveal ET biosynthesis by L. reuteri.

## Methodology

- Narrative review (not systematic review or meta-analysis). 6,466 words.
- 177 references cited.
- Covers literature through late 2020 / early 2021.
- Includes the authors' own published and unpublished data.
- No explicit search strategy or inclusion/exclusion criteria stated.

## Limitations

1. **Narrative review** -- not systematic; selection bias possible in cited studies.
2. **Causation unestablished** -- repeatedly emphasized that whether low ET causes or results from disease is unknown.
3. **Most therapeutic evidence from animal/cell models** -- authors explicitly note many antioxidants that showed promise in animals failed in human neurological trials.
4. **Limited human clinical trial data** -- only one small human supplementation study in young healthy males (21-29 years) showing no significant effect on oxidative biomarkers.
5. **Several findings reported as unpublished data** -- cannot be independently verified.
6. **No dose-response data for human supplementation.**

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (ET mechanisms of action):** Covers OCTN1 transporter biology, antioxidant properties, neurogenesis signaling (S6K1, TrkB), immunoregulation via toll-like receptor signalling in macrophages, mitochondrial protection, and xenobiotic detoxification pathways.
- **Q2 (ET and aging/longevity):** Directly addresses blood ET decline with age and disease, "longevity vitamin" classification, and frailty association. Strong coverage but observational evidence only.
- **Q3 (Supplementation evidence):** Reports the single human trial in young males (no significant effect on oxidative biomarkers); most evidence from animal models. Highlights gap in clinical trial data.
- **Q4 (Cognitive/neurological benefits):** Extensive coverage of neuroprotection, neurogenesis, memory enhancement, antidepressant effects in animal models, and associations with PD, MCI, and cognitive decline.
- **Q5 (Cardiovascular/metabolic):** Key longitudinal study (3,236 subjects, 20 years) with ET as strongest protective metabolite; endothelial protection mechanisms.
- **Q7 (Safety/regulatory):** EFSA and FDA GRAS approval documented.

## Claims to Verify

- The 3,236-subject, 20-year longitudinal study (ref [23]) is a key quantitative claim. The review states "out of 112 metabolites" ET had "the most significant association." The primary source should be checked for exact hazard ratios, confidence intervals, and whether the result survived multiple-testing correction.
- The claim that ET in erythrocytes is "more than 50 times higher than plasma" should be verified from the primary source (ref [4]).
- Several findings are described as "unpublished data" from the authors' laboratory, including the Drosophila PD model, mother-child blood ET correlation, vascular dementia association, and anthracycline cardiotoxicity protection. These cannot be cited as established evidence.
