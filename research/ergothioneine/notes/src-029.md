# src-029: The biology of ergothioneine, an antioxidant nutraceutical

**Citation:** Borodina I, Kenny LC, McCarthy CM, et al. (2020). Nutrition Research Reviews, 33, 190-217. doi:10.1017/S0954422419000301. Cited 238 times.

## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive review of ergothioneine (ERG) biology covering its biosynthesis, transport, antioxidant mechanisms, and potential health benefits. The authors argue that ERG qualifies as an important nutraceutical with possible vitamin status, based on the evolutionary conservation of its dedicated transporter SLC22A4 across vertebrates and its broad cytoprotective effects. Key finding: in a large Swedish cohort (n=3,236), serum ERG was the metabolite most strongly associated with reduced cardiovascular and overall mortality.

## Key Evidence

### Transport and Uptake
- SLC22A4 (OCTN1) is the dedicated, highly selective ERG transporter in humans
- Uptake rate ~100x faster than for tetraethylammonium (previous presumed substrate)
- Concentrative transporter, coupled to 2-3 Na+ influx per ERG molecule
- Tissue expression varies >4,000-fold across cell lines; ERG accumulates in tissues prone to oxidative stress (erythrocytes, bone marrow, liver, kidney, seminal fluid, lens/cornea)
- SLC22A4 homologues exist only in vertebrate animals (mammals, birds, fish)

### Antioxidant Mechanisms
- ERG reacts virtually instantaneously with hydroxyl radicals; more slowly with H2O2 and superoxide
- Also scavenges singlet oxygen, ozone, hypochlorite, and peroxynitrite
- Metal chelation (Cu++, Hg++, Zn++, Co++) prevents metal-catalyzed oxidative damage
- Thione tautomer predominates at physiological pH, making it resistant to autoxidation (unlike glutathione)
- Midpoint potential +0.06V vs -0.2 to -0.4V for typical thiols

### Health Outcomes -- Key Studies
1. **Swedish cohort (n=3,236):** Serum ERG associated with lower coronary disease (HR=0.85, P=0.01), CV mortality (HR=0.79, P=0.002), and all-cause mortality (HR=0.86, P=4x10^-5)
2. **Cognitive function:** 1.5 mushroom servings/week halved mild cognitive impairment incidence; 9 servings/week associated with 5-fold decrease
3. **SLC22A4-/- knockout mice:** Immeasurably low ERG levels; much more sensitive to oxidative stress
4. **Human pharmacokinetics (5-25 mg/day, 7 days):** <4% urinary excretion; main metabolites hercynine and S-methyl-ERG; 8-iso-PGF2alpha (oxidative stress marker) lowered
5. **SLC22A4 polymorphisms** associated with ischaemic stroke, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, Crohn's disease, type 1 diabetes, hearing loss

### Serum Concentrations
- Healthy adults: 23-38 ug/ml (varies by age)
- Crohn's disease: 7 ug/ml (markedly reduced)
- Healthy children (1-10 years): 15-20 ug/ml
- Decreases with age

### Dietary Sources
- Mushrooms are the primary human source
- Oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus spp.): up to 4 mg/g DM; P. citrinopileatus: 10.65 mg/g DM
- Porcini (Boletus edulis): >7 mg/g DM
- Common button mushrooms (A. bisporus): ~0.4 mg/g DM
- ERG proposed as nutritional biomarker for mushroom consumption

### Safety
- Declared safe by European Food Standards Agency
- No detectable mutagenicity or genotoxicity at very high doses
- GRAS status for producer organisms

## Methodology

This is a narrative literature review. The authors surveyed ~600 references spanning ERG biosynthesis, transport biology, antioxidant chemistry, nutritional sources, health outcomes, and safety. The review is structured thematically rather than following PRISMA-type systematic review methodology. Evidence ranges from in vitro biochemical assays and animal models to human observational studies and one small human pharmacokinetic trial.

## Limitations

1. **Narrative review, not systematic:** No formal search strategy, inclusion/exclusion criteria, or quality assessment of included studies
2. **Circumstantial evidence acknowledged:** Authors explicitly state "the evidence that ERG may be a useful nutraceutical... is mostly circumstantial rather than definitive"
3. **Missing mechanistic detail:** Authors note "we lack examples in which ERG is found both to be low in individuals with a particular syndrome and where exogenous administration effects functional improvements"
4. **Cognitive data from mushroom consumption, not pure ERG:** The cognitive benefits are attributed to ERG but confounded by other mushroom constituents
5. **No RCTs with pure ERG available at time of publication:** Authors note one trial was registered but not yet completed
6. **Conflict of interest:** Three authors are named inventors on a patent for biotechnological ERG production in yeast
7. **Old deficiency experiments:** The animal ERG-depletion studies cited are acknowledged as old and needing repetition with modern techniques

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Ergothioneine supplementation benefits):** Directly relevant -- comprehensive review of ERG's antioxidant properties, tissue distribution, and cytoprotective effects. Provides strong mechanistic rationale for supplementation.
- **Q2 (Human health outcomes):** Highly relevant -- reports mortality data from Swedish cohort, cognitive function studies, and associations with multiple chronic diseases.
- **Q3 (Optimal dosing/regimen):** Partially relevant -- reports human pharmacokinetics at 5-25 mg/day for 7 days showing low excretion and oxidative marker reduction, but no dose-response optimization data.
- **Q4 (Longevity/aging):** Relevant -- notes inverse correlation of ERG with age, association with reduced all-cause mortality, and protection against glucose-induced senescence via SIRT1/SIRT6.
- **Q5 (Comparison with other antioxidants):** Relevant -- discusses ERG's superiority to ascorbate in not acting as pro-oxidant, and its resistance to autoxidation vs glutathione.
- **Q6 (Safety/side effects):** Relevant -- reviews safety data including EFSA approval and lack of mutagenicity.
- **Q7 (Deficiency states):** Relevant -- reviews evidence that ERG levels are decreased in Crohn's disease, certain leukemias, and other conditions, though acknowledges no definitive deficiency syndrome established.

## Claims to Verify

- The Swedish cohort study (n=3,236) mortality hazard ratios are cited from reference 509 but the study details are not elaborated in this review.
- The cognitive impairment reduction (50% at 1.5 servings/week, 5-fold at 9 servings/week) is attributed to reference 348 but comes from mushroom consumption, not pure ERG supplementation.
- The assertion that ERG reacts "virtually instantaneously" with hydroxyl radicals lacks a specific rate constant.
