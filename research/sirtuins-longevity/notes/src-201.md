# src-201: It Takes Two to Tango: NAD+ and Sirtuins in Aging/Longevity Control

**Authors:** Shin-ichiro Imai, Leonard Guarente
**Year:** 2016
**Venue:** npj Aging and Mechanisms of Disease
**Citations:** 350
**DOI:** 10.1038/npjamd.2016.17
**Type:** Review article

## Core Findings

This review by two of the most prominent sirtuin researchers (Imai and Guarente) argues that the intimate connection between NAD+ and sirtuins is an evolutionarily conserved mechanism for aging/longevity control. The authors contend that past controversies questioning sirtuins' role in lifespan extension have been resolved by recent confirmatory studies, and propose that age-related NAD+ decline (via reduced NAMPT-mediated biosynthesis and chronic PARP activation) drives sirtuin activity loss, disrupting both nuclear-mitochondrial communication at the cellular level and hypothalamus-adipose tissue communication at the systemic level.

## Key Evidence

### Lifespan Extension in Model Organisms
- Brain-specific SIRT1-overexpressing (BRASTO) mice show significant lifespan extension in both sexes, while whole-body SIRT1-overexpressing mice do not (lines 106-111). This implicates hypothalamic SIRT1 as the critical site.
- Whole-body SIRT6 transgenic mice show lifespan extension only in males (lines 112-114).
- In yeast, CR-mediated lifespan extension (0.5% glucose) is Sir2/Npt1-dependent; NR supplementation extends lifespan through Sir-2.1-dependent pathways in worms (lines 118-142).

### NAD+ Decline with Aging
- NAMPT and NAD+ levels decline with age in multiple organs: pancreas, adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, liver, and brain (lines 230-232).
- Two mechanisms drive decline: reduced NAMPT-mediated biosynthesis and chronic PARP activation causing NAD+ depletion (lines 321-327, 426-429).
- NAD+ decline reduces sirtuin activity, causing pseudohypoxic state via HIF-1alpha stabilization, defective mitochondrial gene expression, and reduced PGC-1alpha/FOXO1 function (lines 333-344).

### NAD+ Supplementation as Strategy
- NMN and NR supplementation proposed as therapeutic interventions against age-associated diseases including type 2 diabetes, Alzheimer's, heart failure, and hearing loss (lines 277-281).
- NAD+ intermediates boost sirtuin activity indirectly by increasing substrate availability.

### Three-Level Regulatory Framework
The NAD+/sirtuin connection is regulated at: (1) NAD+ biosynthesis regulation, (2) modulation by NAD+ derivatives (nicotinamide as non-competitive inhibitor; long-chain fatty acids enhance SIRT6 up to 35-fold), (3) competition between sirtuins, PARPs, and CD38 for NAD+ (lines 224-228, 284-327).

### Systemic Communication (NAD World)
- eNAMPT secreted by adipose tissue remotely controls hypothalamic NAD+ biosynthesis and SIRT1 activity (lines 183-204).
- SIRT1/Nkx2-1 pathway in DMH regulates aging via Prdm13, with DMH-specific Prdm13-knockdown causing decreased sleep quality and increased adiposity (lines 390-398).

## Methodology

- Narrative review synthesizing genetic, pharmacological, and pathophysiological studies across yeast, worms, flies, and mammals.
- No new primary data; interprets published literature through the lens of the "NAD World" conceptual framework (Imai's model).
- Draws on both the authors' own studies and the broader field.

## Limitations

- Both authors have significant competing interests: Imai is co-founder of Metro Midwest Biotech; Guarente is founder of Elysium Health and consults for GSK, Chronos, and Segterra (lines 450-452). These commercial interests in NAD+ precursors and sirtuin activators may bias interpretation.
- The review characterizes the sirtuin controversy as "put to rest" (line 103), but this is the authors' editorial framing -- other researchers (notably those who published the contradictory Burnett et al. 2011 and Herranz et al. 2010 studies) may disagree.
- Claims about NMN/NR therapeutic efficacy are forward-looking and based primarily on animal studies, not human clinical data.
- The NAD World framework (hypothalamus-adipose axis) is largely based on the Imai lab's own work and may not yet be independently validated.
- No human lifespan data is presented; all lifespan evidence is from model organisms.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (Sirtuin activation and lifespan/healthspan):** Strongly relevant. Provides a comprehensive review of sirtuin-mediated lifespan extension across organisms. Key data point: brain-specific SIRT1 overexpression extends lifespan in both sexes of mice, while whole-body does not. SIRT6 extends lifespan only in males. No human lifespan data.
- **Q3 (Direct sirtuin activation vs indirect NAD+ boosting):** Central to this paper. The entire thesis is that NAD+ boosting (via NMN, NR) is the preferred strategy because it addresses the root cause (age-related NAD+ decline) and feeds endogenous sirtuin activity rather than requiring direct sirtuin activation. Discusses the three-level regulatory framework and NAD+ competition with PARPs/CD38.
- **Q6 (Researcher disagreements):** Directly relevant. Both authors are central figures in the sirtuin field. The paper explicitly addresses the Burnett et al. and Herranz et al. contradictory results and argues the controversy is resolved. Their framing represents the pro-sirtuin position in the ongoing debate.

## Claims to Verify

- "BRASTO mice exhibit significant delay in aging and lifespan extension in both male and female mice" -- verify against Satoh et al. 2013 (ref 27) whether the lifespan extension was truly significant in both sexes and what magnitude was observed.
- "Whole-body SIRT1-overexpressing transgenic mice fail to show lifespan extension" -- verify Herranz et al. 2010 (ref 22) for exact findings; note this is cited as evidence of the controversy but may have shown healthspan benefits even without lifespan extension.
- "SIRT6 transgenic mice show lifespan extension, although only males exhibit the phenotype" -- verify Kanfi et al. 2012 (ref 24) for effect size and significance level.
