# Source src-017: Bonkowski & Sinclair (2016) - "Slowing ageing by design: the rise of NAD+ and sirtuin-activating compounds"

**Citation:** Bonkowski MS, Sinclair DA. Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol. 2016 Nov;17(11):679-690. doi:10.1038/nrm.2016.93. PMCID: PMC5107309.

**Type:** Peer-reviewed review article (Nature Reviews Molecular Cell Biology)
**Authors' affiliation:** Harvard Medical School Dept of Genetics; UNSW Dept of Pharmacology
**Competing interests declared:** Yes (details in web version)

## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive 2016 review from David Sinclair's group surveying sirtuin biology and the development of sirtuin-activating compounds (STACs) and NAD+ boosters as potential longevity therapeutics. The authors argue that the field has reached consensus that sirtuins underlie aspects of calorie restriction and are key regulators of ageing, after years of controversy about whether STACs really activate SIRT1 and whether sirtuins extend lifespan. The review provides a detailed table of sirtuin effects across species (yeast, worms, flies, fish, mice) and a comprehensive table of clinical trials of STACs (resveratrol, SRT2104, NR) through 2016.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Sirtuin overexpression and lifespan (Q1)
- **Sirt1 transgenic mice** (high overexpression): recapitulate calorie restriction benefits (metabolic activity, reduced blood lipids, improved glucose metabolism) but **do NOT live longer** (line ~309-312).
- **Sirt1 moderate overexpression**: protected from inflammation, liver cancer, diabetes, hepatic steatosis, but **does NOT live longer** (line ~314-319).
- **Brain-specific Sirt1 (BRASTO)**: 9-16% longer mean lifespan depending on sex, significant increase in maximal longevity (line ~320-322).
- **Sirt6 ubiquitous overexpression in males**: ~15% longer lifespan; effect NOT observed in females (line ~323-327).
- **Resveratrol lifespan extension in model organisms**: yeast (30-58%), worms (~10-65%), flies (8-29%), fish (27-59%), honeybees (33-38%). In mice, resveratrol extends lifespan primarily on high-fat diets (TABLE 1, line ~580-597).
- **Synthetic STACs in mice**: SRT1720 extends lifespan ~8-22%; SRT2104 extends male mouse lifespan ~10% (line ~582-586).

### Clinical trials of STACs (Q2, Q5)
- **Resveratrol human trials**: At least 6 clinical studies through 2016. Majority observed improvements in glucose metabolism and cardiovascular indicators. A randomized double-blind crossover study in healthy obese men (150 mg/day, 30 days) showed reduced resting metabolic rate, reduced systolic blood pressure, improved HOMA index, increased SIRT1 and PGC1a expression, increased AMPK activity and mitochondrial respiration. However, one study in non-obese men found no measurable improvements. A phase II Alzheimer's study showed resveratrol may delay cognitive decline in daily tasks (line ~879-897).
- **SRT2104**: Advanced through multiple phase I trials with few side effects. Phase II trials in elderly volunteers and healthy smokers showed slight weight reduction, 15-30% cholesterol ratio improvement, 19% triglyceride decrease. Psoriasis trial showed significant disease reduction after 84 days (line ~1112-1126).
- **NR clinical trials**: Listed as in phase I (pharmacokinetics) and phase II (cardiovascular, diabetes) (TABLE 2, line ~1057-1079).

### Direct STACs vs NAD+ boosters (Q3)
- **Direct STACs** (resveratrol, SRT1720, SRT2104): primarily activate SIRT1 via allosteric mechanism (binding to STAC-binding domain, lowering Km for substrate). Now in 5th generation with >1000-fold greater in vitro potency than resveratrol.
- **NAD+ boosters** (NMN, NR, CD38 inhibitors, NAMPT activators): can potentially activate ALL seven sirtuins. NR supplementation in 24-month-old mice yielded ~5% lifespan increase. Effects include improved glucose metabolism, mitochondrial function, recovery from heart/ear/eye injury.
- Key tradeoff noted: resveratrol is nonspecific (targets AMPK, phoshodiesterases, F1-ATPase, complex III, PARP1, Tyr-tRNA synthetase in addition to SIRT1). Synthetic STACs are more specific. NAD+ boosters act upstream and affect all sirtuins but also other NAD-dependent enzymes.

### Controversy and criticism (Q4, Q6)
- The STAC mechanism was disputed for years: critics argued resveratrol and synthetic STACs only appeared to activate SIRT1 due to fluorescent artifacts (line ~714-720). The E230K mutation experiment resolved this by showing STACs fail to induce changes in cells expressing the mutant SIRT1, providing evidence of direct activation.
- Earlier debates about whether sirtuins extend lifespan in C. elegans and D. melanogaster were noted as "finally settled" with multiple independent labs now reproducing lifespan extension (line ~130, ~185-193).
- The review is written from Sinclair's perspective (a leading proponent), which should be noted as a potential bias -- the authors declare competing interests.

### Safety profile
- NAD+ boosters (NMN, NR) administered to rodents at 100-1000 mg/kg/day for >6 months with no apparent adverse side effects (line ~1171-1178).
- SRT2104 phase I trials showed few if any side effects (line ~1114-1115).
- Resveratrol efficacy is variable in humans, possibly due to patient selection (greatest benefit in elderly/insulin-resistant), formulation, and bioavailability issues.

## Methodology

This is a narrative review, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. The authors cite >180 references spanning yeast, worm, fly, fish, mouse, primate, and human studies. TABLE 1 provides a species-by-species cross-reference of sirtuin effects on lifespan, CR mediation, and stress pathways. TABLE 2 catalogs clinical trials of STACs (resveratrol, SRT2104, NR) with ClinicalTrials.gov identifiers, organized by trial phase and disease indication.

## Limitations

1. **Author perspective bias**: Sinclair is a leading proponent of sirtuin-longevity hypothesis and a founder of companies developing sirtuin activators. Competing interests are declared.
2. **Selective citation**: As a narrative review from proponents, it may understate negative findings or give disproportionate weight to positive results.
3. **Date cutoff (2016)**: Does not cover more recent developments in sirtuin research, NAD+ trials, or criticisms published after 2016.
4. **No lifespan data in humans**: All lifespan extension data are from model organisms. Human data are limited to disease biomarkers and functional outcomes.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (sirtuin activation and lifespan)**: HIGH. Comprehensive cross-species data on sirtuin overexpression and STAC effects on lifespan, including detailed mouse data with caveats (not all Sirt1 overexpression extends lifespan).
- **Q2 (pharmacological compounds in clinical trials)**: HIGH. TABLE 2 is a detailed catalog of clinical trials through 2016 with NCT identifiers for resveratrol, SRT2104, and NR.
- **Q3 (direct STACs vs indirect NAD+ boosting)**: HIGH. Direct comparison of mechanisms and tradeoffs between direct SIRT1 activators and NAD+ precursors.
- **Q4 (arguments against)**: MODERATE. Discusses the STAC artifact controversy and how it was resolved from the proponent perspective, but does not deeply engage with the strongest skeptic arguments.
- **Q5 (NAD+ precursor functional outcomes)**: MODERATE-HIGH. Covers NR and NMN effects in rodents and early clinical trials.
- **Q6 (researcher disagreements)**: MODERATE. Primarily presents the pro-sirtuin consensus view but acknowledges past controversies and their resolution.

## Claims to Verify

- The "consensus" statement (line 1232) that sirtuins underlie aspects of calorie restriction should be cross-checked against skeptic viewpoints (e.g., Brian Kennedy's position, the initial failed replication attempts in worms/flies).
- The 5% lifespan increase from NR in 24-month-old mice (line ~1187-1188) is attributed to a single study; this should be verified for whether it has been independently replicated.
- The BRASTO mouse 9-16% lifespan increase (line ~321-322) is from a specific brain-specific Sirt1 model; the sex-dependent differences warrant closer inspection of the original study.
