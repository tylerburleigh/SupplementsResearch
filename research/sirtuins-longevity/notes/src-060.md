# src-060: The sirtuin family in health and disease

**Citation:** Wu et al. (2022), *Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy*, 7:402. DOI: 10.1038/s41392-022-01257-8. 695 citations.

**Source type:** Comprehensive review article (17 authors, Shengjing Hospital of China Medical University). Covers all seven sirtuins across diseases, pharmacology, and clinical trials.

**Quality:** ok. Full-text PDF extracted with raw text fallback; headings partially degraded but content is substantive throughout.

---

## Core Findings

1. This review provides an encyclopedic survey of all seven mammalian sirtuins (SIRT1-7) in health and disease, cataloging their roles in cancer, cardiovascular disease, metabolic disease, neurodegeneration, respiratory disease, and musculoskeletal conditions. It is primarily a mechanistic/descriptive review rather than a hypothesis-testing paper.

2. The most valuable contribution for the research questions is the systematic compilation of 63 published RCTs involving sirtuin-related interventions (searched PubMed up to June 2022 plus clinical trial registries). Of these, 20 studies directly tested SIRT activators (resveratrol, SRT2104) or inhibitors (nicotinamide) on physiological function.

3. The review candidly acknowledges that the anti-aging role of sirtuins remains "under debate and has not been fully defined," and that clinical trials to verify therapeutic potential are still lacking.

---

## Key Evidence by Research Question

### Q1: Evidence sirtuin activation causally extends lifespan/healthspan in humans vs model organisms

- The review cites the foundational yeast work: additional copy of Sir2 prolonged yeast lifespan by ~40%; loss of Sir2 shortened it (lines 50-53). Sir2 role in C. elegans senescence also noted (line 60).
- The aging section (lines 5054-5114) discusses SIRT roles in cellular senescence, genomic stability, nutrient sensing, and circadian rhythms as aging-relevant mechanisms, but all evidence is from cell culture and animal models.
- No human lifespan or healthspan extension data is presented. The authors state SIRTs "might be attractive anti-aging molecules involved in improving health, although it is still under debate and has not been fully defined" (lines 5108-5112).

### Q2: Pharmacological sirtuin compounds in human clinical trials

This is the strongest section of the review for the research questions. Key findings:

**Resveratrol (10 RCTs):**
- 500 mg/day in T2D+CHD patients: upregulated PPAR-gamma and SIRT1 in PBMCs (IRCT20181029041490N1)
- 500 mg/day in healthy subjects: stimulated SIRT1 serum concentrations alongside caloric restriction (NCT01668836)
- 500 mg/day in T2D: increased SIRT1 expression with reduced H3K56ac (NCT02244879)
- Resveratrol in Alzheimer's (500-2000 mg/day, 52 weeks): decreased CSF MMP9, modulated neuroinflammation (NCT01504854)
- **Negative results:** No effect on SIRT1 in COPD patients (NCT02245932); no effect on molecular targets in postmenopausal women (NCT00823381); no detectable physiological effects in obese subjects (NCT01150955)

**SRT2104 (7 RCTs):**
- Phase I in T2D (2.0 g/day, 28 days): well tolerated, neutral effects on endothelial function (NCT01031108)
- Phase I in healthy smokers and T2D: improved arterial compliance, reduced augmentation pressure (NCT01031108, 2016)
- Phase I in healthy volunteers: improved serum lipid profile (reduced TC, LDL-C, TG) (NCT01031108, 2013)
- Phase II in T2D: no improvement in glucose or insulin control but improved lipid profiles (EudraCT 2009-010720-26)
- Phase IIa in psoriasis: substantial improvement in 9 subjects, but no dose-response (NCT01154101)
- In UC patients: no significant clinical activity (NCT01453491)
- Reduced LPS-induced cytokine release (IL-6, IL-8) in healthy volunteers (NCT01014117)

**Nicotinamide/NR (3 RCTs):**
- All three RCTs suggested nicotinamide/NR did not act through its putative SIRT target. NR supplementation did not affect SIRT activity in skeletal muscle; 12 months of nicotinamide showed no sustained SIRT inhibition.

### Q3: Tradeoffs between direct sirtuin activation vs indirect NAD+ boosting

- The review distinguishes NAD+ precursors (NR, NMN) from direct SIRT activators but does not deeply analyze tradeoffs between these strategies.
- NR and NMN are described as affecting SIRT activity "mainly by affecting the synthesis of NAD" (line 5227), with NMN production being the rate-limiting factor in mammalian NAD biosynthesis.
- Critically, nicotinamide (the end product of SIRT catalysis) is itself an endogenous SIRT inhibitor (IC50 ~120 uM for SIRT1, ~100 uM for SIRT2), creating a feedback paradox for NAD+ boosting strategies.
- Direct SIRT1 activators (SRT compounds) are far more potent than resveratrol (SRT1720 EC1.5 = 0.16 uM vs resveratrol EC1.5 = 46.2 uM) but most remain in early clinical testing.
- SIRT6 activators (UBCS039, MDL-800) are even earlier stage with limited preclinical data.

### Q4: Arguments against sirtuin-longevity hypothesis

- The review itself acknowledges the debate: "it is still under debate and has not been fully deﬁned" (line 5111-5112).
- Several RCTs showed negative results: resveratrol did not affect molecular targets in multiple studies; SRT2104 showed no glucose control improvement; nicotinamide/NR showed no SIRT activity effects in vivo.
- The review notes that SIRT3 has "dual roles" in osteoarthritis (both protective and promoting progression), illustrating that sirtuin effects are not uniformly beneficial.
- Clinical trials have "relatively small number of trials, study limitations and single study sites" (line 6452-6454).

## Methodology

- Systematic search of PubMed (up to June 23, 2022) plus clinical trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov, ISRCTN, EU Clinical Trials Register, Iranian Registry).
- 63 RCTs included; duplicate independent reviewer screening.
- Review covers molecular mechanisms across all seven SIRT family members through literature synthesis.
- PDF conversion used raw text extraction fallback; layout and headings partially degraded.

---

## Limitations

1. The review is broad but shallow on any single topic -- it covers 7 sirtuins across ~15 disease areas.
2. No meta-analysis or quantitative synthesis of RCT results; individual trial findings are reported descriptively.
3. The NAD+ boosting vs direct activation comparison is implicit rather than systematically analyzed.
4. No critical evaluation of the controversy around resveratrol as a genuine direct SIRT1 activator (the debate about fluorescence assay artifacts is not discussed).
5. Publication bias not assessed for the RCT compilation.

---

## Claims to Verify

- "63 published RCTs" -- this is a stated count from systematic search. The tables list far fewer entries explicitly, with many in Table 1 and 2. The total should be verifiable by counting entries.
- Sample sizes in individual RCTs are taken directly from trial registrations and appear plausible (mostly 10-50 per arm).
- EC1.5 values for SRT compounds are cited from reference 983 (Milne et al. 2007), the original Sirtris papers, which are reliable but have been debated regarding whether SRT compounds activate SIRT1 on physiological substrates.

---

## Relevance Assessment

This is a **highly relevant** source for Q2 (pharmacological compounds in clinical trials) as it provides the most comprehensive compilation of sirtuin-related RCTs available. It is moderately relevant for Q1 (reviews model organism lifespan data but has no human lifespan evidence), Q3 (documents NAD+ precursor mechanisms but does not systematically compare strategies), and Q4 (acknowledges debate but is not primarily a critique).
