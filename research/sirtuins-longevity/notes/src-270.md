## Core Findings (from abstract only)

1. Sirtuins are NAD+-dependent protein deacetylases found across all phyla of life, coupling protein deacetylation to NAD+ consumption and producing the novel metabolite 2'-O-acetyl-ADP-ribose.
2. In yeast, sirtuins regulate gene silencing through histone deacetylation; in multicellular organisms they deacetylate both histones and transcription factors controlling stress, metabolism, and survival pathways.
3. Accumulating evidence (as of 2006) positioned sirtuins as important regulators of organism life span.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (sirtuin activation and lifespan):** Moderately relevant. The abstract states sirtuins are "important regulators of organism life span" based on yeast and multicellular organism evidence available in 2006. However, this is a 2006 review and lacks any human clinical data. Serves better as historical context for how the sirtuin-longevity hypothesis was formulated.
- **Q3 (direct sirtuin activation vs indirect NAD+ boosting):** Highly relevant mechanistically. The core biochemical mechanism described -- NAD+-dependent deacetylation producing OAADPr -- is the foundation for understanding why NAD+ availability governs sirtuin activity. This review (if full text were available) would detail the structural and mechanistic basis for how NAD+ binding and cleavage drives deacetylation, which is central to the direct vs indirect activation tradeoff question.
