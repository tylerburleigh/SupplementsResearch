# src-032: NMN Dose-Dependent RCT in Healthy Middle-Aged Adults

**Citation:** Yi L, Maier AB, et al. (2023). "The efficacy and safety of beta-nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) supplementation in healthy middle-aged adults." *GeroScience* 45:29-43. DOI: 10.1007/s11357-022-00705-1

**Quality:** ok (full text available, peer-reviewed, 130 citations)

**Trial Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04823260; CTRI/2021/03/032421

---

## Core Findings

In a 60-day randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-dependent trial (n=80 healthy adults aged 40-65), oral NMN supplementation at 300, 600, and 900 mg/day significantly increased blood NAD+ concentrations, improved physical endurance (6-minute walking test), and maintained blood biological age compared to placebo. The 600 mg/day dose appeared to represent a plateau for efficacy, with no significant additional benefit at 900 mg/day. NMN was safe and well tolerated at all doses.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Randomized, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, dose-dependent clinical trial
- **Duration:** 60 days with assessments at baseline, day 30, and day 60
- **Participants:** 80 healthy middle-aged adults (mean age 49.3 +/- 6.8 years, 59% female), 20 per group
- **Sites:** Two clinic centers in Pune, India
- **Intervention:** Oral NMN (AbinoNutra(TM)NMN) at 300, 600, or 900 mg/day vs. placebo (rice flour capsules), once daily before breakfast
- **Primary outcome:** Blood NAD concentration (total NAD+ + NADH in serum, colorimetric method)
- **Secondary outcomes:** Safety/tolerability, 6-minute walking test (endurance), blood biological age (Aging.Ai 3.0), HOMA-IR (insulin resistance), SF-36 (quality of life)
- **Analysis:** Per Protocol analysis; MMRM for repeated measures; paired t-test for biological age; Mann-Whitney U for HOMA-IR
- **Compliance:** 75-125% dosing compliance required; all 80 participants completed without protocol violation

## Key Evidence

### Blood NAD+ Concentration (Primary Outcome)
- All three NMN groups showed statistically significant increases vs. both baseline and placebo at days 30 and 60 (all p <= 0.001)
- 600 mg and 900 mg groups had significantly higher NAD than 300 mg group
- No significant difference between 600 mg and 900 mg groups (plateau)
- Placebo: baseline 8.11 +/- 5.16 nM -> day 60: 11.8 +/- 9.4 nM (not significant)
- 600 mg group: baseline 7.95 +/- 3.29 nM -> day 60: 45.3 +/- 11.8 nM (p < 0.001)
- 900 mg group: baseline 10.5 +/- 6.8 nM -> day 60: 48.5 +/- 19.8 nM (p < 0.001)

### Physical Performance (6-Minute Walking Test)
- All NMN groups significantly increased walking distance vs. placebo at both time points (all p < 0.01)
- 600 mg group: baseline 290 +/- 92 m -> day 60: 435 +/- 104 m (p < 0.001 vs baseline)
- 900 mg group: baseline 323 +/- 113 m -> day 60: 480 +/- 128 m (p < 0.001 vs baseline)
- 600 mg significantly outperformed 300 mg; no difference between 900 mg and 600 mg

### Insulin Sensitivity (HOMA-IR)
- No significant differences between any NMN group and placebo at day 60
- Notably, HOMA-IR increased significantly within the 600 mg (p=0.037) and 900 mg (p=0.006) groups compared to their own baselines, but this was not significant vs. placebo
- The authors note this is consistent with other trials in healthy (non-prediabetic) adults

### Blood Biological Age (Aging.Ai 3.0)
- Placebo group: biological age increased significantly from 39.8 +/- 7.2 to 45.4 +/- 8.2 years (p=0.029) over 60 days
- All NMN groups: no significant change from baseline
- Difference between all NMN groups and placebo: p < 0.05
- Caveat: Aging.Ai 3.0 is a hematological clock based on 19 routine blood parameters; not an epigenetic clock

### Quality of Life (SF-36)
- All NMN groups showed significantly better SF-36 scores vs. placebo at day 60 (p < 0.01)
- At day 30, 600 mg and 900 mg groups were significantly better; 300 mg was not (trend)
- Within-group: significant improvements at day 60 for 300 mg (p=0.003), at both day 30 and 60 for 600 mg and 900 mg

### Safety
- Zero treatment-related adverse events, zero serious AEs, zero dropouts
- 9 total AEs: 6 in 5 placebo participants, 3 in 2 participants in 300 mg group; none in 600 mg or 900 mg groups
- All AEs mild or moderate, none attributed to NMN
- No clinically meaningful abnormalities in lab parameters, vital signs, or physical exams

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size:** Only 20 participants per group; underpowered for some secondary outcomes
2. **Short duration:** 60 days is insufficient to assess long-term safety or chronic disease endpoints
3. **Single ethnicity:** All participants were of Indian ethnicity, limiting generalizability
4. **Funding conflict:** Funded by Aba Chemicals and Abinopharm, the NMN manufacturers; first author is an Abinopharm employee
5. **NAD measurement method:** Used colorimetric assay for total NAD+ + NADH in serum rather than more precise LC-MS/MS methods
6. **Biological age tool:** Aging.Ai 3.0 uses routine blood markers, not validated epigenetic clocks
7. **Baseline imbalance:** The 300 mg group was significantly older than placebo (51.2 vs 46.5 years, p=0.04)
8. **No insulin sensitivity benefit in healthy adults:** HOMA-IR null result consistent with the idea that NMN may only improve insulin sensitivity in metabolically compromised populations

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q2 (Pharmacological sirtuin compounds in clinical trials):** NMN is a NAD+ precursor that activates sirtuins (specifically SIRT1 in skeletal muscle vascular endothelial cells, discussed in the paper). This trial provides human clinical evidence for an indirect sirtuin-activating strategy via NAD+ boosting.
- **Q5 (NAD+ precursor trials on functional outcomes):** Directly relevant. This is one of the most comprehensive dose-dependent NMN trials reporting on physical performance (6MWT), insulin sensitivity (HOMA-IR), vascular function (none directly measured), and quality of life (SF-36). Shows significant benefits in physical performance and quality of life but null result on insulin sensitivity in healthy adults.

## Claims to Verify

- The baseline NAD concentration for the 300 mg group (11.8 +/- 11.7 nM) has an unusually large SD, suggesting possible outlier(s) that could inflate apparent treatment effects. Worth noting that this group was also significantly older at baseline (p=0.04).
- The change in NAD concentration for the 600 mg group (from ~8 nM to ~45 nM) represents an approximately 5.5-fold increase, which seems very large for a 60-day supplementation. The colorimetric method used (total NAD+ + NADH in serum) may produce different values than LC-MS/MS methods used in other trials.
