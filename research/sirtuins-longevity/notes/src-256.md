# src-256: SRT1720, SRT2183, SRT1460, and Resveratrol Are Not Direct Activators of SIRT1

**Authors:** Pacholec M, Bleasdale JE, Chrunyk B, Cunningham D, Flynn D, Garofalo RS, Griffith D, Griffor M, Loulakis P, Pabst B, Qiu X, Stockman B, Thanabal V, Varghese A, Ward J, Withka J, Ahn K

**Venue:** Journal of Biological Chemistry, 285(11):8340-8351, March 12, 2010

**DOI:** 10.1074/jbc.M109.088682

**Affiliation:** Pfizer Global Research and Development, Groton, CT

## Core Findings

This paper provides comprehensive biochemical and biophysical evidence that resveratrol and the Sirtris Pharmaceuticals compounds (SRT1720, SRT2183, SRT1460) are **not direct SIRT1 activators**. The apparent SIRT1 activation observed in prior studies was an artifact of the TAMRA fluorophore covalently attached to the peptide substrate used in screening assays. When native (non-fluorophore-labeled) peptide or full-length protein substrates are used, none of these compounds activate SIRT1. Additionally, SRT1720 failed to improve glucose homeostasis or mitochondrial function in ob/ob mice on a high-fat diet, contradicting prior reports from Sirtris.

## Key Evidence

1. **Fluorophore-dependent activation artifact:** With TAMRA-p53 peptide, all four compounds showed strong activation (SRT1720: 741% max, EC1.5 = 0.32 uM; resveratrol: 239% max, EC1.5 = 31.6 uM). With native p53 peptide lacking TAMRA, no activation was observed (activity within ~15% of DMSO control). SRT1720 actually inhibited SIRT1 by ~40% at 30 uM with native peptide.

2. **Full-length protein substrates confirm no activation:** Using purified native full-length Ac-Lys382-p53 (assessed by ELISA and Western blot) and Ac-AceCS1 (assessed by coupled enzyme/HPLC assay), none of the compounds showed SIRT1 activation at 30 uM. Km values were determined for all substrates to ensure assays were run under optimal activation-detection conditions (substrate concentrations well below Km).

3. **Biophysical evidence of fluorophore binding:** NMR showed SRT1460 causes upfield chemical shift of the acetyl (CH3) resonance in TAMRA-peptides but not in matched native peptides (in the absence of SIRT1). SPR confirmed dose-dependent binding of SRT1720 to TAMRA peptide but not native peptide. ITC showed SRT1460 binds to SIRT1-TAMRA peptide complex (KD = 13 uM) but not to SIRT1-native peptide complex.

4. **In vivo failure of SRT1720:** In ob/ob mice on HFD, SRT1720 at 30 mg/kg/day for 18 days showed no effect on glucose (fed, fasting, or OGTT) despite plasma exposure (Cmax = 1.3 uM) matching previously reported levels for 100 mg/kg. The 100 mg/kg dose was not tolerated (3/8 mice died). No improvement in mitochondrial function markers was observed.

5. **Broad off-target activity:** Against >100 targets (receptors, enzymes, ion channels, transporters) at 10 uM: SRT1720 inhibited >50% of 38 targets, SRT2183 of 14, SRT1460 of 20, and resveratrol of 7 targets. All compounds are highly promiscuous and unsuitable as pharmacological tools.

## Methodology

- Recombinant human SIRT1 (full-length, amino acids 2-747) expressed and purified from E. coli
- HPLC-based direct quantification of deacetylated product for peptide assays
- ELISA and Western blot for full-length p53 substrate
- Coupled enzyme assay (AceCS1 activity -> AMP quantification by HPLC) for full-length AceCS1
- NMR (600 MHz), SPR (Biacore 3000), ITC (Microcal VP-ITC) for biophysical binding studies
- In vivo: ob/ob mice (7-8 weeks), HFD (60% fat), SRT1720 at 30 and 100 mg/kg p.o. for 18 days, n=8 per group
- Selectivity panel: >100 targets at 10 uM

## Limitations

- Single in vivo model (ob/ob mice on HFD); does not rule out efficacy in other models
- Could not fully verify SRT1720 binding by NMR due to solubility limitations
- The 100 mg/kg dose group had high mortality, limiting data interpretation at that dose
- Does not address whether these compounds may have SIRT1-independent beneficial effects in vivo
- Authors acknowledge that in vivo effects of resveratrol may occur through mechanisms independent of direct SIRT1 activation

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q3 (Tradeoffs between direct vs indirect activation; resveratrol's direct SIRT1 activation status):**
Directly relevant. This paper provides strong evidence that resveratrol is NOT a direct SIRT1 activator. The apparent activation in prior studies was an artifact of the fluorophore-labeled substrate. This has profound implications for interpreting all studies that relied on fluorophore-based assays to demonstrate SIRT1 activation.

**Q4 (Arguments against sirtuin-longevity; whether resveratrol is a genuine SIRT1 activator):**
Central source for Q4. This is one of the most cited papers arguing against resveratrol as a genuine SIRT1 activator. The paper demonstrates: (1) no activation with native substrates, (2) compounds bind fluorophore not SIRT1, (3) in vivo failure of SRT1720, and (4) broad off-target effects. From Pfizer, not an academic competitor, lending credibility.

## Claims to Verify

- Plasma exposure values: Cmax = 1.3 uM and AUC = 8110 ng/h/ml for 30 mg/kg group stated as matching previous 100 mg/kg report. The 3.3x dose mismatch (30 vs 100 mg/kg) with matching exposure seems worth cross-checking against the original Milne et al. 2007 paper (ref 26).
- Selectivity panel: "over 100 targets" is stated but the exact number varies in description. The specific >50% inhibition counts (38, 14, 20, 7) appear only once and could not be cross-referenced within the paper.
