# src-163: Phase IIa Trial of SRT2104 (SIRT1 Activator) in Psoriasis

**Citation:** Krueger JG et al. (2015) PLOS ONE 10(11): e0142081. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0142081
**Venue:** PLOS ONE (open access, peer-reviewed)
**Citation Count:** 91
**Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

This is the first randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial testing a direct SIRT1 activator (SRT2104, a STAC) in an inflammatory disease (moderate-to-severe psoriasis). Across all active dose groups, 35% of patients achieved good-to-excellent histological improvement vs a historical 5% placebo rate (p<0.0001), with gene expression analysis confirming modulation of IL-17 and TNF-alpha pathways. While the drug was generally well tolerated, serious adverse events occurred in 8% of subjects (all deemed related to treatment), and high inter-patient pharmacokinetic variability (AUC %CV 51-89%) limited dose-response conclusions.

## Key Evidence

### Efficacy
- **Primary endpoint (histological improvement):** 9/26 subjects (35%) across all SRT2104 groups achieved good/excellent histological improvement at Day 84 vs historical 5% placebo rate (p<0.0001).
  - 250 mg: 3/6 (50%), 90% CI [15.3%, 84.7%], p=0.0022
  - 500 mg: 4/9 (44.4%), 90% CI [16.9%, 74.9%], p=0.0006
  - 1000 mg: 2/11 (18.2%), 90% CI [3.3%, 50.0%], p=0.1019
- **PASI scores (secondary):** Dose/exposure-response trend observed. Adjusted mean PASI on Day 84: placebo 15.65 vs 14.41 (250mg), 13.32 (500mg), 11.43 (1000mg). Differences not statistically significant.
- **PGA:** On Day 84, 0% placebo achieved minimal/clear vs 22% (500mg) and 27% (1000mg).
- **Gene expression:** 123 probe sets (77 genes) modulated in responders; 1.66-fold change toward recovery in psoriasis-related transcripts (45% improvement). Key genes suppressed: Kynu (~23-fold), S100A12 (~15-fold), SPRR2c (~10-fold).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Average exposure increased dose-dependently but not fully proportional.
- High inter-subject variability: AUC %CV 51-89%.
- Plasma-skin concentration correlation: r=0.48, p=0.0118.
- Skin concentration >300 ng/g predicted PASI improvement (100% of subjects above this threshold improved).

### Safety
- 27/39 subjects (69%) experienced at least one AE.
- SAEs in 3 subjects (8%): pneumonitis (250mg), pancreatitis (250mg), ALT/bilirubin increased (1000mg). All deemed related to SRT2104.
- 4 subjects discontinued due to AEs.
- 1000 mg group: 100% of subjects reported at least one AE.
- Most common AEs: dizziness, headache, psoriatic arthropathy, GI symptoms, transaminase elevations.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Phase IIa, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-escalation study at 8 US centers.
- **Population:** 40 patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis (PASI >= 10, >= 10% BSA); randomized 4:1 active:placebo within each cohort.
- **Dosing:** 250, 500, or 1000 mg/day orally for 84 days, with sequential dose escalation after 28-day safety review.
- **Primary endpoint:** Histological improvement in skin biopsies (blinded central reader) compared to historical 5% placebo rate (one-sample, one-sided binomial test).
- **Analysis:** Not powered for formal hypothesis testing against concurrent placebo; used 90% CIs (Blyth-Still-Casella); no multiplicity adjustment.
- **Gene expression:** Affymetrix HG U133 Plus 2.0 microarrays; GCRMA normalization; limma with Benjamini-Hochberg correction; DEG defined as FC>2, FDR<0.1.

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size (N=40)** -- not powered for formal hypothesis testing against concurrent placebo.
2. **Historical placebo comparator** rather than statistically powered concurrent placebo comparison; only 7 placebo subjects.
3. **No dose-response in primary endpoint** -- histological improvement was highest at 250 mg and lowest at 1000 mg.
4. **High PK variability** (AUC %CV 51-89%) made exposure-response relationships difficult to establish.
5. **Operational error** prevented PGA evaluation in the 250 mg group.
6. **Gene expression analysis** based on only 2 responders vs 5 non-responders.
7. **Industry-funded** by Sirtris/GSK; multiple authors are GSK employees.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q2: Pharmacological sirtuin compounds in human clinical trials (SRT2104 STAC)
**Highly relevant.** This is a key clinical trial of SRT2104, one of the most studied STACs (Sirtuin-Activating Compounds). It demonstrates that SRT2104 can reach skin tissue, modulate inflammatory gene expression, and produce histological improvement in a human disease. However, the clinical efficacy signal is inconsistent across endpoints (histology yes, PASI trend only), PK variability is problematic, and SAEs occurred. This trial represents the longest SRT2104 exposure to date (84 days) and was conducted by Sirtris/GSK, the company that developed SRT2104.

### Q3: Tradeoffs between direct sirtuin activation vs indirect NAD+ boosting
**Moderately relevant.** SRT2104 is a direct SIRT1 activator (STAC), distinct from NAD+ precursors (NR, NMN) that boost sirtuin activity indirectly. This study demonstrates both the potential (mechanistic engagement via gene expression changes) and the challenges (high PK variability, inconsistent efficacy, SAEs including transaminase elevations) of the direct activation approach. The fact that SIRT1 is NAD+-dependent means even direct activators require sufficient NAD+ levels, but this trial does not directly compare the two strategies. The SAE profile (hepatic enzyme elevations, pneumonitis, pancreatitis) is notable as a potential downside of direct pharmacological activation compared to the generally better-tolerated NAD+ precursors.

## Claims to Verify

1. **Sample size of 40:** Verified from multiple sections ( CONSORT diagram reference, Results section line 240: "A total of forty patients were randomized"). Consistent.
2. **35% histological improvement rate:** 9/26 = 34.6%, reported as 35%. Table 2 says 34.6% with 90% CI [18.0%, 54.2%]. Abstract rounds to 35%. Acceptable rounding.
3. **p<0.0001 for primary endpoint:** Stated in abstract and Table 2 for "All Active" group comparison to historical 5% placebo. This is a one-sample binomial test, which can produce very small p-values with moderate effect sizes. Plausible but not independently verified.
4. **AUC %CV 51-89%:** Stated in abstract and Discussion. Consistent across sections.
5. **100% of subjects with skin concentration >300 ng/g improved PASI:** Stated as post-hoc exploratory finding (line 323-324). Small sample; the exact number of subjects above this threshold is not stated, making this potentially fragile.
