# src-144: Airhart et al. (2017) - NR Pharmacokinetics and NAD+ Effects in Humans

## Core Findings

This open-label, non-randomized PK study in 8 healthy volunteers demonstrated that oral nicotinamide riboside (NR) at 1000 mg twice daily for 9 days significantly increased whole-blood NAD+ levels by approximately 100% (mean increase 26.7 uM, p = 0.001) in every participant, with no adverse events. The study established the first reliable pharmacokinetic profile of NR in human whole blood, showing high inter-individual variability in oral bioavailability and a strong correlation between NR and NAD+ changes (R-squared = 0.72, p = 0.008).

## Key Evidence

- **NAD+ increase**: Whole-blood NAD+ rose from baseline 27 +/- 5 uM to C_ave,ss of 50 +/- 20 uM (approximately 2-fold, range 1.34-2.66x). Increase was significant for trough, peak, and C_ave,ss comparisons (all p = 0.001).
- **Safety**: Zero adverse events across 8 participants during dose escalation from 250 mg to 1000 mg twice daily. No clinically significant changes in potassium, creatine kinase, glucose, uric acid, or ALT. Small but statistically significant decreases in hematocrit (-2%, p = 0.005), hemoglobin (-0.4 g/dL, p = 0.04), and platelets (-20,000/uL, p = 0.03) remained within normal range.
- **NR bioavailability**: Highly variable -- half of participants showed >=100% increase in peak blood NR at t=3 hours, while the other half showed no or modest changes. Elimination half-life ~2.7 hours in subjects with discernible peaks.
- **NR-NAD+ correlation**: R-squared = 0.72, Pearson r = 0.85, p = 0.008 for absolute changes in NR vs NAD+.
- **Steady state**: NAD+ levels plateaued and remained constant over the 12-hour dosing interval even though NR fluctuated, suggesting sustained NAD+ elevation is achievable with twice-daily dosing.
- **Concordance with Trammell et al. (2016)**: Results consistent with Trammell's PBMC NAD+ data after single 1000 mg NR dose; Trammell reported ~54% increase vs this study's ~67% increase.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Open-label, non-randomized PK study; n=8 healthy volunteers (6F/2M, age 33+/-8 years, 75% non-Hispanic white)
- **Dosing**: NR (Niagen, ChromaDex) 250 mg daily x2 days, then 250 mg BID x2, then 500 mg BID x2, then 1000 mg BID x2, with 24-hour PK study on Day 9 after final 1000 mg dose
- **Assays**: LC-MS/MS for NR and NAD+ in whole blood; novel protocols developed to address NR and NAD+ instability in blood (processed within 3-5 minutes of collection)
- **Analysis**: Non-compartmental PK; paired t-tests for baseline vs steady-state comparisons; Holm correction for multiple testing
- **Registration**: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02689882; IRB approved; DSMB oversight

## Limitations

- Very small sample size (n=8); no control group (open-label, non-randomized)
- Predominantly white and female -- limited generalizability
- Healthy volunteers only; unknown effects in disease populations or with metabolic derangements
- Measured whole-blood NAD+, not intracellular NAD+ or tissue-specific NADH/NAD+ ratios
- Short duration (9 days); no long-term safety or efficacy data
- NR bioavailability was highly variable between individuals (mechanism unclear)

## Claims to Verify

- **100% NAD+ increase**: The abstract states "the latter increased by 100%" which is confirmed by the data: baseline 27 uM to C_ave,ss 50 uM, approximately 1.85-fold. The range of 1.34-2.66-fold is consistent. Claim is supported.
- **Sample size**: n=8 is verified in multiple places (enrolled 8, completed 8, analyzed 8). 14 screened, 5 declined, 1 excluded = 8 enrolled. Consistent.
- **Serum potassium decrease**: Table 1 shows 4.1 to 3.7 mEq/L (mean difference -0.4, p = 0.015). All individual levels remained in normal range. Verified.

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q2 (Pharmacological sirtuin compounds, NR safety/bioavailability)**: Directly relevant. This is one of the earliest human PK studies of NR (Niagen), demonstrating safety at 1000 mg BID and characterizing oral bioavailability (variable) and NAD+ elevation. 244 citations suggest it is a foundational reference for NR clinical research.
- **Q5 (NAD+ precursor trials, functional outcomes)**: Moderately relevant. Demonstrates NAD+ elevation in humans but does not measure functional outcomes (only blood NAD+ levels). Notes that NR may have therapeutic potential for diseases with mitochondrial dysfunction, but no functional endpoints were assessed.
