# src-001: Sirtuins and their Biological Relevance in Aging and Age-Related Diseases

**Citation:** Zhao L, Cao J, Hu K, He X, Yun D, Tong T, Han L. (2020). *Aging Dis*, 11(4):927-945. PMCID: PMC7390530. DOI: 10.14336/AD.2019.0820.

**Type:** Narrative review (open access, PMC)

**Affiliations:** Peking University Health Science Center; Peking Union Medical College Hospital

---

## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive narrative review of the sirtuin family (SIRT1-7) covering their enzymatic activities, roles in aging across model organisms, involvement in age-related diseases (neurodegenerative, cardiovascular, metabolic, cancer), and pharmacological modulators (STACs and NAD+ boosters). The review is broadly supportive of the sirtuin-longevity hypothesis but acknowledges key controversies, including replication failures for lifespan extension, the dual oncogenic/tumor-suppressor role of sirtuins, and the lack of standardized disease models leading to contradictory reports.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Lifespan Extension Evidence (Q1)
- Sir2 overexpression in yeast extended replicative lifespan by ~30% (initial report claimed 70%); lifespan extension also observed in C. elegans and D. melanogaster
- **Critical caveat:** "not all laboratories have managed to repeat the initial life span extending effect of sirtuins upregulation" (line 575-576)
- SIRT1 effect on mammalian longevity "remains unconvincing" (line 722-723)
- SIRT3 is the only sirtuin with human aging associations (polymorphisms linked to elderly survival), but replication failures exist
- SIRT6 identified as potentially holding "the most potential for actual life-span extension" in mammals; SIRT6 knockout causes rapid aging
- Sirtuin longevity pathways interact with AMPK, IIS/FOXO, TOR conserved pathways

### Pharmacological Compounds (Q2, Q3)
- Two categories of STACs: (1) direct exogenous activators (resveratrol, SRT1720, SRT2104, SRT1460, SRT2183, piceatannol) and (2) indirect NAD+ boosting (NMN, nicotinamide riboside, CD38 inhibitors like apigenin/quercetin)
- Over 14,000 STACs identified from a dozen chemical classes, activating SIRT1 by lowering Km via allosteric mechanism
- NAD+ boosters can activate all seven sirtuins with a single compound (potential advantage over direct activators)
- Claims SIRT1 activators "have now been passed the first clinical trials, with evidence of safety and efficacy" but provides no specific trial names, phases, or outcomes
- Natural compounds (polyphenols: quercetin, butein, fisetin, kaempferol, catechins) also activate sirtuins

### Cancer Dual Role (Q4)
- All sirtuins except SIRT4 show dual oncogene/tumor suppressor roles depending on cancer type
- SIRT1: upregulated in many cancers (lung, prostate, colon, breast, ovarian, leukemia) but tumor-suppressive in other contexts (intestinal tumors, p53-deletion models)
- SIRT7: classified as oncogene; upregulated in all cancer types studied
- SIRT6: induces apoptosis in cancer cells but not normal cells
- Dual role complicates therapeutic targeting

### Replication Failures and Controversies (Q4)
- Explicitly acknowledges that lifespan extension results have not been replicated by all labs
- "Little standardization of the disease models" leads to "contradictory reports with limited cross-validation"
- SIRT3-human aging correlations failed replication in some populations
- SIRT2 knockout mice lacked expected neurodegenerative phenotypes

### Age-Related Disease Evidence (Q5)
- Extensive coverage of sirtuin roles in neurodegenerative diseases (AD, PD, HD), cardiovascular diseases, and metabolic diseases
- Most evidence comes from animal models (mouse knockout/overexpression), not human trials
- SIRT1 activation in endothelial cells may protect vascular function with aging
- SIRT6 overexpression protects mice from diet-induced obesity with increased glucose tolerance

## Methodology

- Narrative review, not systematic or meta-analytic
- Covers literature through ~2019
- Includes reference tables summarizing sirtuin enzymatic activities, substrates, signaling pathways in aging, and dual cancer roles
- No quantitative synthesis of effect sizes

## Limitations

- **No meta-analysis or systematic search strategy described** -- selection bias possible
- Published 2020; does not cover more recent clinical trial results (e.g., NR and NMN human trials published 2020-2024)
- Claims about clinical trial passage for STACs are vague and uncited
- Primarily focused on preclinical/animal evidence; limited human clinical data
- No discussion of resveratrol bioavailability controversy or specific replication failures (e.g., by David Gems group)
- Does not address leading researcher disagreements explicitly (Q6)

## Claims to Verify

1. **"passed the first clinical trials, with evidence of safety and efficacy"** for SIRT1 activators (line 1599-1600) -- no specific trials cited; needs verification against ClinicalTrials.gov records
2. **Sir2 extends yeast lifespan by "30%"** (line 539) vs initial report of "70%" (line 140-141) -- the paper cites both numbers in different places; the original 1999 Kaeberlein paper should be checked for the actual figure
