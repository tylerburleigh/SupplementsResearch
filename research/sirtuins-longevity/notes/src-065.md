# src-065: SIRT1, SIRT3, SIRT6 in Vascular Biology and Atherogenesis

**Citation:** Sosnowska et al. (2017), *Atherosclerosis*, DOI: 10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2017.08.027
**Type:** Narrative review (accepted manuscript)
**Quality:** ok -- full text extracted from PDF (raw text fallback)

---

## Core Findings

SIRT1, SIRT3, and SIRT6 play significant roles in vascular biology and atherogenesis through regulation of lipid metabolism, oxidative stress, inflammation, endothelial function, and autophagy. Preclinical evidence consistently shows that SIRT activation opposes multiple steps in atherosclerosis development. However, clinical data remain limited, and no large RCTs have confirmed translational benefits of SIRT activators on cardiovascular outcomes.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### SIRT1
- Highly expressed in vascular endothelial cells; regulates apoptosis, inflammation, stress resistance, senescence via deacetylation of FOXOs, NF-kB, p53, PGC-1a
- SIRT1 deficiency promotes vascular aging and atherosclerosis through increased inflammation, oxidative stress, foam cell formation, and impaired NO production
- Modulates cholesterol biosynthesis via LXR activation; promotes reverse cholesterol transport (ABCA1, ABCG1)
- Reduces PCSK9 secretion, increasing LDLR expression in mice
- Reduces scavenger receptor LOX-1 expression in macrophages, slowing foam cell formation
- Regulates autophagy through histone modification and FOXO-dependent pathways

### SIRT3
- Mitochondrial sirtuin; knockout mice show near-normal phenotype but excessive mitochondrial protein acetylation
- Reduces ROS via deacetylation/activation of SOD2
- SIRT3 deficiency induces mild endothelial dysfunction in mice on high-cholesterol diet
- Loss of SIRT3 may augment osteogenic signaling and accelerate vascular calcification

### SIRT6
- Expression decreased in atherosclerotic lesions in both ApoE-/- mice and human patients
- Haploinsufficient SIRT6+/- mice show impaired endothelial-derived relaxation (acetylcholine challenge), elevated VCAM-1, accelerated plaque formation
- Overexpression reduces pro-atherosclerotic gene expression and monocyte adhesion
- Suppresses PCSK9 gene expression via FOXO3 recruitment, lowering LDL-C in mice
- Protects against foam cell formation via autophagy-dependent pathway (lowers miR-33, upregulates ABCA1/ABCG1)

### Clinical Evidence (Pharmacological Modulators)
- **SRT2104** (SIRT1 activator): Safe and well-tolerated in healthy smokers; reduced LDL-C by 11% but **no benefit on vascular, endothelial, or platelet function** vs placebo (line 344-347)
- **Resveratrol**: Strong SIRT1 activator in preclinical models, but a meta-analysis found **no evidence** of resveratrol supplementation impacting plasma lipids or other risk factors (line 408-409)
- **Metformin**: Increases SIRT1 expression/activity, represses inflammatory markers in patients with carotid artery atherosclerosis; identified as most promising candidate for large RCT
- **Genistein, quercetin, berberine, curcumin, ginkgolide B**: All show preclinical SIRT1-activating effects with anti-atherosclerotic properties, but no clinical trial data

### Statins and SIRTs
- Conflicting data: some studies show statins increase SIRT1 expression, others show statins reduce SIRT1 while increasing eNOS, normalizing to healthy levels

## Methodology

- Narrative review covering SIRT1, SIRT3, SIRT6 roles in vascular biology
- Focus on preclinical evidence (cell culture, murine models) and limited clinical data
- Organized by atherogenic mechanism: lipid modification, oxidative stress, inflammation, foam cell formation, autophagy, vascular function
- Includes section on pharmacological modulators with clinical evaluation

## Limitations

- **Clinical data are extremely limited.** The authors repeatedly emphasize the gap between preclinical promise and clinical evidence.
- No original data; narrative review synthesizing published literature.
- SRT2104 trial was in healthy smokers only -- a narrow population not generalizable to broader atherosclerosis.
- Resveratrol meta-analysis null result undermines one of the most studied SIRT activators.
- Paper predates most NAD+ precursor (NMN, NR) clinical trials -- no direct discussion of NAD+ boosting as a therapeutic strategy.
- Manuscript format (accepted, unedited) with some typographic artifacts.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: Evidence sirtuin activation causally extends lifespan/healthspan
- **Moderately relevant.** The review focuses on atherosclerosis and vascular biology rather than lifespan per se, but documents SIRT roles in opposing vascular aging. Life-prolonging effects in yeast are mentioned (line 108). The vascular protection mechanisms (endothelial function, anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidative) are relevant to healthspan. However, no direct lifespan extension data in mammals is discussed.

### Q5: NAD+ precursor trials on functional outcomes (vascular function specifically)
- **Moderately relevant.** The paper discusses SIRT activators (not NAD+ precursors directly) and their effects on vascular function. The SRT2104 trial is the strongest clinical evidence on vascular function outcomes (null result). The mechanistic link between NAD+ and SIRTs is established (SIRTs are NAD+-dependent), but the paper does not review NAD+ precursor supplementation trials. The vascular function section (lines 322-331) is limited to two murine studies of SIRT6.

## Claims to Verify

1. **SRT2104 reduced LDL-C by 11% in smokers** -- this is a specific quantitative claim from a clinical trial (ref 77). Worth verifying the original trial data as this is the most concrete clinical outcome reported.
2. **SIRT6 expression decreased in human atherosclerotic lesions** -- attributed to ref 27. Important translational claim that should be verified from the primary source.
