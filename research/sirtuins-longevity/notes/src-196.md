## Core Findings

This article profiles Charles Brenner, a biochemist at City of Hope, as the most prominent public critic of the sirtuin-longevity hypothesis and of David Sinclair's claims about lifespan extension. Brenner argues that the evidence for sirtuins as "longevity genes" is far weaker than commonly presented, suffering from framing bias and selective reporting. He directly challenges Sinclair's resveratrol and sirtuin research, while advocating for NAD precursor (NR) supplementation for healthspan rather than lifespan extension.

## Key Evidence and Arguments

### Brenner's Core Critiques of Sirtuin-Longevity Hypothesis
1. **Yeast data misrepresentation**: Increasing sirtuin activity extends life in only 1 out of 5 million yeast cells; across the entire population, extra sirtuins actually *undermined* yeast longevity. The claim that sirtuins are conserved longevity genes rests on selective framing.
2. **Framing bias**: Brenner published a 2022 paper arguing that sirtuin-longevity studies suffer from "framing bias" -- researchers design questions assuming sirtuins are longevity genes, then interpret results accordingly. He states review articles propagating these biases are "so rampant that few investigators have considered how weak the case ever was."
3. **Resveratrol not a sirtuin activator**: Brenner claims a "massive research literature" shows resveratrol is not actually a sirtuin activator, undermining Sinclair's foundational 2003 finding and the entire resveratrol-sirtuin-longevity narrative.
4. **Sirtuins are not dominant longevity genes**: "The verdict is in" that sirtuins are not dominant longevity genes selected and passed down through evolution. What is genetically encoded is the *limit* on lifespan.

### Sinclair's Rebuttals
- Dozens of papers show NAD benefits are lost when sirtuins are deleted
- Overexpressing sirtuins in worms, flies, and yeast extends lifespan -- a conserved effect
- Guarente supports this, stating a large volume of published research "cements" sirtuins' role in longevity

### Historical Context of the Dispute
- Brenner and Sinclair briefly collaborated: Brenner was a scientific advisor to Sirtris Pharmaceuticals (founded by Sinclair) in 2008
- Brenner left after GSK acquired Sirtris in 2009
- Brenner went public with critiques after Sinclair's 2019 book *Lifespan* became a bestseller
- Brenner published a scathing review of *Lifespan* in Archives of Gerontology and Geriatrics (Jan 2023)

### Financial Interests (Both Sides)
- **Brenner**: Chief scientific advisor to ChromaDex (sells NR supplements). NAD supplement market estimated at $250M in US.
- **Sinclair**: Co-founded MetroBiotech (running NMN clinical trials). Co-founded Sirtris (acquired by GSK).
- **Guarente**: Co-founded Elysium Health (sells NR and NMN). NAD booster market projected to reach $1B by 2030.
- Article discusses whether these financial interests undermine credibility; bioethicist Johnston notes industry partnerships are standard and universities require disclosure.

### Supporting Voices
- **Matt Kaeberlein** (UW biogerontologist, former Guarente postdoc): Every human clinical trial claiming NAD precursor effects has "major weaknesses" when examined closely.
- **Samuel Klein** (Washington U. St. Louis): Animal study benefits of NAD boosters haven't translated to people; "humans are simply not rodents."
- **Arthur Caplan** (NYU bioethicist): Aging research overhypes every advance; confirmation by further research is essential.

2. **"Extra sirtuins actually undermined yeast longevity"** -- This claim about the aggregate effect on yeast populations contradicts the standard narrative and needs verification against primary literature.
