# src-007: It Takes Two to Tango: NAD+ and Sirtuins in Aging/Longevity Control

## Source Metadata
- **URL**: https://www.nature.com/articles/npjamd201617
- **Likely Venue**: NPJ Aging and Mechanisms of Disease (Nature Partner Journals)
- **Authors**: Shin-ichiro Imai (based on extensive self-citation pattern and "Imai lab" reference)
- **Year**: ~2016
- **Type**: Review article
- **Quality Assessment**: Substantive review with detailed mechanistic discussion, strong referencing

## Core Findings

This review argues that the NAD+/sirtuin axis is an evolutionarily conserved aging/longevity regulator. The author asserts that past controversies over whether sirtuins truly control aging have been resolved by recent studies. The review proposes a "NAD World" framework where NAMPT-mediated NAD+ biosynthesis connects cellular metabolism to systemic aging regulation through hypothalamic-adipose tissue communication.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Sirtuin lifespan extension evidence (model organisms)
- Sir2 overexpression extends lifespan in yeast, worms, and flies (refs 18-20)
- Contradictory studies by Burnett et al. (2011, Nature) and others reported no lifespan extension from Sir2 overexpression in C. elegans and Drosophila
- Author claims controversy "put to rest" by reconfirming studies (refs 23-30)
- Brain-specific SIRT1-overexpressing (BRASTO) mice show lifespan extension in both sexes (ref 27, Satoh et al. 2013 Cell Metab)
- Whole-body SIRT1 overexpression mice do NOT show lifespan extension (ref 22)
- Whole-body SIRT6 transgenic mice show lifespan extension in males only (ref 24, Kanfi et al. 2012 Nature)

### NAD+ decline with aging
- NAMPT and NAD+ levels decline with age in pancreas, adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, liver, and brain
- Inflammatory, ischemic, and degenerative disease conditions also decrease NAMPT-mediated NAD+ biosynthesis
- Chronic PARP activation in aging worms and mice contributes to NAD+ depletion

### Three levels of NAD+/sirtuin regulation
1. **NAD+ biosynthesis regulation**: NAMPT-mediated pathway is the primary regulatory node; induced by CR, fasting, exercise, and stress
2. **Modulation by substrates/derivatives**: Nicotinamide is a non-competitive inhibitor (IC50 for SIRT1: 175 uM for acetylated substrate); NADH unlikely to be physiologically relevant inhibitor; long-chain fatty acids enhance SIRT6 up to 35-fold
3. **Competitive NAD+ consumption**: PARPs and CD38/157 compete with sirtuins for NAD+; PARP1 knockout or inhibition increases SIRT1 activity

### Systemic regulation framework (NAD World)
- eNAMPT secreted from adipose tissue controls hypothalamic NAD+ biosynthesis and SIRT1 activity
- Adipose-specific NAMPT overexpression (ANKI mice) increases circulating eNAMPT, hypothalamic NAD+, and physical activity
- DMH-specific SIRT1/Nkx2-1/Prdm13 pathway regulates sleep quality and adiposity
- Circadian regulation: CLOCK/BMAL drives Nampt transcription, creating NAD+ oscillation that drives SIRT1, SIRT3, and SIRT6 activities

### NAD+ precursor supplementation
- NMN and NR identified as key NAD+ intermediates for therapeutic intervention
- NR promotes lifespan in yeast and worms through Sir2-dependent pathways
- NR activates UPRmt genes (hsp-6, HSP60) in worms, mammalian cells, and skeletal muscle stem cells

## Methodology
- This is a narrative review, not a primary study
- The author (Imai) is a major figure in NAD+/sirtuin biology and originated the "NAD World" concept
- References span primary literature from 1999-2015

## Limitations
- **Author bias risk**: Imai is a leading proponent of the sirtuin-longevity hypothesis and has a strong interest in framing controversies as resolved. The assertion that the controversy has been "put to rest" should be critically evaluated against dissenting literature.
- **No human clinical data**: This review focuses almost entirely on model organisms (yeast, worms, flies, mice) with no human interventional studies discussed
- **Selective citation on controversies**: The review acknowledges contradictory findings (Burnett et al. 2011) but dismisses them quickly, citing reconfirming studies without detailed examination of why the contradictions arose
- **NR/NMN therapeutic claims**: The review expresses optimism about NAD+ precursor supplementation but provides no human trial data
- **SIRT6 sex-specificity unexplained**: The observation that SIRT6 extends lifespan only in males is noted but not mechanistically explained

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (Evidence sirtuin activation causally extends lifespan in humans vs model organisms)**: Strongly relevant. Provides detailed model organism evidence for sirtuin-mediated lifespan extension (Sir2 in yeast/worms/flies, SIRT1 and SIRT6 in mice). No human evidence. Notes the important finding that brain-specific (not whole-body) SIRT1 overexpression is what extends lifespan in mice.

**Q2 (Pharmacological sirtuin compounds in human clinical trials)**: Not directly addressed. No discussion of STACs or other pharmacological sirtuin activators in human trials.

**Q3 (Tradeoffs between direct sirtuin activation vs indirect NAD+ boosting)**: Highly relevant. Provides the mechanistic framework for understanding NAD+ boosting as indirect sirtuin activation. Details three regulatory levels connecting NAD+ to sirtuin activity. Discusses NMN and NR as NAD+ precursors that enhance sirtuin activity indirectly.

**Q4 (Arguments against sirtuin-longevity hypothesis)**: Relevant. Acknowledges the major controversy (Burnett et al. 2011, Herranz et al. 2010) but argues it has been resolved. This source represents the pro-sirtuin perspective on the debate.

**Q5 (NAD+ precursor trials on functional outcomes)**: Partially relevant. Discusses NMN and NR in preclinical contexts but no human trial outcomes.

**Q6 (Leading researcher disagreements)**: Partially relevant. Implicitly frames the Imai/Guarente perspective favoring the sirtuin-longevity hypothesis, though does not name specific opponents or detail the disagreements beyond the general controversy.
