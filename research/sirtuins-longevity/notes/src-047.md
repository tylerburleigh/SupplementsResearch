# src-047: Nicotinamide Mononucleotide: A Promising Molecule for Therapy of Diverse Diseases by Targeting NAD+ Metabolism

**Authors:** Hong W, Mo F, Zhang Z, Huang M, Wei X (2020)
**Venue:** Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology
**Citations:** 153 | **Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive review of NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide) as a therapeutic agent targeting NAD+ metabolism for age-related diseases. The paper synthesizes preclinical evidence across multiple disease models (diabetes, obesity, heart failure, Alzheimer's, vascular dysfunction, retinal degeneration, kidney injury) and catalogs early human clinical trials of NMN supplementation. The authors conclude NMN is a promising NAD+ booster but note that clinical and toxicological evidence remains insufficient as of 2020.

## Key Evidence

### Preclinical NMN Effects (Table 1, lines 1585-1922)
- Extensive table of NMN studies across diverse disease models in mice/rats
- Common dose: 500 mg/kg i.p., or 100-300 mg/kg in drinking water
- Long-term study (Mills et al., 2016): 12 months of NMN in drinking water improved insulin sensitivity, lipid metabolism, and reduced age-related weight gain in C57BL/6N mice
- NMN improved cognitive function in aged mice across multiple studies (lines 1765-1775)
- Vascular benefits: improved endothelium-dependent dilation, reduced arterial stiffness in aged mice (lines 1719-1738)

### Human Clinical Trials (Table 2, lines 1927-2034)
Five registered NMN trials as of 2020:

1. **Phase I (UMIN000021309)**: Keio University, Japan. n=10 healthy men aged 40-60. Single oral NMN. Found safe up to 500 mg, effectively metabolized. NMN not detected in plasma samples.
2. **Phase II (UMIN000030609)**: Keio University, Japan. n=30 healthy men aged 40-60. 8 weeks oral NMN. Assessing safety, kinetics, glucose metabolism.
3. **NCT03151239**: Washington University, USA. n=25 pre-diabetic postmenopausal women aged 55-75. 250 mg/day NMN for 8 weeks. Randomized double-blind placebo-controlled. Cardiovascular and metabolic endpoints.
4. **UMIN000025739**: Hiroshima University, Japan. n=20 healthy men/women aged 50-70. 100 or 200 mg/day for 24 weeks. Randomized dose comparison. Hormonal endpoints.
5. **UMIN000036321**: University of Tokyo, Japan. n=42 healthy non-smoking men aged ~65. 250 mg/day for 12 weeks. Randomized double-blind placebo-controlled. Body composition endpoints.

### NMN vs NR vs NAM Comparison (lines 2057-2093)
- NMN and NR increase NAD+ more effectively than NAM
- NAM acts as a feedback inhibitor of sirtuins and PARPs, undermining its own benefits
- Key pharmacological difference: NMN improves cardiac function via SIRT3 in Friedreich's ataxia cardiomyopathy model, but NR does not
- Both NMN and NR undergo first-pass metabolism and rapid conversion
- NR is unstable and quickly converted to NAM in murine plasma
- Authors call for head-to-head NMN vs NR studies

### NAD+ Biosynthesis and Transport (lines 132-210)
- Three pathways: Preiss-Handler (from nicotinic acid), de novo (from tryptophan), salvage (predominant)
- NMN uptake: CD73 converts extracellular NMN to NR, which enters via ENT transporters, then phosphorylated by NRK1/2 back to NMN
- Slc12a8 identified as specific NMN transporter, highly expressed in small intestine, upregulated in aged mice
- NAMPT is the rate-limiting enzyme; exists as intracellular (iNAMPT) and extracellular (eNAMPT) forms

### Possible Detrimental Effects (lines 1514-1562)
- NMN accumulation promotes Wallerian degeneration after axon injury
- Lower doses may be safer and more effective (especially in brain)
- Intraperitoneal NMN increased sperm oxidative stress in offspring of obese mothers (oral route did not)
- Cancer concern: NAD turnover is higher in tumor cells; NAMPT elevated in many cancers
- SIRT1 has both tumor-suppressor and oncogenic roles depending on context
- Route of administration matters (oral vs injection shows different safety profiles)

## Methodology

- Narrative review article, no original experimental data
- Systematic cataloguing of preclinical studies in Table 1 (40+ studies across 10+ disease categories)
- Clinical trial summary from public registries in Table 2
- Published April 2020, so clinical trial status reflects that time point

## Limitations

- Review published in 2020; clinical trial landscape has evolved significantly since then
- Most evidence is from mouse/rat models; human data still preliminary at time of publication
- No meta-analysis or systematic methodology described
- Authors acknowledge clinical and toxicological evidence is "currently insufficient"
- Several cited clinical trials were still ongoing at publication time
- The paper does not report quantitative results from ongoing trials

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q2 (Pharmacological sirtuin compounds in clinical trials):** Indirectly relevant -- NMN is an NAD+ booster that activates sirtuins, but is not a direct sirtuin activator. Table 2 lists NMN clinical trials, useful as comparison context.
- **Q3 (Direct activation vs indirect NAD+ boosting):** Highly relevant. Detailed comparison of NMN vs NR vs NAM, discussion of pharmacological differences, and call for head-to-head studies. Notes that NAM feedback-inhibits sirtuins, while NMN/NR do not.
- **Q5 (NAD+ precursor trials on functional outcomes):** Directly relevant. Comprehensive catalog of NMN clinical trials with trial IDs, sample sizes, dosing, durations, and endpoints. Also extensive preclinical functional outcome data in Table 1.
