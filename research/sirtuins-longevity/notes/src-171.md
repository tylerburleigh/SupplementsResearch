# src-171: SRT1720, SRT2183, SRT1460, and Resveratrol Are Not Direct Activators of SIRT1

**Authors:** Pacholec M, Bleasdale JE, Chrunyk B, Cunningham D, Flynn D, Garofalo RS, Griffith D, Griffor M, Loulakis P, Pabst B, Qiu X, Stockman B, Thanabal V, Varghese A, Ward J, Withka J, Ahn K

**Venue:** Journal of Biological Chemistry, 2010; 285(11):8340-8351

**DOI:** 10.1074/jbc.M109.088682 | **PMID:** 20061378 | **PMCID:** PMC2832984

**Affiliation:** Pfizer Global Research and Development, Groton, CT

## Core Findings

This paper provides decisive evidence that the most prominent putative SIRT1 activators -- SRT1720, SRT2183, SRT1460 (Sirtris Pharmaceuticals compounds), and resveratrol -- are NOT direct activators of SIRT1. The apparent activation observed in prior studies was an artifact of the fluorescently-labeled (TAMRA) peptide substrates used in assays. The compounds directly interact with the fluorophore moiety rather than with SIRT1 itself. Furthermore, SRT1720 showed no in vivo efficacy in diabetic mouse models and all four compounds are highly promiscuous, hitting many off-target proteins.

## Key Evidence

### 1. Fluorophore-dependent activation artifact
- With TAMRA-p53 peptide substrate: SRT1720 showed 741% maximal activation (EC1.5 = 0.32 uM), SRT2183 285%, SRT1460 434%, resveratrol 239% (EC1.5 = 31.6 uM) -- confirming previously reported results.
- With native p53 peptide (no fluorophore): **No activation observed** for any compound at 30-100 uM. SRT1720 at 30 uM actually showed ~40% inhibition.
- With full-length native substrates (Ac-p53 and Ac-AceCS1): **No activation** for any compound at 30 uM. All activity remained within ~10% of DMSO control.

### 2. Biophysical evidence for fluorophore-compound interaction
- **NMR:** SRT1460 caused upfield chemical shift of acetyl (CH3) resonance on TAMRA-peptides but NOT on matching native peptides lacking TAMRA -- demonstrating direct compound-fluorophore interaction.
- **SPR:** SRT1720 and SRT1460 showed concentration-dependent binding to TAMRA-peptide but not native peptide (even in absence of SIRT1).
- **ITC:** SRT1460 bound SIRT1-TAMRA peptide complex (Kd = 13 uM) but showed no binding isotherm with SIRT1-native peptide complex. Binding was principally entropically driven, not the enthalpically driven interaction previously claimed.

### 3. In vivo failure to replicate
- ob/ob mice on HFD, SRT1720 at 30 mg/kg daily for 18 days: **no effect on any glucose parameter** (fed, fasting, or OGTT AUC), despite plasma Cmax of 1.3 uM and AUC of 8110 ng/h/ml matching previously reported 100 mg/kg exposures.
- At 100 mg/kg: 3 of 8 mice died; remaining mice showed reduced food intake/weight gain -- dose was not tolerated.
- No evidence of enhanced mitochondrial function (cytochrome c oxidase, citrate synthase) in any tissue.
- This directly contradicts the original Milne et al. (2007) Sirtris paper claiming glucose-lowering and mitochondrial benefits.

### 4. Compound promiscuity
- Selectivity screen at 10 uM against 100+ targets (receptors, enzymes, ion channels, transporters):
  - SRT1720: inhibited >50% activity in 38 targets
  - SRT2183: 14 targets
  - SRT1460: 20 targets
  - Resveratrol: 7 targets
- Shared off-targets include adenosine A3 receptor, urotensin receptor, PDE2, norepinephrine transporter
- Authors conclude these compounds are "not useful pharmacological tools for studying SIRT1 pathways"

## Methodology

- Recombinant full-length human SIRT1 (amino acids 2-747) with His6 tag, expressed in E. coli
- Multiple substrate systems: TAMRA-p53 peptide (fluorophore-labeled), native p53 peptide, full-length Ac-p53 (ELISA + Western blot), full-length Ac-AceCS1 (coupled enzyme/HPLC assay)
- All assays designed with substrate concentrations well below Km to maximize activation detection window (10-20 fold)
- Biophysical: 600 MHz NMR, Biacore 3000 SPR, Microcal VP-ITC
- In vivo: ob/ob mice (n=8/group), HFD, 18-day dosing, plasma PK at multiple timepoints
- Selectivity: commercial panel of 100+ targets at 10 uM

## Limitations

- Only tested SIRT1, not other sirtuins (SIRT2-7)
- Limited to p53 and AceCS1 as full-length substrates; other physiological SIRT1 targets could theoretically behave differently
- In vivo study used only ob/ob mice; did not test diet-induced obese mice (different from original Milne paper's model)
- Could not test SRT1720 in NMR due to limited solubility
- SRT1460 ITC binding was weakly enthalpic (different from prior reports using truncated SIRT1 construct)

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q3 (Tradeoffs: STACs vs NAD+ boosting):** This is a landmark paper demonstrating that the leading STACs (SRT1720 and related Sirtris compounds) do not directly activate SIRT1. This fundamentally undermines the STAC approach and strengthens the case for NAD+ boosting as a more viable strategy for enhancing sirtuin activity.

**Q4 (Arguments against sirtuin-longevity hypothesis):** This paper is central to the assay artifact controversy. It demonstrates that the original evidence for pharmacological SIRT1 activation was based on an artifact of fluorescently-labeled substrates. The compounds interact with the fluorophore, not with SIRT1. This challenges a key pillar of the sirtuin-longevity hypothesis -- that small molecules can activate SIRT1 to recapitulate caloric restriction benefits.

## Context

This Pfizer paper was one of the most damaging critiques of the Sirtris Pharmaceuticals program. It directly contradicted claims by Milne et al. (2007, Nature) that SRT1720 and related compounds were potent SIRT1 activators with in vivo efficacy. The controversy contributed to broader skepticism about the sirtuin activation approach and influenced the field's shift toward NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR) as alternative strategies for enhancing sirtuin activity.

## Claims to Verify

- The Cmax of 1.3 uM at 30 mg/kg matching previously reported 100 mg/kg exposure is a key cross-study comparison that should be confirmed against the original Milne et al. paper.
- The claim of 3/8 deaths at 100 mg/kg -- verify this is consistent with the original study's safety profile.
