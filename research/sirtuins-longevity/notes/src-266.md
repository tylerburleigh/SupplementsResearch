## Core Findings (as reported by citing papers)

Lagouge et al. 2006 reported that resveratrol activates SIRT1, which in turn deacetylates and activates PGC-1alpha, leading to:
- Improved mitochondrial biogenesis and function
- Enhanced adipose tissue thermogenesis
- Improved insulin sensitivity
- Protection against diet-induced obesity and metabolic disease in mice

This was published in Cell and became a foundational paper for the sirtuin activation / longevity field, establishing the SIRT1-PGC-1alpha signaling axis as a key pathway.

## Key Evidence from Citing Papers

1. **SIRT1-PGC-1alpha axis** (lines 40-42, 93-95): Multiple citing papers confirm Lagouge et al. 2006 established that resveratrol improves mitochondrial biogenesis through SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of PGC-1alpha.

2. **Anti-obesity dose** (lines 318-320): Resveratrol at 400 mg/kg/day on a high-fat diet enhanced resistance to obesity in mice, reducing total body fat and white adipose tissue storage.

3. **Clinical translation** (lines 459-472): A 2024 clinical trial (n=120 T2DM patients, 90 days, 500 mg/day resveratrol) found significant improvement in glycemic control and neuropathic signs, supporting translational relevance.

## Critical Contradictions (Q4 -- Arguments Against)

**Brenner 2024 preprint** (lines 429-437) directly and forcefully disputes the Lagouge et al. 2006 findings:
- Resveratrol does NOT extend lifespan in yeast or invertebrates (contradicting early sirtuin activation claims)
- Claims that resveratrol extends healthspan in normal and diabetic mice via SIRT1/Pgc1a activation were "found to be baseless in both lean and obesogenic conditions"
- Claims of novel STACs (SIRT1-activating compounds) having high pharmacological potency and specificity for SIRT1 were "proven false"

This is critical for Q4 -- it represents the major scientific challenge to the Lagouge et al. framework.
