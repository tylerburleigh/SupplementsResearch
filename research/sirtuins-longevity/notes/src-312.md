# src-312: NAD+ precursor supplementation in human ageing: clinical evidence and challenges

**Citation:** Vinten KT, Tretowicz MM, Coskun E, et al. Nature Metabolism 7, 1974-1990 (2025). DOI: 10.1038/s42255-025-01387-7

**Quality note:** This source is behind a paywall. Only the abstract, figure captions, reference list, and author information were accessible. No methods, results, or discussion sections were available. The evidence below is drawn solely from the abstract.

## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive review in Nature Metabolism evaluating clinical evidence on NAD+ precursor supplementation in human ageing. The key message is cautionary: while preclinical data strongly support NAD+ precursors (NR, NMN) as therapeutic candidates for healthy ageing, human clinical trials have shown **limited efficacy**. Evidence for age-related NAD+ decline in humans is only consistently observed in a limited number of studies, and the published body of data on NAD+ dynamics in human tissues remains sparse. Extrapolating rodent-based data to humans is described as "not straightforward."

## Key Evidence

1. **Age-related NAD+ decline in humans is not robustly established:** Only a limited number of studies have consistently observed age-related decline in NAD+ levels in humans.

2. **Human clinical trials show limited efficacy:** Despite promising preclinical data for NAD+ precursor supplementation, translation to human clinical benefit has been disappointing.

3. **Tissue-specific NAD+ metabolism matters:** The review evaluates tissue-specific effects of NAD+ precursor supplementation and emphasizes understanding tissue-specific NAD+ metabolism as crucial for defining therapeutic value.

4. **Data on human NAD+ dynamics is sparse:** The overall body of published data on NAD+ dynamics in human tissues remains limited, and rodent-to-human extrapolation is unreliable.

5. **Figures suggest comprehensive scope:** Figure captions indicate coverage of (a) NAD+ level alterations across human tissues during ageing and after precursor supplementation, (b) central NAD+ metabolic pathways, (c) metabolic fate of orally administered NAD+ precursors, and (d) age-driven tissue-specific changes in NAD+ abundance.
