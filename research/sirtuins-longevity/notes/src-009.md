# src-009: The NAD+/sirtuin pathway modulates longevity through activation of mitochondrial UPR and FOXO signaling

**Citation:** Mouchiroud L, Houtkooper RH, Moullan N, et al. *Cell*. 2013;154(2):430-441. doi:10.1016/j.cell.2013.06.016. PMCID: PMC3753670.

**Quality:** ok -- full author manuscript from PMC with complete text.

## Core Findings

NAD+ levels decline with age in both mice and C. elegans, and this decline is causally linked to aging. Restoring NAD+ levels -- either by inhibiting NAD+-consuming PARP enzymes or by supplementing NAD+ precursors (NR, NAM) -- extends lifespan in C. elegans by 15-29% through a sir-2.1-dependent mechanism involving mitochondrial UPR activation and FOXO/DAF-16 nuclear translocation. The NAD+/SIRT1/UPRmt/SOD signaling axis is conserved in mammalian cells.

## Key Evidence

### NAD+ decline is causal in aging
- Aged mice (103 weeks) showed increased PARylation and decreased NAD+ in liver and muscle vs young mice (24 weeks)
- Aged C. elegans showed the same pattern: increased PARylation, decreased NAD+
- pme-1 mutants (worm PARP1 homolog) maintained higher NAD+ levels and accumulated less lipofuscin
- Depleting NAD+ with paraquat or qns-1 RNAi shortened lifespan

### Lifespan extension in C. elegans
- pme-1 mutation: 29% mean lifespan extension
- pme-1 RNAi: 20% extension
- AZD2281 (olaparib, 100 nM): 22.9% extension
- ABT-888 (veliparib, 100 nM): 15% extension
- NR (500 uM): significant lifespan extension
- NAM (200 uM): significant lifespan extension

### sir-2.1 dependence
- All lifespan extension from PARP inhibitors and NR/NAM was completely abrogated in sir-2.1(ok434) mutants
- The outcrossed sir-2.1 transgenic strain MV389 (Viswanathan & Guarente 2011) showed 25% lifespan extension in this study

### Dual mitochondrial stress pathway
- **Early phase (day 1):** ROS burst + UPRmt activation via mitonuclear protein imbalance, mitochondrial fragmentation
- **Late phase (day 3):** Continued UPRmt + DAF-16 nuclear translocation + sod-3 induction (antioxidant defense), mitochondrial hyperfusion
- Both phases are essential: ubl-5 RNAi (blocking UPRmt) abrogates lifespan extension; daf-16 mutation abrogates lifespan extension
- UPRmt appears upstream of antioxidant response (ubl-5 RNAi blocks sod-3 induction)

### Mammalian conservation
- SIRT1 overexpression in primary mouse hepatocytes induced mitonuclear protein imbalance, UPRmt (CLPP, HSP60), and SOD2
- SIRT1 knockout completely blocked UPRmt and SOD2 induction by AZD2281 and NR
- AZD2281 and NR induced mitochondrial biogenesis in AML12 hepatocyte cell line

### Combination studies
- Optimal doses of AZD2281 + NR: no additive benefit (same pathway)
- Suboptimal doses combined: synergistic 11% extension (p=0.01), confirming pathway convergence

## Methodology

- **Organisms:** C. elegans (primary lifespan studies), mice (aged tissue comparison), primary mouse hepatocytes and AML12 cell line (mammalian validation)
- **Lifespan assays:** 60-100 animals per condition, scored every other day, Kaplan-Meier with log-rank test
- **Key genetic tools:** sir-2.1(ok434) mutants, pme-1(ok988) mutants, daf-16 mutants, rrf-3(pk1426) RNAi-sensitized strain, MV389 sir-2.1 transgenic
- **Pharmacological agents:** AZD2281 (olaparib), ABT-888 (veliparib), NR, NAM, paraquat
- **Readouts:** Lifespan, NAD+ levels, PARylation, oxygen consumption (Seahorse XF96), mtDNA/nDNA ratio, ATP levels, motility, GFP reporters for UPRmt (hsp-6) and antioxidant (sod-3), DAF-16::GFP localization

## Limitations

- Lifespan studies are in C. elegans only; no mammalian lifespan data
- Mammalian data limited to cell-based systems (primary hepatocytes, AML12), not whole-animal studies
- No human data whatsoever
- The sir-2.1 overexpression debate is acknowledged but not fully resolved (10% vs 25% extension depending on lab conditions)
- PARP inhibitors at high doses could have off-target effects; the study argues against this via genetic pme-1 confirmation
- Worm lifespan was censored for protruding vulva phenotype, which may affect comparability with other studies

## Claims to Verify

- **29% lifespan extension in pme-1 mutants**: Reported as mean lifespan extension. Verify that Table S1 confirms the statistical details (supplementary table not included in this manuscript).
- **25% lifespan extension in MV389 vs ~10% in Viswanathan & Guarente 2011**: Authors attribute the difference to censoring worms with protruding vulva phenotype. This is a notable discrepancy -- the censoring approach may inflate the extension.
- **60-100 animals per condition**: Standard for C. elegans lifespan, but could not verify exact N from text alone (in Table S1).

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **Q1 (sirtuin activation causally extends lifespan):** Strong evidence from C. elegans. NAD+ boosting extends lifespan via sir-2.1; sir-2.1 overexpression extends lifespan; all effects are sir-2.1-dependent. However, all evidence is in model organisms, not humans.
- **Q3 (direct activation vs indirect NAD+ boosting):** Directly relevant -- shows that both PARP inhibitors (indirect, reducing NAD+ consumption) and NR/NAM (indirect, boosting NAD+ precursors) converge on the same sir-2.1 pathway. No synergy at optimal doses confirms shared mechanism.
- **Q4 (arguments against sirtuin-longevity hypothesis):** Authors acknowledge the Burnett et al. 2011 Nature paper showing absence of Sir2 overexpression effects, and the unlinked mutation issue. They argue the debate is about basal conditions vs stress/disease contexts. Their own sir-2.1 overexpression data supports a modest (~10-25%) effect.
- **Q5 (NAD+ precursor trials on functional outcomes):** Relevant preclinical evidence: NR improved worm motility, oxygen consumption, mitochondrial biogenesis, and ATP levels. Mammalian cell data shows similar effects but no functional organism-level outcomes in mammals.
