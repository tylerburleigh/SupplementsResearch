# src-133: NRK1 Controls NMN and NR Metabolism in Mammalian Cells

**Citation:** Ratajczak et al. (2016). *Nature Communications*, 7:13103. DOI: 10.1038/ncomms13103. Cited 337 times.

**Quality:** ok (full peer-reviewed article, substantive content)

## Core Findings

NRK1 (nicotinamide riboside kinase 1) is the rate-limiting and essential enzyme for converting exogenous NR and NMN into NAD+ in mammalian cells. Extracellular NMN must be dephosphorylated to NR before cellular uptake via equilibrative nucleoside transporters (ENTs), explaining why NR and NMN produce overlapping metabolic effects. NRK1 is dispensable for NAD+ synthesis from nicotinamide (NAM) and nicotinic acid (NA), which use distinct biosynthetic pathways.

## Key Evidence

### NRK1 is rate-limiting for NR/NMN-driven NAD+ synthesis
- In NIH/3T3 cells with low endogenous NRK1/2, overexpression of NRK1 or NRK2 converted them from NR-nonresponsive to NR-responsive (~4-fold NAD+ increase) (Fig 1c).
- Stable NRK1-overexpressing cell lines showed dose-dependent NAD+ increases with NR starting at 100 uM, maximal at ~1 mM (Fig 2b).
- Catalytically inactive NRK1-D36A mutant completely abolished NR-driven NAD+ synthesis.

### NMN must be dephosphorylated to NR extracellularly
- 18O-labeled NMN tracing in HepG2 cells showed rapid appearance of extracellular 18O-NR; 80% of extracellular 18O-NMN was present as 18O-NR by 7h (Fig 4d).
- CMP (competitive inhibitor of NMN dephosphorylation) blocked NMN-driven but not NR-driven NAD+ synthesis (Fig 6a).
- Double-labeled (13C-2H) NR and NMN in primary hepatocytes: NR produced 40-50% double-labeled NMN and NAD+ at 6h, while NMN produced no detectable double-labeled intracellular NMN (Fig 6b).

### NRK1 knockout eliminates NR/NMN utilization in hepatocytes
- NRK1KO primary hepatocytes completely failed to increase NAD+ with NR or NMN, while responding normally to NA and NAM (Fig 5e).
- NRK1KO mice (whole body, C57BL/6NTac background) showed no compensatory NRK2 upregulation.

### In vivo pharmacokinetics in NRK1KO mice
- IP 500 mg/kg NR increased liver NAD+ by ~220% in WT, ~170% for NAM and NMN (Fig 7a-b).
- NR and NMN responses were significantly blunted in NRK1KO mice in liver, kidney, and BAT, but ~60% of the effect remained (attributable to conversion to NAM in plasma).
- NR degrades in plasma: ~66% degraded after 1h with NAM appearing; NMN is stable in plasma.
- Skeletal muscle showed lowest response to all NAD+ precursors; even NRK1/NRK2 double-KO showed comparable increases to WT, suggesting NRK-independent mechanisms in muscle.

## Methodology

- **Cell models:** NIH/3T3, HepG2, AML12, Hepa1.6, C2C12, primary hepatocytes; NRK gain-of-function (transient/stable overexpression) and loss-of-function (siRNA, NRK1KO primary hepatocytes).
- **Animal model:** Constitutive Nmrk1 knockout mice (C57BL/6NTac), exons 3-7 deleted. NRK1/NRK2 double-KO also used.
- **Tracer studies:** 18O-labeled and double-labeled (13C-2H) NR/NMN with LC-MS/MS metabolomics.
- **NAD+ measurement:** EnzyChrom NAD/NADH Assay Kit and targeted LC-MS/MS.
- **In vivo dosing:** 500 mg/kg and 50 mg/kg IP injection, tissues collected at 1h, n=4-5 per group.
- **Statistics:** Two-tailed t-test for two groups; one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post-test for multiple groups. P<0.05 significant.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q5: NAD+ precursor trials on functional outcomes (skeletal muscle, physical performance)
- **Directly relevant to mechanism of NAD+ precursor metabolism in muscle.** Muscle showed the lowest response to NAD+ precursors among all tissues tested (liver, kidney, BAT, muscle). NRK1/NRK2 double-KO mice still responded to NR/NMN in muscle, suggesting muscle has NRK-independent pathways.
- No functional outcomes (strength, endurance, performance) were measured -- this is purely a mechanistic/metabolism study.
- Establishes that NMN must be converted to NR before uptake, which has implications for how oral NMN supplements might work in muscle tissue.

### Q2: Pharmacological sirtuin compounds in human clinical trials
- **Tangentially relevant.** The paper discusses sirtuin activation by NR/NMN (NAD+-dependent) but does not involve clinical trials or direct sirtuin-activating compounds (STACs). NR boosts sirtuin activity indirectly via NAD+ elevation, unlike pharmacological sirtuin activators.
- Notes that NAM is a sirtuin inhibitor, which differentiates NR/NMN from NAM supplementation.
- References a concurrent human clinical trial (Trammell et al. 2016, ref 24) showing NR safely boosts human NAD+ metabolism at the same effective dose used in mice.

## Limitations

1. **Acute dosing only** -- all in vivo experiments used single IP injection with 1h endpoint; no chronic supplementation data.
2. **No functional outcomes** -- only NAD+ levels and metabolomics; no measures of metabolic health, muscle function, or physical performance.
3. **IP route only** -- does not address oral bioavailability, which is the relevant route for supplementation.
4. **Mouse-only in vivo** -- no human data in this paper.
5. **Plasma NAM conversion confound** -- at pharmacological doses, NR and NMN partially convert to NAM in plasma, which could account for some effects via the NAMPT salvage pathway rather than the NRK pathway.
