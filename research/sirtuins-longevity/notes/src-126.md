# src-126: NAD+ metabolism: pathophysiologic mechanisms and therapeutic potential

**Citation:** Xie et al. (2020), *Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy* 5:227. DOI: 10.1038/s41392-020-00311-7
**Type:** Comprehensive review article (856 citations)
**Quality:** ok

## Core Findings

This is a major review of NAD+ metabolism covering biosynthesis, consumption, signaling roles, disease connections, and therapeutic strategies. The paper provides a systematic comparison of NAD+-consuming enzymes and their relative affinities for NAD+, revealing why indirect boosting (via PARP1/CD38 inhibition) may be more effective for sirtuin activation than precursor supplementation alone. It also catalogs extensive registered clinical trials of NAD+ precursors but notes that long-term safety remains unproven.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### NAD+ Consumer Competition (relevant to Q3)
- **SIRT1/SIRT3 Km for NAD+:** 94-888 uM -- their activity fluctuates with cellular NAD+ levels
- **SIRT2, SIRT4, SIRT5, SIRT6 Km:** Below physiological range -- NAD+ is not rate-limiting for these
- **PARP1 Km:** 20-97 uM; accounts for ~90% of NAD+ consumed by the PARP family
- **CD38/SARM1 Km:** 15-25 uM -- lowest affinity, meaning they consume NAD+ preferentially
- **Implication:** PARP1 and CD38, with lower Km values than sirtuins, can outcompete sirtuins for available NAD+. Inhibiting PARP1 or CD38 effectively increases NAD+ availability and activates SIRT1.

### Therapeutic Strategies for NAD+ Boosting (Q3)
Four broad strategies identified:
1. **Precursor supplementation:** NMN, NR, NAM, NA (different pharmacokinetics and tissue specificities)
2. **Inhibition of NAD+ consumers:** PARP inhibitors (niraparib, rucaparib, olaparib -- FDA-approved for cancer); CD38 inhibitors (78c, apigenin, quercetin)
3. **Enhancement of biosynthesis enzymes:** NAMPT enhancers (P7C3, SBI-797812); ACMSD inhibitors (TES-991, TES-1025); NNMT inhibitors
4. **Non-pharmacologic:** Caloric restriction (via NAMPT upregulation), exercise (via AMPK-NAMPT axis)

### Tissue-Specific NAD+ Precursor Effects (Q3, Q5)
- NMN significantly elevates NAD+ in white adipose tissue, heart, and liver but NOT in brown adipose tissue or kidney
- NAM exerts feedback inhibition on SIRT1 and depletes methyl groups via NNMT-mediated methylation -- limiting its utility
- NR bypasses the NAMPT salvage pathway via ENT transport and NRK1/2 phosphorylation, but has short circulatory stability
- NRH (reduced NR) provides better NAD+ boosting via an NRK1/2-independent pathway

### Clinical Trial Landscape (Q5)
- Extensive table of registered clinical trials (NCT numbers) for NR, NAM, and niacin across cardiovascular disease, neurodegeneration, metabolic disorders, cancer, and aging
- **Human data on NR:** Oral NR in aged participants elevated muscle NAD+ metabolome, ameliorated metabolic dysfunction, reduced inflammatory cytokines, increased adiponectin
- **Preclinical NMN data:** Long-term NMN in aged animals rescued mitochondrial function, insulin sensitivity, bone density, eye function, and cognitive decline

### Safety Concerns
- NMN treatment accelerates pancreatic cancer progression via inflammatory environment
- Sirtuins have both oncogenic and tumor-suppressive roles in different contexts
- NAM at high doses depletes methyl groups and inhibits SIRT1 via product feedback
- Niacin causes cutaneous flushing via GPR109A activation
- Long-term safety of NAD+ precursors in humans not yet established

### NAD+ Decline and Aging
- CD38 expression rises with age, playing a vital role in age-associated NAD+ reduction
- Intracellular NAD+ normally maintained at 0.2-0.5 mM depending on cell type
- Subcellular distribution: ~70 uM cytoplasmic, ~110 uM nuclear, ~90 uM mitochondrial (U2OS cells)

## Methodology

Comprehensive narrative review covering NAD+ biosynthesis (de novo, Preiss-Handler, salvage pathways), consumption enzymes, subcellular distribution, systemic metabolism, and disease connections. Evidence drawn from preclinical (cell culture, mouse models) and clinical studies. 653 references cited. No new primary data presented.

## Limitations

- Review article -- no new experimental data
- Published 2020; does not cover more recent clinical trial results (e.g., NIAGEN, MaAC, etc.)
- Clinical trial table lists registered trials but does not report completed trial outcomes
- Many claims about precursor efficacy are based on preclinical animal data
- PDF conversion quality is degraded (some garbled text from OCR of multi-column layout)

## Claims to Verify

- **Km ranges for sirtuins and PARP1:** These are reported from multiple primary sources (refs 5, 36-49). The wide range for SIRT1/SIRT3 (94-888 uM) spans order of magnitude -- should note this reflects different assay conditions and isoforms.
- **90% of NAD+ consumed by PARP family goes through PARP1:** Sourced from refs 47-49; this is a widely cited figure.
- **NMN accelerates pancreatic cancer progression:** Referenced to sources 66 and 260; this is an important safety claim that should be verified in the primary literature.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q3 (Direct sirtuin activation vs indirect NAD+ boosting):** Highly relevant. Provides the mechanistic rationale for why indirect boosting (PARP1/CD38 inhibition, NAD+ precursor supplementation) may be more practical than direct sirtuin activation -- the Km competition framework explains that raising NAD+ levels preferentially benefits SIRT1/SIRT3 whose high Km makes them NAD+-sensitive. Also covers NAMPT enhancers (P7C3, SBI-797812) as a third approach distinct from both direct activation and precursor supplementation.

**Q5 (NAD+ precursor trials on functional outcomes):** Highly relevant. Documents the breadth of registered clinical trials and reports available human functional outcome data for NR (metabolic improvement, anti-inflammatory effects in aged participants). Notes the critical gap: most trials are registered but few published results available as of 2020.
