Abstract 24 
SIRT6 is a protein deacylase, deacetylase, and mono-ADP-ribosylase (mADPr) 25 
regulating biological pathways important for longevity including DNA repair and silencing 26 
of LINE1 retrotransposons. SIRT6 knockout mice die by 30 days of age, whereas SIRT6 27 
overexpression increases lifespan in male mice. Finding safe pharmacological 28 
activators of SIRT6 would have clinical benefits. Fucoidan, a polysaccharide purified 29 
from brown seaweed, has been identified as an activator of SIRT6 deacetylation activity. 30 
Here, we show that fucoidan also activates SIRT6 mADPr activity, which was shown to 31 
be elevated in certain human centenarians. Administering fucoidan to aged mice led to 32 
a significant increase in median lifespan in male mice. Both male and female mice 33 
demonstrated a marked reduction in frailty and epigenetic age. Fucoidan-treated mice 34 
showed repression of LINE1 elements suggesting that the beneficial effects of fucoidan 35 
are mediated, at least in part, by SIRT6. As brown seaweed rich in fucoidan is a popular 36 
food item in South Korea and Japan, countries with the highest life expectancy, we 37 
propose that fucoidan supplementation should be explored as a safe strategy for 38 
activating SIRT6 and improving human healthspan and lifespan. 39

Introduction 42 
Sirtuins are NAD+-dependent deacetylases/deacylases and ADP-43 
ribosyltransferases involved in the regulation of organismal lifespan in numerous 44 
species ranging from yeast to mammals1,2. SIRT6, a mammalian sirtuin, regulates gene 45 
expression, metabolism, epigenome maintenance, and genomic stability. Under 46 
conditions of cellular oxidative stress, SIRT6 is phosphorylated by JNK 3 and promotes 47 
DNA repair by mono-ADP ribosylation and activation of PARP14. Additionally, the ability 48 
of overexpressed SIRT6 to promote DNA double-strand break repair correlates with 49 
species lifespan in rodents 5. SIRT6 knockout mice have a severely shortened lifespan 50 
characterized by genomic instability and defects in DNA repair, as well as broad 51 
degenerative phenotypes and metabolic defects 6. Mice deficient in SIRT6 also 52 
experience de-repression of LINE1 transposons, which causes an inflammatory 53 
response through the accumulation of cytoplasmic LINE1 cDNA 7. Conversely, mice 54 
overexpressing SIRT6 experience extended lifespan and reduced frailty 8. As such, 55 
SIRT6 pharmacological activation is a promising therapeutic target to alleviate age-56 
related frailty and extend lifespan.  57 
SIRT6 has several related but distinct catalytic activities and targets. These 58 
include histone deacetylase activity on H3K9 9, H3K18 10, and H3K56 11,12. Additionally, 59 
SIRT6 can act as a protein mono-ADP ribosyltransferase, with multiple known 60 
substrates involved in chromatin modification and DNA repair, including PARP1 4, 61 
TRIM28 (KAP1) 13, KDM2 14, and BAF170 15. Remarkably, a SIRT6 variant enriched in 62 
human centenarians was shown to have enhanced mono-ADP-ribosylation (mADPr) 63 
activity, pointing toward a specific importance of this activity in the longevity-promoting 64 
effects of SIRT6 16. 65 
Prior studies have identified several small molecule activators of SIRT6 66 
deacetylation activity that showed beneficial biological effects in cell culture. Small-67 
molecule allosteric activators of SIRT6 deacetylation MDL-800 and MDL-801 exerted 68 
antiproliferative effects in non-small cell lung carcinoma cells 17 and improved genome 69 
stability18. The related activator MDL-811 alleviated neuroinflammation in mice 19. A few 70 
natural polyphenolic compounds, including quercetin and cyanidin, have also been 71 
reported to activate SIRT6 deacetylation activity in vitro 20,21. No chemical activators of 72 
SIRT6 mADPr activity have been reported. 73 
Fucoidan is a complex sulfated polysaccharide found in brown algae and other 74 
marine organisms where it serves a structural and protective role. The main monomeric 75 
subunit of the fucoidan polymer is the monosaccharide fucose, although other 76 
monosaccharides are also incorporated into fucoidan in smaller quantities. Seaweed 77 
and fucoidan have a long history as traditional remedies and continue to be used as 78 
complementary medicine for various ailments today in countries such as Japan and 79 
South Korea. Fucoidan was found to have many therapeutic properties 22. Fucoidan is 80 
efficiently absorbed through the intestine in humans 23 and oral supplementation of 81 
fucoidan increases muscle size and strength 24,25, mitigates liver injury 26,27, enhances 82 
immunity and alleviates inflammation28 in mouse models. Fucoidan also showed health 83

Results 96 
Fucoidan stimulates SIRT6 mADPr activity 97 
As SIRT6 mADPr activity is specifically enhanced in certain human centenarians 98 
with rare coding variants in SIRT6 16, we sought to identify activators of this activity. We 99 
tested several molecules previously reported to stimulate SIRT6 deacetylase activity 100 
using our mADPr assay 4. The tested compounds were added to in vitro  reactions 101 
containing purified SIRT6 and 32P-labeled NAD+, and SIRT6 mADPr activity was 102 
quantified by autoradiography. Of the compounds tested, only fucoidan increased SIRT6 103 
mADPr activity (t-test: p = 0.005, Figure 1A ). Interestingly, most of the other SIRT6 104 
deacetylase activators showed an inhibitory effect. We also tested the effect of fucoidan 105 
on the ability of SIRT6 to deacetylate H3K9ac in nucleosomes, and found that fucoidan 106 
stimulated SIRT6 deacetylase activity, as reported previously 29 ( Figure 1B). We next 107 
compared several different fucoidan extracts from different seaweed species and 108 
sources to determine their relative effects on SIRT6 mADPr activity. Highly purified 109 
extract of F . vesiculosus showed the strongest stimulation of ribosylation (t-test: p = 110 
0.001) with highly purified extracts from U. pinnatifida and C. okamuranus  as well as 111 
nutrition supplement grade F . vesiculosus fucoidan supplied by DoNotAge (D.N.A.) also 112 
showing stimulation of ribosylation activity (t-test: p = 0.016, p = 0.016 and p = 0.028, 113 
Figure 1C ). Titration of fucoidan extract concentrations showed that stimulation of 114 
mADPr activity approached saturation at concentrations of 0.2-0.5 mg/mL ( Figure 1D). 115 
L-fucose, the sugar monomer found in fucoidan, had very mild stimulatory effect on 116 
SIRT6 deacetylation activity and no significant effect on mADPr activity (Figure 1B, C). 117 
In summary, most compounds that stimulate SIRT6 deacetylation had no or even 118 
inhibitory effects on mADPr, suggesting that these catalytic activities generally require 119 
different chemical activators. Fucoidan was the only tested compound displaying strong 120 
stimulatory effect of both deacetylation and mADPr activities of SIRT6. 121

Fucoidan reduces frailty in aged mice and extends median lifespan of male mice 123 
To assess the effects of fucoidan on the aging process in vivo, we administered 124 
fucoidan in drinking water and chow (a total average dose of 278 mg/day) to a cohort of 125 
44 mice (22 male and 22 female) and 36 control mice starting at 15 months of age 126 
(Figure 2A). The mice were monitored for the remainder of their life to assess effects of 127 
fucoidan on lifespan. Additionally, mouse health was assessed using the Howlett frailty 128 
index33, between 19 and 25 months of age. Blood samples were collected from live 129 
animals at the ages of 22 and 26 months. For analysis of tissues, smaller cohorts of 5 130 
male and 5 female mice were treated with fucoidan for 1 month before tissues were 131 
harvested. 132 
Fucoidan treatment significantly extended the median lifespan of males by 13% 133 
(Gehan-Breslow test: p = 0.009, Figure 2B). Female lifespan, on the other hand, only 134 
showed a non-significant increase by 3% (Gehan-Breslow test: p = 0.12). Furthermore, 135 
fucoidan-treated mice of both sexes had lower DNA methylation age relative to controls 136 
at 26 months (Mann-Whitney U test, p = 0.037, Figure 2C). Fucoidan treatment did not 137 
have any effect on body mass (Figure S1A ) indicating that mice did not change food 138 
intake in response to fucoidan in chow. This excludes health and longevity effects due to 139 
calorie restriction. Frailty scores for both males and females, assessed monthly starting 140 
at 19 months of age, increased at a slower rate in fucoidan-treated animals relative to 141 
controls (Linear mixed model, interaction term between diet and treatment time, p = 142 
0.046 for males, p = 0.006 for females, Figure 2D). A breakdown of frailty components 143 
by organ/system revealed reduced frailty across nearly all systems in fucoidan-treated 144 
mice (Figure S1B).  145 
To determine whether the lifespan-extending effects of fucoidan are SIRT6-146 
mediated, we measured lifespan in SIRT6 knockout (SIRT6 KO) mice with or without 147 
fucoidan supplementation. In support of a SIRT6-mediated lifespan effect, no significant 148 
lifespan difference between fucoidan-fed and control SIRT6 KO mice could be observed 149 
(Gehan-Breslow test: p = 0.87, Figure S2A ). We additionally looked for molecular 150 
evidence of increased SIRT6 activity in vivo. Detection of mADPr in vivo is complicated 151 
due to the absence of sensitive antibodies. Therefore, we measured acetylation of the 152 
SIRT6 substrate H3K9 (H3K9ac) in the lung and liver of mice treated with fucoidan for 1 153 
month. Fucoidan treatment resulted in reduction of H3K9ac in the lungs of male mice 154 
(Mann-Whitney U test, p = 0.015, Figure S2B ). Additionally, both lungs and liver of 155 
female mice showed a downward trend in H3K9ac (Mann-Whitney U test, p = 0.09, 156 
0.072 respectively, Figure S2B).  157 
In conclusion, fucoidan treatment provides systemic health benefits in both 158 
sexes, and extends the lifespan of male mice by 13%. These effects are likely to be 159 
mediated, at least in part, by enzymatic activation of SIRT6. 160

Fucoidan downregulates aging and inflammation-related pathways and increases 161 
pathways related to DNA repair and chromatin maintenance 162 
We performed RNA-seq on blood samples from 22 months old mice involved in 163 
the aging study. In males, 926 genes were upregulated, and 2272 genes were 164 
downregulated in fucoidan-treated versus control mice ( Figure 3A ), whereas in 165 
females, no gene reached the threshold for differential expression ( Figure S3A). GSEA 166 
on Gene Ontology biological process terms revealed upregulation of terms related to 167 
RNA modification and B and T cell differentiation in males ( Figure 3B). Downregulated 168 
terms were related to blood coagulation, myeloid identity, translation, stress and 169 
interferon response. Interestingly, fucoidan has been previously investigated as an 170 
anticoagulant, with promising results in vivo and in vitro36-38. ORA yielded similar results 171 
as GSEA, confirming upregulation of RNA processing pathways, T cell differentiation, 172 
and down regulation of coagulation and myeloid differentiation (Figure 3C). Additionally, 173 
ORA showed that upregulated genes were enriched for pathways that are regulated by 174 
SIRT6 such as DNA repair, telomere maintenance, histone modification and chromatin 175 
organization. Finally, the term “Aging” was downregulated in male mice, confirming the 176 
anti-aging effect according to the methylation clock. Coagulation was also 177 
downregulated in females, according to GSEA, however inflammatory pathways 178 
(response to lipopolysaccharide, IL-1ß and TNFα) were upregulated (Figure S3B).  179 
As various terms related to lymphoid and myeloid cell identity were respectively 180 
upregulated and downregulated in males ( Figure 3B), we asked if enrichment of these 181 
terms may be due to compositional changes in the blood rather than cell intrinsic 182 
effects. We used mMCP-counter 34 to estimate blood cell composition from bulk data 183 
and found that male mice had a higher B cell and T cell fraction (Mann-Whitney U test, 184 
B cells: p = 0.024, T cells: p = 0.024, Figure 3E ), whereas no major immune cell 185 
populations were different in females ( Figure S3D). Fucoidan was previously shown to 186 
induce B and T cell expansion in vitro 35. Aging is known to induce a myeloid bias in 187 
hematopoietic stem cell differentiation, which in turn contributes to impaired immune 188 
memory and sterile inflammation in aged individuals. Thus, the higher proportion of 189 
lymphoid cells in fucoidan-treated male mice reflect the anti-aging effect of fucoidan.   190 
As terms related to interferon response (IFN) were downregulated in males 191 
(Figure 3B), we measured cytokines levels in the plasma using an ELISA panel. TNF α 192 
and IFNα  and γ  as well as Ccl2 and several pro-inflammatory interleukins such as IL-3, 193 
IL-1β , IL-6 tended to show lower abundance in the plasma of male fucoidan-treated 194 
compared to control mice ( Figure 3D ). Conversely, cytokine levels tended to be 195 
increased in female fucoidan-treated compared to control mice ( Figure S3C ), 196 
suggesting a sex-dependent effect of fucoidan on inflammatory profiles. 197 
In summary, long-term treatment with fucoidan induced strong changes in the 198 
blood of male mice, including upregulation of SIRT-6 related pathways as DNA repair 199 
and chromatin maintenance and the associated downregulation of proinflammatory 200 
cytokines. Conversely, the blood of female mice showed much weaker differences, 201

Fucoidan and Sirt6 overexpression exert a similar anti-inflammatory effect on 204 
male liver 205 
Next, we performed RNA-seq on liver and lung samples collected after 1 month 206 
of fucoidan treatment starting at 15 months of age (Figure 4A ). GO biological process 207 
analysis was then used on genes that were differentially expressed in the same 208 
direction across tissues or sexes (e.g., p.adj < 0.2 and logFC > 0, Figure S4A, B ). 209 
Briefly, pathways upregulated in both male and female lungs were related to protein 210 
folding, while downregulated ones were related to coagulation, consistently with the 211 
results seen in blood. There was minimal overlap between genes upregulated in male 212 
and female livers, as well as between those downregulated in both. Given the liver's 213 
central role in processing dietary metabolites, the differing responses to fucoidan 214 
treatment in male and female livers may explain the sexually dimorphic effects of 215 
fucoidan, including its impact on lifespan extension.  216 
To better understand the effects of fucoidan in the context of aging, we compared 217 
the changes in the tissues of fucoidan fed mice to bulk RNA-seq data from Tabula Muris 218 
Senis (TMS)39. We observed a negative correlation between the effects of aging in male 219 
lungs and those of fucoidan treatment ( Figure 4C, E ). Interestingly, multiple genes 220 
related to protein folding were downregulated during aging but upregulated by fucoidan 221 
treatment. The effect of fucoidan on male liver was not correlated with age-related 222 
changes (Figure S5A). Conversely, the effect of fucoidan on female liver and lung was 223 
positively correlated with the effect of aging (Figure S5B, C ). Thus, it seems possible 224 
that fucoidan treatment may constitute a mild stress in females, at least with short-term 225 
treatment.  226 
Finally, we compared the effect of fucoidan with that of Sirt6 overexpression, 227 
using liver Sirt6 overexpression data generated by Roichman et al8. In male liver, the 228 
effect of fucoidan treatment was positively correlated with that of Sirt6 overexpression, 229 
with both treatments inducing downregulation of pro-inflammatory genes and pathways 230 
(Figure 4D, F ). Among the highly downregulated genes, Saa1/2 and Orm2 are part of 231 
the acute-phase response, which stimulates the liver to secrete various inflammatory 232 
and coagulant proteins in response to infection or injury. The acute phase response is 233 
primarily induced by circulating IL-6 and TNF α , which were reduced in our plasma data 234 
(Figure 3C). Once again, female liver showed the opposite, albeit weaker, trend (Figure 235 
S5D).  236 
In conclusion, short-term treatment with fucoidan produced highly sexually 237 
dimorphic responses. Nonetheless, fucoidan upregulated protein folding and 238 
downregulated coagulation related pathways in both male and female lungs. Protein 239 
folding pathways were downregulated during aging in lung data from TMS, thus 240 
fucoidan appears to restore protein folding to more youthful levels. Finally, fucoidan and 241 
Sirt6 over-expression exerted a similar effect on male liver, resulting in the 242 
downregulation of inflammatory pathways, including the acute phase response. 243

Fucoidan induces LINE1 silencing in lungs 245 
SIRT6 plays important role in silencing LINE1 transposable elements by 246 
recruiting heterochromatin factors and promoting formation of repressive 247 
heterochromatin13. Sirt6 KO mice and cells exhibit LINE1 derepression, expression, and 248 
cytosolic LINE1 cDNA accumulation, resulting in chronic inflammation driven by the 249 
cGAS/STING cytosolic DNA sensor 40. Similarly, LINE1 transposons undergo 250 
derepression during aging 40-42 and silencing them has the potential to increase 251 
healthspan and lifespan.  252 
Given the function of SIRT6 in silencing LINE1 elements, we quantified the 253 
expression of LINE1 families in our liver and lung data. Additionally, we performed 254 
ATAC-seq and MeDIP-seq to measure the chromatin accessibility and DNA methylation 255 
at LINE1s, respectively. In general, some LINE1s families were downregulated, and 256 
others were upregulated, following fucoidan treatment. However, in male and female 257 
lungs, expression of the vast majority of LINE1 families was downregulated, as 258 
highlighted by the asymmetry of the volcano plots ( Figure 5A ). The number of 259 
downregulated families was larger than what would be expected assuming equal 260 
likelihood of down- or up-regulation (Binomial test). Consistently, the majority of LINE1 261 
families had reduced accessibility and increased DNA methylation in the lungs of 262 
fucoidan treated mice. The same analysis performed in liver tissue did not yield 263 
conclusive results ( Figure S5E ). In particular, some omic modalities indicated 264 
repression while others indicated derepression. This discrepancy may be due to 265 
different biases in terms of interrogated genomic regions between the techniques.   266 
Transposable elements are known to undergo activation upon genotoxic stress43. 267 
We therefore tested whether fucoidan treatment ameliorates stress-induced LINE1 268 
activation. We administered paraquat, an inducer of oxidative stress, to mice fed with 269 
fucoidan or control diet for 1 month. Paraquat caused strong upregulation of expression 270 
of the active LINE1 subfamily L1MdA in the lungs of control mice. This activation was 271 
significantly ameliorated in mice receiving fucoidan ( Figure 5B). Interestingly, fucoidan-272 
fed mice also showed lower levels of expression of CDKN1a, encoding the cyclin 273 
dependent kinase inhibitor p21 Cip1, upon paraquat treatment (Figure 5C ), suggesting 274 
that fucoidan has protective effect against genotoxic stress.  275 
In summary, these results indicate that fucoidan treatment promotes silencing of 276 
active LINE1 elements in the lungs, resulting in reduced p21 Cip1 levels, a marker of 277 
genotoxic stress. As SIRT6 promotes LINE1 silencing and DNA repair these results are 278 
consistent with the effect of fucoidan being mediated by SIRT6 activation. 279

Discussion 281 
As SIRT6 has been referred to as “the real aging Sirtuin” 44, significant effort has 282 
been put into identifying chemical SIRT6 activators. Some of these molecules were 283

shown to have beneficial effects in cell culture and in vivo17-19. Here we report that the 284 
SIRT6 activator, fucoidan, extends healthspan and lifespan in vivo in wild type mice, in 285 
particular, in male mice. Importantly, the extension was observed after starting fucoidan 286 
supplementation in middle-aged mice, suggesting that there is a benefit of initiating 287 
fucoidan treatment later in life. However, although there was a life extension only in 288 
male mice, fucoidan treatment led to a marked reduction in frailty in both male and 289 
female mice.  290 
Other SIRT6 activators were identified based on their enhancing effect on 291 
deacetylase activity. In contrast, fucoidan activates both deacetylation and mADPr 292 
activities. Importantly, mADPr activity of SIRT6 is required for LINE1 inhibition 7, a 293 
function which may be the key to reversing age-related inflammation and frailty. 294 
Consistently, we observed decreased inflammation in multiple tissues, and decreased 295 
LINE1 expression in lung tissue.  296 
We show that not every species of brown algae produces fucoidan that activates 297 
SIRT6. Fucoidan is not a defined chemical and may consist of different ratios of fucose 298 
and galactose molecules which may be sulfated on different residues and form 299 
branched structures45. Hence only some species of brown algae produce fucoidan that 300 
has the chemical structure required for SIRT6 activation. As fucoidan is broken down in 301 
digestive tract, it is likely that a smaller, but not monomeric, fucoidan derivative with a 302 
defined chemical structure is responsible for SIRT6 activation. In the future, through 303 
carbohydrate chemistry, it should be possible to identify the minimal fucoidan unit with 304 
SIRT6 activating properties.  305 
Fucoidan is remarkable because of its safety and the geographical locations 306 
where it is a food staple. Brown algae are a popular food in South Korea and Japan, the 307 
two countries with the longest life expectancies. Thus, fucoidan supplementation is a 308 
safe human intervention that may potentially improve quality of life in the elderly 309 
population. While a number of clinical trials had been conducted using fucoidan 310 
supplementation none of them had frailty or aging biomarkers as primary endpoints. 311 
Thus, a human clinical trial with fucoidan focused on healthspan and biomarkers of 312 
aging including analysis of LINE-1 expression and the epigenetic clock is needed.  313 
Limitations of the study:  As fucoidan is a natural product with complex structure we 314 
cannot rule out that it has other cellular targets beyond SIRT6. However, as we show 315 
that fucoidan treatment affects SIRT6 targets such as H3K9ac and LINE1 transposons 316 
we conclude that at least some of the beneficial effects of fucoidan are mediated by 317 
SIRT6. Additionally, fucoidan exerted no beneficial effects on SIRT6 KO mice. 318

EXPERIMENTAL MODEL AND SUBJECT DETAILS 335 
Overview of mouse cohorts and experimental design 336 
We used middle-aged (15-month-old) C57BL/6JN mice obtained from the 337 
National Institute on Aging (NIA) for all in vivo studies.  Animals were maintained under 338 
a 12-hour light/dark cycle with ad libitum access to food and water. Upon arrival, the 339 
mice were housed in groups of 3–4 per cage and closely monitored for well-being. 340 
Following an acclimatization period, animals were randomly and blindly assigned to 341 
different treatment groups. All experimental procedures involving mice were approved 342 
by the University Animal Research Committee (UCAR) at the University of Rochester 343 
and were performed in compliance with institutional guidelines. 344 
Aging study cohort and fucoidan administration 345 
The aging study comprised 80 mice (40 male, 40 female). 44 mice received 346 
fucoidan in their diet, and the remaining 36 were fed a control diet. F . vesiculosus 347 
fucoidan for in vivo  mouse studies was obtained from DoNotAge.org. Fucoidan was 348 
administered to the mice using two methods – by adding it to their drinking water and by 349 
mixing it into their chow. For the drinking water, we prepared Fucoidan at a 350 
concentration of 10 g/L. The drinking water was changed three times a week. Since 351 
each mouse typically drinks 5 mL of water per day, they received roughly 50 mg of 352 
fucoidan daily from this route. At the same time, fucoidan was mixed into the diet by 353 
combining 8 g of the compound with 150 g of dry chow. With adult mice eating around 354 
4-5 g of chow per day, this results in an intake of approximately 203-253 mg of fucoidan 355 
per day. Overall, the mice received a total daily dose of about 253-303 mg of fucoidan 356 
per day, averaging around 278 mg of fucoidan per day. Frailty was scored once a month 357 
beginning at 19 months of age and continuing until they were 25 months old. Frailty 358
