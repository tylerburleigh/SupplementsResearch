# src-206: Creatine Supplementation and Cognitive Function in Adults (Xu et al., 2024)

**Citation:** Xu C, Bi S, Zhang W, Luo L (2024). Frontiers in Nutrition 11:1424972. DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2024.1424972
**Type:** Systematic review with meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO registered CRD42024533557)
**Citations:** 30

## Core Findings

This meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (492 participants, aged 20.8-76.4 years) found that creatine monohydrate supplementation significantly improved memory (SMD = 0.31, p < 0.00001, moderate GRADE certainty), attention time (SMD = -0.31, p = 0.03), and processing speed time (SMD = -0.51, p = 0.04), but did not significantly improve overall cognitive function or executive function. Benefits were more pronounced in individuals with diseases, adults aged 18-60, and females.

## Key Evidence

### Memory (strongest finding)
- SMD = 0.31 (95% CI: 0.18-0.44), Hedges's g = 0.3003
- 24 comparisons from pooled studies, I2 = 21% (low heterogeneity)
- GRADE certainty: **moderate** -- the most robust finding in this review

### Attention time
- SMD = -0.31 (95% CI: -0.58 to -0.03), p = 0.03
- 8 studies, 211 participants, I2 = 18%
- Attention scores themselves were NOT significant (SMD = 0.22, p = 0.49)

### Processing speed time
- SMD = -0.51 (95% CI: -1.01 to -0.01), p = 0.04
- 8 studies, 185 participants, I2 = 63% (moderate heterogeneity)
- Processing speed scores were NOT significant (SMD = 0.01, p = 0.97)

### No significant effects
- Overall cognitive function: SMD = 0.34, p = 0.22, I2 = 75% (high heterogeneity)
- Executive function: SMD = 0.32, p = 0.12, I2 = 0%
- Executive function time: SMD = -0.03, p = 0.89

## Subgroup Analysis Highlights

| Subgroup | Finding | SMD | p |
|----------|---------|-----|---|
| Diseased vs Healthy (attention time) | Significant only in diseased | -0.52 vs -0.18 | 0.02 vs 0.44 |
| Age 18-60 vs >60 (attention time) | Significant only in 18-60 | -0.55 vs 0.12 | 0.002 vs 0.61 |
| Female vs Male (processing speed time) | Significant only in females | -0.87 vs -0.35 | 0.01 vs 0.29 |
| Short vs Long duration | No significant difference | -0.62 vs -0.21 | 0.61 vs 0.23 |

## Methodology

- **Search:** PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science (Jan 1993 - Jun 2024); 2,326 records screened, 16 RCTs included
- **Participants:** 492 total, ages 20.8-76.4; 13 studies in healthy adults, 3 in patients (fibromyalgia, Parkinson's MCI, schizophrenia)
- **Intervention:** All used creatine monohydrate; doses ranged from 3-20 g/day; durations from 7 days to 18 months
- **Analysis:** SMD with Hedge's g, random-effects or fixed-effect model based on heterogeneity; RevMan 5.4 and Stata 16
- **Risk of bias:** Cochrane RoB 2.0; low-to-moderate risk overall
- **Publication bias:** Egger's test non-significant for all outcomes

## Mechanisms Beyond ATP Buffering (Q6)

The discussion outlines several proposed mechanisms:
1. **Brain energy supply:** Phosphocreatine maintains ATP in energy-demanding brain regions
2. **Neurotransmitter function:** May increase acetylcholine synthesis
3. **Neuromodulator role:** Creatine present in synaptic vesicles, released upon stimulation, taken up by synaptosomes (citing Bian et al. 2023 eLife) -- potential direct neurotransmitter role
4. **Neuroprotection:** Mitigates oxidative stress damage to brain cells (citing Sestili et al. 2006)
5. **Synaptic plasticity:** May affect synaptic efficacy and plasticity relevant to learning and memory

These mechanisms are discussed based on cited literature rather than directly tested in the meta-analysis.

## Limitations

1. Small total sample size (492 across 16 studies) limits statistical power
2. Heterogeneity in study designs, testing methods, and populations
3. Most studies focused on healthy adults; limited evidence for elderly and neurodegenerative disease patients
4. GRADE certainty was moderate only for memory; low for processing speed, attention, executive function, and overall cognitive function
5. No significant effects in adults over 60 -- concerning for longevity applications
6. The memory analysis reported 24 comparisons but only 16 studies -- multiple cognitive tests per study inflated the comparison count (1,000 participant-count for memory likely reflects double-counting)

## Claims to Verify

- **Memory participant count (N=1,000):** The text states "Twenty-four studies, encompassing a total of 1,000 participants" for memory, but only 16 studies were included with 492 total participants. This likely reflects counting multiple test comparisons within the same study participants, not distinct participants. The abstract correctly states 492 participants total.
- **PROSPERO registration:** Stated as CRD42024533557; should be verified for protocol consistency.
- **Overall participant count:** 492 participants across 16 RCTs seems reasonable (average ~31 per study), consistent with the small sample sizes noted as a limitation.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (Healthspan/cognitive in non-athletes):** Highly relevant. This is the most comprehensive meta-analysis to date on creatine and cognitive function in adults. Provides Level 1a evidence (SR with meta-analysis) that creatine improves memory and processing speed in non-athletes. The subgroup finding that benefits are stronger in diseased populations and the null finding in adults over 60 are important caveats for longevity applications.

**Q6 (Mechanisms beyond ATP):** Moderately relevant. The discussion section reviews mechanistic literature on neurotransmitter function, synaptic vesicle release, and neuroprotection, but this is a meta-analysis and does not provide primary mechanistic data. Cites Bian et al. 2023 on creatine as a potential neurotransmitter.
