# src-035: Variables Influencing the Effectiveness of Creatine Supplementation as a Therapeutic Intervention for Sarcopenia

**Authors:** Candow DG, Forbes SC, Chilibeck PD, Cornish SM, Antonio J, Kreider RB
**Year:** 2019 | **Venue:** Frontiers in Nutrition | **Citations:** 62 | **Type:** Review/Perspective

## Core Findings

This perspective paper examines why creatine supplementation shows inconsistent results for sarcopenia in aging adults. Three meta-analyses collectively show creatine plus resistance training significantly increases muscle mass (~1.21 kg pooled), upper- and lower-body maximal strength, and some physical performance measures. However, individual study variability is high, driven by baseline creatine status, muscle fiber type, diet, training supervision, sex, and disease status. The primary mechanism in aging adults appears to be reduction of protein degradation (via mitochondrial protection) rather than increased protein synthesis.

## Key Evidence

### Meta-analysis pooled results (aging adults >45-57 years)
- Muscle mass increase: +0.94 kg (Candow et al., >300 participants), +1.33 kg (Devries & Phillips, >200 participants), +1.37 kg (Chilibeck et al., >700 participants)
- Pooled average across meta-analyses: +1.21 kg lean mass
- Upper- and lower-body maximal strength: significant improvements across all three meta-analyses

### Dosing strategies (Q4)
- Loading phase protocols: 20 g/day for 5-7 days, then 3-5 g/day or 0.1 g/kg/day maintenance
- Some studies used steady low-dose: 5 g/day without loading
- One study used 0.3 g/kg/day loading then 0.07 g/kg/day maintenance
- Post-exercise creatine timing may enhance muscle mass gains vs. pre-exercise (SMD 0.52, 95% CI 0.03-1.00, p=0.04) based on small meta-analysis (3 studies, n=80)
- No strength differences between pre- and post-exercise timing

### Safety and null findings (Q5)
- Self-reported adverse events: muscle cramping, muscle pulls/strains, GI irritation (bloating, constipation)
- Postmenopausal women on creatine for 52 weeks: significantly higher adverse event rate vs placebo (p<0.05)
- No adverse effects on kidney function (creatinine clearance, GFR, microalbumin) or liver function (bilirubin, AST, ALT, ALP) across multiple studies
- Safe in clinical populations: Parkinson disease, type II diabetes
- Null findings in: COPD patients (n=80, 7 weeks), type II diabetes (n=25, 12 weeks), small/short studies (Bermon et al., n=32, 52 days)
- Null findings partly attributed to concurrent aerobic+resistance training (muscle interference effect), inadequate supervision, and small sample sizes
- No evidence of cytotoxicity (urinary formaldehyde) in aging males

### Mechanisms beyond ATP buffering (Q6)
- **Calcium handling:** Creatine increases calcium uptake into sarcoplasmic reticulum, shortening muscle relaxation time and enhancing force development
- **Protein degradation reduction:** Primary mechanism in aging adults; creatine reduces urinary 3-methylhistidine (in males), leucine oxidation
- **Mitochondrial protection:** Creatine taken up by mitochondria helps maintain energy status; protects against mitochondrial DNA mutations and oxidative damage (cell culture evidence)
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Reduced TNF-alpha and IL-6 in aging adults taking multi-ingredient supplement containing creatine (cannot isolate creatine effect)
- **GLUT-4 upregulation:** Creatine (5 g/day) increased membrane GLUT-4 content in type II diabetes patients during exercise training
- **Glycogen sparing:** Creatine spared muscle glycogen in rat model during intermittent exercise
- **NOT supported in aging adults:** No evidence for creatine increasing IGF-1, MRFs, satellite cell activation, or mTOR signaling specifically in older populations (evidence exists only in younger individuals)
- **Carnosine enhancement:** Lifetime creatine supplementation in senescence-accelerated mice increased muscle carnosine at middle age (antioxidant, anti-glycation, pH buffering)

## Variables Affecting Response
1. **Baseline muscle creatine content** - lower baseline = greater response
2. **Type II muscle fiber content** - higher type II = greater response; age-related decline may blunt effect
3. **Dietary creatine intake** - vegetarians/low meat eaters respond more favorably
4. **Training supervision** - supervised training yields more consistent positive results
5. **Sex** - women show more consistent positive results than men in individual studies
6. **Concurrent aerobic training** - may cause muscle interference effect, blunting creatine benefits
7. **Sample size and study duration** - small/short studies more likely to show null results

## Methodology
- Perspective/review paper synthesizing evidence from individual RCTs and meta-analyses
- Comprehensive Table 1 summarizing all creatine + resistance training studies in aging adults with dosing, duration, and outcomes
- Mechanisms discussed with reference to cellular, animal, and human studies

## Limitations
- Not a systematic review; narrative perspective with selective emphasis
- Many mechanism claims rely on younger adult or animal/cell culture data extrapolated to aging adults
- Cannot isolate creatine effects in multi-ingredient supplement studies
- Small meta-analysis on timing (only 3 studies, n=80) limits confidence in timing recommendations
- No discussion of creatine for cognitive or non-muscle outcomes

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q4 (Dosing strategy tradeoffs):** Relevant. Documents various dosing protocols used across studies (loading vs. steady low-dose). Provides evidence on timing (pre vs. post exercise). Does not directly compare loading vs. steady low-dose head-to-head but catalogs what has been used.

**Q5 (Risks, contraindications, null findings):** Highly relevant. Dedicated safety section with specific adverse events, kidney/liver function data, and clinical population safety. Documents null findings with methodological explanations.

**Q6 (Mechanisms beyond ATP buffering):** Highly relevant. Detailed discussion of mechanisms including calcium handling, mitochondrial protection, protein degradation reduction, GLUT-4 upregulation, glycogen sparing, anti-inflammatory effects. Also notes what mechanisms are NOT supported in aging adults (IGF-1, satellite cells, mTOR).

## Claims to Verify
- Pooled muscle mass of +1.21 kg across three meta-analyses: this is an average of the three separate meta-analysis results (+0.94, +1.33, +1.37), not a formal pooled estimate from a single meta-analysis
- Meta-analysis on timing: only 3 studies with n=80 total -- this is a very small evidence base for the timing claim
