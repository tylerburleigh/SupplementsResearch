## Core Findings

This article synthesizes evidence from animal and human studies on creatine's role in healthy aging. The strongest claims involve a 2008 mouse study showing 9% lifespan extension with creatine, and a 2025 NHANES-linked study associating 1g/day creatine intake with reduced all-cause mortality. The article also connects declining creatine levels with age to DNA methylation changes (epigenetic clocks) through SAM-dependent methylation pathways. However, it honestly acknowledges that translating animal success to humans has been disappointing in neurodegenerative disease trials (Parkinson's, ALS).

## Key Evidence

### Lifespan Outcomes (Q3)
- **Animal lifespan:** Creatine extended healthy lifespan in mice by 9% (Bender et al., 2008) -- this is the headline finding but is animal data only.
- **All-cause mortality:** NHANES-linked analysis (Ostojic, 2025) found 1g/day creatine associated with decreased all-cause mortality. This is epidemiological, not interventional.
- **Age-related decline:** Endogenous creatine synthesis (41.9 mg/kg/day) is 2.7x higher than dietary intake (15.5 mg/kg/day), and both decline with age, especially after 65 (Nedeljkovic & Ostojic, 2025).
- **Sarcopenia:** Meta-analysis (Chilibeck et al., 2017) found adults over 50 combining creatine with resistance training gained ~3 lbs more lean muscle than placebo. Without exercise, no change.
- **Human trial failures:** Large Parkinson's trials showed no clinical benefit; ALS trials similarly disappointing (Bender & Klopstock, 2016).

### Mechanisms (Q6)
- **DNA methylation / epigenetic aging:** Creatine synthesis uses ~40% of SAM methyl groups. Declining creatine production with age may disrupt DNA methylation patterns measured by epigenetic clocks like GrimAgeMort (Ostojic & Kavecan, 2025).
- **Mitochondrial function:** Creatine supplementation enhanced mitochondrial DNA and upregulated oxidative phosphorylation genes (Gowayed et al., 2019).
- **Lipofuscin reduction:** Creatine-fed mice showed significantly lower brain lipofuscin ("aging pigment") with upregulated neuronal growth genes (Bender et al., 2008).
- **Neuroprotection mechanisms:** 83% reduction in brain lesion volume in mouse ischemia model (Zhu et al., 2004); reduces caspase-3 activation, prevents cytochrome c release, preserves ATP during stress.
- **Brain energetics:** 11% increase in brain creatine levels with 20g/day supplementation in Alzheimer's patients (Smith et al., 2025 pilot).
- **ATP buffering:** Creatine-phosphocreatine system rapidly regenerates ATP in muscle, heart, and brain.

## Methodology

This is a narrative review/blog article, not a primary study. It synthesizes findings from 17 cited peer-reviewed studies spanning 2001-2025. The evidence ranges from animal models (mice) to human epidemiological analyses (NHANES) to clinical trials. The article does not follow a systematic review protocol.

## Limitations

1. **Commercial bias:** Published by Jinfiniti, which sells creatine supplements. Multiple product placements and calls to action embedded in the article.
2. **No original data:** This is a secondary synthesis, not a primary study.
3. **Animal-to-human gap:** Acknowledges that mouse lifespan and neuroprotection findings have not translated to human clinical benefit in neurodegenerative diseases.
4. **Observational mortality data:** The NHANES all-cause mortality finding is epidemiological and subject to confounding (healthier people may consume more creatine-rich foods).
5. **Dose for brain benefits (10-20g/day)** is high and based on limited pilot data.
6. **Cognitive meta-analysis (Xu et al., 2024)** found effects were NOT significant in adults over 60, which limits the aging narrative.

## Claims to Verify

- **"9% lifespan extension"**: Should verify the original Bender et al. 2008 mouse study for exact magnitude and whether it was statistically significant.
- **"1 gram/day decreases all-cause mortality"**: Epidemiological -- the Ostojic 2025 NHANES analysis needs verification for whether it controlled adequately for confounders.
- **"40% of SAM methyl groups used for creatine synthesis"**: This is a widely cited figure but should be verified from the original source (Broca-Brisson et al., 2024 is cited for related work but the 40% claim may come from elsewhere).
- **"83% reduction in brain lesion volume"**: From Zhu et al. 2004 -- verify exact percentage and conditions.

## Relevance Assessment

- **Q3 (Lifespan outcomes):** Moderate relevance. Provides secondary coverage of the mouse lifespan study (Bender 2008) and NHANES mortality data, but these are better sourced from the primary studies. Honest acknowledgment of human trial failures is valuable.
- **Q6 (Mechanisms mapped to longevity pathways):** Moderate relevance. Useful synthesis of multiple mechanisms (SAM/methylation, mitochondrial biogenesis, lipofuscin, ATP buffering), but each is covered at surface level. The SAM/methylation connection to epigenetic clocks is a notable mechanistic link to longevity pathways.
