# src-309: Creatine Supplementation and Strength Training on Cognitive/Emotional Measures in Older Women

**Citation:** Alves et al. (2013), PLoS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0076301. Cited 66 times.

## Core Findings

Creatine supplementation (20 g/d loading for 5 days, then 5 g/d maintenance for 24 weeks) produced **no significant improvements** in cognitive function or emotional state in healthy older women, either alone or combined with strength training. Strength training alone improved depression scores but not cognition. This is a well-designed, adequately powered RCT that found uniformly negative results for creatine on brain-related outcomes.

## Study Design

- **Design:** 24-week, parallel-group, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial
- **N:** 56 healthy older women (aged 60-80, mean 66.8), randomized 1:1:1:1 into 4 groups of 14; 47 completed
- **Groups:** (1) Placebo, (2) Creatine, (3) Placebo + Strength Training, (4) Creatine + Strength Training
- **Dosing:** Creatine monohydrate 20 g/d (4x5 g) for 5 days loading, then 5 g/d single dose maintenance
- **Training:** 2x/week supervised strength training, 3 sets of 12-15 RM for 7 exercises, ~40 min/session
- **Registered:** Clinicaltrials.gov NCT01164020

## Key Evidence

### Emotional outcomes (Geriatric Depression Scale, 15-item)
- PL+ST: delta = -2.2 (95% CI: -5.2 to 0.8)
- CR+ST: delta = -1.7 (95% CI: -3.0 to -0.4)
- PL: delta = +0.1 (95% CI: -1.4 to 1.6)
- CR: delta = +0.6 (95% CI: -1.6 to 2.8)
- Both trained groups significantly better than both non-trained groups (p < 0.01)
- No difference between PL and CR (p = 0.60) or between PL+ST and CR+ST (p = 0.84)
- Effect sizes for GDS: CR+ST = -0.81, PL+ST = -0.59

### Cognitive outcomes
- No significant changes for any variable: MMSE, Stroop (3 conditions), Trail Making Part A, Digit Span (forward/backward), Incidental Memory, Immediate Memory, Learning, Delayed Recall (all p > 0.05)
- Trail Making Part B excluded because only 25/56 could complete it at baseline

### Muscle strength (secondary outcome)
- CR+ST leg press: +18.7% (p = 0.003 vs PL, p = 0.01 vs CR, p = 0.42 vs PL+ST)
- PL+ST leg press: +10.6%
- No additive effect of creatine on strength beyond training alone

### Safety
- Zero self-reported adverse events across all groups over 24 weeks
- 100% self-reported supplement adherence; ~84% training session adherence

## Methodology Notes

- Mixed-model analysis with Kenward-Roger correction for unbalanced design
- Baseline values used as covariate; delta scores as dependent variable
- Post-hoc power analysis: achieved power 0.91-0.99 for GDS outcomes
- Blinding efficacy assessed: 60-69% correct guess rate, no significant difference between groups (p = 0.77)
- Sensitivity analysis excluding GDS=0 subjects (n=3) showed identical results
- Only 6 subjects had GDS > 6 (depression cut-off), limiting analysis of depressive subpopulation

## Limitations

1. **No brain creatine measurement:** The authors note this as the major limitation. Without confirming that creatine supplementation increased brain creatine, the negative findings may reflect poor BBB penetration rather than inefficacy.
2. **Floor effects possible:** Most participants had very low baseline GDS scores (mean 1.9-3.8 out of 15), limiting room for improvement. Only 6 subjects exceeded the depression screening threshold.
3. **Healthy population:** Participants were apparently mentally healthy older women, so results cannot be generalized to those with cognitive impairment or depression.
4. **Women only:** Results may not generalize to older men.
5. **Body composition and functional measures not completed** due to technical issues.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (Healthspan effects in non-athletes - cognitive, emotional):** Highly relevant. This is one of the few RCTs examining creatine effects on both cognitive and emotional outcomes in non-athletic older adults. The negative findings for creatine on cognition and mood are important for a balanced synthesis. However, the healthy status of participants and absence of brain creatine measurement qualify the interpretation.

**Q4 (Dosing strategy tradeoffs):** Provides data on a standard loading+maintenance protocol (20 g/d x 5 days, then 5 g/d x 24 weeks) with excellent safety (zero adverse events) but no efficacy on cognitive/emotional outcomes. This dosing strategy was well-tolerated over 6 months in older women.
