## Core Findings

The article provides a popular-science overview of creatine's potential brain health and mood benefits, asserting that creatine improves cognitive function, reduces depression symptoms, and supports stress resilience. The evidence presented is second-hand -- no primary data is reported; studies are referenced vaguely (e.g., "a 2003 double-blind placebo-controlled study," "a 2012 study published in the American Journal of Psychiatry") without full citations.

## Key Evidence Referenced

1. **2003 DBPC study**: Creatine improved cognitive performance in short-term memory and processing speed tasks, especially in vegetarians. No specific citation provided.
2. **2012 AJ Psychiatry study**: Creatine augmentation of escitalopram improved MDD symptoms faster and more effectively in women. This likely refers to Lyoo et al. (2012), a legitimate study.
3. **31P-MRS brain imaging**: Altered brain energy metabolism in depression that creatine may normalize. No specific study cited.
4. **Proposed mechanisms**: Neuronal protection from oxidative stress, HPA axis modulation, mitochondrial support, serotonin/dopamine signaling influence.
