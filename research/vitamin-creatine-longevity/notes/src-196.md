# Source src-196: The Role of Creatine Supplementation in Cardiovascular Health: A Comprehensive Review

**Authors:** Owczarska et al. (2025)
**Venue:** Quality in Sport (open-access, Nicolaus Copernicus University Press)
**Type:** Narrative literature review
**Citation count:** 0 (published Nov 2025)

## Core Findings

This comprehensive review synthesizes evidence on creatine supplementation's effects on the cardiovascular system, finding mixed but generally promising results. Creatine shows potential for reducing systolic blood pressure acutely, improving microcirculation, lowering homocysteine (a cardiovascular risk factor), and attenuating arterial stiffness, but most studies show no significant long-term changes in blood pressure, lipid profiles, or hemodynamic parameters. The strongest clinical evidence comes from heart failure patients, where creatine combined with exercise significantly reduced inflammatory markers (hs-CRP, IL-6, P-selectin, ICAM-1) in a study of 100 patients.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### Blood Pressure
- Acute SBP reduction observed at 0.5-1 hour post-creatine monohydrate supplementation (p<0.05), but most studies (cited refs 16-21) show no statistically significant changes in blood pressure long-term.

### Vascular Function
- Creatine increased functional density of skin capillaries (p=0.0496) and capillary recruitment during post-occlusive reactive hyperemia (p=0.0043) (De Moraes et al., 2014).
- CrS (10g/day, 3 weeks) attenuated arterial stiffness increase after intense exercise vs. placebo (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., 2011).
- Blood flow improvement in calf/forearm only observed with creatine + resistance training combined, not creatine alone (Arciero et al., 2001).

### Heart Failure
- Canine model: creatine depletion faster than ATP depletion in failing hearts, suggesting compensatory role (Shein/Shen et al., 1999).
- Gordon et al.: 17 patients (EF<40%), 20g/day for 10 days -- no ejection fraction improvement but increased skeletal muscle phosphagen and enhanced performance.
- Hemati et al.: 100 HF patients, 5g/day + exercise for 8 weeks -- significant reductions in hs-CRP, IL-6, P-selectin, ICAM-1.
- Carvalho et al.: No significant difference in 6-minute walk test distance; no difference in peak VO2, anaerobic threshold, or oxygen pulse.

### Lipid Profile
- Some studies show HDL-C increase (p=0.001), triglyceride decrease (p=0.01) at 5 hours post-supplementation (Galvan et al., 2016); total cholesterol (p=0.0486) and LDL (p=0.0027) reductions (De Moraes et al., 2014).
- Other studies show no significant lipid changes with creatine (Kreider et al., 2003; Gualano et al., 2008).

### Homocysteine and Atherosclerosis
- Creatine supplementation may lower homocysteine production, reducing risk of coronary artery disease and cerebrovascular disease (Wyss & Schulze, 2002).

### Anti-inflammatory Effects
- 7-day creatine regimen may inhibit TNF-alpha and CRP rise; reduced LDH activity suggests decreased tissue degradation (Deminice et al., 2013).

## Limitations

1. **Review quality:** This is a narrative review without systematic methodology, risk-of-bias assessment, or meta-analysis. The journal (Quality in Sport) has a modest impact profile (20 ministerial points from Polish Ministry of Education).
2. **Heterogeneity of cited studies:** Small sample sizes, short supplementation periods, varying protocols, and mixed populations make conclusions tentative.
3. **Many cited findings are from single studies** with small samples (e.g., n=17 for heart failure study; n=18 for oxidative stress study).
4. **Confounding factors** (diet, physical activity, baseline fitness) often inadequately controlled in cited studies.
5. **No long-term cardiovascular outcome data** -- all cited studies are short-term surrogate markers.
6. **Zero citations** as of data fetch -- this is a very recent publication and has not yet been validated by the research community.

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q3: Lifespan-related outcomes (cardiovascular disease)
**Directly relevant.** This review provides the most focused examination of creatine's cardiovascular effects among the sources. Key findings:
- Mixed evidence on blood pressure (mostly null long-term)
- Promising but limited evidence on heart failure management (improved muscle performance but not ejection fraction)
- Strongest evidence: anti-inflammatory effects in heart failure patients (n=100, significant marker reductions)
- Homocysteine reduction as a potential pathway for reducing atherosclerotic disease risk
- No lifespan or mortality data -- all outcomes are surrogate markers

### Q6: Mechanisms beyond ATP buffering
**Moderately relevant.** The review discusses several non-ATP-buffering mechanisms:
- Anti-inflammatory: TNF-alpha, CRP, IL-6 inhibition; reduced LDH activity
- Endothelial function: improved microvascular density and reactivity; reduced ICAM-1, P-selectin
- Homocysteine reduction (methylation pathway interaction)
- Arterial stiffness attenuation (sympathetic activity modulation)
- Insulin-mediated creatine transport enhancement (Na+-K+ pump activity)
- However, mechanistic discussion is limited and largely descriptive rather than experimental

## Claims to Verify

1. **Hemati et al. study (n=100):** This is the largest cited clinical study on creatine + exercise in heart failure. The claim that hs-CRP and IL-6 "significantly increased in the control group while they decreased in the intervention group" should be verified in the original paper for effect sizes and statistical methods.
2. **Gordon et al. study (n=17):** Small sample for a clinical heart failure study. The claim of increased phosphagen content and enhanced performance without ejection fraction improvement should be verified for the actual statistical significance levels reported.
3. **Aron et al. study:** Claims 7 days of CrS improved arterial stiffness and atherosclerosis markers in men 55-80. Very short duration for cardiovascular outcomes -- should verify what specific vascular parameters were measured and their clinical significance.
