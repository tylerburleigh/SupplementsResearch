# src-256: Creatine Supplementation During Resistance Training Does Not Lead to Greater Bone Mineral Density in Older Humans

**Authors:** Forbes SC, Chilibeck PD, Candow DG (2018)
**Venue:** Frontiers in Nutrition
**Citations:** 21 (Crossref), 24 (directive)
**DOI:** 10.3389/fnut.2018.00027
**Quality:** ok (full paper text extracted)

## Core Findings

This brief meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (N=193 total participants, adults >50 years) found **no significant effect** of creatine supplementation combined with resistance training on BMD at any clinically important site (whole body, hip, femoral neck, lumbar spine) compared to resistance training alone. All p-values were non-significant (0.26-0.71). While individual studies showed some positive effects on bone markers (2 of 5 studies found at least one beneficial outcome), the pooled evidence does not support a bone-density benefit from creatine in older adults.

## Key Evidence

### Meta-analysis results (pooled across 5 RCTs, N=193)
- **Whole body BMD:** MD 0.00, 95% CI [-0.01, 0.01], p=0.50
- **Hip BMD:** MD -0.01, 95% CI [-0.02, 0.01], p=0.26
- **Femoral neck BMD:** MD 0.00, 95% CI [-0.01, 0.01], p=0.71
- **Lumbar spine BMD:** MD 0.01, 95% CI [-0.01, 0.03], p=0.32

### Individual study breakdown
| Study | Population | Duration | Dose | Key Finding |
|-------|-----------|----------|------|-------------|
| Pinto 2016 [25] | N=32, 60-80 yr M/F | 12 wks | 5 g/d | No effect on BMD/BMC |
| Chilibeck 2015 [26] | N=33, postmenopausal women | 12 mo | 0.1 g/kg/d | Attenuated femoral neck BMD loss |
| Gualano 2014 [27] | N=60, older vulnerable women (66 yr) | 24 wks | 20g/d 5d then 5g/d | No effect on bone mass |
| Chilibeck 2005 [18] | N=29, older men (71 yr) | 12 wks | 0.3g/kg/d then 0.07g/kg/d | Increased arm BMC (not BMD) |
| Tarnopolsky 2007 [28] | N=39, 65-85 yr M/F | 6 mo | 5 g/d (with CLA) | No effect on BMD |

### Mechanisms discussed
- **Indirect:** Creatine enhances muscle mass/strength during RT, increasing mechanical loading on bone
- **Direct:** Creatine kinase reaction in bone cells; creatine increases osteoblast activity and differentiation in vitro
- **Anti-catabolic:** Creatine decreased urinary cross-linked n-telopeptides of Type I collagen in multiple studies, suggesting reduced bone resorption
- Despite these plausible mechanisms, BMD improvements did not materialize in the meta-analysis

### Safety
- No serious adverse events reported across studies
- One study (Chilibeck 2015) reported significantly more mild GI and musculoskeletal events in creatine vs. placebo group
- Renal and hepatic markers unaffected

## Methodology

- **Design:** Systematic review and brief meta-analysis
- **Search:** PubMed and SPORTDiscus (searched Aug 27, 2017), no date/language restrictions
- **Inclusion:** RCTs >=12 weeks, creatine + RT vs. placebo + RT, adults >50 yr or postmenopausal, BMD outcomes
- **Analysis:** Fixed or random effects based on heterogeneity (chi-squared, I-squared); Review Manager 5.3
- **Risk of bias:** Assessed via Cochrane criteria; only 1 of 5 studies rated low risk

## Limitations

1. **Small sample sizes** across all 5 included studies (N=29-60 per study, 193 total)
2. **High risk of bias** in 4 of 5 included studies
3. **High heterogeneity** in duration (12 wks to 12 mo), training frequency (1.5-3x/wk per muscle group), dosing (5 g/d absolute vs. 0.1 g/kg/d relative), and participant characteristics
4. **Short study durations:** Most were 12-24 weeks; bone turnover may require >9 months to detect changes
5. Only 5 RCTs met inclusion criteria, limiting statistical power even in meta-analysis
6. One included study (Tarnopolsky 2007) used creatine + CLA rather than creatine alone

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q1: Healthspan effects in non-athletes (bone density)
**Highly relevant.** This is a direct meta-analysis of creatine's effect on bone density in older adults (>50 yr). The null finding is important: despite plausible mechanisms (anti-catabolic bone effects, enhanced muscle mass), creatine + RT does not improve BMD beyond RT alone. However, the evidence is limited by small sample sizes and heterogeneity. The authors note that studies using higher training frequency and relative dosing found positive effects, suggesting the null result may be partly methodological.

### Q5: Null or negative findings from creatine studies
**Highly relevant.** This paper is essentially a catalog of null findings on BMD. The meta-analysis found no significant effects at any site. Of 5 included studies, 3 found no bone effects at all. This is a clear, well-documented null finding with transparent reporting of limitations.
