# src-197: GI and Fluid Retention Symptoms with Creatine Monohydrate (Loading vs Standard Dose)

**Citation:** Wagner JC, Faulkner M, Faulkner W, Mullins F. (2025). medRxiv preprint. DOI: 10.1101/2025.10.07.25337280

**Quality:** ok. Full text extracted (raw text fallback). Preprint, not peer-reviewed. 24 participants.

---

## Core Findings

Creatine monohydrate supplementation at both standard (5 g/day) and loading doses (20 g/day x 14 days then 5 g/day) was generally safe over 28 days with no serious adverse events, but GI symptoms were extremely common: 79.2% of all participants reported at least one GI symptom (bloating, water retention, puffiness, stomach discomfort). The loading dose group showed a trend toward more frequent and more severe GI symptoms compared to standard dose, with stomach discomfort reaching statistical significance even after controlling for gender.

## Key Evidence

### Overall GI symptom incidence
- 79.2% (19/24) of all participants reported at least one GI symptom
- 81.0% (17/21) of females reported at least one GI symptom
- Most common symptoms: bloating, water retention, puffiness, stomach discomfort

### By dosing group (Group A = 5 g/day; Group B = 20 g/day loading x 14 days then 5 g/day)
- **Group A (n=12):** water retention 50.0%, bloating/puffiness/weight gain each 41.67%
- **Group B (n=12):** bloating 66.67%, stomach discomfort 58.33%, water retention 50.0%, diarrhea/puffiness each 33.33%
- Group B rated puffiness, weight gain, and stomach discomfort as more severe (16.67% rated severe vs 0% in Group A)
- Stomach discomfort was significantly higher in Group B (p < 0.05), even after removing male subjects

### Safety
- All 24 participants completed the 28-day study (100% completion)
- Zero serious adverse events
- One non-serious AE (menstrual cramps), deemed unrelated to creatine
- No clinically significant lab changes in blood or urine panels

### Mechanism discussion
- CM has poor solubility (~18 mg/ml), providing theoretical basis for GI distress
- Rat model data (Alraddadi et al. 2018): oral bioavailability was 53% at low dose vs 16% at high dose, suggesting dose-dependent saturation of intestinal transport
- Claims of 100% bioavailability for CM are based on tracer doses (< 1 g), not typical supplementation doses

## Methodology

- **Design:** 28-day single-center, single-blind, randomized clinical trial
- **Site:** Princeton Consumer Research Corp., Raritan, NJ (May-July 2024)
- **IRB:** UNIVO IRB (IRB2024-SP029); registered at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT07176325)
- **N=24** healthy adults aged 18-60; 21 female, 3 male
- **Group A (n=12):** 5 g/day CM for 28 days (all 3 males here)
- **Group B (n=12):** 20 g/day CM for 14 days (loading), then 5 g/day for 14 days (all female)
- **Outcomes:** Self-reported GI symptoms via structured severity rating scale; blood and urine panels at baseline and day 28
- **No placebo group**

## Limitations

1. **Very small sample size** (n=24 total, n=12 per group) limits statistical power and generalizability
2. **Gender imbalance:** Only 3 males total, all assigned to Group A; Group B was entirely female, confounding dose vs gender effects
3. **No placebo group** -- cannot determine baseline GI symptom rates
4. **Self-reported outcomes** may introduce recall and reporting bias
5. **No control for menstrual cycle phase** -- GI symptoms may vary with menses
6. **Preprint** -- not yet peer-reviewed
7. **28-day duration only** -- cannot assess long-term effects

## Relevance to Research Questions

### Q5: Risks, contraindications (GI side effects, water retention)
**Directly relevant.** This is one of the few studies that systematically quantifies GI symptom incidence and severity with creatine supplementation. Key finding: nearly 80% of participants experienced GI symptoms, though all were mild to moderate in severity. No serious adverse events occurred. Water retention/bloating were among the top reported symptoms. The small sample and lack of placebo limit causal attribution.

### Q4: Dosing strategy tradeoffs (loading vs non-loading)
**Directly relevant.** Directly compares 5 g/day (standard maintenance) vs 20 g/day loading protocol. Key finding: loading dose was associated with numerically higher symptom rates and severity, with stomach discomfort reaching statistical significance. However, the confound of all loading-group participants being female (Group B was all-female) makes it difficult to isolate dose effects from gender effects. The bioavailability data from cited rat studies supports a dose-dependent mechanism.
