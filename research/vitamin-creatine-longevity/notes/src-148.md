# src-148: Effects of creatine supplementation on brain in the healthy population

**Authors:** Swatko et al. (2023)
**Venue:** Journal of Education, Health and Sport (Polish journal, open access)

**Citations:** 0 (as of fetch date)

## Core Findings

Creatine supplementation shows potential to improve cognitive processing, memory, and brain energy metabolism in healthy populations, particularly under conditions of creatine deficit (aging, sleep deprivation, exercise stress). The brain expresses a specific isoform of creatine kinase and has its own creatine synthesis apparatus, but brain creatine levels may require higher or longer dosing than standard muscle-targeted protocols to increase. The review highlights that most evidence for cognitive benefits comes from studies in elderly individuals, sleep-deprived subjects, and clinical populations rather than young healthy adults at baseline.

## Key Evidence

1. **Brain-specific creatine kinase isoform** exists, and the enzymatic apparatus for endogenous creatine synthesis (AGAT, GAMT) is present in the nervous system; creatine transporters are found at the blood-brain barrier, neurons, and oligodendrocytes (lines 104-118).
2. **Brain creatine responds to supplementation** but may require higher/longer dosing than muscle protocols. Optimal brain dosing strategy is unknown (lines 77-80).
3. **Cognitive improvements** observed in reaction time, memory (verbal, long-term, spatial), especially in elderly populations (lines 122-128). References Laakso et al. 2003 for decreased brain creatine in elderly ApoE4 carriers.
4. **Antioxidant and neuroprotective effects** -- creatine reduces brain oxygen demand, has antioxidant action on brain cells, reduces mental fatigue, and protects against neurotoxins (lines 123-128).
5. **Depression RCT** -- Lyoo et al. 2012: SSRI + creatine showed greater improvement than SSRI + placebo in women with major depressive disorder (lines 132-135, reference [26]).
6. **Creatine deficiency syndromes** cause severe neurodevelopmental disorders (intellectual disability, autism, seizures) partially reversible with supplementation (lines 107-110).

## Methodology

This is a **brief narrative review**, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. It synthesizes findings from approximately 26 cited references without formal search strategy, inclusion criteria, or quality assessment. The review covers brain creatine metabolism, effects of supplementation on healthy brain function, and applications in brain diseases. No original data presented.

## Limitations

- **Narrative review only** -- no systematic methodology, risk of cherry-picking favorable studies.
- **Low citation count** (0) and published in a lesser-known Polish journal, suggesting limited peer recognition.
- **No quantitative synthesis** -- effect sizes, confidence intervals, and sample sizes from cited studies are not reported.
- **Bilingual format** (Polish/English) means half the text is in Polish, limiting accessibility.
- Many claims are attributed to cited references without specific quantitative details (e.g., "improved reaction time" without reporting magnitudes or study designs).
- The dosing protocol question (optimal brain creatine loading) remains unresolved.

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q1 (Healthspan effects in non-athletes, cognitive):** Directly relevant. Reviews evidence for cognitive benefits of creatine in non-athletic contexts -- memory, reaction time, mental fatigue reduction, depression augmentation. Particularly highlights benefits in elderly populations and under stress conditions (sleep deprivation, hypoxia). However, evidence is indirect (aggregated from cited studies, not new data).

**Q6 (Mechanisms beyond ATP buffering, brain creatine kinetics):** Directly relevant. Discusses brain-specific CK isoform, endogenous creatine synthesis in CNS, creatine transporters at BBB/neurons/oligodendrocytes, ATP homeostasis during cognitive demand, antioxidant mechanisms, and reduced brain oxygen demand. Notes that brain creatine kinetics differ from muscle (potentially requiring higher/longer dosing).

## Claims to Verify

- "Creatine supplementation caused a reduction in brain tissue oxygen demand" -- attributed to Rawson & Venezia 2011 but no specific study details given.
- "Improved reaction time and memory, especially in the elderly" -- attributed to Laakso et al. 2003 and Kreider et al. 2017 but no effect sizes or sample sizes reported.
- The review conflates evidence from healthy populations with clinical populations (depression, creatine deficiency syndromes, TBI), making it difficult to isolate effects in healthy non-athletes.
