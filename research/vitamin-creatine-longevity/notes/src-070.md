# Source src-070: Impact of Creatine Supplementation on Muscle and Bone Strength in Older Adults

**Citation:** Michalak et al. (2026). Journal of Education, Health and Sport, 88:68067.
**Type:** Narrative Review
**Quality:** ok -- full text available, substantive content

---

## Core Findings

Creatine supplementation combined with progressive resistance training reliably enhances lean mass, dynamic strength (leg press, chest press), and functional performance (sit-to-stand, chair rise) in older adults. However, creatine without resistance training produces inconsistent results. For bone health, pooled meta-analyses show no significant effect on areal BMD at any site, though some individual trials suggest preservation of femoral geometric properties relevant to fracture resistance.

---

## Key Evidence

### Muscle Outcomes

- **Devries et al. 2014 meta-analysis** (N=357, age >=55): Creatine + RT significantly increased total body mass, fat-free mass, leg press strength, chest press strength, and 30-second chair stand vs RT alone.
- **Chilibeck et al. 2017 meta-analysis** (N=721, age >=57): Confirmed greater gains in lean tissue mass and muscular strength with creatine + RT.
- **Davies et al. 2024 meta-analysis** on sit-to-stand: Significant improvement with creatine (SMD 0.51, 95% CI 0.01-1.00, p=0.04); Bayesian analysis showed 66.7% probability of benefit.
- **Handgrip strength**: Largest meta-analysis found no significant improvement (WMD 4.26 kg, p=0.10). Lower-limb and functional measures appear more responsive than static grip measures.
- **Without resistance training**: Results are mixed; higher doses may reduce body fatigue but low doses and acute bolus ingestion fail to significantly improve performance.

### Bone Outcomes

- **Forbes et al. meta-analysis** (5 trials, N=193, age >=50): No significant effect of creatine on BMD at whole body, hip, femoral neck, or lumbar spine.
- **Sharifian et al. meta-analysis** (N=1093, age >=55): No significant difference in BMD between creatine and placebo groups.
- **Chilibeck et al. RCT** (N=237 postmenopausal women, 2 years): No BMD difference, but creatine group preserved femoral geometric properties (sectional modulus, buckling ratio, cortical thickness) while control group deteriorated. These geometric properties are predictive of hip fracture risk and some are independent of BMD.
- **Small trial in older men** (N=29, median age 71, 12 weeks): Significant increase in leg BMD with creatine + RT.
- **Animal models**: Mixed results; one study showed increased lumbar BMD and femur load-to-failure with creatine; another in osteoporosis model found no effect.

### Mechanistic Basis

- Creatine increases intramuscular creatine/phosphocreatine, supporting rapid ATP resynthesis during repeated high-intensity contractions.
- Upregulates mRNA/proteins for protein synthesis, satellite cell activity, and cell survival.
- Activates p38 and Akt/PKB-p70S6K signaling pathways; IGF pathway may mediate muscle dry mass increase.
- Osmotic effect (cell swelling) activates osmosensing pathways favoring anabolism.
- Intramuscular PCr declines with age; supplementation increases stores in both young and older adults, with larger responses in those with lower baselines.

---

## Dosing Strategies (Q4)

- Both lower (<5 g/day) and higher (>=5 g/day) doses significantly increase lean tissue mass vs placebo when combined with RT.
- Upper-body strength (chest press) improved with loading phase + lower maintenance doses.
- Lower-body strength (leg press) improved more with loading phase + higher maintenance doses.
- Authors emphasize substantial heterogeneity across trials precludes firm dosing recommendations.
- Practical dosing regimens for real-world older adult populations remain an open research gap.

---

## Safety Considerations

- Creatine increases serum creatinine, artifactually lowering creatinine-based eGFR without true renal injury.
- Meta-analyses report no meaningful adverse effect on measured GFR in generally healthy individuals.
- Clinicians should consider cystatin C-based estimates or measured clearance for confirmatory testing.

## Methodology

- Narrative review using PubMed, Google Scholar, and ResearchGate.
- Included peer-reviewed studies (2000-2025) on creatine + muscle/bone outcomes in adults.
- Priority given to RCTs, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses.
- No formal systematic methodology (PRISMA) or quality assessment reported.

---

## Limitations

1. Narrative (not systematic) review -- no formal risk-of-bias assessment or PRISMA protocol.
2. Authors are independent researchers (not affiliated with clinical research institutions); AI tool (ChatGPT 5.2) used for stylistic review.
3. Most trials enrolled generally healthy older adults, not formally diagnosed sarcopenic populations.
4. Substantial heterogeneity in dosing regimens, training protocols, and participant characteristics across trials.
5. Few trials assessed biochemical markers of bone turnover.
6. Small sample sizes in individual bone trials limit detection power.
