What Is Creatine (In Simple Terms)?

Creatine is a naturally occurring compound found in small amounts in foods like red meat and fish, and produced in the body from amino acids.

Its primary role is to help regenerate
ATP
— the body’s main energy currency — during short, intense efforts such as lifting weights, sprinting, and repeated high-intensity efforts.

In short: creatine helps you produce
more force, more often
, with better recovery between efforts.

The Performance Benefits (Backed by Science)

Creatine supplementation has consistently been shown to improve:

• Strength and power output
• Repeated sprint and high-intensity performance
• Training volume tolerance
• Lean mass gains over time

These effects are seen across men
and
women, beginners
and
experienced lifters.

For SPC members, this often means:

More quality reps per session

Better progression across training blocks

Less fatigue during demanding phases like HYX, strength blocks or conditioning work

Why Creatine Matters for Women

Creatine has historically been under-recommended to women, largely due to outdated myths around “bulking” or unwanted weight gain.

The evidence tells a very different story.

Research shows creatine can:

Improve strength and power without increasing body fat

Support lean muscle development (not size inflation)

Enhance training quality and recovery

Support cognitive function and mood

Importantly, creatine may be especially beneficial during
peri- and post-menopause
, when natural declines in muscle mass, strength and power accelerate.

For women training at SPC, creatine supports exactly what we prioritise:

strength, confidence, bone health and long-term resilience
.

Creatine and Brain Health

One of the biggest shifts in recent research is the recognition that creatine also plays a role in
brain energy metabolism
.

Studies suggest creatine may:

Improve cognitive performance under fatigue

Support memory and mental processing speed

Help maintain brain function as we age

For busy professionals, parents, and anyone balancing training with high mental demands, this is a major — and often overlooked — benefit.

Creatine, Ageing & Injury Resilience

Creatine supplementation has been shown to:

Help preserve muscle mass with age

Enhance strength gains in older adults when paired with resistance training

Improve power output, which is strongly linked to reduced fall risk

This aligns perfectly with SPC’s long-term approach: training for
performance now
and
health later
.

Is Creatine Safe?

Creatine is one of the most thoroughly studied supplements available.

Long-term research shows:

No negative impact on kidney function in healthy individuals

No hormonal disruption

No dependency or need for cycling

When used correctly, creatine is considered
safe, effective and well-tolerated
.

How Much Creatine Should You Take?

For most people:
3–5 grams per day

No loading phase required.
No cycling needed.

Consistency matters far more than timing, though taking it with food or post-training is often easiest.
