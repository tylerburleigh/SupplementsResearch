What is Creatine?

Creatine is a naturally occurring compound found in the body, particularly in the muscles and brain. It’s synthesized from the amino acids glycine, arginine, and methionine and stored primarily as phosphocreatine, a form that helps produce ATP the main energy currency of your cells.

While your body can make creatine on its own, and you get small amounts from foods like red meat and fish, many people especially those on plant-based diets may have suboptimal levels for peak mental performance.

Creatine has gained attention in psychiatry for its

Frequently Asked Questions

What is creatine?

Creatine is a naturally occurring compound found in muscle cells. It helps produce energy during high-intensity exercise or heavy lifting. Most people get creatine from foods like red meat and fish, but it’s also available as a dietary supplement.

How does creatine work?

Creatine increases your body’s stores of phosphocreatine, which is used to regenerate ATP—the primary energy molecule in your cells. This leads to improved performance in short bursts of intense activity like sprinting or weightlifting.

What are the main benefits of creatine?

Increased strength and power output

Enhanced muscle growth and recovery

Improved high-intensity exercise performance

Cognitive benefits, especially in sleep-deprived or vegetarian individuals

Possible neuroprotective effects

Who should take creatine?

Creatine is widely used by athletes, bodybuilders, and gym-goers. But it’s also beneficial for older adults, vegetarians (who may have lower baseline levels), and individuals looking to support cognitive function or recovery.

Is creatine safe?

Yes. Creatine is one of the most well-researched supplements in the world. Studies consistently show it’s safe when taken at recommended doses, even over long periods. It does not damage the kidneys or liver in healthy individuals.

What’s the best type of creatine?

Creatine monohydrate
is the gold standard. It’s the most researched, most effective, and most affordable form of creatine. Other forms (like creatine HCl or buffered creatine) are marketed as better, but evidence doesn’t support that claim.

How much creatine should I take?

Loading phase (optional):
20g per day, split into 4 doses, for 5–7 days

Maintenance phase:
3–5g per day

Most people can skip loading and just take 5g daily for steady results.

When should I take creatine?

The most important factor is consistency. However, some research suggests that taking creatine
post-workout
with a meal may offer slightly better results due to improved absorption.

Do I need to cycle creatine?

No. There’s no need to cycle on and off. Continuous use is safe and more effective for maintaining muscle saturation and performance benefits.

Can creatine cause water retention or bloating?

Creatine pulls water into the muscles (not under the skin), which may cause a slight weight increase but not bloating in most people. If bloating occurs, splitting the dose into smaller servings may help.

Does creatine help with brain function?

Yes. Emerging research shows creatine may support cognitive performance, especially under stress, sleep deprivation, or in individuals with low dietary creatine intake (e.g., vegans, vegetarians).

Can women take creatine?

Absolutely. Creatine is just as effective and safe for women as it is for men. It can help improve strength, body composition, and cognitive function without causing bulkiness.

Does creatine need to be taken with carbs?

Taking creatine with carbs (or carbs + protein) may slightly improve uptake, but it’s not essential. The most important factor is daily consistency.

Is creatine vegan or vegetarian?

Yes. Synthetic creatine monohydrate (the standard form) is vegan-friendly and not derived from animal sources.

Are there any side effects of creatine?

Creatine is generally well tolerated. Some users may experience minor stomach discomfort if they take too much at once. Drinking enough water and splitting doses can help avoid this.
