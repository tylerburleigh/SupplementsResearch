# src-012: Circulating Endothelial Progenitor Cells and Endothelial Function After Chronic Tadalafil Treatment in ED

**Citation:** Foresta C, Ferlin A, De Toni L, et al. *Int J Impot Res* 18, 484-488 (2006). DOI: 10.1038/sj.ijir.3901465

**Source Quality:** DEGRADED (paywall). Only the abstract is available. Full methods, exact effect sizes, confidence intervals, and detailed results are not accessible. The findings below are based on the abstract only.

---

## Core Findings

This study found that chronic tadalafil treatment significantly increased circulating progenitor cells (PCs) and improved flow-mediated dilation (FMD, a measure of endothelial function) in patients with erectile dysfunction, with a positive correlation between PC levels and endothelial function.

## Study Design and Population

- **Design:** Crossover study with tadalafil and placebo arms. Both ED patients and controls received both treatments (implied by "IIEF-5, FMD and PC count were re-evaluated in all subjects at the end of Tadalafil and placebo treatment").
- **Population:** 26 subjects with erectile dysfunction (with or without cardiovascular risk factors) and 23 age-matched controls.
- **Institution:** Department of Histology, Microbiology and Medical Biotechnologies, University of Padova, Italy.
- **Measures:** Blood tests, IIEF-5, Nocturnal Penile Tumescence Rigidity Monitoring (NPTRM), brachial artery flow-mediated dilation (FMD), progenitor cell (PC) count.

## Key Evidence

### Baseline Differences (ED vs Controls)
- ED patients had significantly lower basal FMD (P < 0.05) compared to controls.
- ED patients had significantly lower basal PCs (P < 0.05) compared to controls.

### Treatment Effects
- Tadalafil treatment produced a significant increase in PCs (P < 0.001) relative to baseline.
- Tadalafil treatment produced a significant improvement in FMD (P < 0.001) relative to baseline.

### Correlations
- Positive correlation between basal FMD and basal PCs (P < 0.05).
- Positive correlation between basal FMD and the magnitude of PC increase after tadalafil treatment (P < 0.05).

## Critical Appraisal

### Did Controls Also Receive Tadalafil?
The abstract states: "International index of erectile function, FMD and PC count were re-evaluated in **all subjects** at the end of Tadalafil and placebo treatment." This suggests both ED patients AND controls received tadalafil and placebo in a crossover design. However, without the full text, we cannot confirm whether the treatment effects reported (P < 0.001 increases) apply only to ED patients or to the full cohort including controls.

### Limitations
1. **Source degradation:** Full text is behind a paywall. Exact effect sizes, confidence intervals, dosing regimen, treatment duration, and crossover washout period are unknown.
2. **Small sample size:** 26 ED + 23 controls = 49 total participants.
3. **Missing quantitative detail:** No absolute values for FMD percentages or PC counts are available from the abstract.
4. **Author overlap:** Foresta et al. also published a related paper on vardenafil and progenitor cells (Int J Impot Res 2005;17:377-380), suggesting this group has a program of research on PDE5 inhibitors and EPCs, which increases confidence in the finding but also potential for publication bias.

## Relevance to Research Questions

This source provides direct evidence for tadalafil's effects on:
- Endothelial function (FMD improvement)
- Circulating progenitor/endothelial progenitor cells
- The mechanistic link between progenitor cell mobilization and endothelial repair
- ED as a sentinel marker for endothelial dysfunction (baseline differences between ED patients and controls)

## Claims to Verify

- **Whether controls received tadalafil:** The phrase "all subjects" in the abstract needs verification from the full text. If controls also received tadalafil and showed PC/FMD improvements, this would strengthen the mechanistic claim.
- **Dosing and duration:** Unknown from abstract. Critical for assessing clinical relevance.
- **Exact effect sizes:** P-values are reported but not the magnitude of change in PCs or FMD percentage points.
