## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive review of the NO/cGMP/PDE5 pathway and the pharmacology, approved clinical applications, and safety of PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil, avanafil). The review covers three approved indications -- erectile dysfunction, lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS/BPH), and pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) -- and notes expanding but unapproved uses in diabetes, cancer, and cardiovascular disease. The author emphasizes that PDE5 inhibitors are not a panacea and that new applications should be guided by RCTs.

## Key Pharmacological Properties of Tadalafil

- **Half-life:** 17.5 hours (vs. sildenafil 3.98 h, vardenafil 3.9 h, avanafil 5.1 h) -- by far the longest among globally available PDE5 inhibitors
- **Tmax:** 2 hours
- **Duration of action:** Up to 36 hours, enabling once-daily dosing or extended on-demand window
- **IC50 for PDE5:** 1.8 nmol/L (bovine PDE source)
- **PDE selectivity:** Highly selective for PDE5; notable cross-reactivity with PDE11 (only 25-fold selectivity vs PDE5, the lowest among the four agents); very low activity against PDE6
- **Dosing:** 20 mg on-demand for ED; 5 mg once daily for LUTS/BPH; also used for PAH (primarily sildenafil is approved for PAH)

## Clinical Applications Beyond ED

1. **LUTS/BPH:** Only tadalafil (5 mg once daily) is FDA-approved for LUTS secondary to BPH, with or without concomitant ED. Multiple RCTs demonstrate improvement in IPSS total, storage, and voiding subscores. Combination with alpha-1-adrenoceptor antagonists is the most effective therapy per network meta-analysis (64 RCTs, 28,196 participants).

2. **PAH:** Sildenafil is the primary PDE5 inhibitor approved for PAH (20-80 mg TID). PDE5 is highly expressed in lung vascular smooth muscle. PDE5 inhibitor therapy improves exercise capacity, WHO functional class, hemodynamics, and echocardiography parameters. Benefit demonstrated in patients with reduced left ventricular ejection fraction but not preserved LVEF.

3. **Investigational/Off-label uses:** Diabetes, cancer, cardiovascular disorders (heart failure, cardiac preconditioning). The author cautions these are not yet approved and require RCT confirmation.

## Safety Profile and Adverse Effects

- Overall AE rate: 37.31% for PDE5 inhibitors vs. 24.03% for placebo
- Serious AE rates: tadalafil 1.10%, vardenafil 1.85%, sildenafil 1.05%
- Common AEs: headache, flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion, myalgia, back pain (vasodilation-mediated)
- Cross-reactivity AEs: vision disturbances (PDE6 inhibition, primarily sildenafil/vardenafil); no known clinical consequences of tadalafil's PDE11 cross-reactivity
- Contraindications: nitrates/nitrate donors, unstable angina, recent MI, certain arrhythmias, poorly controlled hypertension; caution with alpha-1 blockers (postural hypotension)
- AE-related discontinuation: ~25% of discontinuations; most common reason for stopping is lack of efficacy
- FDA has approved daily-use PDE5 inhibitors, supporting long-term safety

## Use in Healthy Populations / Prevention

This review does **not** discuss use of PDE5 inhibitors in healthy populations or for prevention purposes. The focus is entirely on therapeutic applications in diseased states (ED, LUTS/BPH, PAH). There is no mention of prophylactic use, performance enhancement in healthy individuals, or preventive strategies.

## Mechanistic Context

- PDE5 inhibitors are critically dependent on activity of the NO/sGC/cGMP pathway -- they are ineffective without NO production
- PDE5 isoenzymes are widely distributed: corpus cavernosum, vascular/visceral smooth muscle, skeletal muscle, platelets, kidney, lung, spinal cord, cerebellum, pancreas, prostate, urethra, bladder
- The wide tissue distribution suggests potential for effects on many organ systems
- PDE5 inhibitors potentiate anti-platelet effects; sildenafil prolonged bleeding time in healthy volunteers

## Limitations

- Narrative review, not systematic (60 selected articles)
- No meta-analysis or new primary data
- Does not address healthy-population use or preventive applications
- Published 2018; does not cover the most recent clinical trial data
- Some off-label uses (diabetes, cancer) mentioned only in passing with reference to other reviews
