# src-003: Tadalafil Improves Vascular Endothelial Function in BPH Patients

**Source URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28830281

## Core Findings

Daily low-dose tadalafil (5 mg) administered over 12 months to 81 BPH patients significantly improved vascular endothelial function as measured by brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity (baPWV) from 3 months onward, with ankle brachial index (ABI) also improving at 6 months. The study demonstrates that PDE5 inhibition produces sustained cardiovascular benefits beyond its primary indications for LUTS/BPH and erectile dysfunction.

## Study Design

- **Type:** Prospective clinical study (IRB-approved, not described as RCT in abstract)
- **Population:** 81 BPH patients, mean age 66.4 +/- 11.4 years, mean prostate size 30.2 +/- 22.1 ml
- **Intervention:** Tadalafil 5 mg daily for 12 months
- **Assessment timepoints:** LUTS parameters at 0, 1, 3, 6, 12 months; vascular/endothelial measures at 0, 3, 6, 12 months

## Key Evidence

### Vascular/Endothelial Outcomes
| Measure | Finding | Onset | Sustained |
|---------|---------|-------|-----------|
| baPWV | Significant improvement | 3 months | Through 12 months |
| ABI | Significant improvement | 6 months | Stated at 6 months |
| VPT (vibration perception) | No change at any timepoint | N/A | N/A |

The baPWV improvement from 3-12 months is the headline vascular finding, indicating increased arterial elasticity. The ABI improvement at 6 months provides convergent evidence for peripheral vascular benefit. The null result for VPT (neuropathy measure) suggests the vascular improvements are specific to endothelial function rather than a general neurological effect.

### Urological/Erectile Outcomes
- IPSS, OABSS, residual urine: significant improvement from 1-12 months
- Max and mean voiding flow: significantly increased at 6 months
- SHIM score (erectile function): significant improvement after 3 months

## Relevance to Healthy Population Research

**Highly relevant but requires caution:**
- BPH is an age-related condition, not a distinct disease per se; it affects the majority of men over 60. The mean age of 66.4 places these patients in the range where age-related vascular decline is common.
- The vascular improvements (baPWV, ABI) suggest tadalafil may benefit cardiovascular health broadly, not just in disease states.
- Key limitation: no control group is explicitly mentioned in the abstract. The study design is described as "prospective" but not randomized or placebo-controlled based on available text.
- The dose (5 mg daily) is the same low dose commonly used for BPH/LUTS and is relevant to off-label healthy use scenarios.

## Methodology Notes

- Measures were clinically standard: baPWV for arterial stiffness, ABI for peripheral arterial disease screening, VPT for neuropathy
- No p-values, confidence intervals, or effect sizes reported in the abstract (full text behind paywall)
- The study assessed multiple timepoints, which is a strength for understanding onset and durability of effects

## Limitations

1. **Paywall source:** Only abstract available; cannot verify effect sizes, statistical methods, or detailed protocols
2. **Study design unclear:** Abstract describes it as a "prospective clinical study" but does not explicitly state randomization or placebo control
3. **No quantitative results:** No specific baPWV values, percentage improvements, or p-values in abstract
4. **Population specificity:** All subjects had BPH with LUTS; results may not directly generalize to younger, healthier populations
5. **Sample size moderate at 81:** Adequate for detecting moderate effects but not definitive
