# src-333: Endophenotype-based network medicine identifies sildenafil as candidate AD drug

**Citation:** Fang et al. (2021) Nature Aging 1(12): 1175-1188. DOI: 10.1038/s43587-021-00138-z
**Citations:** 163 | **Quality:** ok | **Peer-reviewed:** Yes (Nature Aging)

---

## Core Findings

This study used an endophenotype-based network medicine approach combined with pharmacoepidemiologic analysis of 7.23 million US insurance records to identify sildenafil (a PDE5 inhibitor) as a candidate drug for Alzheimer's disease. Sildenafil use was associated with a 69% reduced risk of AD (HR=0.31, 95% CI 0.25-0.39, P<1.0x10^-8) versus matched non-users, confirmed across four comparator drug cohorts after propensity score adjustment. In vitro mechanistic studies showed sildenafil downregulated GSK3beta and CDK5 phosphorylation in human microglia, increased neurite growth, and decreased p-tau181 in AD patient iPSC-derived neurons.

## Study Type and Methodology

**Multi-stage design:**
1. **Endophenotype network construction** -- Built 13 AD disease modules from seed genes (n=144), transcriptomics (34 datasets from 7 transgenic mouse models), and proteomics (10 datasets from 3 mouse models)
2. **In silico drug screening** -- Scored 1,608 FDA-approved drugs by network proximity (z-score) to AD modules in a human interactome (351,444 PPIs, 17,706 proteins)
3. **Pharmacoepidemiologic validation** -- Retrospective cohort study using MarketScan Medicare Claims database (2012-2017, 7.23 million patients) with Cox regression and propensity score stratification
4. **In vitro mechanistic validation** -- Human microglia HMC3 cells and AD patient iPSC-derived forebrain neurons

**Pharmacoepidemiologic design specifics:**
- Five comparison analyses: sildenafil vs. matched non-users (4:1 ratio), sildenafil vs. diltiazem (n=251,360), sildenafil vs. losartan (n=664,265), sildenafil vs. glimepiride (n=159,597), sildenafil vs. metformin (n=723,082)
- Covariates adjusted: age, sex, race, geographic location, hypertension, T2D, coronary artery disease, mild cognitive impairment
- Propensity score stratified (10 strata) log-rank tests and Cox proportional hazards models
- Drug episodes defined from initiation to discontinuation (90-day gap allowed)

## Key Findings about Sildenafil/PDE5 and Alzheimer's

### Epidemiologic results
| Comparison | Hazard Ratio | 95% CI | P-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sildenafil vs. non-users | 0.31 | 0.25-0.39 | <1x10^-8 |
| Sildenafil vs. diltiazem | 0.35 | 0.29-0.44 | <1x10^-8 |
| Sildenafil vs. losartan | 0.45 | 0.35-0.55 | <1x10^-8 |
| Sildenafil vs. glimepiride | 0.36 | 0.28-0.45 | <1x10^-8 |
| Sildenafil vs. metformin | 0.37 | 0.30-0.45 | <1x10^-8 |

### Subgroup findings
- Effect persisted after excluding individuals with CAD, HT, or T2D
- Effect significant in both mid-older (65-74) and older (75-100) age groups
- Stronger association in males than females (males had higher average daily doses: 75.9 mg vs. 22.1 mg, p=0.03)
- Average daily dosage of sildenafil was NOT associated with AD incidence (HR 0.99, p=0.19)
- Effect significant after adjusting for mild cognitive impairment

### In vitro mechanistic findings
- Sildenafil (30 and 100 uM) reduced LPS-induced phosphorylation of GSK3beta (P=0.023 at 100 uM) and CDK5 (P=0.008 at 100 uM) in human microglia HMC3 cells
- In AD patient iPSC-derived neurons: sildenafil increased neurite growth and decreased p-tau181 accumulation
- Network analysis identified GSK3beta, CDK5, and FYN as the closest AD seed genes to sildenafil targets in the brain-specific PPI network

## Whether Findings Extend to Tadalafil

**This study does NOT directly test tadalafil.** The entire pharmacoepidemiologic analysis and in vitro work focused exclusively on sildenafil. However, several indirect connections exist:

1. **PDE5 inhibitor class effect:** The authors identify sildenafil as a PDE5 inhibitor and note that "PDE inhibitors have beneficial therapeutic effects in preclinical models of AD by modulating neuronal plasticity, reducing tau phosphorylation, improving cognitive impairment, decreasing Abeta plaque accumulation, and enhancing the level of BDNF" (line 384-388).
2. **Tadalafil citation:** Reference 51 cites Garcia-Barroso et al. (2013): "Tadalafil crosses the blood-brain barrier and reverses cognitive dysfunction in a mouse model of AD" -- acknowledging tadalafil's preclinical evidence but not testing it.
3. **Selection bias toward sildenafil:** Sildenafil was selected because it had sufficient patient data for population validation and was not already in AD clinical trials. Tadalafil may not have had equivalent claims data volume.
4. **Class-level inference is reasonable but unproven:** Both drugs inhibit PDE5 but have different pharmacokinetic profiles (tadalafil has longer half-life ~17.5h vs sildenafil ~4h; tadalafil is more selective for PDE5).

## Population Studied

- **Data source:** MarketScan Medicare Claims database, 2012-2017
- **Population:** 7.23 million U.S. commercially insured (Medicare Advantage/fee-for-service) individuals
- **Age:** Predominantly 65+ (Medicare population); subgroup analyses for 65-74 and 75-100
- **Sildenafil cohort:** n=116,412 users; primarily males receiving treatment for erectile dysfunction
- **Comparator cohorts:** diltiazem (n=251,360), losartan (n=664,265), glimepiride (n=159,597), metformin (n=723,082)
- **AD outcome:** Defined by ICD-9/ICD-10 codes, excluding other dementia types (e.g., vascular dementia)
- **Important:** Population is older adults with insurance claims data, NOT healthy volunteers. Most sildenafil users had prescriptions for ED; some female users had pulmonary hypertension.

## Limitations and Caveats

1. **No causality established** -- Observational claims data cannot determine causation or its direction
2. **Unmeasured confounders** -- APOE genotype, education level, socioeconomic status not available in claims data
3. **Prescription bias** -- Sildenafil may be more likely prescribed to healthier/wealthier individuals; although annual total costs did not differ significantly (p=0.36)
4. **ICD code accuracy** -- AD diagnosis from claims codes may have misclassification; no biomarker confirmation
5. **Sex imbalance** -- Majority of sildenafil users were male; female subgroup analyses were underpowered
6. **No dose-response** -- Average daily dosage was not associated with AD incidence (HR 0.99, p=0.19), which paradoxically weakens a causal argument
7. **Network medicine limitations** -- Incompleteness of drug-target networks may bias z-scores; literature bias in known PPIs
8. **Selection of sildenafil over other candidates** -- Dantrolene and deferoxamine were excluded due to insufficient patient data, not lack of efficacy
9. **No tadalafil or other PDE5i testing** -- Cannot confirm whether findings generalize across the PDE5 inhibitor class

## Claims to Verify

1. **"7.23 million individuals"** -- Stated in abstract (line 48) and methods (line 647). This is the total MarketScan Medicare dataset, not the analysis cohort. The actual analysis cohorts are smaller (sildenafil n=116,412 plus comparators totaling ~2.3 million). The 7.23M figure is the database population, not participants in the comparative analysis.
2. **"69% reduced risk"** -- Derived from HR=0.31. Confirmed in text at multiple points.
3. **P-values all "<1x10^-8"** -- These are extremely small, suggesting very large effect sizes in very large samples. Worth noting that with ~100K+ sildenafil users, even small effects can be highly significant.
4. **iPSC line** -- Only one AD patient iPSC line (HVRDi001-A-1 from WiCell) was used for the neuron experiments, which is a very small N for mechanistic claims.
