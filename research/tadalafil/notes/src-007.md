# src-007: Pelliccione et al. (2014) - Tadalafil and Endothelial Cell Damage/Repair Markers

## Citation
Pelliccione F, D'Angeli A, D'Andrea S, Barbonetti A, Pezzella A, Necozione S, Falone S, Amicarelli F, Francavilla F, Francavilla S. "Tadalafil treatment had a modest effect on endothelial cell damage and repair ability markers in men with erectile dysfunction and vascular risk factors." **Asian Journal of Andrology**, 16(2):290-294, Mar-Apr 2014. DOI: 10.4103/1008-682X.122347

## Core Findings
Tadalafil 20 mg every other day for 4 weeks improved erectile function (P=0.0028) but had only a **modest effect** on laboratory measures of endothelial cell damage/dysfunction and repair ability in men with ED and vascular risk factors. ET-1 and t-PA were reduced, but sE-selectin was unchanged and the inhibitory effect of patient serum on CACs/CFUs from healthy men was unaffected.

## Study Type, Population, and Dosing
- **Design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial with 4-week treatment period and 4-week run-in (no PDE5i, aspirin, antioxidants, or vasoactive drugs)
- **Population:** 36 men aged 30-70 years with ED for >6 months and vascular risk factors, recruited from Andrology Clinic, University of L'Aquila, Italy; 10 healthy men served as controls
- **Dosing:** Tadalafil 20 mg (Cialis) or placebo, every other day for 4 weeks (14 capsules total)
- **Exclusions:** Endocrine diseases (except type 1/2 diabetes), hypogonadism (T < 250 ng/dL), pelvic surgery/trauma, prostatic disease, neurological/psychiatric disorders, overt vascular disease, active peptic ulcer, alcohol/substance abuse, major hematological/renal/hepatic abnormalities, nitrate therapy
- **Primary outcome:** Effect of tadalafil on serum-mediated inhibition of CACs and CFUs from healthy men
- **Secondary outcome:** Effect on circulating markers of endothelial damage/dysfunction (ET-1, sE-selectin, t-PA)

## Key Findings

### Erectile Function
- SHIM score improved with tadalafil (baseline 9.11 +/- 6.28 to 14.00 +/- 8.13) vs placebo (baseline 10.15 +/- 6.88 to 8.8 +/- 7.5); F=10.38, **P=0.0028**

### Endothelial Repair Surrogate (CACs/CFUs)
- Serum from ED patients inhibited CACs and CFUs from healthy men more than serum from healthy controls (P < 0.017)
- Tadalafil treatment did **not** reverse this inhibitory effect on CACs or CFUs
- This is a negative finding: the surrogate for endothelial repair capacity was not improved by tadalafil

### Endothelial Damage/Dysfunction Markers
- **ET-1:** Reduced after tadalafil vs baseline and placebo (F=7.26, **P=0.011**); levels restored to those of healthy men. Both patient groups had elevated ET-1 at baseline vs controls.
- **t-PA:** Reduced after tadalafil (F=8.99, **P=0.005**). However, t-PA was NOT elevated at baseline compared to healthy men, so the clinical significance is uncertain.
- **sE-selectin:** Elevated at baseline in ED patients vs healthy men, but **no change** after tadalafil treatment.

### Overall Conclusion
Tadalafil improved erectile function but had only a modest effect on laboratory surrogates of endothelial health. The authors suggest that the beneficial effects of PDE5i on endothelial cells are "balanced by the detrimental effects on vascular homeostasis exerted by the VRFs" and that the hypothesis of PDE5i benefiting vascular homeostasis requires further analysis via longitudinal studies with clinical vascular outcomes.

## Methodology
- Ex vivo assay: MNCs from 10 healthy men cultured with 20% human serum from ED patients (before and after treatment) to generate CACs and CFUs
- CACs identified by double positivity for DiLDL and FITC-UEA-1; counted in 10 random fields by two independent investigators
- ELISA for sE-selectin, ET-1, and t-PA (all in duplicate)
- Statistical analysis: Kruskal-Wallis, Wilcoxon rank-sum with alpha adjustment, two-factor ANOVA for repeated measures (SAS software)

## Distinction from src-004
**src-004 and src-007 are the same paper** (identical DOI: 10.4103/1008-682X.122347, same authors, same journal, same year/volume/pages). src-004 was sourced from PMC (PubMed Central), src-007 from the LWW (Wolters Kluwer) journal website. The content is equivalent. This is a duplicate source that should not be double-counted in evidence synthesis.

## Limitations
1. **Small sample size:** n=36 ED patients total (divided between tadalafil and placebo groups, so ~18 per arm)
2. **Short duration:** Only 4 weeks of treatment
3. The ex vivo assay uses serum effects on healthy donor cells, not direct measurement of patient endothelial repair capacity
4. t-PA was reduced by tadalafil but was not elevated at baseline, making the clinical significance uncertain
5. No direct measurement of patient CACs/CFUs; only serum-mediated effects on healthy donor cells were assessed

## Claims to Verify
- The exact split between tadalafil and placebo groups is not stated; assumed ~18 per arm from n=36, but the paper does not specify per-group n
- All statistical tests used two-factor ANOVA for repeated measures; the P values (0.0028, 0.011, 0.005) should be verified against the F statistics in context of the small sample

## Relevance to Research Question
This paper directly addresses the effects of tadalafil on endothelial function biomarkers in men with ED and vascular risk factors. Its main contribution is the **modest/negative finding** -- tadalafil improved some markers (ET-1, t-PA) but not others (sE-selectin) and had no effect on the endothelial repair surrogate (CACs/CFUs). This qualifies more optimistic findings from earlier studies (Bocchio 2008, Rosano 2005, Foresta 2006) that reported stronger endothelial benefits from PDE5i treatment. The paper explicitly calls for longitudinal studies with clinical outcomes rather than surrogate markers.
