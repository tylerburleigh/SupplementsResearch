# Source src-009: Potential beneficial impacts of tadalafil on cardiovascular diseases

## Study Type and Scope

- **Type:** Narrative review
- **Year:** 2025 (published J Chin Med Assoc, 88(4):267-272; PMID 39789694)
- **Authors:** Lan, Chiang, Chen, Chang (National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University, Taiwan; Taoyuan General Hospital; National Taiwan University Hospital; Taipei Medical University)
- **DOI:** 10.1097/JCMA.0000000000001205
- **Scope:** Reviews clinical and preclinical evidence for tadalafil's effects across multiple cardiovascular disease areas: PAH, atherosclerosis, CAD, heart failure, myocardial infarction, stroke, diabetic vascular disease, and cardiomyopathy.

## Key Findings

### Mechanism
- Tadalafil is a PDE5 inhibitor that increases intracellular NO and cGMP levels, stimulates endothelial NO synthase activity, and increases NO bioavailability.
- PDE5 is widely expressed (PDE5A2 in penile endothelium, aortic smooth muscle, aortic endothelium), providing rationale for cardiovascular effects beyond ED.
- The authors note tadalafil may have cardiovascular protective effects beyond PDE5 inhibition, particularly in cardiomyopathy, but this requires further investigation.

### Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (FDA-approved)
- Tadalafil is FDA-approved for PAH treatment.
- Reduces pulmonary arterial pressure, improves exercise capacity, reduces clinical deterioration.
- Clinical trial: 405 patients, doses of 2.5-40 mg daily for 16 weeks increased walking distance and time to clinical worsening (Galie et al., 2009).

### Atherosclerosis and CAD
- Chronic tadalafil (20 mg every other day x 4 weeks) improved endothelial function in 32 men with increased cardiovascular risk (Rosano et al., 2005).
- 20 mg single dose showed no adverse effects on myocardial blood flow in 7 stable CAD patients (Weinsaft et al., 2006).
- 10 mg single dose in 23 stable CAD patients: no significant effect on ischemia duration, but lower systolic BP (Patterson et al., 2005).
- 5 mg and 10 mg single dose in 50 chronic stable angina patients: small decrease in systolic and diastolic BP (Kloner et al., 2003).
- Long-term 5 mg daily for 48 weeks reduced arteriosclerosis (pulse wave velocity) in elderly patients with LUTS (Hayashi et al., 2019).

### Heart Failure and Myocardial Infarction
- Single 20 mg dose improved arterial stiffness and left ventricular diastolic function in 30 ED patients without known atherosclerotic risk factors (Ozdabakoglu et al., 2017).
- Preclinical: tadalafil reduced cardiac hypertrophy, improved LV function in rat heart failure model; reduced infarct size in rat acute MI model; reduced cardiac hypertrophy and pulmonary edema in mouse MI model.

### Stroke (Controversial)
- Some positive effects: increased cerebral vascular density and neurogenesis in rat embolic stroke model; improved cerebral microvascular oxygenation in stroke patients with small-vessel disease.
- BUT: case reports of stroke after tadalafil administration; one study showed reduced blood flow to both perilesional and distant brain regions post-stroke.
- **Authors caution: tadalafil should be used very carefully or avoided in subjects at high risk of cerebrovascular ischemic events.**

### Diabetic Vascular Disease
- Limited evidence: 5 mg daily for 4 weeks in 20 patients with type 2 diabetes and ED improved capillary width and blood flow rate (Lee et al., 2023).
- Further studies needed due to complexity of diabetes pathology.

### Cardiomyopathy
- Preclinical only: tadalafil attenuated doxorubicin-induced cardiomyopathy in mice via cGMP pathway without interfering with chemotherapy.
- Delayed cardiomyopathy progression in Duchenne muscular dystrophy models (mdx mice, golden retrievers).

## Healthy Populations / Prevention

- The review does not specifically address tadalafil use in healthy populations for prevention. Most clinical evidence is in patients with existing cardiovascular risk factors or disease.
- One notable finding: tadalafil improved arterial stiffness and LV diastolic function in ED patients "without known atherosclerotic risk factors or cardiac diseases" (line 251-252), suggesting potential benefit even in relatively lower-risk populations.
- The authors speculate on "universal NO-related cardiovascular benefits" but state existing data are insufficient for general clinical use and large-scale studies are needed.

## Risk Profile

- **Contraindicated** with organic nitrates due to life-threatening hypotension risk via NO/cGMP pathway.
- **Most common side effects:** Headache and dyspepsia, which decrease with continued administration.
- **High cardiovascular safety** recognized in large patient populations.
- **PDE5 inhibitors infrequently associated with major adverse cardiovascular events.**
- **Stroke caution:** Should be used very carefully or avoided in subjects at high risk of cerebrovascular ischemic events.
- Overall, tadalafil has high cardiovascular safety profile, but stroke data are controversial and warrant caution.

## Limitations

- Narrative review (not systematic); may have selection bias in included studies.
- Most evidence for heart failure, MI, and cardiomyopathy comes from preclinical animal models.
- Sample sizes in clinical studies are generally small (7-68 patients).
- No direct discussion of healthy population use or primary prevention.
- Authors repeatedly state further large-scale clinical trials are needed for each cardiovascular indication.
- Stroke evidence is contradictory; net effect unclear.

## Relevance to Research

This review provides a comprehensive overview of tadalafil's cardiovascular effects, with particular strength in:
1. Mechanistic rationale for cardiovascular benefit (NO/cGMP pathway)
2. Safety profile and contraindications
3. Dosing information across multiple indications
4. FDA-approved PAH indication

Key gaps for research on healthy populations:
- No direct evidence for primary prevention in healthy individuals
- Limited discussion of long-term prophylactic use
- All clinical evidence is in disease or high-risk populations
