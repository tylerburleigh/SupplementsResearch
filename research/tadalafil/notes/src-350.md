## Core Findings

This perspective article reviews the evidence base for repurposing FDA-approved PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil) for Alzheimer's disease and related dementias (ADRD). The authors conclude that PDE5 inhibitors merit further prospective clinical trials, with drug selection depending on brain penetration, tolerability, half-life, and off-target effects. While animal data strongly support cognitive benefits and real-world data suggest sildenafil is associated with reduced dementia risk, clinical trial evidence remains limited and mostly neutral.

## Study Type and Methodology

- **Type:** Perspective/review article (not a systematic review, though informed by literature searches via PubMed)
- **Scope:** Covers pharmacological properties of PDE5 inhibitors, preclinical animal data, human clinical trial evidence, and pharmacoepidemiologic studies
- **Authors include:** Researchers from St George's University of London, Columbia University, Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, Cleveland Clinic -- includes both preclinical and clinical expertise

## Key Findings About PDE5 Inhibitors for Dementia

### Mechanism
- PDE5 is expressed in brain neurons and vascular myocytes within subcortical white matter
- PDE5 inhibitors augment NO/cGMP/PKG/CREB signaling pathway, which participates in synaptic strengthening (long-term potentiation)
- Two hypothesized sites of action: vascular (increasing cerebral perfusion) and neuronal (augmenting synaptic strengthening)

### Preclinical Evidence (Strong)
- Multiple laboratories report cognitive benefits of PDE5i in rats, mice, and non-human primates
- Sildenafil improved memory, amyloid plaque load, inflammation, and neurogenesis in APP/PS1 transgenic mice (Puzzo et al., 2009)
- Sildenafil improved memory, tau hyperphosphorylation in aged J20 mice (Garcia-Barroso et al., 2013)
- Tadalafil has shown contradictory findings in transgenic AD mouse models
- Novel PDE5i compounds (quinoline-based 7a, naphthyridine-based 6c) show high potency and BBB penetration

### Epidemiologic Evidence (Mixed)
- **Supportive:** Fang et al. (Nature Aging, 2021) -- sildenafil associated with ~30-60% reduced AD risk in insurance claims data for 7.23 million older individuals
  - Propensity score-stratified analyses confirmed association across four drug cohorts
  - Sildenafil increased neurite growth and decreased p-tau181 in AD patient iPSC-derived neurons
  - **Limitations:** Does not establish causality; potential confounding by wealth/status; time-related biases
- **Neutral:** Desai et al. (Brain Communications, 2022) -- no significant association between PDE5i initiation and incident ADRD in PAH patients
  - Used active-comparator, new-user design (strength)
  - **Limitations noted by authors:** PAH is rare in elderly; short follow-up (~6 months); low PDE5i doses may not achieve adequate brain concentrations

## Clinical Trial Evidence

### PASTIS Trial (Tadalafil for VCID)
- Phase II RCT: single-dose tadalafil 20 mg vs placebo in older adults with small vessel disease
- N=65 enrolled, N=55 completing protocol
- Primary outcome: change in subcortical CBF by arterial spin labeling
- **Result:** No significant difference between tadalafil and placebo for CBF
- Largest effect: trend for increased blood flow within white matter hyperintensities (9.8% increase, P=0.096)
- Post hoc: trend for increased CBF in participants aged >65
- No serious adverse events
- Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT02450253

### Sildenafil Pilot Studies in AD
- Study 1: Single 50mg sildenafil decreased spontaneous neural activity in right hippocampus (N=10 AD patients)
- Study 2: Single 50mg sildenafil increased cerebral metabolic rate of oxygen and CBF (N=12 AD patients)
- Both lacked control groups; difficult to interpret quantitatively

### Other Clinical Studies
- Small series of ED patients showed diverse regional CBF changes after sildenafil and tadalafil, no consistent pattern
- No clear placebo-controlled evidence of acute CBF effects in humans

## Distinction Between Healthy and Diseased Populations

- Healthy young adults showed **no change** in CBF after acute PDE5i treatment (cited studies: Jahshan 2017; Kruuse 2009)
- Small studies in healthy volunteers (N=6-10) found no cognitive effects of acute sildenafil
- Cognitive benefits in animal models were primarily seen in disease models (APP/PS1, scopolamine-induced amnesia) or aged animals
- This suggests PDE5i cognitive effects may be specific to diseased/aged populations rather than enhancement in healthy individuals

## Pharmacological Comparison of Key PDE5 Inhibitors

| Drug | PDE5 IC50 | Key Off-Targets | Brain:Plasma Ratio | Half-Life |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Sildenafil | 1-9 nM | PDE6 (10-40 nM) | 0.028-0.050 | Short (~4h) |
| Tadalafil | 1-7 nM | PDE11 (10-300 nM) | 0.10-0.12 | 16 hours |
| Vardenafil | 0.1-1.0 nM | PDE6, PDE1, PDE9, PDE11 | -- | Short |

- Tadalafil has better brain penetration and longer half-life than sildenafil
- Sildenafil has PDE6 cross-reactivity (potential visual side effects)
- Tadalafil has PDE11 cross-reactivity

## Limitations of the Evidence

1. **Clinical trials are small and brief:** Most human studies have N<30 and use acute single-dose designs
2. **PASTIS trial was neutral:** Single-dose tadalafil showed no significant CBF effect; may need chronic dosing
3. **Epidemiologic evidence is conflicting:** Fang et al. supportive but subject to confounding; Desai et al. neutral but in a different population (PAH)
4. **Animal-to-human translation uncertain:** Animal models may not adequately model the complexity of vascular and AD pathologies in aging humans
5. **Brain penetration data in older humans is lacking:** Most PK data from animals or young healthy volunteers
6. **Drug-specific effects unclear:** Most animal studies used sildenafil; tadalafil data more contradictory
7. **Optimal dosing regimen unknown:** Acute vs chronic, dose level, treatment duration all undetermined

## Authors' Recommendations for Future Trials

- Chronic treatment for at least 12 months in a placebo-controlled RCT
- Recruit both sexes, including patients aged >=80 years
- Long-term follow-up
- Drug selection should consider: tolerability in older people, brain penetration, plasma half-life, and off-target effects
- New compounds with high BBB permeability and ~24-hour half-life would be optimal
