# src-002: Rosano et al. - Chronic Treatment with Tadalafil Improves Endothelial Function in Men with Increased Cardiovascular Risk

**Source:** PubMed abstract (PMID 15661417), published in European Urology, 2005. Commentary in J Urol by Seftel (2005).

**Content quality:** Abstract only -- full text behind paywall. Abstract contains complete study design, key results with statistics, and conclusions. No methods detail beyond randomization scheme.

## Core Findings

Chronic tadalafil administration (20 mg alternate days, 4 weeks) produced a more than 2-fold improvement in brachial artery flow-mediated dilation (FMD) in men with increased cardiovascular risk, and this benefit was sustained for at least 2 weeks after treatment discontinuation. The improvement was accompanied by increased nitric oxide bioavailability (higher nitrite/nitrate) and reduced endothelin-1, with these changes being strongly and inversely correlated (adjusted R2=0.81).

## Study Design

- **Type:** Randomized controlled trial (RCT), placebo-controlled
- **Population:** 32 patients with increased cardiovascular risk (and ED)
- **Intervention:** Tadalafil 20 mg on alternate days vs. matching placebo
- **Duration:** 4 weeks treatment + 2-week post-treatment follow-up (6 weeks total)
- **Measures:** Brachial artery FMD, plasma nitrite/nitrate, plasma endothelin-1
- **Timepoints:** Baseline, 4 weeks (end of treatment), 6 weeks (2 weeks post-discontinuation)

## Key Results

### FMD (Primary Outcome)
| Timepoint | Tadalafil | Placebo |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Baseline  | 4.2+/-3.2% | 4.1+/-2.8% |
| 4 weeks   | 9.3+/-3.7% (p<0.01 vs baseline) | 4.0+/-3.4% (p=NS) |
| 6 weeks   | 9.1+/-3.9% (p=0.01 vs baseline; p=NS vs 4 weeks) | No change |

The FMD improvement represents an absolute increase of ~5.1 percentage points (121% relative increase from baseline). This is a large effect size for FMD interventions.

### Nitrite/Nitrate Levels (NO Bioavailability)
- Baseline: 38.2+/-12.3
- 4 weeks: 52.6+/-11.7 (p<0.05)
- 6 weeks: 51.1+/-3.1 (p<0.05)
- Sustained at 2 weeks post-discontinuation

### Endothelin-1 Levels (Vasoconstrictor)
- Baseline: 3.3+/-0.9
- 4 weeks: 2.9+/-0.7 (p<0.05)
- 6 weeks: 2.9+/-0.9 (p<0.05)
- Strong inverse correlation with nitrite/nitrate increase (adjusted R2=0.81, p<0.0001)

## Sustained Benefit After Discontinuation

A critical finding is that the endothelial benefit persisted for at least 2 weeks after stopping tadalafil. FMD at 6 weeks (9.1+/-3.9%) was not significantly different from the 4-week value (9.3+/-3.7%). This aligns with tadalafil's long half-life (~17.5 hours) but the sustained effect well beyond washout suggests possible vascular remodeling or sustained NO pathway upregulation rather than mere pharmacologic effect.

## Relevance to Healthy Populations

**Population characteristics:** The study enrolled men with "increased cardiovascular risk" and ED. This means they had risk factors (likely hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, smoking history) but may not have had diagnosed CVD. The baseline FMD of 4.2% indicates impaired endothelial function (normal FMD is typically >7-8%).

**Extrapolation considerations:**
- The magnitude of improvement in healthy individuals would likely be smaller, as baseline FMD would be higher and ceiling effects would reduce delta
- However, the mechanism (PDE5 inhibition increasing cGMP, enhancing NO signaling, reducing endothelin-1) should still operate in healthy vasculature
- The sustained benefit after discontinuation is particularly relevant for any potential chronic use protocol
- The dose (20 mg alternate days) is the same used for ED, suggesting this is achievable at standard therapeutic dosing

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size (n=32)** -- no power calculation reported in abstract; may be underpowered for secondary endpoints
2. **Abstract only** -- full methods, randomization details, and adverse events not available
3. **Population specificity** -- men with CV risk and ED; results may not generalize to healthy individuals or women
4. **Short follow-up** -- only 2 weeks post-treatment; durability beyond this is unknown
5. **Surrogate endpoint** -- FMD is a validated surrogate but not a hard clinical outcome
6. **No dose-response data** -- single dose tested; optimal dose for vascular effects unknown

## Claims to Verify

- The sample size of 32 is small for an RCT. Need full text to confirm allocation ratio (likely 16:16 but not stated).
- The 6-week nitrite/nitrate SD of +/-3.1 is notably smaller than baseline (+/-12.3) and 4-week (+/-11.7) SDs. This could indicate a reporting error, or it may reflect a subset measurement. Worth flagging for verification if full text becomes available.
- The R2=0.81 correlation between nitrite/nitrate and endothelin-1 is very high; need full text to confirm whether this was per-group or pooled analysis.
