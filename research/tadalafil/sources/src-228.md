Associations between phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors and vascular function: a systematic review and meta-analysis on randomized-controlled trials

Download PDF

Abstract
Background
Phosphodiesterase type-5 inhibitors (PDE5is) are used for the treatment of erectile dysfunction (ED) and have potential cardioprotective effects. The impacts of PDE5is on cardiovascular parameters, which may be associated with the occurrence and progression of subclinical cardiovascular diseases, remain uncertain. In this study, we evaluated the effects of PDE5is on vascular parameters.
Methods
Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that compared the effects of PDE5is and placebo on vascular parameters and were published from 1998 to 2022 were identified from PubMed, Scopus and Web of Science. Mean differences (MDs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were pooled. A sensitivity analysis was conducted to confirm the robustness of the pooled results. The keywords that were searched in the databases are as follows: ((systolic blood pressure) OR (SBP) OR (diastolic blood pressure) OR (DBP) OR (mean arterial pressure) OR (MAP) OR (Pulse Wave Velocity) OR (PWV) OR (intima-media thickness) OR (cIMT) OR (augmentation Index) OR (AI) OR (FMD) OR (flow-mediated dilation) OR (reactive hyperemia index) OR (RHI) OR (Endothelial microparticles) OR (EMP) OR (EPCs) OR (Endothelial Progenitor Cells) OR (PSV) OR (peak systolic velocity)) AND ((PDE5 Inhibitors) OR (PDE5i) OR (Sildenafil) OR (Vardenafil) OR (Tadalafil) OR (Lodenafil) OR (Udenafil) OR (Avanafil)).
Results
Sixty-three studies involving 3242 subjects were included. Overall, PDE5is decreased systolic blood pressure (MD: -2.80 mmHg, 95% CI: -4.24, -1.37,
P
< 0.001), diastolic blood pressure (MD: -1.80 mmHg, 95% CI: -2.37, -1.22,
P
< 0.001), carotid intima‒media thickness (MD: -0.01 mm, 95% CI: -0.02, -0.01,
P
< 0.001), and pulse wave velocity (MD: -0.75 cm/s, 95% CI: -1.01, -0.49,
P
< 0.001). In addition, PDE5is increased the peak systolic velocity (MD: 3.70 cm/s, 95% CI: 3.52, 3.88,
P
< 0.001), flow-mediated dilation (MD: 2.47%, 95% CI: 1.24, 3.71,
P
< 0.001), concentration of endothelial progenitor cells (MD: 475.29 cells/mL, 95% CI: 51.38, 899.20,
P
= 0.03), and concentration of endothelial microparticles (MD: 4.86%, 95% CI: 0.65, 9.07,
P
= 0.02). However, the effects of PDE5is on the augmentation index, brachial artery diameter and reactive hyperemia index were not statistically significant.
Conclusion
Compared with the placebo, PDE5is improved vascular parameters, indicating the potential of PDE5is for treating subclinical cardiovascular diseases. Further research is needed to confirm the role of the improvement on vascular parameters by PDE5isin preventing and treating cardiovascular diseases.
Systematic review registration
The present study protocol was reviewed and approved by PROSPERO. The title is “Associations between PDE5is and vascular parameters: A systematic review and meta-analysis” (
https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/
) (Reg. No. CRD42023387924).

Background
The cardiovascular disease (CVD) burden is worsening, with an increasing number of people being afflicted. Moreover, CVD is now the leading cause of death globally, with approximately 17.9 million deaths annually attributed to cardiovascular ailments [
1
]. Stroke and acute coronary syndrome are key drivers [
2
]. The age of onset for CVD is also decreasing annually, with increasing threats facing more middle-aged and young people [
3
]. Prevention and control of cardiovascular illness have become top priorities for global and national health authorities [
4
].
Subclinical CVD, including atherosclerosis and arterial stiffness, is a strong predictor of the future development of clinical CVD, and the prevalence of subclinical CVD increases with age [
5
]. It predicts a variety of poor health outcomes, including accelerated aging, frailty, cognitive decline, and all-cause mortality [
6
,
7
,
8
]. Several vascular parameters are considered biomarkers of subclinical CVDs, such as carotid intima‒media thickness (cIMT) [
9
], blood pressure (BP) [
10
], pulse wave velocity (PWV) [
11
], brachial artery diameter (BAD), flow-mediated dilation (FMD), reactive hyperemia index (RHI), endothelial progenitor cells (EPCs) and endothelial microparticle (EMP) [
12
]. Identifying subclinical CVD and its associated risk factors can help reduce the progression of clinical CVD events as well as CVD-related disability and mortality [
13
].
Phosphodiesterase type-5 inhibitors (PDE5is), such as sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil and udenafil, play increasingly important roles in cardiovascular protection [
14
]. PDE5is exert wide effects on smooth muscle cells, including coronary and other blood vessels, by producing vasodilation. PDE5is can increase nitric oxide levels in vessels, which induces relaxation and mitigates atherogenic lesions [
15
]. Animal experiments have shown that PDE5is can inhibit myocardial fibrosis and ischemia‒reperfusion injury, shielding cardiomyocytes [
16
,
17
,
18
]. Clinical trials have shown that long-term sildenafil use may decrease the incidence of cardiovascular events, such as nonfatal myocardial infarction [
19
,
20
]. Sildenafil can also improve coronary reperfusion injury after occlusion [
21
,
22
].
However, the impacts of PDE5is on cardiovascular parameters, which are associated with the occurrence and progression of subclinical CVD, remain unclear because of the lack of suitable measurement methods in the past. Recent developments in noninvasive techniques have made it easier to assess the structure and function of blood vessels. For example, cIMT is a strong marker of arteriosclerosis and atherosclerosis. The PWV indicates arterial stiffness and serves as a functional marker [
23
,
24
]. FMD, EPCs and other metrics are considered endothelial function markers. These novel findings may help elucidate the effects of PDE5is on endothelial function [
25
,
26
]. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to appraise and synthesize existing evidence regarding the effects of PDE5is on vascular parameters.
Methods
This study was performed in strict accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) statement guidelines [
27
]. The review was registered in PROSPERO (
https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/
, registration number CRD42023387924).
Data sources
We searched for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that assessed the effects of PDE5is on vascular parameters in PubMed, Web of Science and Scopus from 1998 to 2022 and compared PDE5is with placebo. The computer-based searches combined free texts and MeSH terms related to PDE5is, subclinical CVD and vascular parameters (Supplementary Table 1).
Search strategy
Full texts were obtained and independently evaluated by two reviewers after screening potentially eligible abstracts and titles. To identify potential articles missed by the search strategy, we searched the references cited in relevant studies, as well as the “Cited Reference Search” feature in the Web of Science [
28
]. Additionally, in the case of multiple publications, details of the most complete/recent publications would be used.
Selection criteria
RCTs that assessed the effects of PDE5is on vascular function, including: systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), mean arterial pressure (MAP), cIMT, peak systolic velocity (PSV), PWV, augmentation index (AI), BAD, RHI, FMD, EPCs, EMP, by comparing PDE5is with placebo were included. Non-English publications, guidelines, reviews, observational studies, head-to-head studies, and studies without valid comparisons or relevant outcomes were excluded. Non-placebo-controlled trials were excluded.
Data extraction
The data were extracted by one reviewer and checked independently by a second reviewer. The means with standard deviations or medians with quartiles of vascular parameters were extracted. A standardized predesigned data collection form was used for data extraction.
Description of outcome measures
In our study, we assessed the effects of PDE5is on vascular parameters, including MAP, SBP, DBP, cIMT, FMD, BAD, PSV, PWV, AI, RHI, EPCs and EMP. A detailed description of the measurements of these parameters is provided in Supplementary Table 2.
Risk of bias and certainty of evidence
The Cochrane Collaboration’s risk of bias tool was used to assess the risk of bias of the RCTs [
29
]. The risk was quantified in each domain as low risk, moderate risk, serious risk, or critical risk. All studies were independently assessed by two reviewers. Funnel plots were used to assess the possibility of publication bias. Asymmetry in the plot was interpreted as suggestive of a high risk of publication bias. In cases of significant asymmetry, sensitivity analyses were performed to quantify its potential impact on the pooled results. Symmetrical plots were considered to indicate a low risk of publication bias.
Statistical analysis
Review Manager software (version 5.4; The Cochrane Collaboration, UK) was used. Medians with quartiles were transformed into means with standard deviations for pooled estimates via the webpage tool in the BOX-COX manner developed by McGrath et al. [
30
]. Pooled estimates of MDs were generated via a random effects model, the results of which are displayed in corresponding forest plots [
31
]. Additionally, we reported heterogeneity (I
2
) and between-study variance (τ
2
; squared). Subgroup analysis was performed on the basis of disease status. A sensitivity analysis was conducted to confirm the robustness of the pooled results.

Discussion
The efficacy of PDE5is in reducing the incidence of cardiovascular events has been previously reported [
83
]. Moreover, the use of PDE5is also has long-term beneficial effects and is associated with a lower risk of overall mortality [
84
]. PDE5is have considerable therapeutic potential in various diseases, such as hypertension, myocardial infarction, stroke, peripheral artery disease, chronic kidney disease and diabetes [
85
]. Even in patients with resistant hypertension, sildenafil can rapidly improve diastolic function and hemodynamic characteristics [
75
]. PDE5is have protective effects on the heart, and walking distance improved in patients with limb and hip claudication after sildenafil treatment [
86
,
87
]. Animal experiments have shown that PDE5is can directly affect brain tissue through the blood‒brain barrier, indicating a potential association between PDE5is and cerebrovascular diseases [
88
]. In addition, PDE5is are commonly used to treat ED in patients with chronic kidney disease [
89
], and a phase 3 clinical trial revealed that a novel form of PDE5i reduces proteinuria in patients with chronic kidney disease [
90
]. PDE5is can also improve the overall survival of type 2 diabetes patients and improve the heart index [
91
]. Vascular parameters play comprehensive and important roles in the cardiovascular system and are also related to cognitive function and renal dysfunction [
92
,
93
]. Therefore, studies on the associations between PDE5is and vascular parameters can expand our understanding of PDE5i use and help in the management of relevant patients.
However, the impacts of PDE5is on cardiovascular parameters, which may be associated with the occurrence and progression of subclinical CVDs, remain uncertain. Therefore, we performed this systematic review and meta-analysis on relevant RCTs. Compared with placebo, PDE5is had significant effects on BP and most vascular parameters.
Among the 63 RCTs included in the study, 20 reported minor adverse effects when PDE5is were used. However, major adverse effects were not reported in any of the studies. Among the common adverse effects, the incidence of headache is 11%−37.5% [
32
,
40
,
58
,
70
,
74
,
76
,
77
], the incidence of dizziness is 20.8% [
77
], the incidence of flushing is 8.3%−25% [
37
,
42
,
46
,
47
,
48
,
49
], and the incidence of dyspepsia is 5.8%−16.7% [
51
,
70
,
76
]. Notably, the rate of adverse events in the PDE5i group was not significantly different from that in the placebo group, which confirmed the safety of PDE5i use.
Comparison with previous studies
According to a meta-analysis including 24 unique studies [
94
], PDE5is did not increase the risk of adverse cardiovascular outcomes, including BP endpoints, cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality, in patients with ED and diabetes. Additionally, in a meta-analysis reported by Hwang et al. [
95
], PDE5is improved pulmonary hemodynamics and exercise capacity in heart failure patients with a reduced ejection fraction but not in heart failure patients with a preserved ejection fraction. However, this meta-analysis did not reveal a cardiovascular benefit from PDE5is. Considering the therapeutic potential of PDE5is in various CVDs, we still hypothesized that the cardiovascular effects of PDE5is may be long-term. Therefore, in this systematic review and meta-analysis, we innovatively studied the effects of PDE5is on vascular and endothelial parameters because abnormalities in these parameters play roles in many CVDs.
cIMT is an intermediate phenotype of early atherosclerosis and a predictive marker for atherosclerotic progression [
23
]. PDE5is can block PDE5 activity, increase cGMP levels, activate cGMP-dependent protein kinase G, promote vascular smooth muscle relaxation and improve intimal hemodynamics after PKG activation, thereby reducing cIMT [
85
]. A previous meta-analysis revealed that increased cIMT is an important predictor of future CVD events [
96
]. Associations between cIMT and coronary events such as angina, MI and coronary interventions as well as cerebrovascular events have been investigated in various longitudinal studies [
97
]. These findings effectively demonstrated that IMT, particularly cIMT, can serve as an important risk predictive marker for CVDs. Our meta-analysis revealed that PDE5is significantly decreased cIMT without obvious heterogeneity among the included studies.
PWV is considered the gold standard method for measuring arterial stiffness. PDE5 inhibitors may lower the PWV through mechanisms such as vasodilation, anti-inflammatory effects, and the inhibition of vascular wall hypertrophy [
94
]. Although PDE5is significantly decreased the PWV, no significant effects on the ABI or AI were detected. Only one study reported a significant effect on PSV. Owing to the limited number of relevant studies, more research is needed to assess the effect of PDE5is on vascular stiffness, which is related to the PWV, ABI, AI, and PSV.
PDE5is may also improve endothelial function, which can be assessed by FMD, by activating the NO-cGMP signaling pathway [
94
]. A meta-analysis revealed that a 1% increase in FMD is correlated with a 13% reduction in CVD risk [
98
]. Our meta-analysis revealed that PDE5is significantly increased FMD. Notably, in the subgroup analysis, PDE5i significantly increased FMD in CVD patients but not in smokers or healthy individuals. In addition, PDE5is did not improve BAD or the RHI in smokers.
EPCs and EMPs are popular novel noninvasive biomarkers for assessing endothelial function and endothelial injury [
99
]. Our meta-analysis revealed that PDE5is significantly increased the number of EPCs and EMPs. Considering the limited number of studies, more research is needed to elucidate the relationship between the use of PDE5is and EPCs/EMPs.
Clinical significance
This meta-analysis aimed to help understand the underling mechanisms whereby PDE5is prevent CVDs and accentuate the significance of early amelioration of these vascular parameters for the prevention of CVDs. It provides cues for screening the appropriate individuals for PDE5i treatment. Based on our findings, in the event of vascular aberrations, PDE5i administration may be requisite to improve vascular status and mitigate the risk of subsequent cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disorders. Especially for people who are conventionally not susceptible to high-CVD-risk, abnormal vascular parameters are worthy of the attention, and need to be effectively managed. Additionally, apart from cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, vascular parameters are inextricably linked to disorders such as diabetes, kidney disease, and peripheral artery disease. The reduction of PWV has been confirmed to be associated with the alleviation of renal function impairment in patients with chronic kidney disease [
100
]. The increase in FMD, the decrease in PWV and the reduction in AI are closely related to the alleviation of walking ability limitation caused by peripheral arteriosclerosis [
101
]. cIMT can measure the severity of patients with abnormal blood glucose, a decrease in cIMT indicates an improvement in the patient's condition [
102
,
103
]. Subsequent to the improvement of these vascular indices, the quality of life of patients was conspicuously enhanced [
104
]. Early screening of vascular parameters is of significant importance for individuals not yet diagnosed with CVDs, as it facilitates early prevention and intervention. For instance, in healthy subjects, clinicians should monitor changes in vascular indicators such as BP, PWV, and FMD, and consider incorporating PDE5i into the treatment regimen when these indicators deteriorate. For those already diagnosed with CVDs, the disease status of the subjects exerting a non-negligible influence on the effect of PED5is on vascular parameters. This study aimed to explore the effect of PED5is on vascular parameters in individuals with different disease conditions, such as CAD, healthy, and ED, which may provide more targeted medical recommendations for distinct populations and help improve the progression of CVDs. Besides, in our study, smokers were included in the “others” subgroup in some forest plots. In smokers, the use of PDE5i did not improve indicators such as FMD and BAD. Smoking may be a critical factor contributing to the ineffectiveness of PDE5i in this population. Therefore, smoking cessation should be the primary goal for this group. Therefore, the management of vascular parameters also exert an impact on the prevention and treatment of other diseases, not merely CVDs. In brief, our study demonstrated that PDE5i treatment may be suitable and necessary for individuals with abnormal vascular parameters.
Limitations
Despite our best efforts, this manuscript has several limitations warranting acknowledgment. First, significant heterogeneity was observed in the meta-analysis, such as differences in specific PDE5is, dosages, treatment durations, patient populations, and vascular parameter measurement methods. We did not conduct meta-regression to explore this heterogeneity further, instead using a random-effects model and subgroup analyses, though subgroup stratification by disease status failed to fully mitigate heterogeneity (e.g., the “Others” subgroup included unclassifiable smokers) and robust subgroup analyses were hindered by limited study numbers and inconsistent treatment regimens. Second, the impact of confounding factors and specific interventions could not be fully evaluated. While randomization in included trials theoretically ensured comparability between PDE5i and placebo groups (for lifestyle factors, disease history, and medications like cardiovascular drugs) and baseline disease conditions were comparable across RCTs, most studies did not report confounder-adjusted results, precluding our analysis of such factors, and unmeasured/unreported confounders (e.g., alcohol consumption, antioxidant supplementation) cannot be ruled out due to limited recording of these variables in a small subset of studies. Finally, our analysis was constrained by the scope of original studies. We could not assess physiological state’s effect on vascular response, compare outcomes across participant groups (ED, pulmonary hypertension, healthy controls), evaluate drug-drug interactions or concomitant medications affecting vascular function/the cGMP pathway, or compare pre- to post-intervention changes in vascular parameters, as most studies did not report these data. Consequently, the meta-analysis results should be interpreted with appropriate caution.
Conclusions
This meta-analysis comprehensively explored the effects of PDE5is on vascular parameters. In summary, PDE5is can improve vascular parameters, indicating the potential of PDE5is for treating subclinical CVDs. Further research is needed to confirm the role of the improvement on vascular parameters by PDE5is in preventing and treating CVDs.
