# src-267: Therapeutic Effects of EGCG in Relation to Molecular Pathways Controlling Inflammation, Oxidative Stress, and Apoptosis

**Citation:** Mokra D, Joskova M, Mokry J. Int J Mol Sci. 2022 Dec 25;24(1):340. doi: 10.3390/ijms24010340. PMCID: PMC9820274, PMID: 36613784.

**Type:** Comprehensive narrative review (peer-reviewed, open access, MDPI)

**Quality:** ok -- full text available with substantive methods/pathway/mechanism discussion

## Core Findings

This review provides a detailed mechanistic map of how EGCG, the most abundant polyphenol in green tea (>50% of catechins), modulates inflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis through three tiers of molecular targets: cell surface receptors, intracellular signaling pathways, and nuclear transcription factors. The paper synthesizes evidence across cancer, neurological, cardiovascular, respiratory, and metabolic diseases, drawing primarily from preclinical (in vitro and animal) studies with some clinical/epidemiological findings. The dual nature of EGCG is highlighted -- antioxidant at low/moderate concentrations (<=10 uM) but prooxidant at high concentrations (>10 uM), with the prooxidant action potentially useful for cancer cell apoptosis.

## Key Evidence/Data Points

### Composition and Pharmacokinetics
- EGCG is >50% of green tea catechins, ~16.5% of water-extractable tea fraction
- A cup of brewed tea contains ~200-300 mg EGCG
- EGCG elimination half-time: 3.9 h (vs EGC at 1.7 h); peak plasma at 1-4 h post-ingestion
- Low oral bioavailability due to glucuronation, methylation, sulfation in saliva/intestine/liver
- Galloyl moiety on EGCG responsible for stronger biological effects vs other catechins

### Mechanism: Cell Surface Receptors
- **67LR**: EGCG exclusively binds this receptor; activates Akt/eNOS/NO/cGMP/PKCdelta apoptotic pathway for anti-tumor action
- **TLR4**: EGCG inhibits TLR4 signaling (anti-inflammatory); alleviates insulin resistance in adipose tissue and liver
- **Growth factor receptors**: EGCG inhibits EGFR, VEGFR, PDGFR, IGFR-1 (anti-proliferative, anti-angiogenic)

### Mechanism: Intracellular Signaling
- **MAPK**: EGCG modulates ERK1/2, JNK/p38 (both inhibition and activation depending on context); suppresses NLRP3 inflammasome via MAPK inhibition
- **COX-2**: EGCG inhibits COX-2 without affecting COX-1; reduces prostaglandin E2
- **AMPK**: EGCG activates AMPK; promotes hepatic autophagy, lipid metabolism, reduces hepatosteatosis
- **PI3K/Akt/eNOS**: Context-dependent -- inhibited in cancer/asthma, activated for neuroprotection and endothelial function

### Mechanism: Nuclear Transcription Factors
- **NF-kB**: EGCG inhibits (anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor); central to EGCG's anti-inflammatory action
- **Nrf2/HO-1**: EGCG activates (antioxidant defense, cytoprotective genes); requires p38 MAPK
- **AP-1**: EGCG inhibits (anti-proliferative); prevents MMP overexpression
- **STAT1/STAT3**: EGCG inhibits both (anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor); suppresses tumor-promoting inflammation

### Dose-Response / Antioxidant vs Prooxidant
- Low/moderate EGCG (<=10 uM): antioxidant, stimulates protective signaling via low ROS
- High EGCG (>10 uM): prooxidant via autooxidation generating H2O2; useful for cancer cell apoptosis
- Direct antioxidant: ROS scavenging, metal chelation
- Indirect antioxidant: NF-kB/AP-1 inhibition, iNOS/COX-2 inhibition, SOD/GST induction, Nrf2 activation

### Clinical/Epidemiological Evidence
- **Ohsaki Cohort** (40,530 Japanese adults, 40-79y): Green tea consumption inversely associated with CVD mortality
- **Norwegian study** (~20,000 adults, 35-49y): Green tea reduced blood cholesterol and blood pressure
- **Obesity RCTs**: EGCG 4-8 weeks decreased triglycerides and BP but not body weight; high-dose EGCG in women with central obesity caused significant weight loss and reduced waist circumference without side effects
- **Diabetes**: People drinking >=4 cups tea/day may have 16% lower risk of type 2 diabetes (one study); another study showed no association at >3 cups/day -- inconsistent results
- **Neurological**: Epidemiological studies in Japan, China, Singapore link tea drinking to improved cognitive function and prevention of cognitive dysfunction; animal studies show EGCG reduces beta-amyloid accumulation and tau hyperphosphorylation
- **Cancer**: Multiple clinical studies cited showing positive effects of EGCG administration in various tumor types

### Respiratory Applications
- EGCG showed benefit in COVID-19: blocked spike-ACE2 binding, inhibited viral main protease (3CLpro), mitigated cytokine storm via TLR4/NF-kB suppression
- Benefit in asthma, COPD, pulmonary fibrosis, silicosis models

## Methodology

- Narrative review of English-language articles from PubMed
- No systematic search strategy reported; not a meta-analysis
- Covers primarily preclinical evidence (in vitro and animal studies)
- Organized by disease category and molecular pathway

## Limitations

1. **Primarily preclinical**: Most evidence comes from in vitro and animal studies; clinical data is limited and cited secondarily
2. **No systematic methodology**: Not a systematic review or meta-analysis; potential for selection bias in cited studies
3. **Dosing context unclear**: Many studies use supraphysiological EGCG concentrations that may not be achievable through oral tea consumption
4. **No safety/toxicity review**: Despite concluding that adverse effects of high-dose EGCG should be evaluated, the paper does not systematically review hepatotoxicity or safety data
5. **No direct comparison of EGCG supplements vs whole tea**: The review focuses on EGCG in isolation

- **Q2 (Safety/Hepatotoxicity)**: Low relevance -- flags safety as needed but does not review specific hepatotoxicity data or regulatory guidance
