# src-446: EGCG Reduces Vascular Aging via SIRT1/Autophagy/Ferroptosis

**Citation:** Zhang Y, Wang T, Wang L, et al. "Epigallocatechin-3-Gallate from Green Tea Reduces Vascular Aging and Endothelial Cell Senescence by Modifying Autophagy and Ferroptosis through the Sirtuin 1 Signaling Pathway." *ACS Omega.* 2025 Aug 19;10(34):38740-38752. doi: 10.1021/acsomega.5c03880. PMC12409518.

**Quality Assessment:** Full paper text available from PMC. Peer-reviewed, open-access (CC-BY-NC-ND 4.0). Published 2025 in ACS Omega. Substantive methods, results, and discussion.

---

## Core Findings

EGCG reduces vascular aging by upregulating SIRT1 in endothelial cells, which in turn promotes autophagy (via LC3I/II and Beclin-1 upregulation, P62 reduction) and inhibits ferroptosis (via Nrf2/HO-1/GPX4/FTH1 pathway). Both pharmacological inhibition (EX527) and genetic knockdown (siRNA) of SIRT1 reversed EGCG's protective effects, confirming SIRT1 as the critical mediator. In aged mice, 16 weeks of oral EGCG significantly improved multiple vascular aging metrics.

## Key Evidence

### In Vitro (HUVECs, DOX-induced senescence model)
- **Safe dose range:** EGCG at 10-50 uM did not affect HUVEC viability; 70 and 90 uM were cytotoxic (p < 0.001)
- **Optimal concentration:** 50 uM EGCG selected for mechanistic experiments
- **Senescence reduction:** EGCG dose-dependently reduced SA-beta-gal-positive cells and senescence markers (P16, P21, P53; p < 0.001)
- **SIRT1 upregulation:** EGCG markedly increased SIRT1 expression and activity (p < 0.001)
- **Autophagy enhancement:** EGCG reduced P62 and increased Beclin-1, LC3I, and LC3II (p < 0.05); LC3B puncta increased (p < 0.01)
- **Ferroptosis inhibition:** EGCG reduced ROS, ferrous iron, lipid peroxidation (BODIPY); restored mitochondrial membrane potential (JC-1); upregulated FTH1, GPX4, Nrf2, HO-1 (all p < 0.01)
- **SIRT1 dependency confirmed:** EX527 (10 uM, SIRT1 inhibitor) and SIRT1 siRNA both reversed EGCG's anti-senescence, pro-autophagy, and anti-ferroptosis effects

### In Vivo (Aged C57BL/6 mice)
- **Animals:** 15-month-old male mice, n=6 per group (Young, Aged, Aged+EGCG)
- **Dosing:** 50 mg/kg EGCG via oral gavage, once daily, 16 weeks
- **Vascular aging metrics (all significant):**
  - Aortic SA-beta-gal staining: significantly decreased (p < 0.001)
  - Pulse wave velocity: 3.02 +/- 0.30 -> 2.45 +/- 0.24 m/s (p < 0.01, ~19% reduction)
  - Aortic intima-media thickness: 81.77 +/- 4.20 -> 69.7 +/- 5.62 um (p < 0.01)
  - Collagen area: 48.5% +/- 2.51% -> 39.32% +/- 2.01% (p < 0.001)
- **Inflammation:** Plasma TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-1beta significantly decreased (p < 0.01)
- **Oxidative stress:** SOD increased, MDA decreased in EGCG group
- **SIRT1 expression:** Significantly increased in aorta of EGCG group (p < 0.001, confirmed by IF)
- **Western blot (aorta):** P53 and P16 decreased; GPX4, LC3I/II, SIRT1 increased (all p < 0.05)

## Methodology

- **In vitro model:** DOX (2 uM) induced senescence in HUVECs; EGCG pretreatment 24h
- **In vivo model:** Naturally aged mice (15 months at start), 16-week intervention
- **Validation approaches:** Western blot, immunofluorescence, SA-beta-gal staining, CCK-8 viability, ROS probes (DCFH-DA, DHE), JC-1 mitochondrial potential, BODIPY C11 lipid peroxidation, ferrous iron staining, ELISA for cytokines, pulse wave velocity (ultrasound), H&E and Masson histology
- **SIRT1 specificity:** Dual approach -- pharmacological (EX527) and genetic (siRNA) inhibition
- **Statistics:** ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc; n=3 for blots, n=6 for staining/ELISA; all experiments repeated >=3 times

## Mechanistic Signaling Cascade

```
EGCG -> SIRT1 upregulation -> (1) Nrf2 activation -> HO-1, GPX4, FTH1 upregulation -> ferroptosis inhibition
                             -> (2) Autophagy promotion -> LC3I/II, Beclin-1 upregulation, P62 reduction
                             -> P53 deacetylation -> reduced cellular senescence
```

This study integrates both autophagy and ferroptosis under the SIRT1 umbrella, offering a more comprehensive view than studies focusing on single downstream pathways.

## Limitations

1. **No human data** -- in vitro only used HUVECs; in vivo only male C57BL/6 mice
2. Only endothelial cells examined; vascular smooth muscle cells not addressed
3. Only SIRT1, ferroptosis, and autophagy explored; other mechanisms (AMPK, mTOR, etc.) not investigated
4. Small in vivo sample size (n=6 per group)
5. Only male mice used; sex differences not addressed

## Relevance to Research Questions

**Q5 (Mechanistic debate -- antioxidant vs signaling pathways, sirtuins):** Strongly relevant. This paper provides direct evidence that EGCG acts through SIRT1 signaling (not merely as a direct antioxidant) to produce anti-aging effects. The dual regulation of both autophagy and ferroptosis by SIRT1 is a key mechanistic insight. SIRT1 inhibition reversed all EGCG benefits, establishing causality.

**Q4 (Optimal delivery strategy):** Moderately relevant. Oral gavage at 50 mg/kg/day for 16 weeks was effective in mice. In vitro dose-response shows 50 uM as optimal (with toxicity above 70 uM). However, no pharmacokinetic data, bioavailability analysis, or comparison of delivery routes was performed.

## Claims to Verify

- PWV values (3.02 -> 2.45 m/s) and intima-media thickness (81.77 -> 69.7 um) appear only in the results narrative; these are specific enough to cross-check against figure panels
- The claim that EGCG "extends lifespan of rats" (line 373) is cited but not demonstrated in this study
