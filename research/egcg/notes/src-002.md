# src-002: Green Tea Mitigates the Hallmarks of Aging and Age-Related Multisystem Deterioration

**Citation:** Yilmaz Y. Aging Dis. 2025 Apr 4;17(2):712-730. DOI: 10.14336/AD.2025.0398. PMCID: PMC12834415.

**Type:** Narrative review (literature search Jan 2013 - Mar 2025, 417 initial hits, narrative synthesis)

**Quality:** Peer-reviewed, open-access. Substantive full text with detailed mechanistic and clinical evidence. Quality is high.

---

## Core Findings

This narrative review systematically examines how green tea (GT) and its primary catechin EGCG address all twelve hallmarks of aging, synthesizing evidence from in vitro, animal, and human studies. The author argues that GT's pleiotropic, multi-target nature makes it a more comprehensive geroscience intervention than single-target pharmaceuticals. Human evidence links GT consumption to improved cognitive function (44% reduction in cognitive decline risk at 2-3 cups/day over 20 years), cardiovascular protection (meta-analysis of 772,922 participants), muscle preservation (68.8% sarcopenia risk reduction), and metabolic benefits.

## Key Evidence by Research Question

### Q3: Epidemiological Evidence on Green Tea and Mortality / Longevity

- **Telomere preservation (observational):** In 2,006 elderly Chinese individuals, >3 cups/day GT associated with telomeres ~0.46 kb longer than <0.28 cups/day, equivalent to ~5 years of life (ref 43).
- **Longitudinal telomere study:** In 1,952 subjects over 6 years, daily GT (>=1 cup) associated with less telomere shortening, more pronounced in women and younger participants (50-64 years) (ref 44, Sohn et al.).
- **Interventional telomere study:** In obese women, 8 weeks of decaffeinated GT supplementation produced a 103% increase in telomere length (ref 45, Nonino et al.).
- **Cognitive decline:** 1,155 middle-aged individuals followed ~20 years; 2-3 cups/day GT associated with 44% reduced cognitive decline risk (62% in men). Notably, no benefit at >=4 cups/day (ref 146).
- **Dementia meta-analysis:** Regular tea consumption associated with 29% lower all-cause dementia risk, 12% lower AD risk, 25% lower vascular dementia risk (ref 151, Jang et al.).
- **Cardiovascular:** Meta-analysis of 772,922 participants: low-to-moderate GT (1-4 cups/day) protective against coronary heart disease (ref 159). In 259,267 subjects, dose-dependent relationship with CVD protection; >=4 cups/day showed greatest protection against myocardial infarction (ref 160).
- **Sarcopenia:** Cross-sectional study of Japanese older adults: >=1 cup/day GT associated with 68.8% sarcopenia risk reduction vs. <1 cup/week (ref 164).
- **Epigenetic aging:** 18-month RCT of Green-MED diet (3-4 cups GT/day) showed ~8.9 months favorable difference in epigenetic age (ref 53). Note: confounded by multi-component diet.

### Q5: Mechanistic Debate (Antioxidant vs. Signaling Pathways)

The review strongly supports a signaling-pathway-centric view of GT's anti-aging mechanisms, going well beyond simple antioxidant effects:

- **Autophagy:** GT polyphenols stimulate autophagy via mTOR inhibition, AMPK activation, and sirtuins. Long-term EGCG in aged mice enhanced sirtuin expression and autophagic flux in adipose/intestinal tissues, increasing lifespan (ref 70).
- **mTOR/PI3K inhibition:** EGCG acts as an ATP-competitive inhibitor of both PI3K and mTOR, mimicking nutrient deprivation signals (ref 81).
- **AMPK activation:** GT polyphenols activate AMPK in adipose tissue and liver, enhancing fatty acid oxidation and suppressing lipid synthesis (ref 79).
- **Sirtuins:** EGCG restores cardiomyocyte contractility via sirtuin 1 upregulation (ref 85). GT polyphenols alleviate arterial aging via sirtuin 3 signaling (ref 71). EGCG proved most effective among tested compounds (vs. anthocyanidin, resveratrol) at reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion and activating sirtuin 3 (ref 98).
- **Mitochondrial biogenesis:** In C. elegans, EGCG extends lifespan via amplified mitochondrial biogenesis and improved respiratory capacity (ref 88). EGCG derivatives increased cytochrome c (1.4-fold), oxygen consumption (2.1-fold), ATP production (1.5-fold), and NAD+/NADH ratios (2.2-fold) in hepatoma cells (ref 89).
- **Mitophagy:** Catechin-rich extracts promoted dose-dependent mitophagy in human fibroblasts at 5-10 uM concentrations (ref 73).
- **Genome stability:** Total GT extract demonstrated superior DNA-protective effects compared to individual components like EGCG or caffeine alone (ref 29) -- relevant to Q7 (whole tea vs isolated EGCG).
- **Cellular senescence:** EGCG demonstrated superior anti-senescent efficacy compared to quercetin and resveratrol in endothelial cells (ref 97).
- **Anti-inflammatory (inflammaging):** EGCG inhibits NF-kB via covalent modification; NF-kB inhibition considered a key mechanism for lifespan extension in healthy rats (ref 122).
- **Telomeres:** EGCG prevents H2O2-induced telomere attrition in cardiomyocytes (ref 41). Preserves telomere length and telomerase activity in heart/muscle-specific MnSOD-deficient mice (ref 42).

### Q2: Safety / Hepatotoxicity / Regulatory

- EFSA ANS recommends limiting GT extract to <800 mg/day EGCG due to hepatotoxicity concerns (ref 175).
- Catechins from traditionally prepared GT infusions generally demonstrate acceptable safety profiles.
- GT catechins can significantly impact drug pharmacokinetics: 72% of analyses showed decreased systemic exposure (18-99%) for various medications, primarily via enhanced P-glycoprotein efflux activity (ref 177).
- Tea plants accumulate aluminum in foliar tissues, but evidence suggests negligible health risks at conventional consumption levels (ref 176).

### Q1: Human RCTs with EGCG Supplementation

- **DNA damage RCT:** Acute (200 mL, 1.5%) and regular GT (200 mL 1% twice daily, 7 days) reduced lymphocytic oxidative DNA damage by ~30% vs. water control (ref 32).
- **Smokers RCT:** 4 cups (960 mL) decaffeinated GT/day reduced urinary 8-OHdG vs. water control in heavy smokers (ref 33).
- **DNA oxidation RCT:** 300 mL 1% GT daily for 4 weeks reduced basal oxidative DNA damage by ~20% vs. water in 18 healthy volunteers (ref 34).
- **Diabetes RCT:** GT consumption associated with ~15% lower DNA damage and 50% higher hOGG1 activity in type 2 diabetes patients (ref 36).
- **Blood pressure meta-analysis:** 24 clinical trials, 1,697 participants; GT associated with SBP -1.17 mm Hg, DBP -1.24 mm Hg (ref 154). Another meta-analysis (9 studies, 680 participants): SBP -2.99 mm Hg, DBP -0.95 mm Hg (ref 155).
- **Cognition RCT:** Decaffeinated GT catechins (336.4 mg/day) enhanced working memory in adults aged 50-69; single dose improved attentional control (ref 147).
- **Glucose meta-analysis:** 17 trials, 1,133 participants; GT decreased fasting glucose by -0.09 mmol/L and fasting insulin by -1.16 uIU/mL (ref 170).
- **Liver meta-analysis:** 4 studies; GT reduced ALT by -12.81 U/L and AST by -10.91 U/L in steatotic liver disease (ref 173).

### Q7: Whole Tea vs. Isolated EGCG

- Total GT extract demonstrated superior DNA-protective effects compared to individual components like EGCG or caffeine alone (ref 29). This suggests synergistic effects of the whole-tea matrix.
- Endothelial function improvement appears independent of EGCG, suggesting other bioactive compounds in GT may be responsible (ref 158).
- EGCG comprises 50-80% of total catechin profile; GT retains 30-50% of dry leaf mass as polyphenols due to minimal processing (ref 24-25).

### Q6: Adversarial / Case Against EGCG

Key limitations and caveats noted:
- Poor bioavailability, extensive first-pass metabolism, limited biodistribution, and low accumulation in target tissues (ref 174).
- Substantial variability in concentrations/dosages across studies; many failed to quantify polyphenol content.
- Observational studies prone to reverse causation (healthier people more likely to consume GT).
- Cognitive benefit showed non-linear dose-response: 2-3 cups/day protective but >=4 cups/day was not (ref 146).
- EGCG paradoxically promotes telomeric shortening in neoplastic cells (dual-edged effect, ref 46-47).
- GT-drug interactions: 72% of pharmacokinetic analyses showed decreased systemic drug exposure (18-99%) (ref 177).

## Methodology

- Narrative review with systematic search across 5 databases (Medline/PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Google Scholar, ScienceDirect), Jan 2013 - Mar 2025.
- Initial 417 articles screened by title/abstract; full-text review with manual reference list searching.
- Included in vitro, animal, and human clinical trials examining GT or bioactive components in aging context.
- Synthesis organized by: (1) effects on twelve hallmarks of aging, and (2) GT effectiveness against age-related multisystem decline.

## Limitations

- Narrative review (not systematic with PRISMA); potential selection bias.
- Human evidence largely observational; few large, long-term RCTs specifically testing GT for aging endpoints.
- Confounders in observational studies (diet, lifestyle, socioeconomic status).
- The Green-MED epigenetic aging RCT is confounded by multiple dietary interventions (walnuts, Mankai, calorie restriction) making it impossible to attribute effects to GT alone.
- No meta-analysis or quantitative synthesis performed by the author.

## Relevance Assessment

This source is **highly relevant** to the research question, particularly Q3 (epidemiological evidence), Q5 (mechanisms), and Q7 (whole tea vs. isolated EGCG). It provides the most comprehensive mapping of GT/EGCG to all twelve aging hallmarks found in the literature. The human clinical data summaries (with effect sizes and sample sizes) are valuable for Q1. Safety data addresses Q2. The limitations section contributes to Q6. This is a foundational source for the synthesis.

## Claims to Verify

- **103% telomere length increase in 8 weeks (ref 45):** This is an extraordinarily large effect size for a short intervention. The original study should be checked for methodological details (measurement technique, baseline imbalance, sample size).
- **68.8% sarcopenia risk reduction (ref 164):** Very large effect; check if this is a cross-sectional odds ratio subject to confounding rather than a causal estimate.
- **44% cognitive decline reduction at 2-3 cups but no effect at >=4 cups (ref 146):** Non-linear dose-response is plausible but the specific cutoff should be verified in the original study.
- **8.9 months epigenetic age difference (ref 53):** Confounded by multi-component Green-MED diet; cannot be attributed to GT alone.
