What is EGCG?
Epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG)
is the most potent catechin antioxidant found in green tea. Catechins are a type of antioxidant known for their ability to neutralize free radicals and reduce oxidative stress, protecting cells and preventing damage that contributes to chronic inflammation, aging, and disease.
EGCG can be absorbed orally or through intravenous therapy. While many people drink green tea or take green tea supplements, these methods are not the most effective way to access the antioxidant power of EGCG. Poor absorption and rapid metabolism cause low bioavailability in oral ECGC, limiting its potential. IV administration bypasses these barriers, resulting in much higher blood levels and more consistent therapeutic effects.

Side Effects of a EGCG IV
EGCG is generally well-tolerated when administered by trained medical professionals like the team at Meeting Point Health. For most people, side effects are mild and temporary. High doses of EGCG can cause severe side effects, including toxicity of the liver and kidneys, making it essential to evaluate and monitor a patient’s health throughout therapy. The most common side effects are:
Diarrhea
Dizziness
Headache
Nausea
Vomiting
In rare cases, high doses may cause:
Kidney Damage
Liver Damage
Before beginning EGCG IV therapy, it is essential to disclose any past health concerns—especially those related to the liver and kidneys—so that our team can determine safe dosage and whether green tea IV therapy is right for you.
