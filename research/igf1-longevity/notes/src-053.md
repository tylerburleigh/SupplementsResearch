## Core Findings

This review consolidates evidence that IGF-1 has both antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects that can reduce alterations associated with neurodegenerative diseases (NDs). A consistent pattern emerges across multiple ND models: IGF-1 deficiency correlates with increased oxidative stress (OS) response, while IGF-1 overexpression or supplementation correlates with reduced OS. However, the review also highlights the dualistic nature of IGF-1 -- reduced IGF-1 signaling can enhance survival in some models by limiting ROS production and stress responses, creating a controversial picture of IGF-1's role.

## Key Evidence

1. **IGF-1 enhances antioxidant enzymes:** IGF-1 upregulates SOD, catalase, GPx, GR, and thioredoxin reductase expression and activity (lines 81-90).

2. **Dual role of IGF-1 in aging brain:** Reduced IGF-1 signaling can enhance survival by limiting ROS production, but IGF-1 deficiency is linked to cognitive impairments and higher risk of poor cognitive performance (lines 91-98).

3. **AD models:** Adenoviral-mediated IGF-1 expression protects hippocampal neurons from amyloid-beta oligomer toxicity and prevents memory impairment in mice (Selles et al., 2020). IGF-1 downregulation exacerbates OS in AD progression.

4. **PD models:** Age-related decline in serum IGF-1 may be a PD risk factor. IGF-1 supplementation activates pro-survival cascades protecting dopaminergic neurons via PI3K/AKT signaling (Ayadi et al., 2016; Qin et al., 2021).

5. **IGF-1 inhibition paradox:** Ligustilide-induced IGF-1 signaling inhibition activates FoxO1 via Klotho upregulation, reducing OS by inducing antioxidant enzyme transcription (Kuang et al., 2014). This supports the "pro-longevity" role of reduced IGF-1 signaling.

6. **Neuroinflammation:** IGF-1 promotes anti-inflammatory microglial phenotype via TLR4/NF-kB pathway; IGF-1 gene therapy modulates microglia and astrocyte phenotypes; exogenous IGF-1 reduces inflammatory cytokines and reactive species levels.

7. **Consistent pattern across ND models:** "A correlation between IGF-1 deficiency and increased OS response and IGF-1 overexpression and reduced OS response" (lines 535-538).

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

- **Directly relevant** to understanding the paradox of IGF-1 in aging: higher IGF-1 may protect against neurodegeneration via antioxidant mechanisms, but reduced IGF-1 signaling is associated with longevity in some model organisms.
- **Supports the "IGF-1 paradox"** -- both elevated and diminished IGF-1 signaling may have context-dependent benefits for different aspects of aging.
- **Klotho-FoxO pathway** mentioned as a mechanism by which IGF-1 inhibition may promote antioxidant defenses, relevant to longevity mechanisms.
- **Therapeutic implications:** Suggests modulating IGF-1 signaling as a therapeutic avenue, but with caveats about pleiotropic effects and cell-type specificity.

## Limitations and Caveats

1. **Narrative review without systematic methodology:** No explicit search strategy, inclusion/exclusion criteria, or quality assessment of included studies reported.
2. **Preclinical focus:** Almost all evidence is from animal models and cell cultures; limited human data discussed.
3. **No quantitative synthesis:** No meta-analysis or effect size calculations.
4. **Therapeutic delivery challenges:** IGF-1 has poor BBB penetration; off-target effects and cancer risk (tumorigenic in obesity/endocrine cancer) complicate therapeutic application.
5. **Pleiotropic and context-dependent effects:** IGF-1 effects vary by cell type and peptide concentration, making broad conclusions difficult.
6. **Contradictory findings acknowledged:** Cheng et al. (2005) reported limited impact of IGF-1 on oxidative damage in transgenic mice; the review does not fully resolve these contradictions.
7. **Not directly about longevity/mortality:** The focus is on neurodegeneration and neuroinflammation; longevity outcomes are discussed only indirectly.
