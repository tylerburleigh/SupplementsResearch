# src-014: Low insulin-like growth factor-1 level predicts survival in humans with exceptional longevity

**Citation:** Milman S, Atzmon G, Huffman DM, Wan J, Crandall JP, Cohen P, Barzilai N. *Aging Cell.* 2014;13(4):769-771. doi:10.1111/acel.12213. PMCID: PMC4116456, PMID: 24618355.

## Core Findings

Low IGF-1 levels predict longer survival in exceptionally long-lived humans (nonagenarians), but this effect is sex-specific (observed in females but not males) and strongest among individuals with a history of cancer. This is the first study to demonstrate that IGF-1 levels measured in late life predict incremental survival in humans with exceptional longevity, providing translational evidence for the conserved GH/IGF-1 longevity pathway.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Prospective observational cohort study (Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and multivariable linear regression)
- **Sample:** n = 184 nonagenarians (median age ~96.8 years) from the LonGenity study at Albert Einstein College of Medicine
- **IGF-1 measurement:** Single measurement at study enrollment; dichotomized at median (96 ng/mL) into Low IGF-1 (n=93) and High IGF-1 (n=91) groups
- **Outcomes:** Duration of survival after enrollment
- **Covariates in adjusted models:** Age, HDL cholesterol, cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes; additional models adjusted for glucose and insulin
- **Population characteristics:** Predominantly female (~75%), median BMI ~21.5, low prevalence of diabetes (~7%) and CVD (~25%)
- **Supplementary details:** Experimental procedures were provided in a supplemental file (not included in PMC content)

## Key Data Points

### Female-specific survival effect
- Kaplan-Meier: Females with IGF-1 below median had significantly longer survival, P < 0.01
- Linear regression (adjusted): Each 1 ng/mL increase in IGF-1 associated with 0.1-month decrease in survival (95% CI: -0.18 to -0.02), P = 0.01
- IGF-1 explained 6.2% of survival variance in females
- No effect in males (KM P = 0.83; regression P = 0.89)

### Cancer subgroup
- History of cancer in ~22% of subjects (34/151 with data)
- Low IGF-1 group: median survival 49.6 months; High IGF-1 group: median survival 20.7 months (P < 0.01)
- Each 1 ng/mL increase in IGF-1: -0.27 months survival (95% CI: -0.45 to -0.09), P < 0.01
- IGF-1 explained 23% of survival variance in cancer subgroup
- No significant effect in those without cancer history (P = 0.42)

### Baseline characteristics (Low vs High IGF-1)
- Low IGF-1: median 55 ng/mL (IQR 36-72); High IGF-1: median 121 ng/mL (IQR 112-158)
- Glucose was marginally lower in low IGF-1 group (102.2 vs 113.4 mg/dL, P = 0.05)
- No significant differences in BMI, HDL, insulin, HOMA-IR, cancer prevalence, diabetes, CVD, or cognitive impairment between groups

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

- **Direct evidence that low IGF-1 predicts human longevity:** Translates findings from C. elegans, dwarf mice, and other animal models to an exceptionally long-lived human cohort
- **Sex-specific effect:** Consistent with rodent models showing female-preferential longevity effects of attenuated GH/IGF-1 signaling; raises important questions about interaction with female hormonal milieu
- **Cancer mediation:** The dramatically stronger effect in the cancer subgroup (23% vs 6.2% variance explained) suggests IGF-1's effect on human survival may be predominantly mediated through cancer susceptibility
- **Anti-aging intervention implications:** Authors explicitly argue against GH replacement in older adults as an "anti-aging" strategy, as higher IGF-1 was associated with shorter survival
- **Connection to IGF-1 receptor mutations:** References prior work by the same group showing IGF-1 receptor mutations are enriched in centenarians (Suh et al., 2008)
- **Connection to GH receptor deficiency:** Cites Guevara-Aguirre et al. (2011) showing GH receptor-deficient individuals are protected from cancer and diabetes

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size (n=184):** Limits statistical power, especially for sex-stratified and cancer subgroup analyses
2. **Single IGF-1 measurement:** Cannot establish causality or determine whether low IGF-1 existed throughout the lifespan
3. **Survivorship bias:** All subjects were already nonagenarians at enrollment, representing an already-selected population
4. **Predominantly female sample (~75%):** Limits generalizability of male-stratified results
5. **No data on IGF-1 trajectory:** Unknown whether measured IGF-1 reflects lifelong levels or age-related decline
6. **Limited generalizability:** Exceptional longevity cohort may not represent the general aging population
7. **Supplementary experimental procedures unavailable:** Methods details were in a supplemental file not included in the PMC content
8. **Potential confounding by comorbidities:** Although adjusted for CVD and diabetes, residual confounding is possible (e.g., nutritional status, frailty)
9. **No causal mechanism demonstrated:** Observational design cannot distinguish whether low IGF-1 actively promotes survival or is a marker of other protective factors

## Claims to Verify

- **n = 184 nonagenarians:** Stated consistently in abstract and table; appears reliable
- **Median IGF-1 cutpoint of 96 ng/mL:** Clearly stated; IQR ranges (36-72 vs 112-158) are consistent with this split
- **23% variance explained in cancer subgroup:** This is a large R-squared for a single predictor; worth noting it may reflect the strong prognostic value of IGF-1 specifically in cancer patients
- **Glucose difference (102.2 vs 113.4, P=0.05):** Marginal significance; the actual clinical significance of this difference in nonagenarians is debatable
