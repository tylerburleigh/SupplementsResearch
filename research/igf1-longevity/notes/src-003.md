# src-003: Late-life targeting of the IGF-1 receptor improves healthspan and lifespan in female mice

**Citation:** Mao, K., Quipildor, G.F., Tabrizian, T. et al. Nature Communications 9, 2394 (2018). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04805-5

**Quality:** Full text available, peer-reviewed, high-quality preclinical study.

## Core Findings

This study provides the first evidence that pharmacologic IGF-1R blockade via a monoclonal antibody (L2-Cmu, a murinized version of the clinical antibody ganitumab) can delay aging when initiated late in life. Treatment starting at 18 months of age in CB6F1 female mice increased median lifespan by 9% and reduced cancer deaths, while improving multiple healthspan measures. Benefits were female-specific; males showed no lifespan improvement and experienced exacerbated inflammation.

## Methodology

- **Study design:** Preclinical intervention trial with parallel cohorts for feasibility (6-month) and longevity (lifelong)
- **Intervention:** L2-Cmu (anti-IGF-1R monoclonal antibody), 20 mg/kg, weekly i.p. injections
- **Treatment start:** 18 months of age (advanced age for mice)
- **Mouse strain:** CB6F1 (C57BL/6 x BALB/c F1 hybrid)
- **Sample sizes:**
  - Feasibility/healthspan cohort: n=24-38 per group, per sex
  - Longevity study (females only): n=45 per group
  - Various assays used subsets (n=4-22 per group per sex)
- **Comparator:** Vehicle (weekly i.p. injection)
- **Endpoints:** Lifespan, healthspan (exercise tolerance, grip strength, balance beam, cardiac function), histopathology, inflammatory markers, glucose homeostasis, metabolomics
- **Survival analysis:** Cox proportional hazard regression with cage as random effect and batch as covariate

## Key Data Points

### Lifespan (females only)
- Median lifespan increase: 9% (P=0.03)
- Mean lifespan improvement: P=0.023
- Hazard ratio: 0.622 (95% CI: 0.407-0.952, P=0.029)
- No effect on maximum lifespan (P=0.971)
- n=45 per group

### Healthspan (females)
- Exercise tolerance: ~50% improvement over old controls (P<=0.05)
- Grip strength: ~2-fold improvement (P<=0.05)
- Motor coordination: fewer slips on medium and hard balance beams (P<=0.05)
- Cardiac: preserved diastolic function (E/A ratio), reduced LVPWd and fibrosis (P<=0.05)
- Inflammation: multiple age-elevated cytokines restored to youthful levels

### Healthspan (males)
- Exercise tolerance: ~28% improvement only (P<=0.05)
- No improvement in grip strength
- Coordination only marginally improved on medium beam
- No cardiac benefit; inflammation markedly exacerbated

### Cancer
- Cancer deaths significantly reduced in females (P<=0.05)
- Endometrial hyperplasia severity reduced in females
- Male tumor burden tended to increase (P=0.07)

### Mechanism
- L2-Cmu: Ki=3.3 nM for both IGF-1 and IGF-2 at IGF-1R
- ~65% inhibition of IGF-1R and hybrid receptor activation in vitro
- Near complete IGF-1R phosphorylation inhibition in lung, ~70% in heart in vivo
- Glucose homeostasis preserved in both sexes
- Prevented age-related rise in hypothalamic IGF-1R levels in both sexes

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

This study is highly relevant to several research questions:

1. **IGF-1 pathway and longevity (Q1):** Directly demonstrates that pharmacologic IGF-1R inhibition extends lifespan in mammals, consistent with genetic models (IGF-1R haploinsufficiency, dwarf models). Key finding: benefits achievable even with late-life initiation.

2. **Human translational potential (Q2):** Uses a murinized version of ganitumab (AMG 479), a clinical-stage IGF-1R antibody already in human cancer trials. Notes that functional IGF-1R mutations are enriched in centenarians and low IGF-1 predicts female nonagenarian survival.

3. **Safety and side effects (Q3):** Demonstrates that late-life IGF-1R blockade does not impair glucose homeostasis -- a major safety concern. However, male-specific inflammation exacerbation is a significant caveat.

4. **Sex differences (Q4):** Provides one of the clearest demonstrations of sex-specific longevity effects from IGF-1 pathway manipulation. Female-only benefit is consistent with prior genetic IGF-1R haploinsufficiency data.

## Limitations

1. **Female-specific:** Longevity study conducted only in females; male interim data showed no survival signal and increased inflammation.
2. **No maximum lifespan effect:** Only median/mean lifespan improved; the oldest animals were not extended.
3. **Rodent IGF-2 difference:** In adult rodents, systemic IGF-2 is undetectable, but in adult humans IGF-2 levels are substantial. L2-Cmu blocks IGF-2 binding to IGF-1R, so human outcomes may differ from mouse results.
4. **Dose and timing not optimized:** Single dose (20 mg/kg) and single starting age (18 mo) tested; the authors acknowledge that starting earlier might yield greater benefits.
5. **Strain specificity:** CB6F1 hybrid strain; generalizability to other strains or species is unknown.
6. **Sample sizes for some assays** were modest (n=4-8 per group for cardiac, metabolomic, and western blot analyses).
