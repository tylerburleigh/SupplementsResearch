## Core Findings

This is a comprehensive review of aging mechanisms (the nine hallmarks of aging framework) and therapeutic strategies targeting age-related diseases. It covers the insulin/IGF-1 signaling (IIS) pathway as the first pathway demonstrated to regulate aging, and evaluates anti-aging interventions including metformin, rapamycin, resveratrol, senolytics, and caloric restriction. The IGF-1 pathway is discussed as a central nutrient-sensing mechanism whose attenuation extends lifespan across organisms.

## IGF-1 Specific Content

### Deregulated Nutrient Sensing (Lines 242-276)
- The insulin/IGF-1 (IIS) pathway was the **first pathway demonstrated to regulate aging** and age-related diseases.
- **Attenuating IIS signaling consistently extended lifespan** across organisms (Mathew et al. 2017).
- The IIS axis also includes AMPK and sirtuins as nutrient sensors.
- mTOR inhibition (downstream of IIS) extends lifespan in yeast, C. elegans, and fruit flies.

### Altered Intercellular Communication (Lines 375-430)
- **Insulin-IGF1 signaling** is listed alongside renin-angiotensin and adrenergic signaling as neurohormonal signals that mediate aging through increased inflammatory reactions.
- Pharmacological inhibition of NF-kB signaling could prevent age-related features in mouse models.

### Metformin and IGF-1 (Lines 707-803)
- Metformin leads to **decreased insulin levels and IGF-1 signaling** (Liu et al. 2011).
- Also activates AMPK, inhibits mTOR, reduces ROS and DNA damage.
- Extended lifespan of C. elegans by changing microbial folate and methionine metabolism.
- Side effects: vitamin B12 deficiency, lactic acid accumulation, GI irritation. 745 proteins regulated by metformin -- off-target effects uncertain.

### Caloric Restriction and IGF-1 (Lines 1042-1136)
- CR extends lifespan of **rodents and humans by reducing insulin, glucose, and IGF-1 signaling** (Hwangbo et al. 2020; Pifferi and Aujard 2019).
- CR induces sirtuin expression, reduces inflammation, improves insulin resistance.
- Continuous CR in healthy nonobese individuals reduced cardiovascular disease incidence by ~30% (Most et al. 2018).
- **Limitations:** CR can decrease bone mineral density, increase osteoporotic fracture risk; not suitable for elderly with malnutrition or low BMI (<21 kg/m^2).

## Other Anti-Aging Drugs Covered

### Rapamycin (Lines 804-881)
- Inhibits mTOR kinase, extends lifespan in yeast, C. elegans, fruit flies, and multiple mouse strains (C57BL/6, 129/SV, UM-HET3).
- **Key caveat:** Long-term overdose inhibits mTORC2, causing metabolic dysfunction and reduced lifespan in male mice.
- Clinical trial showed topical rapamycin increased p16(INKA4A) and collagen IV, improving skin tissue appearance (Chung et al. 2019).

### Resveratrol (Lines 882-954)
- Activates sirtuin family; extends lifespan in yeast and C. elegans.
- In high-calorie diet mice, reduced death risk by ~31% (Baur et al. 2006).
- **Human trials inconclusive:** One 26-week trial (23 overweight elderly) showed improved glucose metabolism and memory; a meta-analysis of 225 patients showed no positive effect on memory/cognition.
- Low bioavailability limits clinical application.

### Senolytics (Lines 955-1041)
- Dasatinib + Quercetin (D+Q) clear senescent cells and alleviate aging phenotypes.
- Navitoclax causes severe thrombocytopenia and neutropenia.
- D+Q improved clinical performance in 14 IPF patients (Justice et al. 2019).
- Dual nature: senescent cells also serve as anti-cancer barrier -- indiscriminate removal may be harmful.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Narrative review (not systematic or meta-analytic).
- **Scope:** Covers the nine hallmarks of aging, age-related diseases (neurodegenerative, cardiovascular, cancer, immune, musculoskeletal), and five therapeutic strategies.
- **Sources:** Extensive literature synthesis (~170 references) spanning primarily 2006-2020.
- **Limitations:** No quantitative meta-analysis; relies on individual study citations without formal quality assessment. No new primary data.

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

- **High relevance** for understanding the IIS/IGF-1 pathway's role in aging as a nutrient-sensing mechanism.
- Provides mechanistic context for why IGF-1 reduction (via metformin or CR) may extend lifespan.
- Useful for identifying drug candidates that modulate IGF-1 signaling (metformin, rapamycin, CR mimickers).
- Does NOT provide direct human clinical data on IGF-1 levels as a longevity biomarker or causative factor.
