# Source src-017: The longevity effects of reduced IGF-1 signaling depend on the stability of the mitochondrial genome

**Citation:** Shemtov SJ, McGann E, Carrillo L, et al. Sci Adv. 2026 Apr 3;12(14):eaea4279. doi: 10.1126/sciadv.aea4279
**PMCID:** PMC13048258 | **PMID:** 41931604
**Venue:** Science Advances (peer-reviewed, open access)

---

## Core Findings

This study demonstrates that the lifespan-extending effects of IGF-1 signaling suppression critically depend on mitochondrial genome integrity. Reducing IGF-1 signaling via Pappa (Pappalysin-1) deletion extended WT mouse lifespan by ~35% but **completely failed** to extend the lifespan of mitochondrial mutator mice (PolgD257A), which carry an error-prone version of mitochondrial DNA polymerase gamma. Most canonical longevity programs (enhanced proteostasis, DNA repair, telomere maintenance, chromatin regulation) that are normally triggered by reduced IGF-1 signaling were blocked or blunted in mutator mice. These results reveal a hierarchy among aging hallmarks, with mtDNA integrity acting as a foundational requirement for other longevity interventions to function.

## Methodology

- **Study design:** Cross-breeding of two mouse models -- PolgD257A (mitochondrial mutator, lifespan reduced ~40%) and Pappa-/- (reduced IGF-1 signaling via IGFBP4 persistence, lifespan extended ~35%) -- to generate six genotype groups: WT, PolgD257A, Pappa-/-, PolgD257A;Pappa-/-, Pappa+/-, and PolgD257A;Pappa+/-
- **Animals:** C57Bl6/j background; mice assayed at 3 and 12 months; lifespan cohort aged to natural death; sample sizes generally 3-32 per group per sex
- **Key assays:** Lifespan tracking, body weight/size, grip strength, treadmill endurance, echocardiography, blood composition, serum cytokine immunoassays (IL-2, IL-6, TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma, IGF-1), duplex sequencing of mtDNA, COX/SDH histochemistry for clonal expansion, RNA-seq of cardiac tissue (n=6 per group), immunofluorescence
- **Statistical methods:** One-way ANOVA with Fisher's LSD, Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn's test, or Welch's ANOVA; DESeq2 for RNA-seq; Benjamini-Hochberg correction for pathway enrichment

## Key Data Points

- **Lifespan:** All PolgD257A mice died before 17 months; first Pappa-/- death at 28 months; Pappa deletion did not extend PolgD257A lifespan at any dose
- **Cardiac transcriptomics:** 16-fold upregulation of Myh7 (P<0.0001), 3-fold upregulation of Nppb (P<0.0001), 3-fold increase in Gdf15 (P=0.0005), 7-fold increase in Fgf21 (P=0.03) in PolgD257A hearts
- **Clonal expansion:** Pappa deletion reduced COX-negative cardiomyocytes (P=0.0046 heterozygous, P=0.0002 homozygous) without changing mutation frequency or spectrum
- **Longevity pathways blocked:** mTOR reduction (P=0.046 in Pappa-/-), Terf1 upregulation (P=0.0004), Hdac1 (P=0.005), Ezh2 (P=0.0002), Lmnb1 (P=0.002), Nemp1 (P=0.006) -- all failed to activate in PolgD257A;Pappa-/-
- **Proteostasis failure:** 27/32 primary HSPs downregulated in PolgD257A hearts; none rescued by Pappa deletion; ATP-independent Hsp40 cochaperones unaffected
- **Sex differences:** Male PolgD257A mice showed more severe inflammation (elevated IL-2, IL-6, TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma), cardiomyopathy, and muscle dysfunction than females; Pappa deletion improved these in males only
- **Dose-dependent effects:** Heterozygous Pappa deletion (Pappa+/-) often conferred greater tissue-specific benefits than homozygous deletion in the PolgD257A background

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

This paper is **highly relevant** to the central question of whether IGF-1 reduction extends lifespan. It provides strong mechanistic evidence that:

1. IGF-1 suppression extends mammalian lifespan (confirmed in Pappa-/- mice)
2. The lifespan benefit is **contingent on** mitochondrial genome integrity -- when mtDNA is unstable, IGF-1 reduction confers no survival benefit
3. A hierarchy exists among aging hallmarks: mtDNA integrity overrides IGF-1 signaling, calorie restriction, exercise, and antioxidant interventions
4. IGF-1 signaling interacts with mitochondrial function through multiple mechanisms: clonal expansion of mtDNA mutations, ATP-dependent proteostasis, metabolic reprogramming
5. Dose matters: partial IGF-1 reduction may be more beneficial than complete suppression under conditions of mitochondrial stress
6. Implications for human translation: IGF-1-lowering therapies may fail in individuals with significant accumulated mtDNA damage (i.e., the elderly)

## Limitations and Caveats

- **Mouse model:** PolgD257A is an extreme, accelerated mutagenesis model; naturally aging humans accumulate mtDNA mutations far more slowly, so the barrier to IGF-1-based interventions may be less absolute
- **Lifespan data not numerically tabulated:** Exact median/maximum lifespans and hazard ratios for each group were not reported in text; data presented in figures
- **Pappa mechanism is indirect:** Pappa deletion reduces IGF-1 bioavailability by preventing cleavage of IGFBP4; it does not directly reduce IGF-1 production. Some effects could be IGFBP4-mediated
- **PolgD257A mice already have reduced IGF-1:** Old mutator mice exhibit lower circulating IGF-1, which may limit the additional impact of Pappa deletion (floor effect)
- **Cardiac-focused transcriptomics:** RNA-seq performed only on heart tissue; longevity pathway blockade may differ across tissues
- **No human data:** Entirely murine study; translational relevance inferred but not demonstrated

## Claims to Verify
