# Source src-007: Identification of functional rare coding variants in IGF-1 gene in humans with exceptional longevity

**Citation:** Ali, A., Zhang, Z.D., Gao, T. et al. *Identification of functional rare coding variants in IGF-1 gene in humans with exceptional longevity.* Scientific Reports 15, 10199 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-94094-y

**Venue:** Scientific Reports (Nature), Open Access

**Status:** Peer-reviewed, published March 25, 2025

---

## Core Findings

This study identified two rare functional coding variants in the IGF-1 gene (p.Ile91Leu and p.Ala118Thr) in an Ashkenazi Jewish longevity cohort, providing human genetic evidence that diminished IGF-1 signaling contributes to exceptional longevity. The Ile91Leu variant (centenarian-specific) weakens IGF-1 binding to its receptor IGF-1R, while the Ala118Thr variant is significantly associated with lower circulating IGF-1 levels. Both variants attenuate IGF-1R activity through different mechanisms, supporting the conserved role of reduced IGF-1 signaling in longevity.

---

## Methodology

**Study design:** Whole exome sequencing (WES) analysis combined with computational molecular dynamics (MD) simulations.

**Sample:** 2,108 individuals from two Ashkenazi Jewish longevity cohorts (Longevity Genes Project and LonGenity study) at Albert Einstein College of Medicine. WES was performed on 2,332 subjects initially; after quality filtering (low coverage, call rate, discordant sex), 2,108 remained.

**Population composition:**
- Centenarians (age >= 95 years)
- Offspring of centenarians (at least one parent lived to >= 95)
- Controls (no parent survived beyond 95)

**Variant identification:** Focused on rare coding variants with MAF <= 0.01. Functional significance assessed via CADD scores (>= 20 threshold). Only 2 IGF-1 coding variants passed all thresholds.

**Molecular dynamics:** Extended all-atom MD simulations (3 x 500 ns replicates) of wild-type and mutant IGF-1 bound to the full extracellular domain of IGF-1R using the OPLS forcefield. Binding free energy calculated via MM-GBSA method.

## Key Data Points

### Variant identification
- **Two IGF-1 coding variants found:** p.Ile91Leu and p.Ala118Thr (both heterozygous, MAF <= 0.01, CADD >= 20)
- **p.Ile91Leu:** Found in 2 female centenarians only; novel variant; located at IGF-1/IGF-1R binding interface
- **p.Ala118Thr:** Found in 2 male centenarians, 3 offspring, 3 controls; located near E-peptide cleavage site

### IGF-1 levels
- **Ala118Thr carriers had significantly lower IGF-1 levels** vs non-carriers (p < 0.01, adjusted for age and sex)
- Ile91Leu carriers: serum IGF-1 levels not measured

### Height (proxy for IGF-1 function)
- Ile91Leu carriers: 158.7 +/- 1.8 cm vs non-carriers 163.4 +/- 9.0 cm (p = 0.36, adjusted for sex)
- Ala118Thr carriers: 166.1 +/- 7.1 cm vs non-carriers 166.7 +/- 9.9 cm (p = 0.84)

### MD simulation results
- Wild-type IGF-1 showed substantially higher free energy of binding (stronger affinity) vs mutant Ile91Leu in all simulation runs
- Wild-type IGF-1 maintained more hydrogen bonds with IGF-1R (means: ~23.9, 25.9, 21.9) vs mutant (~20.7, 24.1, 18.6)
- Mutant IGF-1 exhibited higher backbone fluctuations, especially at interfacial residues
- Mutant caused subtle conformational changes in IGF-1R inter-protomer interactions

### Health status of variant carriers
- All carriers in the longevity cohort remained free of diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and had good cognitive function

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

1. **First report of IGF-1 coding variants in human longevity cohorts.** Previous longevity GWAS missed these because they focus on common variants (MAF > 5%). This study used a rare-variant approach in a well-characterized longevity cohort.

2. **Mechanistic evidence for the IGF-1/longevity link in humans.** The Ile91Leu variant directly weakens IGF-1/IGF-1R binding (shown computationally), while Ala118Thr reduces circulating IGF-1 levels (shown statistically). Both mechanisms converge on reduced IGF-1 signaling, consistent with model organism data.

3. **Parallels with IGF-1R variants.** The authors note prior findings of IGF-1R variants (p.Ala67Thr) in centenarians that also cause diminished IGF-1R activation, reinforcing the reduced-signaling hypothesis.

4. **Supports the UK Biobank epidemiological evidence.** The paper cites a ~450,000-participant UK Biobank study showing higher IGF-1 in older adults is associated with greater mortality risk.

5. **Implications for therapeutics.** The authors suggest rare longevity-associated variants and their mechanisms could serve as gerotherapeutic drug targets.

---

## Limitations and Caveats

1. **Very small number of variant carriers.** Only 2 individuals carried Ile91Leu and 8 carried Ala118Thr. Statistical power for associations is extremely limited.

2. **MD simulations are computational, not experimental.** The binding affinity findings are based on MM-GBSA calculations from MD simulations. The authors explicitly state these should be "interpreted qualitatively rather than quantitatively." No in vitro or in vivo binding assays were performed.

3. **Serum IGF-1 not measured for Ile91Leu carriers.** The primary mechanistic evidence for this variant is entirely computational.

4. **Population-specific findings.** The Ashkenazi Jewish population is genetically homogeneous; generalizability to other populations is uncertain.

5. **No functional validation.** The study calls for "further experimental studies to confirm molecular mechanisms." No cell-based or animal-based experiments were conducted.

6. **Cross-sectional design.** Cannot establish causality between variants and longevity; the variants may be markers rather than drivers.

7. **IGF-1 levels have mixed epidemiological evidence.** The paper acknowledges that studies of IGF-1 and mortality show mixed results, with some (mainly in younger populations) finding higher IGF-1 associated with lower disease risk.
