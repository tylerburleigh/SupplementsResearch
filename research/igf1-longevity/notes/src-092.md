# src-092: Five Gene Variants in Nonagenarians, Centenarians and Average Individuals

**Citation:** Kolovou V, Bilianou H, Giannakopoulou V, et al. Arch Med Sci. 2017;13(5):1130-1141. DOI: 10.5114/aoms.2017.68942

**Quality:** ok (38,805 chars, 512 sentences, raw PDF text extraction)

## Core Findings

This Greek case-control study evaluated 5 SNPs across 4 candidate longevity genes (TERT, IGFBP3, FOXO3A, ADIPOQ) in 256 nonagenarians/centenarians (age 90-113) vs. 149 average-lifespan controls (age 18-80). Only FOXO3A polymorphisms showed significant associations with longevity; TERT, IGFBP3, and ADIPOQ variants did not differ between groups. The FOXO3A gene is part of the insulin/IGF-1 signaling pathway, linking this finding to IGF-1 biology. The IGFBP3 polymorphism (A-202C, rs2854744), which modulates circulating IGF-1 levels, showed no significant association with exceptional longevity in this cohort.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Case-control genetic association study
- **Population:** All participants of Caucasian origin, Greek descent for >=3 generations
- **Study group:** n=256 (239 nonagenarians age 90-99, 17 centenarians age 100-113; median 93 +/- 4 years); 64.5% female
- **Control group:** n=149 (age 18-80, median 56 +/- 27 years); 49.7% female
- **Genotyping:** PCR-RFLP with validation by repeat analysis (~20% of samples) and direct sequencing (100% concordance)
- **SNPs tested:** TERT rs2736098, IGFBP3 rs2854744, FOXO3A rs13217795, FOXO3A rs2764264, ADIPOQ rs2241766
- **Statistical analysis:** Pearson chi-square or Fisher's exact test; significance at p<0.05; SPSS v22.0
- **Ethics:** Approved by Onassis Cardiac Surgery Center ethics committee; Declaration of Helsinki compliant

## Key Data Points

### FOXO3A (part of IGF-1 signaling pathway) -- Significant associations
- **rs2764264 C allele:** 3.9% in longevity group vs. 9.5% in controls, p=0.023
- **rs2764264 TT genotype in men:** 45.6% (study) vs. 28.0% (control), p=0.050
- **rs13217795 T allele:** 64.4% in nonagenarians vs. 44.1% in centenarians, p=0.018
- **rs2764264 T allele:** 69.7% in nonagenarians vs. 50.0% in centenarians, p=0.017

### IGFBP3 (modulates IGF-1 bioavailability) -- No significant associations
- **Genotype distribution:** p=0.392 (AA 28.4%, AC 50.4%, CC 21.2% overall)
- **A allele frequency:** 80.3% (study) vs. 75.6% (control), p=0.302
- **C allele frequency:** 19.7% (study) vs. 24.4% (control), non-significant

### TERT and ADIPOQ -- No significant associations
- TERT rs2736098 genotype distribution p=0.685
- ADIPOQ rs2241766 genotype distribution p=0.124

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity

1. **Direct IGF-1 pathway relevance (moderate):** The study examined IGFBP3 rs2854744, which modulates circulating IGF-1 levels (IGFBP-3 binds ~90% of circulating IGF-1). The null finding for IGFBP3 suggests this particular polymorphism does not contribute to exceptional longevity in Greeks, though this does not rule out a role for IGF-1 itself.

2. **Indirect IGF-1 pathway relevance (strong):** FOXO3A is a key transcription factor in the insulin/IGF-1 signaling pathway. The significant FOXO3A longevity associations found here support the broader IGF-1 signaling pathway as important for human longevity, consistent with animal model evidence (FOXO knockout effects in Drosophila and mice cited in the paper).

3. **Cross-population comparison:** The FOXO3A findings are consistent with Japanese, Chinese, Italian, German, and Danish studies, strengthening the evidence that IGF-1 pathway genes influence human lifespan.

4. **Negative result for IGFBP3 specifically:** While other studies (He et al. in Chinese cohort, Deal et al. in Physicians' Health Study) found associations between IGFBP3 polymorphisms and either longevity or circulating IGFBP-3 levels, this study did not replicate those findings, possibly due to different SNPs tested, population differences, or limited sample size.

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size** -- only 405 total, with just 17 centenarians (results for nonagenarians vs. centenarians "might be exaggerated" per authors)
2. **Case-control design** -- potential bias from differences in case/control characteristics
3. **No environmental/lifestyle data** -- smoking, exercise, diet not evaluated
4. **Single population** -- Greek/Caucasian only; may not generalize
5. **Only one IGFBP3 SNP tested** (rs2854744) -- other IGFBP3 polymorphisms might show different results
6. **Gender imbalance** -- 64.5% female in study group vs. 49.7% in controls (p=0.004)
7. **No correction for multiple testing** -- with 5 SNPs tested, some findings may not survive Bonferroni correction (p=0.023 would not survive p<0.01 threshold)
