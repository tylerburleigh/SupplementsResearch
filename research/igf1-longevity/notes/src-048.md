## Core Findings

This review synthesizes evidence that royal jelly (RJ) and its bioactive components (MRJPs, royalactin, 10-HDA, pantothenic acid) extend lifespan and improve healthspan across multiple species (honeybees, Drosophila, C. elegans, crickets, silkworms, mice) primarily through modulation of insulin/IGF-1 signaling (IIS), mTOR, EGFR signaling, dietary restriction pathways, and oxidative stress reduction. However, no in vivo human lifespan studies exist, and translation to humans remains speculative.

## Key Evidence on IGF-1 and Insulin Signaling

### C. elegans
- RJ at 10 ug/mL optimally enhanced longevity by 7-9%; pRJ at 10 ug/mL increased mean lifespan by 7-18%
- Mechanism: downregulation of insulin/IGF-1 signaling -- pRJ-Fr.5 upregulated dod-3, downregulated ins-9 (insulin-like peptide gene)
- 10-HDA extended lifespan via IIS downregulation and TOR signaling inhibition; did not extend lifespan in eat-2 (dietary restriction) mutants
- Royalactin extended mean lifespan by 18-34% via EGFR signaling (not IIS)

### Drosophila
- Freeze-dried RJ was significantly associated with heightened expression of insulin-like peptide dilp5, insulin receptor (InR), and mTOR -- but this stimulation was NOT associated with lifespan extension in females
- MRJPs (2.5% w/w) lengthened lifespan via EGFR-mediated signaling (S6K, MAPK) and enhanced antioxidant capacity (CuZn-SOD), not primarily through IIS modulation
- RJ effects in Drosophila are dose-dependent and strain-dependent; high doses (40-70%) shortened lifespan

### Mice
- Powdered RJ (50 and 500 ppm) prolonged mean lifespan of C3H/HeJ mice by 25%
- Mechanism attributed primarily to reduced oxidative stress (lower 8-hydroxy-2-deoxyguanosine), not directly to IGF-1 modulation

### Human Data
- RJ contains an insulin-like peptide similar to vertebrate insulin
- RJ administration to healthy athletes significantly decreased plasma insulin and increased thyroxine
- 20g RJ before oral glucose tolerance test significantly reduced glucose levels
- MRJPs (0.1-0.3 mg/mL) prevented senescence in human fibroblasts, associated with mTOR downregulation
- **No in vivo human lifespan studies exist**

## Relevance to Research Questions

- **IGF-1 downregulation and longevity:** Provides moderate evidence that RJ components extend lifespan via IIS downregulation in C. elegans. In Drosophila, the relationship is more nuanced -- IIS upregulation did not extend lifespan. The IGF-1 connection in mammals is indirect.
- **Human interventions targeting IGF-1:** RJ shows insulin-modulating effects in humans (decreased insulin, improved glucose tolerance), but no direct evidence linking these to longevity outcomes.
- **Safety and dosing:** RJ is generally safe (20g in humans produced no adverse effects), but effects are dose-dependent with potential for adverse outcomes at very high doses.

## Limitations and Caveats

1. **No human in vivo lifespan data** -- the authors explicitly state they could not locate any such study
2. **Mechanisms are species-specific** -- IIS downregulation drives longevity in C. elegans but not consistently in Drosophila or mammals
3. **Dose-response is complex** -- optimal doses vary by species and RJ preparation; high doses can be harmful
4. **No systematic review methodology** -- risk of selective citation
5. **RJ composition varies** by source, season, geography, and processing method, complicating reproducibility
6. **In vitro findings may not translate** -- MRJPs are rapidly digested in the stomach, raising bioavailability concerns
7. **EGFR pathway association with human longevity** (in Japanese males only) suggests ethnic/genetic variation in response
