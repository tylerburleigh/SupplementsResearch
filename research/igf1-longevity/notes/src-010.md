# src-010: IGF-1 and Glucose Handling in Familial Longevity (Leiden Longevity Study)

## Source Identification

- **URL:** https://www.aging-us.com/article/100071/text
- **Venue:** Aging (open-access journal)
- **Study:** Leiden Longevity Study
- **Authors:** Not fully extracted from text; includes Westendorp, van Heemst, Slagboom, and others (Leiden University Medical Centre)
- **Year:** Pre-2009 (references up to 2008, one reference listed as "In press" for 2009)

## Core Findings

This study compared IGF-1 axis parameters and glucose metabolism between middle-aged offspring of familial nonagenarians (n=1171) and their partners as controls (n=542) from the Leiden Longevity Study. The two main findings are: (1) offspring had significantly lower non-fasted glucose levels and lower diabetes prevalence than partners, suggesting better glucose handling associated with familial longevity; (2) there were **no significant differences** in serum IGF-1, IGFBP3, IGF-1/IGFBP3 molar ratios, height, weight, or BMI between offspring and partners. The improved glucose handling in familial longevity is not explained by differences in circulating IGF-1 or IGFBP3 levels.

## Methodology

### Study Design
- Cross-sectional comparison of offspring of nonagenarian siblings vs. their partners (controls)
- Part of the Leiden Longevity Study, which recruited 420 families with at least two long-lived siblings (age >=89 for males, >=91 for females)

### Sample
- **Offspring:** n=1171 (633 females, 538 males); median age 59.2 years (IQR 55.0-64.1)
- **Partners:** n=542 (302 females, 240 males); median age 58.8 years (IQR 54.3-63.7)
- After excluding diabetics: offspring n=1125, partners n=500
- Caucasian population from the Netherlands
- No selection criteria on health or demographic characteristics for recruitment

### Measures
- Non-fasted serum glucose, insulin, IGF-1, IGFBP3, lipids
- Anthropometrics: height, weight, BMI
- IGF-1 and IGFBP3 measured via Immulite 2500 (CVs <8%)
- Glucose and lipids measured via Hitachi Modular or Cobas Integra 800 (CVs <5%)

### Statistical Analysis
- Linear regression adjusted for age, sex, and sibling correlation (robust SEs)
- Linear mixed models for IGF-1/IGFBP3 ratio vs. glucose association
- Cumulative distribution curves for IGF-1, IGFBP3, height

### IGF-1 Decline with Age
- Female partners: -0.14 nmol/L/year (SE 0.04, p<0.001)
- Male partners: -0.16 nmol/L/year (SE 0.05, p=0.001)
- No significant difference in rate of decline between offspring and partners (interaction p=0.79 females, p=0.83 males)

### IGF-1/IGFBP3 Ratio and Glucose Association
- 1 SD increase in IGF-1/IGFBP3 ratio associated with -0.10 mmol/L glucose (SE 0.05) in partners (p=0.05)
- No significant interaction between groups (p=0.70)

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

1. **Null finding on circulating IGF-1:** This is an important negative study. Despite strong evolutionary conservation of the IIS-longevity link from worms to mice, circulating IGF-1 and IGFBP3 levels do not differ between offspring of long-lived families and controls. This challenges the hypothesis that lower circulating IGF-1 is a biomarker or mediator of human familial longevity.

2. **Glucose handling as the key phenotype:** The preserved glucose handling/insulin sensitivity in offspring (without IGF-1 differences) parallels findings in long-lived mammalian IIS mutants and centenarians. The authors note this also resembles caloric restriction effects in humans (improved insulin sensitivity without IGF-1 reduction).

3. **Genetic variants vs. circulating levels:** The authors note that rare IGF-1R mutations associated with IGF-1 insensitivity (not lower IGF-1 levels) have been found enriched in centenarians (Suh et al., 2008). The null finding on circulating levels does not rule out that genetic variation in the IIS pathway contributes to longevity through altered signaling sensitivity rather than circulating hormone levels.

4. **Context on prior conflicting literature:** The introduction provides a thorough review of the IIS-longevity literature, noting that in humans, low IGF-1 is associated with cardiovascular disease, diabetes, frailty, cognitive decline, and increased mortality risk at old age, while high IGF-1 is associated with increased cancer risk -- creating a paradox for the longevity-IGF-1 hypothesis.

## Limitations

1. **Cross-sectional design:** Cannot establish causality or temporal relationships. IGF-1 levels at a single timepoint may not reflect lifetime exposure.
2. **Non-fasted samples:** Glucose and insulin measurements were non-fasted, which introduces variability. However, this was consistent across both groups.
3. **Age range limited to middle age:** Participants were ~59 years old. IGF-1 differences might emerge at older ages.
4. **Circulating levels vs. tissue sensitivity:** The study measured circulating IGF-1/IGFBP3 but not tissue-level IGF-1 signaling or receptor sensitivity. As the authors note, centenarians can have high IGF-1 levels coexisting with low IGF-1 signaling due to receptor mutations.
5. **Potential for residual confounding:** Although partners serve as a reasonable control (shared environment), genetic differences beyond IGF-1 may drive the glucose differences.
6. **Sample completeness:** Only 70% of eligible participants had all required data (serum, medical history, medication, questionnaire).
7. **Generalizability:** Caucasian Dutch population only.

## Claims to Verify

- The 41% lower mortality risk for nonagenarian siblings (referenced from Westendorp et al., 2009, "In press") is a secondary claim from another paper -- do not attribute to this study.
- The diabetes prevalence figures (3.9% vs 7.7%) are directly from Table 1 and verified.
