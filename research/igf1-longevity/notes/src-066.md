## Core Findings

This comprehensive review examines gender differences in life expectancy, mortality, age-related disease patterns, and the biological mechanisms underlying these differences. The paper argues that gender differences in aging and longevity are pervasive yet poorly studied, and calls for systematic gender-specific medicine. Key finding relevant to IGF-1: genetic and pharmacological interventions targeting the IIS (insulin/IGF-1 signaling) pathway show stronger lifespan-extending effects in females than males across animal models, and human genetic variation in IIS pathway components (including IGF-1) has a stronger influence on old-age survival in women.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

### IGF-1 / Nutrient-Sensing Pathways (Lines 524-630)
- **Animal models:** IIS/TOR signaling inhibition has stronger lifespan effects in females. Specific examples:
  - Drosophila with impaired insulin-like signalling: significant female lifespan extension
  - IGF-1R heterozygous knockout mice: long-lived females with higher oxidative stress resistance; no significant effect in males (citing Holzenberger et al., ref 107)
  - S6K1 deletion (mTOR pathway component): significantly increased lifespan and improved age-sensitive biomarkers in females only (fewer memory T-cells, more naive T-cells, lower leptin, lower fat mass) (ref 108)
  - Rapamycin: increases lifespan more in female than male UM-HET3 mice at each dose (ref 109)
- **Human genetics:** Genetic variation in IIS components (GHRHR, GH, IGF-1, insulin, IRS1) has higher influence on body size and is more beneficial for old-age survival in women vs. men (ref 111)
- **Sex hormone modulation of GH/IGF-1 axis:** Oestradiol reduces hepatic sensitivity to GH; testosterone enhances the growth-promoting effects of the somatotropic axis, increasing risk of age-related pathologies (prostate cancer, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy) (refs 13, 112-115)
- **Dietary restriction and IIS/mTOR:** DR down-regulates IIS/mTOR pathway, activating anti-aging responses and extending lifespan. Intermittent fasting shown effective in overweight/obese pre-menopausal women (reducing weight, fat mass, waist circumference, improving insulin sensitivity, cholesterol, CRP, blood pressure) (ref 116)

### Demographic and Epidemiological Data
- EU-28: 5.5-year life expectancy gap between males and females (2013)
- In Italy: life expectancy gap reached maximum of 6.75 years in 1979-1980
- Women's survival advantage is counterbalanced by worse quality of life in advanced age (more disability, degenerative diseases)
- CVD death rates for IHD are 70% higher for men than women in EU countries
- T2D increases HF risk 3.4-fold in women vs. 2-fold in men
- Centenarian F/M ratio varies geographically in Italy: 7:1 in North-West, 4:1 in South/Islands

### Immunosenescence and Gender
- Progression to immunosenescence is faster in men than women
- Females produce more vigorous immune responses but are 2-10x more susceptible to autoimmune diseases
- Centenarians show markers of inflammation (inflammaging) but do not suffer most detrimental effects

## Methodology

This is a narrative review/synthesis paper, not a primary study. It integrates:
- Demographic data from EU, US, and Italian national statistics
- Epidemiological data from large cohort studies (Framingham Heart Study, OECD data)
- Animal model evidence from IGF-1R knockout, S6K1 knockout, and rapamycin studies
- Human genetic association studies on IIS pathway components
- Data from centenarian cohorts (Italian centenarians, GEHA project with 2,200 ultra-nonagenarians)

No new primary data are presented.

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

**Moderate-to-high relevance.** This paper:
1. Provides a gender-differentiated view of IIS/IGF-1 pathway effects on longevity -- an underappreciated dimension
2. Summarizes animal model evidence that IGF-1R knockdown extends lifespan preferentially in females
3. Reports that human genetic variation in IGF-1 and related IIS pathway components has gender-differential effects on old-age survival
4. Highlights that sex hormones (oestradiol, testosterone) modulate the somatotropic (GH/IGF-1) axis in opposing directions
5. Argues that gender must be considered in evaluating IGF-1-targeted longevity interventions

**Limitations for the research question:** The IGF-1 content is a subsection of a broader review on gender and aging. It does not provide new primary data on IGF-1 specifically, and the human evidence cited is from genetic association studies rather than intervention trials.

## Limitations and Caveats

1. **Review article, not primary data:** All findings are synthesized from prior studies; no new experiments or cohorts reported
2. **IGF-1 is one subsection:** The paper covers many topics beyond IGF-1 (CVD, cancer, neurodegeneration, immunity, mtDNA, gut microbiome, X chromosome inactivation)
3. **Animal-to-human extrapolation:** The strongest IGF-1/lifespan evidence comes from animal models (mice, Drosophila); human evidence is limited to genetic associations
4. **Cited references are indirect:** The IGF-1R knockout mouse finding (ref 107) is attributed to Holzenberger et al. but not re-analyzed here
5. **No quantitative meta-analysis:** Effect sizes are not pooled or formally compared across studies
6. **Published 2016:** Does not include more recent IGF-1 intervention trials or updated genetic association studies
7. **Italian/European focus:** Much demographic data is from Italy and EU; generalizability may be limited

## Claims to Verify

- The statement about IGF-1R heterozygous knockout mice showing lifespan extension only in females (line 557-560) should be verified against the original Holzenberger et al. paper, as this is a key quantitative claim about gender-specific IGF-1 effects
- The human genetic variation claim about IIS pathway components (GHRHR, GH, IGF-1, insulin, IRS1) being more beneficial for old-age survival in women (line 576-581) relies on ref 111, which should be checked for its specific effect sizes and populations studied
