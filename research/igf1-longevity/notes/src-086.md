# Source src-086: Strength, But Not Muscle Mass, Is Associated With Mortality in the Health ABC Study Cohort

**Citation:** Newman AB, Kupelian V, Visser M, et al. Journal of Gerontology: Medical Sciences. 2006;61A(1):72-77.

**Relevance to IGF-1/Longevity:** Low -- The paper primarily addresses muscle strength vs. muscle mass as predictors of mortality. IGF-1 is mentioned only in passing as a potential hormonal explanatory factor (references 25, 26), with no direct measurement or analysis of IGF-1 in this study.

---

## Core Findings

In the Health, Aging and Body Composition (Health ABC) Study cohort of 2,292 adults aged 70-79, muscle strength (both quadriceps and grip) was a strong independent predictor of all-cause mortality, while muscle mass (measured by CT and DXA) was not. This demonstrates that muscle quality, not quantity, drives the well-established strength-mortality association. The strength-mortality relationship persisted after adjusting for physical activity, comorbidities, inflammatory markers (IL-6, TNF-alpha, CRP), smoking, and body composition.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Prospective cohort study (Health ABC Study)
- **Sample:** N=2,292 participants with complete data (from original 3,075 enrolled); aged 70-79 at baseline; 51.6% women, 38.8% Black
- **Recruitment:** Medicare listings in Pittsburgh, PA and Memphis, TN; excluded those with difficulty walking 1/4 mile, climbing 10 steps, ADL disability, active cancer treatment, or plans to move
- **Strength measures:** Isokinetic knee extension (Kin-Com dynamometer) and isometric grip strength (Jaymar dynamometer)
- **Body composition:** CT scan (mid-thigh muscle and fat area) and DXA (regional lean mass, total fat mass)
- **Follow-up:** Mean 4.9 years (SD 0.9), 6 years total surveillance with 6-month contacts
- **Analysis:** Gender-stratified Cox proportional hazards models; sequential adjustment for muscle size, then demographics/behavior/health/inflammation

## Key Data Points

| Measure | Men (N=1,124) | Women (N=1,168) |
|---|---|---|
| Deaths | 180 | 106 |
| Mortality rate per 1000 py | 33.1 | 18.1 |
| Crude HR per SD quadriceps strength | 1.51 (95% CI 1.28-1.79) | 1.65 (95% CI 1.19-2.30) |
| Adjusted HR per SD quadriceps (DXA model) | 1.45 (95% CI 1.21-1.74) | 1.47 (95% CI 1.02-2.14) |
| Adjusted HR per SD quadriceps (CT model) | 1.36 (95% CI 1.12-1.65) | 1.56 (95% CI 1.05-2.30) |
| Adjusted HR per SD grip strength (DXA) | 1.36 (95% CI 1.10-1.60) | 1.67 (95% CI 1.08-2.58) |
| CT leg muscle area adjusted HR | 1.26 (95% CI 1.02-1.55) | 0.94 (95% CI 0.66-1.35) |
| DXA leg lean mass adjusted HR | 0.98 (95% CI 0.75-1.28) | 0.96 (95% CI 0.61-1.51) |

- SD for quadriceps strength: 38 Nm; SD for grip strength: 10.7 kg
- No threshold effect in the strength-mortality relationship across the full range
- Black participants had ~25-30% higher mortality rates but similar strength-mortality associations (no interaction by race)

## IGF-1 Connection

IGF-1 is mentioned only in the Discussion section as a candidate hormonal factor that might explain the strength-mortality link:

> "Potentially, strength-related hormonal factors such as testosterone (24) and insulin-like growth factor-I (25,26), which decline with age and strength, might explain why strength appears to be such a powerful marker of risk."

The two referenced IGF-1 studies are:
- Cappola AR et al. (2001): Association of IGF-1 levels with muscle strength and mobility in older women. J Clin Endocrinol Metab.
- Harris TB et al. (1997): Association of IGF-1 with body composition, weight history, and past health behaviors in the very old (Framingham Heart Study). J Am Geriatr Soc.

The authors note that IGF-1 assessment was "in progress" in Health ABC at the time of publication.

## Limitations

1. **Nondisabled cohort only** -- participants were all free of disability at baseline, limiting generalizability to more impaired populations
2. **Short follow-up** -- mean 4.9 years; cause-specific mortality could not be analyzed due to insufficient deaths
3. **Only 85% eligible for isokinetic testing** -- exclusions for hypertension, stroke, knee replacement, or severe pain
4. **No IGF-1 data analyzed** -- mentioned as future work, not included in this publication
5. **No muscle biopsy data** -- neurogenic aspects of muscle aging (motor unit remodeling, fiber type shifts) could not be assessed
6. **Age range limited to 70-79** -- findings may not extend to younger or older adults
7. **DXA lean mass may overestimate lean mass in obese individuals** (includes intermuscular fat)

## Claims to Verify

- Sample size of 2,292 is stated as those with complete data from 3,075 enrolled. The original N=3,075 is described in Methods and the analytic N=2,292 is clearly stated -- this appears reliable.
- Mortality rate of 286 deaths over mean 4.9 years -- consistent with the reported crude rates (33.1/1000 py for men over 5,445 py = ~180 deaths; 18.1/1000 py for women over 5,855 py = ~106 deaths; total ~286). Cross-check confirms consistency.
