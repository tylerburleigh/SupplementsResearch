## Core Findings

This comprehensive review establishes mTOR as a central node coordinating multiple hallmarks of aging (nutrient sensing, proteostasis, mitochondrial function, cellular senescence, stem cell function). Inhibition of mTOR by rapamycin or genetic means extends lifespan across evolutionarily conserved organisms. The insulin/IGF-1 signaling (IIS) pathway converges on mTORC1 via PI3K/AKT/TSC2, linking IGF-1 signaling directly to aging biology. Selective mTORC1 inhibition (sparing mTORC2) emerges as the optimal therapeutic strategy for lifespan extension.

## Key Evidence and Data Points

1. **IIS/mTOR convergence:** Insulin and IGF-1 activate PI3K/AKT which inactivates TSC2, leading to mTORC1 activation. Suppression of this IIS/mTOR axis is a proposed mechanism for dietary restriction benefits (lines 157-272).

2. **Rapamycin extends lifespan across species:** Genetic deletion of TOR/S6K homologues or pharmacological inhibition by rapamycin extends lifespan in S. cerevisiae, C. elegans, D. melanogaster, and M. musculus (lines 200-213). Key citations include Harrison et al. 2009 (Nature) for mice and Powers et al. 2006 for yeast.

3. **Protein restriction and IGF-1:** Low protein intake is associated with major reduction in IGF-1, cancer, and overall mortality in populations aged 65 and younger but not older (Levine et al. 2014, Cell Metab). Methionine restriction extends mouse lifespan and alters IGF-I and insulin levels (Miller et al. 2005) (lines 1368-1394).

4. **mTORC2 inhibition reduces lifespan:** Selective suppression of mTORC2 reduces lifespan (citing Lamming et al. 2012, Science) and causes insulin resistance, making mTORC1-specific inhibition the preferred therapeutic approach (lines 962-968).

5. **CR and mTOR overlap:** CR does not further extend lifespan when TORC1 is genetically inhibited (yeast, worms, flies), suggesting shared mechanisms. However, rapamycin and CR induce distinct transcriptomic/metabolomic changes, indicating some mTOR-independent CR effects (lines 280-303).

6. **Metformin as indirect mTOR inhibitor:** Metformin inhibits mitochondrial complex I, causing AMPK-dependent mTORC1 inhibition. Modulates lifespan in C. elegans and mice. TAME trial proposed (~3000 elderly patients, age 65-79) to test metformin effects on age-related outcomes (lines 987-1028).

7. **mTORC1 rapamycin incompleteness:** Rapamycin incompletely inhibits 4E-BP phosphorylation, meaning some mTORC1 outputs are spared. Active-site mTOR inhibitors and third-generation inhibitors (Rapalink-1) more completely suppress 4E-BP (lines 942-958).

8. **Human trial data:** RAD001 (rapalog) enhanced influenza vaccination response and reduced PD-1+ T lymphocytes in healthy elderly patients. RAD001 + BEZ235 low-dose combination selectively inhibited mTORC1 (not mTORC2) and enhanced immune function while reducing infections (lines 928-986).

## Methodology

This is a comprehensive narrative review synthesizing literature on mTOR signaling and aging. It covers:
- Genetic and pharmacological TOR inhibition studies across model organisms
- Molecular mechanisms of mTORC1/2 regulation (amino acid sensing, growth factor signaling, energy sensing)
- mTOR's role in hallmarks of aging (proteostasis, mitochondrial function, senescence, stem cells)
- Tissue-specific mTOR functions in aging (heart, CNS, adipose, skeletal muscle)
- Therapeutic perspectives (rapamycin, rapalogs, metformin)

No original experimental data; all findings are from cited primary studies.

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity

**High relevance.** This review directly addresses the IGF-1/mTOR connection in aging:
- IIS pathway (insulin + IGF-1) is identified as a major determinant of longevity that converges on mTORC1
- IGF-1 binds IGF1R, activating PI3K/AKT, which inhibits TSC2 and activates mTORC1
- Protein restriction reduces IGF-1 levels, which is proposed as a mechanism for its lifespan benefits
- Methionine restriction alters IGF-I levels and extends lifespan in mice
- The review frames IGF-1 signaling reduction as pro-longevity via mTORC1 suppression

## Limitations

1. Review article -- no original experimental data; relies on synthesis of published studies
2. Most lifespan data from model organisms (yeast, worms, flies, mice), with limited direct human evidence
3. Complex and sometimes contradictory findings (e.g., mTOR activity both increased and decreased in aging depending on tissue/sex)
4. The relationship between IGF-1 specifically and mTOR-mediated aging is discussed but not isolated from the broader IIS pathway (insulin + IGF-1)
5. Therapeutic recommendations (rapamycin dosing, intermittent schedules) remain largely theoretical pending clinical trials

## Claims to Verify

- "Low protein intake is associated with a major reduction in IGF-1, cancer, and overall mortality in the 65 and younger but not older population" -- this is a secondary citation (Levine et al. 2014) and the age-dependent reversal is an important claim that should be verified from the primary source.
- "Methionine-deficient diet extends mouse lifespan, slows immune and lens aging, alters glucose, T4, IGF-I and insulin levels" -- secondary citation (Miller et al. 2005); verify specific IGF-I alteration magnitude from primary source.
- "Selective suppression of mTORC2 reduces lifespan" -- verify from Lamming et al. 2012 whether lifespan reduction was statistically significant and in which specific mouse models.
