# Source Note: src-145

## Citation

De Haes W, Frooninckx L, Van Assche R, Smolders A, Depuydt G, Billen J, Braeckman BP, Schoofs L, Temmerman L. (2014) "Metformin promotes lifespan through mitohormesis via the peroxiredoxin PRDX-2." *PNAS*, 111(22):E2501-E2509. doi:10.1073/pnas.1321776111

## Core Findings

This study establishes that metformin extends lifespan in *C. elegans* through a mitohormetic mechanism, where increased mitochondrial ROS production paradoxically activates protective stress responses that increase longevity. The authors identify the peroxiredoxin PRDX-2 as the critical molecular link that translates the ROS signal into downstream pro-longevity signaling via the PMK-1/SKN-1 pathway. Metformin also improves healthspan by attenuating age-related morphological decline.

## Methodology

- **Organism:** *Caenorhabditis elegans* (wild-type N2, plus mutant strains prdx-2(gk169), acdh-1(ok1489), and reporter lines)
- **Approach:** Quantitative proteomics (2D-DIGE) followed by hypothesis-driven functional validation
- **Metformin concentration:** 50 mM on plates, 25 mM in liquid culture
- **Lifespan assays:** Multiple independent experiments (5-10 replicates per condition), scored daily, analyzed with log-rank and Cox proportional hazard tests
- **Sample sizes:** N = 54-172 worms per condition across experiments
- **Key measurements:** Respiration (Clark electrode), metabolic heat (microcalorimetry), ROS (Amplex Red), mitochondrial function (Oroboros Oxygraph), Western blot (PRDX-2 dimers, phospho-PMK-1), electron microscopy, BCAA quantification

## Key Data Points

1. **Lifespan extension:** Metformin increased mean lifespan in N2 worms by ~30% (12.79 to 16.70 days; p = 1.48E-9; N = 172 control, 169 treated, 10 independent experiments)
2. **Respiration and metabolism:** 45% increase in respiration (p < 0.05, n = 3), 38% increase in metabolic heat production (p < 0.01, n = 3-4)
3. **ROS production:** Significant increase in H2O2 after 24h metformin exposure (p < 0.05, n = 5-7)
4. **Antioxidant blockade:** NAC (5 mM) and BHA (25 uM) completely abolished metformin's lifespan benefit (p < 0.001 for both)
5. **PRDX-2 requirement:** prdx-2 deletion caused extreme lifespan decrease under metformin (6.76 vs 10.61 days mean lifespan; p < 0.001); NAC partially rescued this effect
6. **Complex I inhibition:** Metformin specifically inhibited complex I (not complex II) of ETC in vitro; unlike rotenone, metformin increased rather than decreased mitochondrial ROS production (p < 0.001, n = 3)
7. **Metabolic changes:** 55% reduction in free BCAAs (p < 0.01, n = 6); 11.4% reduction in fat stores (p < 0.001, n = 30-33); 4.3-fold upregulation of ACDH-1
8. **Critical period:** Adult-only or first-5-days-of-adulthood metformin exposure sufficient for lifespan extension; larval-only exposure had no effect

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

- **Indirect relevance to IGF-1:** The paper references Zarse et al. (2012) on impaired insulin/IGF-1 signaling extending lifespan via ROS signaling (mitohormesis), and Miller et al. (2005) on methionine restriction altering IGF-I levels in mice. The BCAA degradation findings connect to the insulin/IGF-1/TOR signaling axis, since leucine is a potent TOR activator and metformin-induced BCAA reduction could suppress TOR signaling.
- **Metformin as a longevity intervention:** Directly relevant to pharmacological interventions for longevity. The mitohormetic mechanism and PRDX-2/peroxiredoxin signaling pathway are proposed as evolutionarily conserved principles of pro-longevity signaling.
- **Signaling pathway:** The metformin -> complex I inhibition -> AMPK -> increased respiration -> ROS -> PRDX-2 -> PMK-1 -> SKN-1 cascade intersects with insulin/IGF-1 signaling through shared downstream components (DAF-16 independence noted, but SKN-1 involvement).
- **Note:** This is primarily a metformin/mitohormesis study in *C. elegans*, not a direct study of IGF-1. Its relevance to IGF-1 and longevity is moderate, providing mechanistic context for how metabolic interventions that overlap with reduced insulin/IGF-1 signaling can extend lifespan.

## Limitations

1. **Model organism:** All experiments in *C. elegans*; direct translatability to humans unproven
2. **Metformin concentration:** 50 mM is high; human therapeutic plasma levels are much lower (micromolar range), though gut concentrations may be comparable
3. **DAF-16 independence:** The DAF-16/FOXO transcription factor (central to IGF-1 signaling) is not required for metformin's lifespan effect, suggesting the IGF-1 pathway may not be the primary mediator
4. **Bacterial effects:** Previous work showed metformin also acts through bacterial folate metabolism; this paper focused on direct worm effects but did not fully disentangle the two mechanisms
5. **No mammalian validation:** The PRDX-2 signaling cascade is proposed as conserved based on literature but not directly tested in mammalian models in this study

## Claims to Verify

- The 30.57% lifespan increase figure in Table S1 is the difference in mean lifespan divided by control mean lifespan; this is consistent with the raw numbers (16.70 - 12.79) / 12.79 = 30.57%
- The BCAA reduction of 55% and fat reduction of 11.4% are from single experiments with n = 6 and n = 30-33 respectively; reasonable but could benefit from replication
- Sample sizes in Table S1 are well-documented with multiple independent experiments (5-10 per condition), lending credibility to the lifespan data
