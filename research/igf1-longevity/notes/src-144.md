# src-144: Hepatic Transcriptome Effects of Graded Calorie Restriction

**Paper:** Derous et al. (2017). "The effects of graded levels of calorie restriction: XI. Evaluation of the main hypotheses underpinning the life extension effects of CR using the hepatic transcriptome." *AGING*, Vol. 9, No. 7.

**Source ID:** src-144
**Status:** ok
**Quality:** ok (peer-reviewed, open-access, full text available)

---

## Core Findings

This study used graded levels of calorie restriction (0-40% CR) in male C57BL/6 mice to evaluate multiple hypotheses for CR-mediated lifespan extension via hepatic transcriptome analysis. Of the four main longevity-associated signaling pathways (insulin/IGF-1, NF-kB, mTOR, sirtuins), three were altered in a manner consistent with increased lifespan. Sirtuin signaling was the exception -- only SIRT4 and SIRT7 were negatively correlated with CR, and their role in regulating lifespan was judged to be limited. The authors conclude that the benefits of CR on lifespan likely reflect combined impact on multiple aging-related processes rather than a single dominant pathway.

Key finding for IGF-1 research: Both circulating IGF-1 levels and hepatic IGF-1 signaling pathway transcription were progressively reduced in a dose-dependent manner with increasing CR severity, and these changes correlated with improved glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity.

## Methodology

- **Study design:** Controlled experiment with 6 treatment groups, hepatic transcriptome profiling (RNA-seq)
- **Species/strain:** Male C57BL/6 mice (*Mus musculus*)
- **Sample size:** 48 mice total (12AL n=8, 24AL n=8, 10CR n=8, 20CR n=8, 30CR n=7, 40CR n=9); RNA successfully isolated from 37 individuals
- **Age at CR start:** 20 weeks (early adulthood equivalent)
- **CR duration:** 3 months (to age 8 months)
- **CR levels:** 0% (24h ad libitum), 12h ad libitum (reference group), 10%, 20%, 30%, 40% calorie restriction
- **Tissue:** Liver
- **Sequencing:** Illumina Hi-seq 2000, 50 bp single-ended reads; 97.99% alignment rate; 12,183 unique genes analyzed after filtering
- **Analysis:** Pearson correlations of gene expression with CR level; Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) for pathway-level interpretation; z-scores for activation state prediction
- **Reference group:** 12AL (12h ad libitum feeding) was used as reference to avoid "time since last meal effect" confound
- **Differential expression:** At 30CR only 4 genes had FDR < 0.05; at 40CR this jumped to 855. Using absolute log2FC > 1 and p < 0.05, DEG counts were: 24AL=88, 10CR=138, 20CR=533, 30CR=316, 40CR=608

## Key Data Points

### Insulin/IGF-1 Signaling
- PI3K/AKT pathway: z-score = -3.138, p = 0.019 (significantly downregulated)
- IGF-1 signaling pathway: z-score = -1.091, p = 0.002 (significantly downregulated)
- INSR correlated positively with CR (reflecting lower circulating insulin)
- IRS family members, PI3K, ACLY correlated negatively with CR
- Downstream pathways significantly altered: gluconeogenesis (z=0.370, p<0.001), lipolysis (p=0.046), protein synthesis (z=-2.115, p<0.001)
- 13 insulin/IGF-1 pathway genes correlated with food anticipatory activity, 7 with urinary MUPs, 10 with BMR

### mTOR Signaling
- Overall mTOR signaling downregulated: z-score = -0.18, p < 0.001
- Both mTORC1 and mTORC2 components negatively correlated with CR
- No difference between mTORC1 and mTORC2 in extent of downregulation
- S6K1 expression NOT significantly altered by CR
- Downstream pathways: autophagy (z=0.833, p<0.001), lipid metabolism (p<0.001), angiogenesis (z=0.829, p<0.001), protein synthesis (z=-2.115, p<0.001)

### NF-kB Signaling
- IkB positively correlated with CR; NF-kB/RelB complexes negatively correlated
- Reduced signaling indicating less inflammation with increasing CR
- 11 NF-kB pathway genes correlated with circulating IGF-1

### Sirtuin Signaling
- Only SIRT4 and SIRT7 negatively correlated with CR
- SIRT4 KO mice have 30% increase in circulating insulin during ad libitum feeding
- Authors postulate SIRT signaling is NOT necessary for the beneficial effects of CR on longevity

### Oxidative Stress
- ROS production predicted decreased (z = -0.305, p < 0.001)
- NRF2 negatively correlated with CR (p = 0.001)
- eIF2 signaling positively correlated (z = 4.718, p < 0.001)
- Antioxidant enzyme activities (SOD, GPx, catalase) all decreased with CR
- Oxidative damage unchanged -- mice modulated oxidative defense while maintaining damage levels constant

### Cancer Signaling
- Cancer predicted strongly decreased (z = -3.285, p < 0.001)
- Hedgehog, TGF-beta, CTNN-beta signaling negatively correlated with CR
- SMAD family tumor suppressors positively correlated with CR

### Autophagy
- Significantly increased (z = 0.833, p < 0.001)
- Autophagy gene expression correlated negatively with circulating IGF-1
- 8 autophagy-related genes positively associated with CR level

### Fuel Utilization
- Glycolysis (p = 0.016), TCA cycle (p < 0.001), fatty acid beta-oxidation (z = 2.872, p < 0.001), gluconeogenesis (z = 0.370, p < 0.001) all positively correlated with CR
- ETC complexes II, IV, V positively correlated
- Mitochondrial dysfunction/apoptosis negatively correlated (z = -1.504, p < 0.001)

### FGF21 and H2S
- FGF21 gene expression did NOT correlate with CR extent
- Cth (CSE) positively correlated; Cbs negatively correlated with CR
- Circadian rhythm genes Per1, Per2, Cry1, Cry2 positively correlated with CR

### Xenobiotic Metabolism
- Significantly upregulated (z = 1.183, p < 0.001)
- CYP, FMO, and ALDH family genes all strongly positively associated with CR

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

This paper is highly relevant to IGF-1 and longevity research because:

1. **Dose-response evidence for IGF-1:** It provides graded (dose-response) transcriptomic evidence that the insulin/IGF-1 pathway is progressively downregulated with increasing CR in direct proportion to the level of restriction. This linear relationship supports the hypothesis that IGF-1 signaling reduction is causally linked to lifespan extension.

2. **Integration with other pathways:** The paper situates IGF-1 within a network of longevity pathways (mTOR, NF-kB, autophagy, cancer signaling), showing that IGF-1 reduction co-occurs with changes in multiple other pathways. This supports the view that CR-mediated lifespan extension is multi-factorial.

3. **Cross-pathway correlations:** IGF-1 circulating levels correlated with gene expression in NF-kB (11 genes), oxidative stress (34 genes), and autophagy (negative correlations), suggesting IGF-1 as a systemic integrator of CR effects.

4. **FGF21 vs. CR:** The finding that FGF21 was NOT upregulated by CR (up to 40%), despite IGF-1 signaling being reduced, suggests that FGF21 is not a necessary mediator of CR-induced IGF-1 pathway downregulation.

5. **Dose-dependent cancer protection:** The strong anti-cancer transcriptional signature (z = -3.285) under CR was associated with reduced IGF-1, IL6, and leptin, all of which promote tumor growth.

## Limitations

1. **Mouse study only:** Male C57BL/6 mice; no female data, no human data. Sex-specific effects are known (e.g., S6K1 knockout increases lifespan in female but not male mice).
2. **Short-term CR:** Only 3 months of CR; does not capture long-term adaptive responses or lifespan data from this cohort.
3. **Single tissue:** Liver only; may not reflect changes in other tissues relevant to aging.
4. **Correlation-based analysis:** The Pearson correlation approach identifies linear relationships but cannot establish causality.
5. **No direct lifespan data in this study:** The dose-response inference about lifespan depends on the known linear relationship between CR level and lifespan in C57BL/6 mice from other studies.
6. **CR vs. protein restriction confound:** CR diets involved simultaneous calorie and protein reduction (up to 40% protein restriction), making it difficult to isolate pure calorie effects.
7. **No functional validation:** Transcriptomic changes were not validated at the protein level or through functional assays (except for previously measured circulating hormones and enzyme activities in companion papers).
8. **Small sample sizes per group:** 7-9 mice per treatment group; 37 total with successful RNA isolation.

## Claims to Verify

- "SIRT4 KO mice have a 30% increase in circulating levels of insulin during ad libitum feeding" (line ~2447-2449): This is cited from Haigis et al. (2006) but should be verified in the original source.
- DEG counts (24AL=88, 10CR=138, 20CR=533, 30CR=316, 40CR=608) show a non-monotonic pattern (decrease from 20CR to 30CR then increase at 40CR) that is not explained in the text.
