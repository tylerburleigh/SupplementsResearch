# src-130: Mediation of IGF-1-induced skeletal myotube hypertrophy by PI(3)K/Akt/mTOR and PI(3)K/Akt/GSK3 pathways

**Citation:** Rommel C, Bodine SC, Clarke BA, Rossman R, Nunez L, Stitt TN, Yancopoulos GD, Glass DJ. *Nature Cell Biology* 3, 1009-1013 (November 2001). Regeneron Pharmaceuticals.

**Note:** Metadata quality was "degraded" (paywall flag), but full paper text was recovered via raw PDF extraction. Content is substantive and authoritative.

---

## Core Findings

This paper dissects the intracellular signaling pathways by which IGF-1 promotes skeletal muscle hypertrophy using cultured C2C12 myotubes. The authors demonstrate that IGF-1 induces hypertrophy exclusively through the PI(3)K/Akt pathway, with two major downstream branches: (1) the Akt/mTOR pathway (activating p70S6K and 4E-BP1/PHAS-1 to promote protein synthesis) and (2) the Akt/GSK3 pathway (phosphorylating and inhibiting GSK3, which normally suppresses the translation initiation factor eIF2B). Critically, the paper refutes prior claims that calcineurin/NFAT mediates IGF-1-induced hypertrophy, showing instead that IGF-1 via Akt actively antagonizes calcineurin signaling in mature myotubes.

## Methodology

- **System:** C2C12 myoblast cell line (mouse) differentiated into myotubes in vitro
- **Approaches:** Combination of pharmacological inhibitors (LY294002 for PI(3)K, rapamycin for mTOR, CsA for calcineurin, PD98059 for ERK), genetic constructs (constitutively active and dominant-negative forms of Akt, SHIP-2, p70S6K, GSK3beta, calcineurin), immunoblotting for pathway activation markers, and myotube diameter measurement
- **Key design feature:** Separation of pre-differentiation (myoblast) from post-differentiation (myotube) effects to distinguish differentiation from hypertrophy
- **IGF-1 dose:** 10 ng/ml (R3-IGF-1)
- **Hypertrophy quantification:** Myotube diameter measured across 10 random fields, ~10 myotubes per field, mean of 10 measurements per myotube
- **No human or animal subjects** -- purely in vitro cell culture study

## Key Data Points

1. **PI(3)K inhibition (LY294002, 10 uM):** Completely blocked IGF-1-induced hypertrophy and caused mild atrophy of control myotubes
2. **mTOR inhibition (rapamycin, 2 ng/ml):** Significantly blunted but did not abolish IGF-1-induced hypertrophy, consistent with multiple Akt branches contributing
3. **Constitutively active Akt:** Sufficient to cause hypertrophy in the absence of IGF-1
4. **Kinase-inactive Akt:** Caused thinner myotubes (atrophy)
5. **Dominant-negative GSK3-beta:** Caused profound hypertrophy, identifying GSK3 as a key mTOR-independent target
6. **Constitutively active p70S6K:** Caused hypertrophy but was less potent than constitutively active Akt, consistent with p70S6K being one of several downstream effectors
7. **Calcineurin inhibition (CsA, 5 uM):** Did not block IGF-1-induced hypertrophy in differentiated myotubes; actually potentiated hypertrophy
8. **Constitutively active calcineurin:** Did not cause hypertrophy; led to thinner myotubes
9. **Ara-C (DNA synthesis inhibitor, 10 uM):** Did not block IGF-1-mediated hypertrophy, demonstrating uncoupling from DNA replication
10. **IGF-1 + calcium ionophore:** IGF-1 antagonized calcium-ionophore-induced NFAT-C1 dephosphorylation and nuclear translocation

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

- **Mechanistic pathway mapping:** This paper provides foundational mechanistic detail on how IGF-1 activates the Akt/mTOR and Akt/GSK3 pathways in muscle, which are central to the IGF-1/insulin signaling axis implicated in longevity. The mTOR pathway in particular is a major target of longevity interventions (rapamycin, caloric restriction mimetics).
- **mTOR activation by IGF-1:** Demonstrates that IGF-1 signals through mTOR, which is consistent with the longevity literature showing that reduced IGF-1/insulin signaling and mTOR inhibition extend lifespan in model organisms.
- **GSK3 as an IGF-1/Akt target:** Identifies GSK3 inhibition as an mTOR-independent arm of IGF-1 signaling relevant to protein synthesis, which has implications for sarcopenia and age-related muscle loss.
- **Therapeutic implications:** The authors suggest pharmacological inhibitors of SHIP-2 or GSK3, or activators of Akt/mTOR/p70S6K, as potential therapies for muscle atrophy -- relevant to longevity interventions targeting age-related muscle wasting.
- **Limitation:** In vitro study using C2C12 mouse myoblast cell line; no in vivo or human data. Findings need confirmation in animal models and humans for longevity relevance.

## Limitations

1. **In vitro only:** All experiments in cultured C2C12 mouse myotubes; no in vivo validation in this paper (though companion paper by Bodine et al. in same issue provides in vivo confirmation)
2. **Cell line limitations:** C2C12 cells may not fully recapitulate primary muscle fiber biology
3. **Single IGF-1 dose tested** for most experiments (10 ng/ml)
4. **Partial pathway dissection:** Authors acknowledge they cannot rule out additional Akt targets contributing to hypertrophy
5. **No aging or longevity endpoints:** Study focuses on hypertrophy signaling, not lifespan or age-related outcomes
6. **PDF extraction quality:** Raw text extraction from PDF; some figure data embedded as garbled text (figure legends intact)
