# src-036: The Role of Insulin/IGF-1 Signaling in the Longevity of Model Invertebrates, C. elegans and D. melanogaster

**Authors:** Altintas O, Park S, Lee SJV
**Year:** 2016
**Venue:** BMB Reports, Volume 49, Issue 2, Pages 81-92
**DOI:** 10.5483/BMBRep.2016.49.2.261
**Type:** Review article

## Core Findings

This review comprehensively covers the insulin/IGF-1 signaling (IIS) pathway and its role in aging and longevity in C. elegans and D. melanogaster. The central finding is that reduced IIS extends lifespan across these model organisms through conserved downstream effectors (FOXO/DAF-16, HSF-1, SKN-1/NRF2), and that this conservation extends to mammals including humans. The review establishes that IIS is the most evolutionarily conserved aging-regulatory pathway known.

## Key Evidence

### C. elegans IIS and Longevity
- Inhibition of daf-2 (insulin/IGF-1 receptor) or age-1 (PI3K) dramatically extends lifespan
- DAF-16/FOXO is the primary downstream transcription factor; its nuclear localization (when IIS is reduced) activates longevity-promoting genes
- Three key downstream transcription factors: DAF-16/FOXO, HSF-1, and SKN-1/NRF2
- HSF-1 overexpression alone is sufficient to extend lifespan; its knockdown suppresses daf-2 longevity
- SKN-1 overexpression extends lifespan in a DAF-16-independent manner
- Multiple kinases modulate DAF-16: JNK, AMPK, MST1/CST-1
- C. elegans genome encodes 40 insulin-like peptides (ILPs), mostly expressed in neurons
- IIS regulates lifespan systemically via endocrine signaling (tissue-nonautonomous effects)

### Drosophila IIS and Longevity
- Conserved pathway components: dInR, CHICO (IRS), PI3K/Dp110, dPDK1, dAkt1, dFOXO
- Genetic inhibition of DILPs, dInR, CHICO, or 14-3-3 epsilon extends lifespan
- Overexpression of dPTEN or dFOXO extends lifespan and/or delays heart aging
- Eight DILPs encoded in the genome; major production in median neurosecretory cells (IPCs)
- Ablation of IPCs lengthens lifespan (endocrine regulation)
- dFOXO overexpression in head fat body or muscle is sufficient for longevity
- DILP6 (fat body) represses DILP2 (brain) in a feedback mechanism

### Conservation to Mammals
- Heterozygous Igf1r+/- mice live longer than wild-type (Holzenberger et al., 2003)
- Lower circulating IGF1 levels correlate with mouse longevity
- Genetic variants of IGF1 receptor and FOXO3A are associated with human longevity

### Disease Model Protection
- Reduced IIS protects against neurodegenerative disease models: Alzheimer's (Aβ), Huntington's (polyQ), Parkinson's (α-synuclein), ALS (SOD1)
- Protection mediated by DAF-16/FOXO and HSF-1
- Reduced IIS enhances stress resistance (oxidative, heat, hypoxic, osmotic, UV, heavy metal)
- Reduced IIS enhances innate immunity and pathogen resistance

## Methodology

This is a narrative review article. No primary data or original experiments are presented. The authors synthesize findings from approximately 200 primary research papers spanning 1983-2015. The review focuses on mechanistic insights from genetic studies in C. elegans and D. melanogaster, with connections to mammalian findings.

## Limitations

1. **Review format**: No new quantitative data; synthesizes existing literature
2. **No human intervention data**: Focuses on invertebrate model organisms with only brief mention of mammalian/human genetic associations
3. **No discussion of IGF-1 lowering interventions**: Does not cover pharmacological or dietary strategies to modulate IGF-1 in humans
4. **Date limitation**: Published 2016; does not include more recent findings on IIS and longevity
5. **Limited quantitative data**: Lifespan extension magnitudes are described qualitatively ("dramatically extends") rather than with specific percentages or effect sizes

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity Research

This source is highly relevant as a foundational reference for understanding the evolutionary conservation of IIS-mediated longevity. It provides:
- Comprehensive mapping of IIS pathway components in two key model organisms
- Evidence that the IIS-longevity link extends from invertebrates to humans
- Mechanistic basis for why IGF-1 modulation is a promising longevity intervention target
- Context for understanding FOXO3A human longevity associations

## Claims to Verify

- "Heterozygous IGF1 receptor-knockout mice (Igf1r+/-) live longer than wild-type" - attributed to Holzenberger et al., 2003, Nature 421, 182-187. This is a well-known finding but the magnitude of lifespan extension should be verified from the primary source.
- "Lower circulating IGF1 level correlates with mouse longevity" - attributed to Yuan et al., 2009. The specific correlation strength and whether it applies across all mouse strains should be verified.
- "Genetic variants of IIS components, including IGF1 receptor and FOXO3A, are associated with human longevity" - the specific variants and their effect sizes are not detailed in this review and would need verification from primary GWAS studies.
