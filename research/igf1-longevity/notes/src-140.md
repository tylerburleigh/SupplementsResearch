# src-140: Topical rapamycin reduces markers of senescence and aging in human skin

**Citation:** Chung CL, Lawrence I, Hoffman M, et al. *GeroScience* 41, 861-869 (2019). DOI: 10.1007/s11357-019-00113-y
**Trial Registration:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03103893
**Study Type:** Prospective, randomized, placebo-controlled, exploratory interventional trial

## Core Findings

Topical application of rapamycin (10 uM / 0.001%) to the dorsal hands of adults over 40 significantly reduced the senescence marker p16INK4A (P = 0.008) and increased collagen VII protein (P = 0.0077) in human skin after 6-8 months of daily treatment. Clinical improvement in skin aging (reduced wrinkles, increased dermal volume, improved tone) was observed in 11 of 13 assessed participants. This is among the first human trials demonstrating that rapamycin can reduce markers of aging in human tissue.

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity

The paper explicitly notes that the mTOR pathway is a direct target of the IGF-1 signaling pathway, which the authors describe as "a major driver of aging" (citing Kenyon 2010). Rapamycin inhibits mTOR, thereby modulating downstream effects of IGF-1 signaling. This provides a mechanistic link between IGF-1 signaling, mTOR inhibition, and measurable anti-aging effects in human tissue. However, the study does not directly measure IGF-1 levels or IGF-1 signaling activity.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Prospective, randomized, placebo-controlled, single-center trial at Drexel University College of Medicine
- **Population:** Adults >40 years with photoaging and dermal volume loss, no diabetes or hypercholesterolemia
- **Intervention:** Topical rapamycin 10 uM (0.001%) cream vs. placebo (DMSO vehicle) applied to dorsal hands every 24-48h for up to 8 months. Self-paired design (one hand rapamycin, one hand placebo).
- **Enrollment:** 36 participants
- **Completers:** 17 (53% attrition: 9 lost to follow-up, 7 non-compliant, 3 dropped out)
- **Biopsies with reliable material:** 8 pairs
- **Blood draws for systemic detection:** 13 (no detectable rapamycin in any sample)
- **Primary endpoint:** p16INK4A protein levels in skin biopsies
- **Secondary endpoints:** Other senescence markers, clinical skin appearance, histological markers
- **Clinical assessments:** Merz Hand Grading Scale, Likert wrinkle scale (0-4), modified Glogau Photoaging Classification
- **Molecular analyses:** IHC for p16INK4A, p21, tp53, collagen VII, cytokeratin 5/6; NanoString nCounter for 54 mRNAs

## Key Data Points

| Measure | Result | P-value |
|---------|--------|---------|
| p16INK4A protein reduction (IHC) | Significant decrease, primarily epidermal | P = 0.008 |
| Collagen VII protein increase (IHC) | Strong increase in basement membrane | P = 0.0077 |
| Collagen VII mRNA reduction (NanoString) | Most significant mRNA change | P = 0.025 (uncorrected) |
| p21 and tp53 protein trends | Trend toward reduction | NS |
| Clinical improvement | 11/13 subjects improved | -- |
| Systemic rapamycin detection | None detected (LOD 1 ng/ml) | -- |
| Adverse events (treatment-related) | None reported | -- |

## Limitations

1. **Very small effective sample size:** Only 8 paired biopsy samples produced reliable material for primary molecular analysis, from 17 completers out of 36 enrolled.
2. **High attrition:** 53% dropout rate (19/36), primarily due to loss to follow-up and non-compliance.
3. **Limited senescence markers:** Only p16INK4A showed statistically significant changes; p21 and tp53 did not reach significance. Authors acknowledge the limited marker set.
4. **Exploratory design:** No multiple testing correction on NanoString gene expression data (54 genes screened, best P = 0.025 uncorrected).
5. **No direct IGF-1 measurement:** The mechanistic link to IGF-1 signaling is stated in the introduction/discussion but not tested experimentally.
6. **Self-paired design:** Each participant served as their own control (rapamycin on one hand, placebo on the other), which controls for systemic factors but may introduce crossover effects.
7. **Short duration and tissue-specific:** Results limited to skin; cannot generalize to systemic aging or longevity outcomes.

## Claims to Verify

- The P-values (0.008 for p16INK4A, 0.0077 for collagen VII) are reported in both abstract and results but sample sizes for each specific test (n=8 for p16INK4A by IHC, n=6 for collagen VII IHC) are quite small for these significance levels -- worth confirming the statistical tests used.
- "No treatment-related adverse events" should be interpreted cautiously given small sample size and high attrition.
