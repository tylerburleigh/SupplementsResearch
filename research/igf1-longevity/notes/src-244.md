# src-244: Inflammaging: A New Immune-Metabolic Viewpoint for Age-Related Diseases

**Citation:** Franceschi C, Garagnani P, Parini P, Giuliani C, Santoro A. Nature Reviews Endocrinology (2018). DOI: 10.1038/s41574-018-0059-4.

**Quality:** ok (110,203 chars, alpha ratio 0.761, 1429 sentences). Full peer-reviewed review article from Nature Reviews Endocrinology.

## Core Findings

This review integrates the concepts of inflammaging (chronic, sterile, low-grade inflammation during ageing) and metaflammation (metabolic inflammation driven by nutrient excess), arguing they share largely overlapping molecular mechanisms. The authors propose that the seven pillars of ageing converge on inflammation, and that metabolic diseases can be conceptualized as accelerated ageing. They also review emerging biomarkers (DNA methylation, glycomics, metabolomics, lipidomics) capable of distinguishing biological from chronological age.

## Relevance to IGF-1 and Longevity

This paper has **limited direct relevance to IGF-1 specifically**. It mentions "insulin-like signalling" in the context of calorie restriction pathways (line 642), alongside insulin, mTOR-S6K, RAS-PKA, and SIRT1 activation. It does not discuss IGF-1 levels, IGF-1 receptor signaling, or IGF-1 modulation as an intervention. Its primary value for the research session is contextual: it establishes the inflammaging framework that connects nutrient-sensing pathways (including insulin/IGF signaling) to ageing, and it provides evidence on calorie restriction as a longevity intervention.

## Methodology

This is a narrative review article, not an original study. It synthesizes evidence from:
- Human centenarian studies (Italian, Chinese, Japanese cohorts)
- Mouse models of calorie restriction and hibernation
- Gut microbiota transplantation studies (aged to germ-free young mice)
- Biomarker studies (DNA methylation clocks, IgG glycomics, metabolomics/lipidomics)

No single sample size or statistical analysis applies, as the paper reviews multiple studies.

## Key Data Points

1. **Calorie restriction pathways:** Involves downregulation of insulin/IGF-like signaling, mTOR-S6K, and glucose-RAS-PKA, plus activation of SIRT1 (lines 641-653).
2. **Gut microbiota and inflammaging:** Aged mouse microbiota transplanted to young germ-free mice induces inflammaging, increasing TH cells and TNF (lines 522-539).
3. **Centenarian gut microbiota:** Unexpected increase in diversity in centenarians from Italy, China, and Japan, with enrichment of Akkermansia, Bifidobacterium, and Christensenellaceae in >105 years (lines 547-562).
4. **Protein restriction for longevity:** Reduction of dietary proteins/amino acids is the most effective macronutrient restriction; single amino acid (tryptophan) restriction can increase lifespan (lines 661-668).
5. **IgG glycomics as age biomarker:** IgG glycans account for up to 58% of variance in chronological age across 5,117 individuals from 4 European populations (lines 1219-1232).
6. **Metformin and glycomics:** 1,500 mg/day metformin for 2 months improved insulin sensitivity and plasma N-glycan profile in 38 prediabetes patients (lines 1328-1335).
7. **Horvath's epigenetic clock:** Based on 353 CpG sites; centenarians show decelerated methylation age, Down/Werner syndrome show accelerated age (lines 1068-1082).

## Limitations

1. **No IGF-1 focus:** The paper does not specifically address IGF-1 levels, signaling, or interventions targeting IGF-1.
2. **Review article:** No original data; relies on synthesis of published work.
3. **Gut microbiota causality:** While aged microbiota induces inflammaging in mice, human causality remains correlational.
4. **Heterogeneity:** The review covers broad topics (immunity, metabolism, gut microbiota, biomarkers) and does not provide deep mechanistic analysis of any single pathway.

## Summary Assessment

This is a high-quality, highly cited review in a top-tier journal. It provides strong contextual framework for understanding how nutrient-sensing pathways (including insulin/IGF signaling) relate to inflammaging and longevity. However, its direct evidence for IGF-1-specific research questions is limited. It is most useful as background for understanding the broader inflammaging context and calorie restriction mechanisms.

## Claims to Verify

- The claim that IgG glycans account for "up to 58% of the variance in chronological age" (line 1226) should be cross-referenced with the original Kristic et al. 2014 paper, as this is a high and potentially fragile number.
- The metformin study (n=38) is very small; the generalizability of the glycan profile findings is uncertain.
